# 1. Decision identity

The exact future question is:

> **What characteristics should an operational case possess to be considered
> suitable for bounded empirical-preparation consideration of H3?**

The question concerns the characteristics of a potentially suitable case or
scenario. It does not ask which particular case should be selected, whether H3
is true, or whether empirical work should be authorized.

A case may appear suitable for further preparation and later prove unusable
because access, Evidence, observability, authority, risk, or attribution is
insufficient. An operationally important case may be unsuitable for H3, while a
bounded ordinary case may expose H3's uncertainty more clearly. Suitability is
claim-relative and preparatory, not an endorsement of the case.

# 2. Purpose of H3 inquiry

H3 — Experience Amplification asks whether Management OS may enable managers to
obtain substantially more Learning from each completed operational case through
structured re-engagement with preserved operational history than from the
original real-world experience, ordinary recollection, or one-time review
alone.

The H3 uncertainty concerns additional, Evidence-grounded reflection from a
completed episode: recognition of missed signals, reconsideration of earlier
interpretations, examination of Decision-time uncertainty, comparison of
intended and actual Change, reflection on observed Outcome, and formation of
bounded lessons. It does not claim that more reflection is automatically better,
that an alternative Decision would have produced a known Outcome, or that one
case creates durable managerial capability.

```text
Experience Amplification
≠
Replay

Replay
≠
Alternative historical truth

Reflection
≠
Capability improvement
```

Replay can be one method of structured re-engagement, but H3 is broader than a
replay activity. Experience Amplification would require materially richer,
Evidence-grounded Learning, not merely navigation through a case or production
of additional commentary. H3 remains unvalidated, and this package creates no
empirical claim about it.

# 3. Candidate suitability dimensions

The following dimensions are qualitative and non-compensatory. They define no
score, weight, total, threshold, ranking, or automatic suitability result. A
strength on one dimension cannot offset a material Evidence, semantic,
authority, privacy, safety, or attribution problem on another.

## 3.1 Preserved operational history

The case should have a recoverable sequence from initial conditions and signals
through developing understanding, Decision where applicable, actual Change,
Outcome, and later revision. History should preserve versions and corrections
rather than present a polished retrospective narrative only.

Designed or stored chronology alone is insufficient. The history must retain
source identity, temporal boundaries, and known missingness so that later
reflection does not silently rewrite the episode.

## 3.2 Sufficient Decision traceability

Where a Decision occurred, the case should make its attributable human owner,
Evidence basis, uncertainty, alternatives, rationale, constraints, and timing
reconstructable. A final Decision label without its decision-time context offers
little basis for H3 reflection.

Traceability does not mean the Decision was correct, optimal, or fully
documented. The case should preserve what cannot be reconstructed as uncertainty.

## 3.3 Reconstructable Context

Material operational context and relationships should be recoverable without
requiring omniscience. The manager should be able to distinguish what was known
at the time, what emerged later, and what remains absent or disputed.

Context richness should be bounded to the H3 question. Maximum detail can
increase burden, obscure turning points, and create an unrealistic impression
of completeness.

## 3.4 Identifiable turning points

The case should contain moments at which new Evidence, revised Interpretation,
a constraint, a Decision, a Change deviation, or an Outcome altered the
managerial understanding. These moments give structured re-engagement something
material to revisit.

Turning points should be discoverable from the history rather than authored as
a hidden answer. A facilitator or AI must not predetermine which moment is the
correct explanation.

## 3.5 Observable Outcome

The case should contain an observed Outcome relevant to the episode, including
positive, negative, mixed, null, contrary, or indeterminate consequences. The
Outcome should be distinguishable from activity, completion, intended effect,
and retrospective opinion.

Outcome observability for case suitability does not establish Evidence
sufficiency, attribution, product contribution, or validation readiness.

## 3.6 Preserved uncertainty

The history should retain unresolved questions, competing explanations,
missing Evidence, dissent, measurement limitations, and attribution boundaries.
H3 reflection becomes misleading if the record converts uncertainty into a
single settled causal story.

Sufficient uncertainty means the case supports honest reconsideration. It does
not mean the case should be artificially ambiguous or impossible to understand.

## 3.7 Meaningful alternative reasoning opportunities

The episode should permit plausible alternative interpretations, questions,
information-seeking choices, or Decisions grounded in what was knowable at a
particular point. Alternatives should expose reasoning limits and tradeoffs,
not reward one prewritten answer.

An alternative branch is modeled reasoning. It is not an alternative historical
Decision, actual Change, or Outcome.

## 3.8 Replay potential

The preserved sequence should be capable of staged re-engagement so that later
information can be withheld during Decision-time reflection and restored
without changing the historical record. Original and modeled branches must
remain distinguishable.

Replay potential is one suitability consideration, not a requirement that H3
be implemented as a replay feature or validation protocol.

## 3.9 Learning richness

The case should offer potential for bounded Learning about missed signals,
assumptions, tradeoffs, semantic handoffs, Change fidelity, Outcome
interpretation, attribution limits, or conditions under which a lesson does not
transfer.

Learning richness is not the quantity of insights, comments, branches, or time
spent. A concise limitation grounded in Evidence may be richer than numerous
generic reflections.

## 3.10 Authority visibility

The case should make visible who owned operational Decisions, source records,
access, Change, Outcome interpretation, and later Learning adoption. Distributed
or disputed authority can be suitable when it remains explicit rather than
being silently collapsed.

AI, a replay tool, or a case facilitator cannot inherit operational or
interpretive authority through the exercise.

## 3.11 Evidence diversity

The case may benefit from several relevant source types, perspectives, and
temporal records with provenance, contestability, and correction history.
Diversity can expose relationships and disagreement that a single final summary
would hide.

More recorded material is not automatically more or sufficient Evidence.
Modeled content, AI output, recollection, and source-owned observations retain
different evidentiary positions.

## 3.12 Manageable scope

The episode should be bounded enough for participants to reconstruct its
material history and revisit reasoning without turning H3 into a broad
organizational transformation, implementation program, or indefinite archive
review.

Scope should be proportionate to the H3 uncertainty and participant burden.
Manageability does not mean triviality or operational insignificance.

## 3.13 Qualitative use of the dimensions

The dimensions should be discussed together while their boundaries remain
distinct. A future consideration may record strengths, limitations, missing
conditions, and unresolved questions. It must not calculate an aggregate
suitability result, rank candidates, or infer selection from the number of
dimensions a case appears to cover.

# 4. Unsuitable characteristics

A candidate should not be treated as suitable for further H3 preparation when
material characteristics such as the following are present and cannot be
bounded or corrected without changing its identity:

- **Trivial case:** the episode contains too little managerial uncertainty,
  consequence, or reasoning to support material re-engagement.
- **Predetermined answer:** the narrative, title, facilitator, or generated
  content embeds a preferred Cause, Decision, or lesson.
- **Almost no preserved context:** the participant must invent rather than
  reconstruct the conditions and relationships relevant to the episode.
- **Outcome already obvious:** later consequences are supplied as a moral or
  causal answer rather than an observed, contestable Outcome.
- **No real managerial reasoning:** the episode is merely a routine activity or
  automated event with no reconstructable human judgment relevant to H3.
- **Missing history:** the sequence, Decision-time information, actual Change,
  or later Outcome cannot be recovered sufficiently even for suitability
  preparation.
- **Impossible replay:** original and later information cannot be separated, or
  a modeled branch would overwrite the preserved historical record.
- **Insufficient uncertainty:** no plausible alternative Interpretation,
  limitation, missed signal, or unresolved condition remains.
- **Unrealistic completeness:** the case supplies every fact, relationship, and
  causal link conveniently, teaching certainty rather than Evidence discipline.
- **Authority collapse:** the case lets AI, a workflow, or a generic team decide
  what belonged to accountable human or source authority.
- **Outcome-reward design:** the authored Outcome exists to validate one
  Decision or produce a desired lesson.
- **Disproportionate risk or burden:** privacy, safety, employment, customer,
  legal, or operational consequences cannot be bounded for preparatory use.

Unsuitability for H3 does not mean the operational episode is unimportant,
invalid, poorly managed, or unsuitable for another legitimate purpose.

# 5. Synthetic contribution

Synthetic cases may help:

- train participants to separate Evidence, Interpretation, Decision, Change,
  Outcome, and Learning;
- test staged disclosure and replay interaction;
- expose semantic and authority risks;
- explore alternative-reasoning prompts without affecting real operations;
- test whether modeled and historical branches remain distinguishable;
- inspect UX, information-model, workflow, and AI-reasoning behavior; and
- reveal when H3 or a suitability question is too broad.

Synthetic cases cannot establish:

- empirical Evidence;
- observed product value;
- actual Experience Amplification;
- historical completeness;
- factual Outcome;
- causal contribution;
- H3 validation or validation readiness;
- real-case suitability; or
- superiority of one case, scenario, or hypothesis.

No synthetic case is considered or selected by this package. All six cases in
the fixed pool remain modeled, training-only, and non-empirical regardless of
their conceptual richness or replay potential.

# 6. Historical case considerations

A historical case could be relevant to H3 when it is a real completed episode
with recoverable provenance, context, human reasoning, actual Change where
applicable, observed Outcome, uncertainty, corrections, and legitimate access
boundaries. Its preserved history should support comparison between what was
knowable at the time and what became visible later.

Historical consideration should examine:

- whether the episode boundary is coherent and complete enough for the bounded
  reflection question;
- whether Decision-time and later information can be separated;
- whether source identity and correction history remain recoverable;
- whether original participants, later actors, or both could re-engage without
  hindsight being presented as prior knowledge;
- whether ordinary recollection or one-time review offers a legitimate
  reference for the H3 uncertainty;
- whether privacy, consent, access, safety, and organizational-memory boundaries
  can be preserved;
- whether selection bias favors unusually memorable or well-documented cases;
  and
- whether later operational developments would confound the source of any
  additional Learning.

Real history does not guarantee complete Evidence, reliable recollection,
replay feasibility, or suitability. This package identifies no historical case.

# 7. Live case considerations

A live bounded case could be relevant when a real operational episode can be
observed prospectively or contemporaneously and later completed history may
support structured re-engagement. The live context may preserve decision-time
Evidence more faithfully, but it also introduces operational, ethical,
participation, observation, and timing risks.

Live-case consideration should examine:

- whether the case can reach a meaningful completion and Outcome within a
  bounded horizon;
- whether observation can preserve rather than distort accountable managerial
  reasoning;
- whether participation and Evidence access could be separately and lawfully
  authorized;
- whether validation interests might influence the operational Decision or
  Change;
- whether intended and actual Change can remain distinct;
- whether later re-engagement can occur without delaying operational response;
- whether novelty or observation effects would dominate reflection; and
- whether adverse, null, mixed, and indeterminate histories would remain
  available rather than being filtered out.

A live case is not selected or authorized here. Suitability consideration would
not grant observation, participation, Evidence access, validation, or
operational authority.

# 8. Replay considerations

## What makes replay meaningful?

Replay is meaningful when it stages a preserved episode at genuine turning
points, presents only information available at the relevant historical moment,
retains source provenance and uncertainty, invites several plausible reasoning
paths, and later restores the actual Decision, Change, and Outcome without
overwriting them. It should make the learner explain Evidence use, assumptions,
authority, tradeoffs, and limits.

Meaningful replay can support reflection on why an alternative seemed plausible
and what additional Evidence might have mattered. It does not need to calculate
an alternative Outcome.

## When does replay become superficial?

Replay becomes superficial when it:

- reveals the known Outcome before decision-time reasoning;
- rewards recall of the historical answer;
- offers cosmetic branches that converge on a preferred choice;
- supplies complete causal knowledge unavailable in the episode;
- counts interaction, time, or branch volume as Learning;
- converts AI-generated explanations into Evidence;
- ignores authority, missingness, or actual Change fidelity; or
- produces a modeled Outcome presented as what would have happened.

## How does replay differ from historical reconstruction?

Historical reconstruction asks what actually occurred, what was known, how
understanding changed, what humans decided, what Change was enacted, and what
Outcome was observed. Its claims remain bounded by historical Evidence and
missingness.

Replay creates a modeled branch for alternative reasoning using preserved
historical inputs. The branch may explore another Interpretation, question,
Evidence request, or Decision, but it is not the historical Decision and cannot
establish an alternative actual Change or Outcome.

```text
Historical reconstruction ≠ replay branch
Alternative reasoning ≠ alternative historical truth
Modeled Change ≠ actual Change
Modeled Outcome ≠ observed Outcome
```
