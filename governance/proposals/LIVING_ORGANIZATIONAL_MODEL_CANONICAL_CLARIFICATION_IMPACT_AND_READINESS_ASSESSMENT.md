## Existing ownership preserved

- Operational Operating Model: sole organization-relative representation, stable references, topology assertions, provenance, sensitivity, freshness, correction.
- Operational System: real-world bounded system and process participation/condition semantics.
- Source systems/capabilities: native department/person/system/SOP/report/meeting/artifact content where authoritative.
- Owner Identity: Account, Workspace, Membership, authentication, authorization identity.
- Operational Matter: situation identity, macro-state, lifecycle, lineage, Decision/Outcome references.
- Investigation: inquiry, Evidence use, reasoning, uncertainty, process-understanding projection.
- Improvement: optional deliberate-change work continuity.
- Case Evidence Set: Evidence classification, influence, provenance, snapshots.
- Decision/Impact/Execution/Outcome/Stabilization: respective human judgment and source-owned post-Decision records.
- Operational Memory: permission-aware historical indexing and pattern context.
- Living Organizational Model surfaces: projections only.

## New canonical responsibility assessment

The names **Operating Model Element Reference** and **Operating Model Relationship Assertion** make existing Operating Model duties structurally explicit. They do not introduce an independent semantic owner.

InformationContribution adds a genuinely new named relationship specialization because existing Matter-scoped Artifact Relationship semantics cannot represent it. The new responsibility is narrowly bounded under the existing Operating Model relationship owner and does not create:

- an InformationContribution aggregate;
- a Person-owned history store;
- a source-content duplicate;
- an Evidence relationship;
- a new top-level architecture/capability;
- a seventh P1–P6 view.

## Risks for independent review

1. “Operating-Model-owned element” could become an unbounded master-data store unless minimum identity/content is constrained by kind and source availability.
2. Relationship Assertion may be too broad unless family-specific endpoint constraints and mutation owners remain mandatory.
3. InformationContribution under Operating Model authority may conflict with source-context ownership if its context or material content is copied.
4. Named-person Element Reference may be mistaken for Account, HR identity, or consent authority.
5. Current-state derivation from multiple assertions may require an authority precedence rule beyond effective time and dispute state.
6. Permitted deletion with a tombstone may either leak restricted existence or lose necessary reference integrity unless the tombstone is explicitly non-reconstructive and authorization-bound.
7. “Reviewed Operating Model update” needs a precise human authority boundary without creating a new workflow/lifecycle.
8. Cross-reference amendments could expand beyond clarification and accidentally resume Engineering or implementation.

## Adoption readiness disposition

**READY FOR INDEPENDENT CRITICAL REVIEW; NOT READY FOR CANONICAL ADOPTION UNTIL REVIEW, CORRECTION, AND REPEAT REVIEW COMPLETE.**
