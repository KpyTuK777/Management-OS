## C1 — hard boundary for Operating-Model-owned element content

When no authoritative source owns an eligible organizational element, the Operating Model may own only the minimum organization-relative representation needed for governed topology:

- stable Workspace-bounded identity;
- element kind;
- human label;
- concise operational purpose or scope only where necessary to distinguish/use the element;
- current/temporal applicability and retirement state;
- source/provenance, authorization, sensitivity, freshness, and correction metadata;
- explicit unknown/disputed/incomplete state;
- references to separately owned domain content.

It must not absorb:

- HR/personnel identity or employment records;
- Account, authentication, Membership, contact, compensation, performance, or sensitive personal data;
- SOP content/version authority;
- system configuration, credentials, telemetry, or administration;
- report/meeting content, attendance, communications, recordings, or calendar commitment state;
- KPI observations, targets, calculations, or performance conclusions;
- process execution, Evidence, Investigation/Improvement state, Decision, Outcome, or Memory content;
- any field merely because an integration or authoritative source is unavailable.

Missing source content remains unknown, unavailable, or represented by a bounded source reference. Lack of integration does not transfer source authority to the Operating Model.

## C2 — source-authority precedence for current state

Current-state derivation follows authority before time:

1. an authorized authoritative source record governs within its declared scope;
2. an accountable-human-approved Operating-Model-owned assertion governs only where no higher authoritative source owns that meaning;
3. attributable declarations and observations remain qualified accounts;
4. derived or inferred representations remain explicit projections and cannot become governed fact without human/source approval.

Recording recency, repetition, system origin, contributor seniority, projection use, or Watson output cannot change this order.

When applicable records conflict:

- conflicts between equal-authority assertions produce disputed state until attributable human/source resolution;
- a lower-authority account cannot displace a higher-authority record but remains inspectable when materially relevant and permitted;
- a stale or unavailable higher-authority source may make current state stale/unknown rather than allow silent promotion of a lower source;
- source correction supersedes the Operating Model’s cached/reference projection according to source authority while preserving justified historical snapshots;
- privacy filtering occurs before any current-state result is projected.

The Operating Model may reconcile and record the result only through valid source or human authority; it does not calculate truth by timestamp.

## C3 — InformationContribution content and context non-ownership

InformationContribution owns only the attributable supplied-by relationship and its relationship-specific metadata.

Its material and context roles must be stable external references:

- material/information content remains owned by its Artifact, report, communication, Investigation material, or other source capability;
- Investigation, Improvement, Decision work, Matter, or other context retains identity, state, access, and lifecycle;
- Evidence inclusion, classification, influence, and snapshots remain with Case Evidence Set;
- source-content correction remains with the source owner.

The InformationContribution may preserve only:

- source material ID and source owner;
- exact source version or retrieval time where applicable;
- a minimum faithful excerpt or display transformation when necessary for attributable inspection;
- transformation class, creator, time, and limitation;
- relationship metadata adopted in the proposal.

A stored excerpt is not independent content authority, Evidence, or a substitute when the source becomes inaccessible. Redaction/deletion/correction of source content propagates according to policy while justified historical snapshots remain owned by their consuming capability.

## C4 — privacy-safe deleted-reference integrity

A deletion tombstone is an authorization-bound integrity marker, not ordinary organizational content or a universal visible placeholder.

Rules:

- deleted stable IDs are never reused, reassigned, or silently resolved to another element;
- ordinary current projections, search, counts, Map edges, Workbenches, suggestions, Memory, exports, derived indexes, and Watson do not expose the tombstone;
- an authorized historical consumer receives only the minimum state needed to understand that a previously referenced record is unavailable/deleted, without reconstructive attributes or forbidden identity;
- an unauthorized consumer receives non-revealing absence and cannot distinguish deletion from other unavailability;
- the tombstone retains only Workspace, non-reusable identifier, deletion/integrity class, authorized timestamp/actor reference where policy permits, and minimum lineage needed to prevent false resolution;
- decision-time snapshots remain immutable historical records but apply current redaction/access policy and may show a non-reconstructive deleted-source limitation;
- derived caches/indexes must remove reconstructive content and preserve only authorized integrity behavior.

Whether a tombstone is retained at all follows the adopted retention/deletion policy and cannot be inferred from technical convenience.

## C5 — explicit reviewed Operating Model update authority

A reviewed Operating Model update requires the following authority chain:

```text
proposal or source change
  -> validation against owning source and scope
  -> accountable human/source-owner approval
  -> Operating Model record/reconciliation action
  -> attributable history and projection refresh
```

- For source-backed elements or relationships, the native source owner approves and performs the source change; the Operating Model reconciles its reference/assertion after validation.
- For Operating-Model-owned elements or assertions, the identifiable human holding delegated Operating Model semantic/update authority approves the change within scope.
- Record custody or synchronization may faithfully apply an already authorized result but cannot create approval or alter meaning.
- Investigation, Improvement, Decision Laboratory, Operational Impact Review, Monitoring, Stabilization, Operational Memory, Watson, integrations, and projections may propose, reference, or explain a candidate; none may approve or apply an Operating Model update by itself.
- A Decision about operational change does not automatically mutate the model; source validation and the applicable update authority are still required.
- Update history records actor, authority basis, source, effective time, recording time, previous/new assertion, rationale, and unresolved uncertainty.

This clarification allocates authority; it does not create a new workflow, macro-state, queue, or implementation service.

## Corrected canonical reading

With this supplement:

- reviewed updates require explicit source or accountable-human authority;
- projections and automation remain consumers, never semantic owners.
