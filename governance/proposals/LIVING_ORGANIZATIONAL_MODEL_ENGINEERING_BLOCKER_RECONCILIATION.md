## 1. Organizational element identity and ownership — resolved

The adopted **Operating Model Element Reference** supplies one stable, Workspace-bounded identity. It is source-backed where a higher authoritative source exists and may be minimally Operating-Model-owned only where no such source owns the organization-relative identity. Its content ceiling limits the fallback to identity, kind, label, topology purpose or scope, temporal status, provenance, access, freshness, correction state, and source references. External HR, SOP, system, report, meeting, KPI, Evidence, execution, Investigation, and Improvement truth remains externally owned.

Engineering may therefore represent organization-relative elements without selecting a competing canonical owner or copying source content.

## 2. Organizational relationship ownership — resolved

The adopted **Operating Model Relationship Assertion** owns typed, temporal, governed organization-relative relationships under the Operational Operating Model. Its adopted families cover containment, reporting, role occupancy, department membership, responsibility and decision authority, operational participation, dependency, information/material flow, process/system/SOP/measure association, interdepartmental interaction, work/history linkage, and InformationContribution.

Engineering must not reuse Matter-scoped Artifact relationships as organizational authority. It may implement a distinct repository contract that materializes these adopted Operating Model semantics without creating a second model.

## 3. InformationContribution allocation — resolved

InformationContribution is adopted as a specialized Operating Model Relationship Assertion. It remains distinct from source-content ownership, authorship, responsibility, subject, recorder, approval, Decision, Evidence, credibility, and trustworthiness. Material and work context stay externally owned and are referenced, not copied. One governed relationship may therefore feed multiple authorized projections without duplication.

## 4. Temporal, correction, and current-state authority — resolved

The clarification distinguishes effective, recording, observation/communication, source-version/retrieval, decision-snapshot, correction, dispute, supersession, withdrawal, redaction, and deletion times. Current state is derived by authority before recency: authoritative source; then accountable-human-approved Operating Model assertion where no higher source exists; then qualified declarations/observations; then explicit derived or inferred projection.

It also allocates update authority: native source owners change source-backed state; identifiable humans with applicable semantic authority approve Operating-Model-owned changes; custody and synchronization only apply an authorized result. Engineering may now choose a minimal version-and-assertion pattern while preserving these distinctions and historical context.

## 5. Projection invariants — resolved

The adopted invariants require one governed identity, one mutation owner, authorization and privacy filtering before projection, qualified current versus historical state, references rather than copies, no context promotion to Evidence or Decision, propagation of correction/redaction/deletion, separation of UI state, preservation of each work capability's ownership, and filtered non-authoritative future AI consumption.

These invariants bound Organization Map, Workbenches, Investigation, Improvement, Operational Memory, and future Watson context without making any projection authoritative.
