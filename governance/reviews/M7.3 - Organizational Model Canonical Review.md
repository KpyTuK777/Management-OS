## Review Authority and Scope

This independent Canonical Review evaluates the exact Architecture Approved,
dependency-use revision against the canonical Constitution, canonical

repository-native M7.3 order. It records a disposition; it does not amend the
model or perform the separate transition required for canonical authority.

## Canonical Findings

### 1. Constitutional consistency

**Pass.** Every function derives from a constitutional power, invariant, or
accountability requirement. No assignment overrides constitutional precedence,
expands authority through silence, or conflicts with accountable human
authority.

### 2. Separation of powers

**Pass.** Direction, Creation, Judgment, and Transition remain distinct in
responsibility, permitted action, and prohibited substitution. Multiple
functions may be assigned to one human only when acts remain separately
attributable and cannot self-authorize.

### 3. Organizational coherence

**Pass.** Direction supplies bounded purpose; Creation prepares the result;
Judgment independently evaluates it; Transition establishes only an authorized
state; Semantic Stewardship protects source-owned meaning; and Record Custody
faithfully preserves established state. No function depends on unowned
authority.

### 4. Hidden authority and unique semantic ownership

**Pass.** Capability, access, seniority, automation, custom, participation, and
record maintenance explicitly create no authority. Semantic Stewardship serves
the one owning source and cannot transfer, duplicate, or silently amend its
meaning. No second governance control plane is introduced.

### 5. Accountability completeness

**Pass.** Every function has explicit answerability. Every consequential act
must identify the accountable human authority, source and scope, delegation,
affected semantic owner, judgment authority, and transition authority.
Accountability cannot terminate at a tool, AI system, team label, repository,
process, or unnamed collective.

### 6. Delegation integrity

**Pass.** Delegation is a bounded, attributable, inspectable, revocable subset
of existing authority. It cannot enlarge power, transfer constitutional source,
erase accountability, enable unbounded redelegation, or delegate away
independent judgment and human answerability. Automation remains a bounded
delegate rather than an authority source.

### 7. Decision-class exclusivity

**Pass.** Purpose, priority, production, semantic, conformance,
state-transition, and record decisions each have one owner and an explicit
prohibited substitution. Participation in one class grants no authority in
another; semantic proposals remain subject to independent judgment and
legitimate transition.

### 8. Governance-body boundary

**Pass.** The model creates no mandatory body. Any later body remains optional,
scope-bounded, attributable to humans, subject to the same separation and
delegation constraints, and incapable of acquiring authority by existence or
practice.

### 9. Implementation independence

**Pass.** Functions are title-independent and remain valid across changes in
people, organization size, AI systems, tooling, repositories, platforms, and
methods. No current implementation mechanism is a hidden prerequisite.

### 10. Later-stage boundary

**Pass.** The interaction model defines logical authority dependencies and
explicitly denies that they are a workflow, review procedure, sequence,
cadence, gate implementation, or autonomous protocol. M7.4–M7.7 substance is
excluded.

### 11. Stable downstream foundation

**Pass.** Later governance can assign functions and define authorized mechanics
without changing the enduring allocation, separation, delegation,
accountability, and decision boundaries.

## Risk Assessment

No blocking canonical risk remains.

One advisory risk is retained: M7.4 or M7.5 could incorrectly encode the
model's logical relationship among powers as a self-authorizing operational
sequence. The model already prohibits that interpretation. Future review must
test lifecycle and protocol definitions against this boundary; no M7.3
correction is required.

## Disposition

```text
Canonical
```

The Organizational Model is suitable for canonicalization as the stable
organizational foundation for later stages. This review authorizes but does not
perform the separate canonicalization and repository reconciliation transition.
M7.3 remains the sole active stage and M7.4 is not opened by this review.
