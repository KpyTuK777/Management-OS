## Canonical Findings

### 1. Internal consistency

**Pass.** Purpose, authority, immutable principles, hierarchy, separation of
powers, invariants, amendment principles, precedence, interpretation, scope, and
conformance describe one coherent constitutional system. No section grants a
power that another section constitutionally prohibits.

The preservation of legitimately established product purpose permits valid
future product change while the prohibition on informal purpose drift remains
intact.

### 2. Constitutional authority integrity

**Pass.** Constitutional authority is expressly bounded to development
governance. It remains subordinate to applicable external obligations and
non-delegable human responsibility, does not replace accountable product and
business authority, and does not absorb product or architecture semantic
ownership.

The scope-aware hierarchy prevents precedence from becoming authority outside a
source's owned concern.

### 3. Coherence of principles

**Pass.** The fourteen immutable principles and twenty-three invariants are
mutually reinforcing:

- accountable human authority and automation integrity preserve responsibility;
- bounded authority and the bounded effect of silence prevent both implied power
  and unintended paralysis of legitimately delegated lower governance;
- separation, non-self-approval, evidence integrity, and transition authority
  prevent self-authorizing change;
- unique ownership, scope restraint, and precedence prevent semantic capture;
- traceable history, controlled evolution, and amendment integrity prevent
  silent constitutional change;
- legitimacy over power and principled reconciliation resolve concurrent
  principles without maximizing institutional reach.

No principle requires the abolition of another. Irreconcilable material change
is correctly routed to explicit constitutional amendment rather than creative
interpretation.

### 4. Terminology consistency

**Pass.** `Authority`, `scope`, `semantic owner`, `direction`, `creation`,
`judgment`, `transition`, `evidence`, `interpretation`, `amendment`, and
`canonical` retain consistent meanings throughout the document.

`Accountable human authority` is defined as a constitutional quality of
identifiable, answerable human responsibility and is not used as a future role
or organizational body.

### 5. Hierarchy and interpretation compatibility

**Pass.** The hierarchy establishes precedence while interpretation first tests
scope, construes power narrowly, preserves valid specific refinement, and
requires whole-document coherence. Constitutional silence neither grants new
authority nor cancels conforming authority already delegated.

The legitimacy-over-power meta-principle prevents the hierarchy from being read
as permission to maximize institutional discretion.

### 6. Amendment integrity

**Pass.** Amendment is explicit, attributable, independently judged,
non-retroactive, and history-preserving. Ordinary interpretation, subordinate
governance, implementation, precedent, urgency, and operational necessity cannot
amend the Constitution indirectly.

Section 3 protects the substance of every immutable principle. The protected
principles enumerated in amendment principle 9 reinforce that general rule and
do not create an implied permission to abolish unlisted immutable principles,
because whole-document coherence and explicit protected-substance language
control interpretation.

### 7. Unique semantic ownership

**Pass.** The Constitution solely owns constitutional legitimacy and limits
within development governance. It expressly preserves canonical product,
professional, domain, experience, presentation, and architecture ownership. It
creates no second roadmap, backlog, architecture process, product model, or
implementation authority.

### 8. Later-stage boundary integrity

**Pass.** The Constitution defines powers as enduring constitutional concepts
without allocating them to future roles. It defines no organizational chart,
reporting line, governance body, operational workflow, review procedure,
Development Lifecycle, autonomous execution protocol, methodology, repository
structure, or M7.3–M7.7 substance.

### 9. Implementation independence

**Pass.** The Constitution remains valid across changes in organization,
lifecycle, automation, platform, tooling, storage, interface, and
implementation. Repository-visible governance truth is explicitly defined as
durable record authority rather than dependence on a particular repository
product.

No hidden dependency on Git, GitHub, a current directory layout, a present role
assignment, or an implementation mechanism exists in the constitutional text.

### 10. Compatibility with M6 and M7.1

**Pass.** The Constitution preserves M6 professional, domain, experience,
presentation, adapter, accessibility, mapping, and conformance ownership. It
constrains governance legitimacy without reinterpreting M6 meaning.

It conforms to M7.1 by remaining normative, separately ordered, evidence-backed,
independently reviewed, correctly indexed, status-explicit, and unable to
self-canonicalize.

### 11. Stability for subsequent stages

**Pass.** M7.3 Organizational Model, M7.4 Development Lifecycle, M7.5 Autonomous
Development Protocol, M7.6 Retrospective, and M7.7 Governance Review can depend
on the Constitution without requiring it to encode their organization,
procedures, automation, or tooling.

The Constitution states what must remain true and remains stable if those later
mechanisms change.
