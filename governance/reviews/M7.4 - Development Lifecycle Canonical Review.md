## Canonical Findings

### 1. Constitutional and organizational consistency

**Pass.** Every lifecycle stage and gate preserves the canonical Constitution's
bounded authority, accountable human authority, separation of powers,
non-self-approval, evidence integrity, traceable change, and implementation
independence. Gate ownership conforms to the canonical Organizational Model.

### 2. Authority-state semantics

**Pass.** Directed, In Creation, Ready for Judgment, Under Judgment, Correction
Required, Transition Ready, Authoritative, and Closed or Superseded are durable
authority conditions. They are explicitly distinguished from workflow, tool,
repository, ticket, cadence, and execution states.

### 3. Separation and non-self-authorization

**Pass.** Direction, Creation, Judgment, and Transition remain separately
attributable. No lifecycle state, activity, artifact, completion event,
publication, commitment, deployment, elapsed time, or repeated use authorizes
its own progression.

### 4. Judgment, correction, and iteration

**Pass.** Creation cannot approve its result; Judgment cannot invent evidence
or perform transition; correction cannot close its own finding; and substantive
change requires renewed independent judgment. Iteration, concurrency, and
severable progression preserve identity, scope, evidence, and judgment.

### 5. Transition restraint

**Pass.** Transition Authority may establish only the state and scope permitted
by an attributable judgment. It cannot broaden the disposition, repair the
result, infer missing judgment, or silently transfer approval to changed
substance.

### 6. Traceability and history

**Pass.** Lifecycle records preserve direction, accountable authority,
delegation, semantic ownership, evidence, judgment, correction, transition,
closure, withdrawal, and supersession. Prior authority and failed or corrected
states cannot be silently erased.

### 7. Implementation and automation boundary

**Pass.** Lifecycle meaning is independent of people, titles, organization,
method, automation, tools, repositories, platforms, cadence, and implementation.
No construct grants autonomous execution, AI-agent authority, automation
permission, retry, scheduling, queueing, orchestration, or recovery behavior.

### 8. Stable M7.5 foundation

**Pass.** A future Autonomous Development Protocol may define authorized
execution mechanics while remaining constrained by M7.4's authority states,
gate ownership, fixed-subject judgment, transition restraint, traceability, and
human-accountability invariants. M7.4 does not predefine or authorize that work.
