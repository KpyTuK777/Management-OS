## Findings

1. The lifecycle stages are enduring authority conditions rather than workflow,
   tooling, repository, cadence, or execution states.
2. Direction, Creation, Judgment, and Transition own distinct gates, and no
   stage, activity, artifact, or elapsed time authorizes its own progression.
3. Creation cannot approve its result; evidence cannot issue a verdict;
   Judgment cannot perform transition; and Transition cannot broaden or repair
   the disposition it receives.
4. Correction, re-entry, iteration, concurrency, and severable progression
   preserve fixed review subjects, independent re-judgment, semantic ownership,
   and complete prior history.
5. Traceability preserves the authority source, accountable human authority,
   evidence, judgment, correction, transition, closure, and supersession record
   without selecting a storage or repository implementation.
6. Failure, urgency, degraded capability, operational need, publication,
   commitment, deployment, or repeated use cannot create authority or bypass a
   gate.
7. The lifecycle remains independent of people, titles, organizational design,
   automation, tools, repositories, platforms, cadence, and implementation
   methodology.
8. Autonomous execution, AI-agent coordination, automation permissions, retry,
   scheduling, queueing, orchestration, and M7.5–M7.7 substance remain excluded.

No blocking Architecture Review finding remains unresolved.

## Advisory Observations

- M7.5 must map any operational or autonomous behavior onto these authority
  states without treating stage progression as execution permission.
- Any future automation of gates must preserve the owning constitutional
  function, attributable human accountability, fixed review subject, and the
  prohibition against self-authorization.
- Tool or workflow states may represent lifecycle state but must not become an
  independent authority source.

These observations preserve downstream review concerns and require no M7.4
correction.
