1. reconstruct the authority chain from constitutional direction through

   independent review, canonical disposition where applicable, separate

# 5. Integrated Governance Architecture

## 5.1 Authority chain

The integrated authority chain is coherent:

```text
Product direction
    ↓ bounded by
Constitutional authority and prohibitions
    ↓ expressed through
Organizational functions and separation of powers
    ↓ governed across
Development Lifecycle authority states and gates
    ↓ constrains
Autonomous protocol participation and execution
    ↓ observed by
Historical retrospective and final governance review
```

No lower layer creates authority for a higher layer. The retrospective and this
review observe the system but do not legislate for it.

## 5.2 Separation of powers

Direction, creation, judgment, and transition remain separately owned. Product
direction cannot approve architecture; creation cannot approve its own output;
evidence cannot issue judgment; review does not itself perform transition; and
transition cannot broaden the reviewed scope.

The same separation persists under autonomous participation. Protocol identity
does not become governance identity, delegation does not transfer authority,
and autonomous output remains subject to the same evidence and review gates.

## 5.3 State coherence

The Governance Structure defines repository artifact states, the Development
Lifecycle defines authority states for governed work, and the Autonomous
Development Protocol defines protocol conditions. These state domains interact
without collapsing into one another. Progress, execution, evidence production,
or protocol recovery cannot silently satisfy a governance gate.

## 5.4 Evidence, judgment, and transition

The corpus consistently treats evidence as attributable support rather than
judgment. Reviews own explicit findings and dispositions. Canonical authority
arises only after the required positive review and a separate repository
transition. The recorded M7 history demonstrates these distinctions under both
ordinary progression and correction.

## 5.5 Human accountability and autonomous execution

The Autonomous Development Protocol permits bounded autonomous contribution
without creating autonomous governance authority. Accountable constitutional
functions retain acceptance, review, interruption, recovery, and transition
responsibility. Silence, elapsed time, execution success, and generated evidence
do not imply authorization.

## 5.6 Historical learning

M7.6 records process strengths, friction, classifications, recommendations, and
residual risks without modifying canonical governance. Its advisory status
preserves institutional learning while preventing retrospective interpretation
from becoming an amendment shortcut.

# 6. Completeness Evaluation

The governance system contains the minimum enduring elements necessary for its
declared scope:

- a repository-visible governance structure and artifact taxonomy;
- a constitution establishing authority, limits, and interpretation rules;
- an organizational model assigning functions and preserving separation;
- a lifecycle defining authority states, gates, transitions, and traceability;
- an autonomous protocol subordinating autonomous execution to that framework;
- a historical retrospective preserving lessons without amendment authority;
- independent review records, correction evidence, and separate transition
  history;
- a final Governance Review gate for integrated milestone evaluation.

No required M7.1–M7.6 artifact or authority transition is absent from the fixed
baseline. Completeness here is architectural completeness within the declared
M7 scope; it is not a claim that governance can never evolve.

# 7. Internal Coherence Evaluation

Terminology, ownership, dependency direction, and authority boundaries align
across the corpus:

- constitutional functions remain stable across organization, lifecycle, and
  protocol layers;
- lifecycle stages remain authority states rather than workflow states;
- organizational roles do not acquire powers through execution or delegation;
- evidence remains non-authoritative until judged at the proper gate;
- review and canonicalization remain distinct events;
- historical and advisory artifacts cannot amend normative sources;
- indexes and control-plane records report authority but do not create it.

No contradictory ownership, circular authority dependency, hidden approval
path, or inconsistent state definition was found.

# 8. Operational Consistency Evaluation

The recorded M7 lifecycle shows that the governance architecture operated
consistently when definitions passed directly, when substantive correction was
required, and when only governance evidence was deficient. Missing review
records and immutable revision identity stopped progression until corrected.
Incorrect successor terminology was rejected in favor of the canonical stage
identity. Earlier evidence remained preserved.

The process required manual reconciliation across several files, as M7.6
observed. That is procedural friction and a future improvement opportunity, not
an architectural inconsistency. The actual transitions retained one active
stage and one active backlog task and did not use tooling output as authority.

# 9. Repository-Visible Inconsistency Review

## 9.1 Blocking inconsistencies

None found in the fixed baseline.

All M7.1–M7.6 stages report their completed authority condition consistently in
their primary artifacts, class indexes, roadmap, backlog dependencies, and
journal history. Review and correction evidence remains discoverable.

## 9.2 Non-blocking observations

1. Status reconciliation is distributed and therefore remains vulnerable to
   clerical drift.
2. Review-submission prerequisites are explicit across the system but are not
   represented by one mandatory readiness artifact.
3. Some historical commits combine more than one narrow governance event,
   although exact reviewed revisions and content identities remain attributable.

These observations repeat evidence-supported M7.6 lessons. They are not defects
in current canonical meaning and do not authorize corrective work.

# 10. Suitability as a Future Baseline

The M7 corpus is suitable as the governance baseline for future Management OS
evolution because it supplies stable authority boundaries while remaining
implementation-independent. Future work can inherit its roles, gates,
traceability, review independence, and amendment discipline without depending
on a scheduler, orchestration engine, repository host, or runtime architecture.

Suitability does not pre-authorize future work. Any evolution must begin under
separately recorded direction, remain within existing authority, and use the
applicable lifecycle. A proposed change to canonical governance requires its own
governed amendment; an advisory recommendation is not sufficient.

# 11. Observations and Authoritative Conclusions

## 11.1 Evaluative observations

- The architecture proved capable of stopping invalid progression without
  losing prior evidence.
- The strongest recurring friction concerned evidence readiness and manual
  reconciliation, not unresolved governance law.
- Canonical stage reservations prevented terminology drift from becoming a
  repository transition.
- Autonomous participation was incorporated without weakening accountable human
  judgment or constitutional ownership.

Observations are explanatory and advisory. They do not create obligations.

## 11.2 Review conclusions

Subject to independent Architecture Review, this M7.7 evaluation concludes:

1. **Complete:** the M7 governance architecture contains every artifact and
   authority relation required by its canonical structure.
2. **Internally coherent:** authority, ownership, state, evidence, review, and
   transition semantics are mutually consistent.
3. **Operationally consistent:** recorded stage execution conforms to the
   system's separation and correction rules, including when progression was
   blocked.
4. **Baseline-suitable:** the corpus is sufficiently stable,
   implementation-independent, and bounded to govern future Management OS
   evolution.
5. **No remaining architectural inconsistency:** no unresolved defect requires
   reopening M7.1–M7.6.

These conclusions evaluate existing governance. They introduce no new law,
authority, stage, or amendment.
