# 3. What Worked

## Evidence-discipline observations

- Fixed commit/blob/source identities kept later official findings, company
  statements, chronology assessments, and Evidence gaps distinguishable.
- Claim-level attribution and hindsight status prevented later regulatory detail
  from becoming event-time actor knowledge.
- Separate claim classes preserved direct source material, chronology, official
  findings, aggregate Outcomes, and uncertainty without analyst synthesis.
- Differently scoped financial figures remained separate rather than receiving an
  invented reconciliation.

## Autonomous-execution observations

- Prechecks and one-file commit gates made the expected state inspectable after
  every phase.
- The continuation gate supported an uninterrupted Phase 3–10 run because the next
  bounded action was uniquely prescribed and no human disposition was required.
- Mandatory stop boundaries prevented Phase 11 reflection answers from being
  inferred after the historical branch completed.
- The Phase 2 bootstrap exposed a bounded correction pattern: correct objective
  metadata, repeat review, then commit.

## Governance observations

- Existing authorization was sufficient for protocol execution; explicit human
  direction was used only at the Phase 11 commencement boundary.
- The later human deferral was recorded in the existing human-response surface
  rather than creating a duplicate Decision or closure mechanism.
- The `debug.log` event demonstrated preservation of an unexpected user-owned
  worktree state until provenance and deletion authority were explicit.

# 4. Friction and Redundancy

## Methodology observations

- Repeating the full claim schema for every atomic claim improved auditability but
  created substantial document volume and reading cost.
- Phase boundaries sometimes required the same source paragraph to appear in
  different bounded roles, increasing repetition while preserving semantic
  separation.
- Status text in ROADMAP and BACKLOG became historically stale after phase commits,
  but changing it would have required a separate transition and was outside the
  walkthrough authority.

## Usability observations

- Repository Markdown, hashes, and long claim records are reviewable governance
  surfaces, not a usable product interaction for a management user.
- The workflow lacked progressive disclosure, navigable Evidence inspection,
  persistent Working Set, and direct human-response capture.
- Serhii received reports about autonomous execution rather than personally
  traversing the case, so the primary H3 experience question could not be answered.

## Operational friction

- Invoking the VS Code CLI from the repository generated `debug.log`, creating an
  avoidable provenance interruption despite read-only intent.
- Mechanical identity and field-count checks were effective but sometimes required
  correction of the check itself rather than the artifact, as with the Phase 11
  dimension-count regex.

# 7. Canonicalization-Candidate Assessment

No candidate is adopted or recommended by this retrospective.

## Candidate 1 — Autonomous execution shall continue until the next indispensable human judgment

- **Originating observation:** Phase 3–10 proceeded mechanically under fixed
  authority; Phase 11 reflection required attributable human input and stopped.
- **Demonstrated benefit:** reduced unnecessary interruption while preserving a
  clear human judgment boundary.
- **Scope:** bounded autonomous work with fixed authority, unique next action,
  reliable evidence, and explicit suspension conditions.
- **Risks:** “continue” could be misread as persistence beyond authority, ambiguity,
  tool reliability, material risk, or human stop conditions.
- **Canonical-consideration sufficiency:** `PARTIALLY READY` — demonstrated in one
  repository workflow, not across operating contexts.
- **Current treatment:** `POST-WALKTHROUGH CANONICALIZATION CANDIDATE`; remain observational.
- **Potentially affected artifacts:** Workbench Laws, M7.5 Autonomous Development
  Protocol, lifecycle guidance, and autonomous-work operating rules.
- **Authority required for adoption:** separate canonical-change proposal, review,
  attributable Decision, and repository transition by the owning authorities.

## Candidate 2 — Evidence extraction as an explicit layer between Sources and historical claims

- **Originating observation:** the source manifest and assessments supplied
  source-level context, but claim construction repeatedly depended on locating
  exact source passages and translating them into ledger records.
- **Demonstrated benefit:** an attributable extraction layer could separate source
  text/locator capture from admissibility, synthesis, and rendered claims.
- **Scope:** research and walkthrough evidence handling; not general product canon by default.
- **Risks:** duplicate records, extraction drift, false authority upgrade, and
  premature conversion of excerpts into accepted Evidence.
- **Canonical-consideration sufficiency:** `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE` for canonical
  adoption; one case demonstrates friction but not the stable semantics of a new layer.
- **Current treatment:** remain observational and eligible for bounded prototype consideration only under future authority.
- **Potentially affected artifacts:** source-citation requirements, claim-ledger
  method, Evidence architecture, and future interaction design.
- **Authority required for adoption:** methodology proposal and review; canonical
  architecture authority if the layer changes canonical Evidence semantics.

## Candidate 3 — Distinguishing human-performed walkthrough from AI-delegated walkthrough

- **Originating observation:** the autonomous agent executed Phases 2–10, while
  Serhii recorded no direct participation and insufficient basis to assess the interaction.
- **Demonstrated benefit:** prevents repository completion and methodological
  conformance from being mistaken for human product experience or H3 validation.
- **Scope:** empirical-learning provenance, user-research records, validation inputs,
  and walkthrough participation identity.
- **Risks:** a binary label may hide mixed participation unless contribution and interaction modes remain attributable.
- **Canonical-consideration sufficiency:** `READY FOR CANONICAL CONSIDERATION`, not adoption.
- **Current treatment:** explicit methodological observation; no canonical effect.
- **Potentially affected artifacts:** empirical-learning method, validation records,
  participant provenance, and future product telemetry or research schemas.
- **Authority required for adoption:** separate methodology/canonical proposal,
  independent review, attributable Decision, and transition.
