## Authority Condition

This document is a constitutional candidate. It defines the proposed
constitutional foundations of the Management OS development organization but
has no approved, dependency-use, or canonical authority in its present state.

It was prepared under the repository-native
[M7.2 Constitution Order](../orders/M7.2%20-%20Constitution%20Order.md), the

It can acquire authority only through the review and canonicalization lifecycle
owned by those upstream sources. Its definition commit cannot approve or
canonicalize it.

# 1. Constitutional Purpose

Governance exists to keep the development of Management OS legitimate,
coherent, attributable, reviewable, and subordinate to accountable human
authority while the organization, technology, automation, and methods evolve.

The Constitution protects enduring conditions under which change may acquire
authority. It prevents urgency, repetition, technical capability, organizational
convenience, or autonomous execution from silently becoming permission.

Constitutional governance shall:

1. preserve the approved purpose and integrity of Management OS;
2. keep authority explicit, bounded, attributable, and inspectable;
3. separate direction, creation, review, and authoritative transition;
4. ensure that evidence and reasoned judgment precede consequential authority;
5. preserve accountable human responsibility for governance outcomes;
6. protect canonical ownership and prevent silent redefinition;
7. permit evolution without allowing change to erase its provenance;
8. remain valid independently of organization design, workflow, automation,
   platform, tool, repository host, or implementation method.

# 2. Constitutional Authority

The Constitution governs the legitimacy of Management OS development
governance. Once canonical, every lower governance rule, organizational
arrangement, lifecycle, protocol, order, and governance interpretation must
conform to it within its declared scope.

Constitutional authority is bounded:

- it governs how development authority may be recognized and constrained;
- it does not replace accountable human product or business authority;
- it does not redefine canonical product, professional, domain, experience, or
  presentation meaning owned by approved sources;
- it does not create implementation authority;
- it does not grant authority merely because an actor, system, or tool can act;
- it does not become canonical through authorship, publication, repetition,
  automation, or use.

No person, automated system, document, order, precedent, or practice may claim
constitutional authority except through an explicit constitutional source and
its valid authority state.

# 3. Immutable Governance Principles

These principles are immutable within ordinary governance. No lower authority,
temporary exception, order, process, tool, emergency claim, repeated practice,
or autonomous action may waive or contradict them. A constitutional amendment
may clarify their application but shall not abolish their protected substance.

## 3.1 Accountable human authority

Governance remains accountable to identifiable human authority. Automation may
assist, analyze, recommend, prepare, validate, or execute within granted scope,
but capability, speed, confidence, or prior success never creates independent
authority.

## 3.2 Explicit and bounded authority

Every exercise of authority must have an attributable source, a defined scope,
and a knowable boundary. Silence, ambiguity, convenience, implied consent,
technical access, possession of information, or absence of objection does not
grant authority.

## 3.3 Separation of powers

Direction, creation, independent judgment, and authoritative transition are
distinct powers. A governed change cannot acquire authority solely from the act
that proposed, authored, produced, or executed it.

## 3.4 No self-approval

Authorship, execution, evidence production, publication, or commitment cannot
approve its own result. Approval must arise from authority independent of the
result-producing act.

## 3.5 Evidence before authoritative judgment

Consequential governance judgment must be supportable by attributable evidence.
Evidence may support judgment but cannot issue a verdict, grant authority, or be
rewritten to match a desired outcome.

## 3.6 Repository-visible governance truth

Authoritative governance state must be preserved in attributable, inspectable,
durable records. Conversation, memory, summary, recollection, convention, or
unrecorded agreement cannot supersede the authoritative record.

The constitutional principle is durable record authority, not dependence on a
particular repository product or tool.

## 3.7 Unique semantic ownership

Every governed meaning has one identifiable primary semantic owner within a
given scope. References, projections, indexes, implementations, and dependent
artifacts may consume that meaning but cannot silently duplicate or redefine it.

## 3.8 Scope restraint

Authority must be interpreted and exercised only within its granted scope. A
valid decision in one scope does not automatically create authority in another.
Urgency or dependency does not expand scope.

## 3.9 Traceable change and preserved history

Every authoritative change must preserve its source, rationale, evidence,
disposition, and relationship to the authority it changes. Supersession may
change current authority but cannot erase historical authority or provenance.

## 3.10 Integrity under automation

Automation must preserve the same authority, ownership, evidence, reviewability,
and accountability boundaries required of human action. Automated repetition
cannot normalize a constitutional violation or convert behavior into precedent.

## 3.11 Implementation independence

Governance meaning must survive changes in roles, organization, lifecycle,
method, automation, platform, tooling, storage, interface, and implementation.
An implementation may realize governance but cannot define its constitutional
meaning merely by existing.

## 3.12 Controlled constitutional evolution

The Constitution may evolve only through explicit constitutional amendment.
Informal interpretation, lower rules, operational necessity, or accumulated
practice cannot amend it indirectly.

# 4. Hierarchy of Authority

Authority is resolved first by legitimate external obligation and accountable
human authority, then by constitutional precedence and scope. The hierarchy is:

1. **Applicable law, binding external obligation, and non-delegable accountable
   human responsibility.** Governance cannot authorize their violation.
2. **Approved product and business authority within its owned scope.** It owns
   product purpose, priority, and decisions reserved to accountable human
   judgment; it cannot silently amend this Constitution.
3. **This Constitution, once canonical.** It governs the legitimacy and limits
   of development governance.
4. **Approved canonical product and architecture sources within their owned
   semantic scope.** They remain authoritative for product, professional,
   domain, experience, presentation, and architecture meaning and must conform
   to constitutional governance without being redefined by it.
5. **Approved subordinate governance.** Future organizational, lifecycle,
   protocol, policy, and equivalent normative sources must conform to the
   Constitution and to applicable canonical meaning.
6. **Authorized bounded direction.** Orders and equivalent instructions may
   activate work within upstream authority but cannot create durable law.
7. **Governed work products and realizations.** Definitions, implementations,
   configurations, and automated actions have only the authority explicitly
   granted to them.
8. **Evidence, reviews, indexes, journals, and historical records.** These
   preserve observations, judgment, discoverability, status, and history within
   their classes; they do not silently create upstream meaning.

The hierarchy is scope-aware rather than a license for one source to absorb the
responsibility of another. When two sources govern different concerns, both
remain authoritative within their own boundaries.

# 5. Separation of Powers

The development organization must preserve four constitutionally distinct
powers regardless of how future organization assigns them:

## 5.1 Direction power

Direction determines legitimate purpose, priority, and bounded authorization to
begin governed work. Direction cannot approve an unknown future result merely by
authorizing its creation.

## 5.2 Creation power

Creation proposes, defines, implements, or otherwise produces a governed result.
Creation cannot grant authority to its own output.

## 5.3 Judgment power

Judgment evaluates a result against applicable authority and evidence.
Judgment must remain independent of the result-producing act and cannot silently
rewrite the evaluated source.

## 5.4 Transition power

Transition changes authoritative state after required judgment. A publication,
commit, deployment, or status label cannot substitute for a valid transition.

Future organizational arrangements may allocate, delegate, or support these
powers only while preserving their constitutional distinction, accountability,
and prohibition against self-authorization. This Constitution does not assign
future roles or define procedures for exercising the powers.

# 6. Governance Invariants

The following conditions must always hold:

1. Governance authority is attributable to an explicit legitimate source.
2. Every authority has a bounded scope.
3. No lower source may redefine a higher source within the higher source's
   scope.
4. No source may acquire authority merely through use, repetition, or technical
   enforcement.
5. Creation and approval are distinguishable acts.
6. No governed result approves or canonicalizes itself.
7. Evidence remains distinct from judgment and authority.
8. Judgment remains distinct from amendment.
9. Transition requires the authority appropriate to the resulting state.
10. Every governed meaning has one primary semantic owner in its scope.
11. Conflicting authority claims are resolved by legitimacy, scope, precedence,
    and explicit source evidence rather than convenience.
12. Authority cannot be inferred from silence, memory, or inaccessible context.
13. Historical authority and supersession remain attributable.
14. Automation inherits constraints but never authority beyond its grant.
15. Degraded tooling or unavailable automation cannot erase accountable human
    authority or constitutional obligations.
16. Exceptional conditions cannot create permanent authority by precedent.
17. Constitutional ambiguity cannot be resolved by expanding power.
18. A constitutional violation cannot be cured solely by documenting that it
    occurred.
19. Subordinate governance must remain interpretable without dependence on
    hidden system behavior.
20. The Constitution remains stable across organizational and technological
    change.

# 7. Amendment Principles

Constitutional amendment is permitted so that governance can remain legitimate
and coherent as Management OS evolves. Amendment is constrained by these
principles:

1. An amendment must be explicit and identify the constitutional text and
   authority it proposes to change.
2. An amendment must state its purpose, scope, rationale, dependencies, and
   foreseeable authority consequences.
3. An amendment must be distinguishable from ordinary interpretation,
   subordinate governance, correction, and implementation change.
4. An amendment cannot acquire authority through urgency, repetition, precedent,
   tooling, or operational necessity alone.
5. Amendment judgment must be independent of the act that authored the proposed
   amendment.
6. The evidence and reasoning supporting an amendment must remain attributable.
7. An amendment must preserve historical text, prior authority, and
   supersession provenance.
8. An amendment must not retroactively legitimize an act that lacked authority
   when performed.
9. An amendment may clarify immutable principles but cannot abolish accountable
   human authority, explicit bounded authority, separation of powers,
   non-self-approval, evidence integrity, or traceable governance truth.
10. No amendment is effective until it has acquired constitutional authority
    through the governance applicable to constitutional change.

This section defines constraints on amendment, not an amendment workflow. The
operational lifecycle for future amendments belongs to later governance.

# 8. Constitutional Precedence

Within development governance, a canonical Constitution prevails over any
subordinate organizational rule, lifecycle, protocol, policy, order, practice,
tool behavior, or automation that conflicts with it.

Precedence has these limits:

- it applies only within constitutional scope;
- it does not displace applicable law or non-delegable human responsibility;
- it does not transfer semantic ownership from canonical product or architecture
  sources;
- it does not convert constitutional silence into permission;
- it does not allow interpretation to create omitted procedures or roles;
- it does not operate retroactively to erase historical facts or prior valid
  authority.

A conflicting lower source is non-authoritative to the extent of the conflict.
The unaffected portion may remain valid when it is separable and still satisfies
constitutional purpose.

# 9. Constitutional Interpretation Principles

Constitutional interpretation shall follow these principles:

1. **Text and purpose together.** Read the text according to its ordinary
   governance meaning and the constitutional purpose it protects.
2. **Narrow construction of power.** Ambiguity about authority is resolved
   against expansion of power.
3. **Preservation of human accountability.** No interpretation may transfer
   ultimate accountability to an automated system, artifact, or procedure.
4. **Scope before precedence.** Determine whether sources govern the same
   concern before treating them as conflicting.
5. **Specific within valid scope.** A more specific lower rule may refine, but
   never contradict or weaken, a constitutional obligation.
6. **Whole-document coherence.** Interpret provisions so that immutable
   principles and invariants remain effective together.
7. **No authority from silence.** Omitted permission, role, workflow, exception,
   or mechanism is not constitutionally granted.
8. **No hidden dependency.** Interpretation cannot depend on undisclosed
   context, private recollection, or inaccessible system behavior.
9. **Prospective integrity.** Interpretation explains existing constitutional
   meaning; it cannot amend the Constitution or retroactively legitimize an
   unauthorized act.
10. **Technology neutrality.** Tool behavior and current implementation inform
    facts but do not control constitutional meaning.
11. **Preservation of independent judgment.** Interpretation cannot collapse
    creation, evidence, judgment, and transition into one authority-producing
    act.
12. **Explicit amendment when meaning must change.** A material change in
    constitutional meaning requires amendment rather than creative
    interpretation.

# 10. Constitutional Scope

This Constitution applies to:

- governance of Management OS development and architecture;
- every human or automated actor exercising delegated development authority;
- every normative governance source, order, governed work product, evidence
  record, review judgment, transition, index, and historical governance record;
- every present or future organization, lifecycle, autonomous-development
  capability, methodology, tool, platform, and implementation used to develop
  Management OS;
- constitutional interpretation and amendment.

This Constitution does not:

- define organizational roles, reporting lines, delegation assignments, or
  decision bodies beyond the constitutional separation of powers;
- define operational workflows or review procedures;
- define the Development Lifecycle;
- define autonomous execution procedures, permissions, retries, escalation, or
  recovery;
- define implementation methodology;
- define repository or directory structure;
- define product features, domain models, interface behavior, platform choices,
  or implementation mechanisms;
- replace canonical sources that own product, professional, domain, experience,
  presentation, or architecture meaning;
- authorize M7.3–M7.7 work.

Later governance may define excluded subjects only within its authorized stage
and only in conformity with this Constitution after it becomes canonical.

# 11. Constitutional Conformance

A governance source conforms constitutionally only if:

1. its authority and scope are explicit;
2. it respects the hierarchy and scope of upstream sources;
3. it preserves the separation of direction, creation, judgment, and transition;
4. it cannot approve itself;
5. it preserves accountable human authority;
6. its evidence, judgment, and status remain distinguishable;
7. it maintains unique semantic ownership;
8. it preserves attributable history and supersession;
9. its automated realization does not broaden authority;
10. it introduces no interpretation that should instead be a constitutional
    amendment.

This section defines constitutional tests, not a review procedure or operational
workflow.
