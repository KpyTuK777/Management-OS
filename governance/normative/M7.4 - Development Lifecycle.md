# 1. Purpose

The Development Lifecycle defines how governed work moves through legitimate
authority states over time. It operationalizes the Constitution and
Organizational Model by specifying the conditions under which direction may
enter creation, a result may enter independent judgment, judgment may require
correction or authorize transition, and Transition Authority may establish an
authoritative state.

The lifecycle governs **authority progression**, not the mechanics used to
perform work. It remains valid regardless of people, organizational titles,
work practices, automation, tools, repositories, platforms, cadence, or
implementation method.

# 2. Lifecycle Concepts

## 2.1 Governed matter

A **governed matter** is a bounded objective, definition, implementation,
change, correction, or other work product whose result may affect an
authoritative Management OS state.

Each matter has one lifecycle identity and declared scope. Related matters may
be divided or combined only by Direction Authority without erasing their prior
identity, authority, evidence, or history.

## 2.2 Lifecycle stage

A **lifecycle stage** is a durable authority condition that determines which
constitutional function may act and what progression is permitted. A stage is
not a meeting, team, tool state, branch, ticket column, elapsed-time period, or
execution instruction.

## 2.3 Lifecycle gate

A **lifecycle gate** is a constitutional decision boundary. Crossing a gate
requires its named authority, entry evidence, and explicit disposition. The
passage of time, completion of activity, publication, commitment, deployment,
tool status, or repeated use cannot cross a gate.

## 2.4 Lifecycle record

A **lifecycle record** is the attributable history of a governed matter's
identity, scope, authority source, stage changes, evidence, judgments,
corrections, transitions, and terminal disposition. The record reports
authority state but cannot create it.

## 2.5 Iteration

**Iteration** is bounded reconsideration or revision inside, or legitimate
re-entry into, a lifecycle stage. Iteration changes no authority merely because
work occurred; every affected gate remains applicable.

# 3. Enduring Lifecycle Stages

## 3.1 Directed

### Meaning

Direction Authority has established a legitimate, bounded objective and
authorized preparation of a governed result.

### Entry conditions

- the objective, scope, exclusions, and authority source are explicit;
- accountable Direction Authority is identifiable;
- applicable canonical dependencies and semantic owners are identified;
- no known conflict makes the direction illegitimate.

### Permitted authority

Direction Authority may clarify, narrow, stop, defer, or legitimately redirect
the matter. Creation Authority may accept the bounded work or identify missing
authority and refuse entry into creation.

### Exit condition

Creation begins only when Creation Authority accepts the direction within its
allocated scope. Direction does not pre-approve the future result.

## 3.2 In Creation

### Meaning

Creation Authority is preparing or correcting a governed result within valid
direction and applicable semantic boundaries.

### Entry conditions

- the matter is Directed;
- Creation Authority and accountable human authority are identifiable;
- required dependencies are available at the authority state demanded by the
  matter;
- known conflicts, assumptions, and exclusions are visible.

### Permitted authority

Creation may make bounded production decisions, iterate, prepare attributable
evidence, propose semantic change, and return for direction clarification.
Creation may not approve, judge, or transition its result.

### Exit condition

The matter may leave creation only when a bounded result, required evidence,
declared limitations, dependency references, and review scope are complete
enough for independent judgment.

## 3.3 Ready for Judgment

### Meaning

Creation has declared a stable review subject and evidence set. This state is a
request for judgment, not a claim of conformance or approval.

### Entry conditions

- the exact result and reviewed scope are identifiable;
- evidence is attributable and distinguishable from claims and judgment;
- applicable authority and acceptance concerns are cited;
- the result-producing participation is disclosed;
- known uncertainty, exclusions, and unresolved risk are explicit.

### Permitted authority

Judgment Authority may accept the matter for evaluation or refuse entry when
scope, authority, independence, or evidence is insufficient. Any substantive
change to the review subject ends this state and returns the matter to In
Creation.

### Exit condition

Judgment Authority explicitly accepts the declared subject and scope for
independent evaluation.

## 3.4 Under Judgment

### Meaning

Judgment Authority is independently evaluating the fixed review subject against
applicable authority and evidence.

### Entry conditions

- Ready for Judgment conditions remain true;
- Judgment Authority, scope, and independence are explicit;
- no participant is using creation of the evaluated result to authorize it.

### Permitted authority

Judgment may examine evidence, request clarification, identify findings, state
uncertainty, and issue only a disposition available within its authority.
Judgment cannot invent missing evidence, silently change the subject, or perform
the authoritative transition.

### Exit conditions

Judgment ends with exactly one attributable disposition:

- **Requires Corrections**, moving the matter to Correction Required;
- **Approved for Transition**, moving the matter to Transition Ready for the
  approved target state; or
- **Not Approved**, moving the matter to Transition Ready solely for an
  explicit closure transition without creating an authoritative result.

Clarification that changes no meaning may be added to evidence without leaving
Under Judgment. Any substantive change invalidates the fixed subject and
returns the matter to In Creation.

## 3.5 Correction Required

### Meaning

Judgment has identified finite blocking conditions that prevent the reviewed
subject from progressing in its current form.

### Entry conditions

- an attributable Requires Corrections disposition exists;
- each blocking finding identifies the violated authority, missing evidence,
  inconsistency, or scope defect;
- the required return gate is explicit.

### Permitted authority

Creation Authority may prepare bounded corrections. Direction Authority must
act if correction would alter purpose, scope, priority, or authorization.
Judgment retains ownership of the finding and cannot author the correction
through interpretation of its verdict.

### Exit condition

A correction set, change account, updated evidence, and unchanged or newly
authorized scope exist. The matter returns to Ready for Judgment at the gate
named by the disposition. Prior findings and reviewed revisions remain visible.

## 3.6 Transition Ready

### Meaning

Independent Judgment has issued a disposition authorizing one bounded target
state: either progression of the exact reviewed result or closure of a result
that was not approved. A positively judged result is not yet authoritative
merely because judgment is complete.

### Entry conditions

- an attributable Approved for Transition or Not Approved disposition exists
  and identifies the bounded target state;
- the exact reviewed result, scope, evidence, limitations, and authorized target
  state are identifiable;
- no substantive change has occurred after judgment;
- transition prerequisites declared by applicable authority are satisfied.

### Permitted authority

Transition Authority may verify prerequisites and either perform the authorized
transition or refuse it. It cannot broaden the disposition, repair the result,
or infer missing judgment.

### Exit condition

Transition Authority records an explicit state transition or records refusal
with the unsatisfied prerequisite. Refusal returns the matter to the applicable
earlier stage without erasing the positive judgment; a changed result requires
new judgment.

## 3.7 Authoritative

### Meaning

Transition Authority has established the exact state authorized by valid
judgment. The result may now govern or be used as a dependency within its
approved scope.

### Entry conditions

- all Transition Ready conditions remain true;
- accountable Transition Authority is identifiable;
- the resulting authority state, effective scope, provenance, and dependencies
  are explicitly recorded;
- all authoritative status representations can be reconciled without conflict.

### Permitted authority

The authoritative result governs within its scope. Maintenance that changes no
meaning may preserve or improve representation. Any proposed substantive change
is a new governed matter and receives a new lifecycle identity or explicitly
linked revision identity.

### Exit condition

An authoritative result remains authoritative until a legitimate later
transition closes, supersedes, withdraws, or replaces it. Mere inactivity,
replacement in practice, tool state, or neglect cannot change its authority.

## 3.8 Closed or Superseded

### Meaning

Transition Authority has terminated active progression or replaced an
authoritative result under valid upstream direction and judgment.

### Entry conditions

- the closure or supersession authority is explicit;
- affected scope, replacement relationship, and continuing dependencies are
  known;
- history, prior authority, evidence, and judgments remain discoverable.

### Exit condition

This is terminal for the lifecycle identity. Renewed work begins as a new or
explicitly linked governed matter; it cannot silently reactivate the prior
identity.

# 4. Authority-State Transition Model

The permitted primary transitions are:

```text
Directed
    → In Creation
    → Ready for Judgment
    → Under Judgment
        ├── Requires Corrections → Correction Required
        │                              → Ready for Judgment
        ├── Approved for Transition → Transition Ready
        │                                → Authoritative
        │                                     → Transition Ready
        │                                          → Closed or Superseded
        └── Not Approved → Transition Ready
                              → Closed or Superseded
```

Permitted backward movement is bounded:

- Directed may remain Directed while scope is clarified or may be submitted for
  an explicit closure transition;
- In Creation may return to Directed when legitimate direction must change;
- Ready for Judgment returns to In Creation after any substantive change;
- Under Judgment returns to In Creation when its fixed subject changes;
- Correction Required returns only to the review gate named by judgment;
- Transition Ready returns to an earlier applicable stage when prerequisites
  fail and returns to In Creation if the result changes;
- Authoritative change begins a new linked lifecycle rather than editing history
  in place.

No other transition is permitted unless later approved governance adds it
without violating the Constitution or Organizational Model.

# 5. Review and Transition Gates

## 5.1 Direction Gate

Owned by Direction Authority. It creates the Directed state by establishing
legitimate purpose, scope, priority, boundaries, and authorization. It cannot
judge or pre-approve the future result.

## 5.2 Creation Exit Gate

Owned by Creation Authority only as a declaration of readiness. It freezes the
subject proposed for review and assembles evidence. Passing it creates no
approval and binds no judgment.

## 5.3 Judgment Entry Gate

Owned by Judgment Authority. It confirms sufficient scope, authority, evidence,
and independence to begin evaluation. Acceptance of a matter for review creates
no favorable presumption.

## 5.4 Judgment Disposition Gate

Owned by Judgment Authority. It issues Requires Corrections, Approved for
Transition, or Not Approved within declared scope. The disposition cannot
change the result or perform transition.

## 5.5 Transition Gate

Owned by Transition Authority. It verifies judgment and prerequisites, then
establishes exactly the authorized state. It cannot manufacture, reinterpret,
or broaden judgment.

## 5.6 Closure and Supersession Gate

Owned by Transition Authority under legitimate upstream direction and required
judgment. It preserves the previous state and links any successor rather than
rewriting authority history.

# 6. Correction and Re-entry

1. A correction path begins only from an attributable finding or from a
   creator-identified defect before judgment is complete.
2. Judgment owns the meaning and closure condition of its findings; Creation
   owns the correction result.
3. A finding must be blocking or advisory. Only blocking findings prevent the
   approved transition.
4. Requires Corrections must name the return gate. It cannot create an
   indefinite or self-interpreting correction state.
5. Correction evidence distinguishes the prior subject, changed subject,
   findings addressed, changes made, and unchanged scope.
6. A corrected subject receives independent re-judgment. Prior positive results
   cannot be transferred to changed substance unless the original disposition
   explicitly bounded an unaffected portion.
7. New direction is required when correction changes purpose, scope, priority,
   or authority source.
8. A non-approved or closed matter may re-enter only through a new Direction
   Gate and an explicitly linked lifecycle identity.
9. Correction never deletes prior evidence, findings, dispositions, or state
   history.

# 7. Permitted Iteration

Iteration is permitted when it increases fidelity without bypassing authority:

- Creation may iterate freely inside valid direction before declaring Ready for
  Judgment.
- Direction and Creation may clarify the matter while preserving the source and
  recording any material boundary change.
- Judgment may iterate its analysis while the fixed subject, scope,
  independence, and evidence integrity remain stable.
- Correction may repeat the judgment loop until blocking findings are resolved,
  the matter is not approved, or Direction closes it.
- Multiple governed matters may progress concurrently when their identities,
  dependencies, authorities, evidence, and transitions remain distinguishable.
- An approved portion may progress independently only when judgment explicitly
  declares it semantically separable and Transition Authority can establish its
  state without implying approval of the remainder.

Iteration is prohibited when it:

- treats repeated activity as approval;
- changes the reviewed subject without re-entering judgment;
- lets Creation close its own blocking finding;
- lets Judgment create the result or missing evidence;
- lets Transition repair or broaden the approved result;
- treats prior approval as authority for materially changed substance;
- obscures failed, abandoned, corrected, or superseded history.

# 8. Lifecycle Invariants

1. Every governed matter has one identifiable lifecycle identity and scope.
2. Every stage entry is attributable to the authority that owns its gate.
3. Direction, Creation, Judgment, and Transition remain separately
   attributable for every consequential progression.
4. No stage, activity, artifact, tool state, or elapsed time authorizes its own
   progression.
5. Creation cannot issue authoritative judgment on its result.
6. Evidence cannot issue a verdict or create authority.
7. Judgment cannot perform transition or create missing evidence.
8. Transition cannot exceed or substitute for judgment.
9. Record custody reports stage and authority state but cannot create either.
10. Semantic ownership remains with the applicable authoritative source through
    every stage and iteration.
11. Delegation preserves source authority, scope, separation, attribution, and
    identifiable human accountability.
12. A substantive change after judgment invalidates progression based on that
    judgment unless explicitly outside its severable scope.
13. Correction and re-entry preserve the complete prior history.
14. Closure or supersession cannot erase a result's former authority or
    provenance.
15. Exceptional conditions, urgency, or degraded capability do not bypass a
    gate or relax constitutional authority.
16. Concurrent progression cannot merge identities, evidence, judgments, or
    transitions.
17. Lifecycle silence creates neither authority nor a prohibition against lower
    governance acting within constitutionally delegated scope.
18. Constitutional legitimacy takes precedence over speed, completion,
    institutional convenience, or lifecycle progression.

# 9. Lifecycle Traceability

Every consequential lifecycle record must preserve:

- governed-matter identity and version or revision relationship;
- current and prior lifecycle stages;
- legitimate direction, scope, exclusions, and authority source;
- accountable human authority for each gate decision;
- organizational function exercised and any delegation;
- applicable semantic owners and authoritative dependencies;
- exact result subject to judgment;
- attributable evidence and declared limitations;
- judgment scope, findings, reasoning, and disposition;
- correction requests, changed subject, and re-entry gate;
- transition prerequisites, target state, decision, and effective scope;
- closure, withdrawal, or supersession relationship;
- unresolved risk and advisory findings;
- chronology sufficient to distinguish observation, judgment, and transition.

Traceability must be durable, inspectable, and protected from silent rewriting.
This requirement defines information integrity, not a repository schema, file
format, system, identifier syntax, or tool implementation.

# 10. Failure, Stop, and Exceptional Conditions

A matter does not gain authority by reaching a deadline, exhausting effort,
losing a participant, encountering degraded tooling, or becoming operationally
necessary.

When a required authority, dependency, evidence set, independent judgment, or
transition prerequisite is absent, progression stops at the last legitimately
established state. Direction Authority may defer, narrow, redirect, or close the
matter within its scope. It cannot declare missing conformance or transition
evidence to exist.

Recovery resumes from the earliest stage whose entry conditions are again true.
It preserves the interrupted history and does not invent continuity across an
authority gap.

# 11. Conformance

A development lifecycle or lifecycle instance conforms only when:

1. its stages map unambiguously to the authority conditions defined here;
2. every progression crosses the applicable named gate;
3. each gate decision is made by its owning organizational function;
4. direction, creation, evidence, judgment, and transition remain
   distinguishable;
5. correction and iteration cannot bypass independent re-judgment;
6. lifecycle state cannot become its own authority source;
7. semantic ownership and dependency authority remain intact;
8. accountable human authority and delegation remain traceable;
9. concurrent and partial progression preserve scope separation;
10. history, findings, prior states, and supersession remain discoverable;
11. exceptional conditions do not create authority;
12. implementation choices do not alter lifecycle meaning.

# 12. Scope and Exclusions

This Development Lifecycle owns:

- enduring lifecycle stages and their authority meaning;
- authority-state transitions;
- stage entry and exit conditions;
- review and transition gates;
- correction and re-entry paths;
- permitted iteration boundaries;
- lifecycle invariants;
- lifecycle traceability requirements;
- conformance conditions for later lifecycle realizations.

This Development Lifecycle does not define:

- autonomous execution behavior;
- AI-agent identity, coordination, handoff, or supervision;
- automation permissions or capability grants;
- retry counts, scheduling, queueing, orchestration, timeout, or recovery logic;
- repository, branch, commit, pull-request, issue, file, or deployment mechanics;
- tooling-specific workflows or state mappings;
- staffing, meeting, quorum, voting, or communication mechanics;
- implementation methodology or delivery framework;
- M7.5 Autonomous Development Protocol substance;
- M7.5–M7.7 authority or work.

Later governance may implement or specialize this lifecycle only within its
authorized scope and without altering the authority meaning defined here.
