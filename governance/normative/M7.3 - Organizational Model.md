# 1. Purpose

The Organizational Model defines the enduring arrangement of governance
responsibilities through which Management OS development realizes the
Constitution's accountable human authority, bounded power, unique ownership,
separation of powers, evidence integrity, and non-self-approval principles.

It defines organizational functions rather than fixed job titles, named people,
AI agents, committees, tools, or implementation structures. A conforming
organization may rename, combine operational support, distribute participation,
or change technology while preserving every authority boundary and accountable
relationship defined here.

The model states **who must be accountable for what kind of authority and how
those authorities relate**. It does not state the operational sequence by which
work is performed.

# 2. Organizational Principles

1. **Function before title.** Constitutional responsibility attaches to a
   defined governance function, not to a particular title, person, agent, team,
   vendor, or tool.
2. **Human accountability remains identifiable.** Every exercise of
   constitutional authority resolves to at least one identifiable accountable
   human authority.
3. **Authority follows responsibility.** A role receives only the authority
   required for its declared responsibility domain.
4. **No authority by capability.** Access, expertise, automation, seniority,
   possession of information, or technical ability does not create governance
   authority.
5. **Powers remain distinguishable.** Direction, creation, judgment, and
   transition remain separately attributable even when one human supports more
   than one function in different matters.
6. **No self-authorization.** No actor or body may use one assigned function to
   authorize its own output under another function.
7. **Semantic ownership remains unique.** Every governed meaning has one primary
   semantic stewardship assignment in each scope.
8. **Delegation never enlarges power.** Delegated authority is a bounded subset
   of authority already held.
9. **Accountability cannot disappear through delegation.** Assignment,
   automation, outsourcing, or collective action cannot leave authority without
   identifiable human answerability.
10. **Organization is implementation-independent.** Constitutional functions
    remain valid across changes in personnel, role names, organization size,
    automation, tooling, repository, platform, and method.

# 3. Constitutional Governance Roles

The following roles are constitutional functions. Their names describe
responsibility domains and may be mapped to different local titles without
changing their meaning.

## 3.1 Direction Authority

### Responsibility domain

The Direction Authority owns legitimate purpose, product or governance priority,
bounded authorization to begin work, and decisions constitutionally reserved to
accountable human direction.

### Authority

The Direction Authority may:

- establish or legitimately amend product and governance purpose within its
  owned scope;
- set priorities and boundaries;
- authorize a bounded governance objective;
- stop, defer, or redirect work within its authority;
- reserve a decision for accountable human judgment.

### Boundaries

The Direction Authority cannot:

- approve an unknown future result merely by authorizing its creation;
- create technical, architectural, or normative meaning outside its semantic
  scope;
- treat priority as evidence of conformance;
- waive the Constitution or authorize a lower source to do so;
- convert silence, urgency, funding, or access into expanded authority.

### Accountability

The Direction Authority is accountable for the legitimacy, clarity, scope, and
consequences of the direction it establishes. Its constitutional responsibility
must remain attributable to an identifiable human authority.

## 3.2 Creation Authority

### Responsibility domain

The Creation Authority owns preparation of governed definitions,
implementations, corrections, and other authorized work products within an
explicit direction and approved semantic boundaries.

### Authority

The Creation Authority may:

- make bounded technical and drafting decisions necessary to produce the
  authorized result;
- propose new or changed meaning for independent judgment;
- prepare evidence attributable to the work performed;
- identify conflicts, missing authority, or required escalation;
- decline or stop work that cannot be completed within legitimate authority.

### Boundaries

The Creation Authority cannot:

- approve, canonicalize, or transition its own output;
- broaden the authorized objective by implementation choice;
- redefine an upstream semantic owner;
- present an implementation constraint as constitutional or normative law;
- hide material uncertainty, conflict, limitation, or provenance from judgment.

### Accountability

The Creation Authority is accountable for fidelity to direction, technical
integrity within scope, explicit dependencies and exclusions, and attributable
representation of the result and its evidence.

## 3.3 Judgment Authority

### Responsibility domain

The Judgment Authority owns independent evaluation of a governed result against
applicable authority, evidence, scope, and required authority state.

### Authority

The Judgment Authority may:

- determine whether a result conforms within the evaluated scope;
- identify blocking or advisory findings;
- withhold, limit, or grant a disposition within its authority;
- require bounded correction where the evaluated authority permits it;
- state unresolved risk, uncertainty, or limitation.

### Boundaries

The Judgment Authority cannot:

- author the evaluated result through the act of judging it;
- invent missing evidence or treat a desired outcome as evidence;
- silently amend the evaluated source;
- expand the reviewed scope or grant authority not available from upstream
  sources;
- use independence as freedom from constitutional accountability.

### Accountability

The Judgment Authority is accountable for independence from the
result-producing act, fidelity to governing authority, evidence-based reasoning,
explicit scope, and an attributable disposition. Final authoritative judgment
must remain attributable to identifiable human authority.

## 3.4 Transition Authority

### Responsibility domain

The Transition Authority owns the legitimate change of an artifact, stage, or
governance state after the required upstream authority and judgment exist.

### Authority

The Transition Authority may:

- recognize that required authority conditions have been satisfied;
- establish the resulting authoritative state;
- reconcile authoritative status sources;
- refuse a transition whose prerequisites or evidence are incomplete;
- preserve closure, supersession, and provenance relationships.

### Boundaries

The Transition Authority cannot:

- substitute transition for missing judgment;
- alter the substance or disposition being transitioned;
- infer approval from publication, commitment, deployment, repetition, or use;
- create a state beyond that authorized by the applicable judgment;
- erase prior state, correction history, or provenance.

### Accountability

The Transition Authority is accountable for the legitimacy, exactness,
consistency, and traceability of the resulting authority state.

## 3.5 Semantic Steward

### Responsibility domain

The Semantic Steward is the organizational function accountable for the
integrity, uniqueness, boundaries, and evolution of one governed meaning within
a declared scope.

Semantic stewardship realizes—but does not replace—the semantic ownership held
by the authoritative artifact or model.

### Authority

The Semantic Steward may:

- explain the owned meaning and its boundary;
- identify duplication, contradiction, or unauthorized reinterpretation;
- propose bounded changes to the owning source;
- assess whether dependent representations preserve the owned meaning;
- require dependencies to cite rather than duplicate the authoritative source.

### Boundaries

The Semantic Steward cannot:

- approve its own proposed change merely because it stewards the meaning;
- extend ownership into an adjacent concern;
- treat interpretation, implementation, or indexing as a silent amendment;
- override higher authority or independent judgment;
- transfer semantic ownership through delegation without an authoritative
  change to the owning source.

### Accountability

The Semantic Steward is accountable for semantic coherence, non-duplication,
boundary clarity, dependency integrity, and preservation of the authoritative
source.

## 3.6 Governance Record Custodian

### Responsibility domain

The Governance Record Custodian owns faithful preservation, discoverability,
attribution, and status consistency of authoritative governance records.

### Authority

The Governance Record Custodian may:

- preserve and index governance records;
- reconcile clerical representations with an already authorized source state;
- identify missing, broken, ambiguous, or inconsistent records;
- protect historical attribution and supersession visibility;
- reject clerical changes that would alter substantive meaning or authority.

### Boundaries

The Governance Record Custodian cannot:

- create direction, semantic meaning, evidence, judgment, or transition
  authority through record maintenance;
- alter a disposition or source meaning;
- turn an index, journal, summary, or storage state into upstream authority;
- conceal or rewrite historical records;
- infer governance state from private memory or unrecorded context.

### Accountability

The Governance Record Custodian is accountable for faithful records and
discoverability, not for the substantive decisions those records report.

# 4. Governance Bodies

The Constitution does not require a fixed committee, council, board, team size,
or institutional form. Constitutional roles may be realized by individual human
authorities or by bodies when collective judgment or continuity is appropriate.

A governance body is constitutionally valid only when:

1. its responsibility domain and authority source are explicit;
2. its constitutional power or powers are separately attributable;
3. its scope and decision boundaries are knowable;
4. accountable human participation and answerability remain identifiable;
5. collective identity does not conceal individual accountability;
6. creation participants cannot use the body to approve their own result;
7. delegation into or from the body preserves all delegation boundaries;
8. the body cannot acquire authority through custom, attendance, expertise,
   tooling, or repeated practice;
9. its existence does not duplicate a semantic owner or create a second control
   plane;
10. its decisions remain attributable to the legitimate authority exercised.

No governance body is created by this model. A later authorized organizational
assignment may establish a body only in conformity with these conditions and
without changing the constitutional powers it realizes.

# 5. Responsibility Domains

Every governance responsibility belongs to one primary domain:

| Domain | Primary organizational function | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and priority | Direction Authority | Legitimate purpose, priority, bounded authorization, reserved human direction | Definition, evidence, conformance judgment, or state transition |
| Definition and realization | Creation Authority | Authorized work product and bounded production decisions | Approval, canonicalization, or upstream semantic authority |
| Independent evaluation | Judgment Authority | Evidence-based findings and disposition within review scope | Authorship, silent amendment, implementation evidence, or transition |
| Authority state | Transition Authority | Legitimate state change and reconciliation after judgment | Missing judgment, source substance, or expanded disposition |
| Governed meaning | Semantic Steward | Integrity and boundary of one authoritative semantic scope | Adjacent meaning, self-approval, or authority outside the owning source |
| Governance record | Governance Record Custodian | Preservation, attribution, indexing, and faithful status reporting | Direction, meaning, evidence verdict, or state creation |

One participant may support multiple domains only when each exercise is
explicitly identified and the combination cannot authorize that participant's
own output. Responsibility-domain combination never merges the underlying
constitutional powers.

# 6. Authority Allocation

## 6.1 Allocation conditions

An allocation of governance authority is valid only when:

1. it originates from a legitimate upstream source;
2. the assigned function and responsibility domain are explicit;
3. scope, limits, and accountable human authority are identifiable;
4. the allocation does not exceed the source authority;
5. incompatible participation is excluded for the affected decision;
6. semantic ownership remains unique;
7. the allocation remains inspectable and correctable;
8. automation or tooling does not become the authority source.

## 6.2 Multiple-power assignments

The same identifiable human authority may hold more than one constitutional
function when organizational scale requires it, but cannot exercise those
functions in a way that causes one act to authorize its own result.

At minimum:

- the creator of a governed result cannot supply its authoritative judgment;
- judgment cannot create the evidence required to justify its desired verdict;
- transition cannot manufacture or broaden judgment;
- direction cannot pre-approve the unknown output it authorizes;
- record custody cannot create the authority it reports.

When separation cannot be preserved for a specific decision, authority must be
withheld rather than inferred.

## 6.3 Temporary and exceptional allocation

Temporary absence, urgency, degraded tooling, or exceptional conditions do not
relax constitutional allocation boundaries. Temporary allocation must remain a
bounded exercise of existing authority and cannot become permanent authority by
precedent.

This principle defines an authority boundary, not a substitution or emergency
procedure.

# 7. Delegation Boundaries

Delegation transfers a bounded permission to exercise part of an authority; it
does not transfer the constitutional source or erase accountability.

A valid delegation must:

1. originate from authority that legitimately holds the delegated power;
2. identify the delegated function, scope, limits, and accountable human source;
3. grant no more authority than the delegator holds;
4. preserve applicable semantic ownership and separation constraints;
5. remain distinguishable from transfer of responsibility or canonical
   ownership;
6. be inspectable, revocable, and incapable of becoming permanent through use;
7. prohibit redelegation unless the source authority explicitly permits a
   bounded subset;
8. preserve evidence and attribution for consequential delegated action.

The following cannot be delegated away:

- identifiable human answerability for governance authority;
- responsibility for ensuring that a delegation is legitimate and bounded;
- constitutional obligations attached to the delegated function;
- accountability for unauthorized expansion known or reasonably attributable
  to the delegating authority;
- the requirement for judgment independent of the result-producing act.

Automation may receive delegated execution or analytical permission within
scope. It cannot receive ultimate human accountability, create its own
delegation, expand its authority from observed practice, or become the final
source of constitutional judgment.

# 8. Accountability Relationships

Accountability is an answerability relationship, not mere participation,
visibility, consultation, or technical ownership.

## 8.1 Direction accountability

Direction is accountable for legitimate purpose, priority, authorization scope,
and reserved human decisions. Creation may challenge unclear direction but
cannot silently repair or broaden it.

## 8.2 Creation accountability

Creation is accountable to the authorized direction and all applicable semantic
owners. It is answerable to independent judgment for fidelity, evidence,
limitations, and conformance claims.

## 8.3 Judgment accountability

Judgment is accountable to the Constitution, applicable canonical sources,
declared review authority, evidence, and evaluated scope. It is not accountable
to creation for producing a preferred outcome.

## 8.4 Transition accountability

Transition is accountable to the authoritative judgment and the source that
permits the resulting state. It must refuse an unsupported or inconsistent
transition.

## 8.5 Stewardship accountability

Semantic stewardship is accountable to the owning source and its legitimate
authority history. Record custody is accountable to authoritative sources and
dispositions, not to convenience or preferred narrative.

## 8.6 Human accountability chain

Every consequential governance act must permit a reviewer or affected authority
to identify:

- the human authority accountable for the power exercised;
- the source and scope of that authority;
- any delegation through which it was exercised;
- the semantic owner affected;
- the authority responsible for judgment and transition.

An accountability relationship is constitutionally defective when it terminates
only at an AI system, tool, repository, process, team label, or unnamed
collective.

# 9. Decision Authority Boundaries

| Decision class | Owning authority | Permitted decision | Prohibited substitution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose decision | Direction Authority | Establish or legitimately amend purpose within owned product or governance scope | Creation, judgment, automation, or precedent cannot infer purpose |
| Priority decision | Direction Authority | Select, stop, defer, or redirect bounded objectives | Technical convenience cannot set governance priority |
| Production decision | Creation Authority | Choose bounded means and content necessary to prepare an authorized result | Production cannot approve or broaden the result's authority |
| Semantic decision | Owning source supported by its Semantic Steward | Define or propose change to meaning within the owned scope | Indexes, implementations, consumers, or adjacent stewards cannot redefine it |
| Conformance decision | Judgment Authority | Determine findings and disposition within granted judgment scope | Direction, creation, evidence, or desired transition cannot dictate the verdict |
| State-transition decision | Transition Authority | Establish the state authorized by valid upstream judgment | Publication, commitment, deployment, or use cannot substitute for transition |
| Record decision | Governance Record Custodian | Faithfully represent and preserve established authority state | Record maintenance cannot create or change that state |

No decision class automatically grants another. A direction decision may
authorize creation but does not predetermine conformance. A conformance decision
may authorize transition but does not itself perform it. A transition may
establish status but does not rewrite semantic meaning.

# 10. Interaction Between Constitutional Powers

The constitutional powers interact through authority dependencies:

```text
Direction
    authorizes bounded purpose
        ↓
Creation
    produces a governed result and attributable evidence
        ↓
Judgment
    independently determines a disposition
        ↓
Transition
    establishes only the authority state permitted by that disposition
```

Semantic Stewardship constrains meaning across all four powers. Governance
Record Custody preserves the attributable sources and results of all four
powers.

This model is a logical authority relationship, not an operational workflow,
review procedure, or Development Lifecycle. Later governance may define
different sequences, iterations, corrections, concurrency, escalation, or
automation only while preserving these dependencies.

Interaction invariants:

1. Direction precedes legitimate creation in authority, even when discovery
   informs later direction.
2. Creation cannot bind judgment to a preferred disposition.
3. Judgment cannot make its own missing evidence true.
4. Transition cannot occur beyond the disposition actually granted.
5. Semantic stewardship cannot replace independent judgment.
6. Record custody cannot replace transition.
7. Feedback, correction, or iteration cannot reverse authority direction.
8. A later power may reject an incomplete upstream condition but cannot silently
   repair the upstream authority.
9. Automation may support any power within delegation but cannot merge powers or
   terminate human accountability.
10. Organizational convenience cannot collapse the separation required for the
    decision at issue.

# 11. Organizational Conformance Invariants

An organizational realization conforms only if:

1. all six constitutional governance functions are assigned or explicitly
   preserved;
2. every exercise of governance authority resolves to identifiable human
   accountability;
3. direction, creation, judgment, and transition remain separately
   attributable;
4. no actor or body can approve its own governed output;
5. semantic ownership remains unique and source-based;
6. delegations are narrower than and attributable to their source authority;
7. collective bodies cannot conceal accountability or acquire authority by
   custom;
8. automation has only delegated authority and never ultimate accountability;
9. decisions remain within the boundaries of their decision class;
10. record custody reports but does not create authority;
11. changes in names, personnel, organization, tooling, repository, or
    implementation do not alter constitutional meaning;
12. no organizational assignment waives or informally amends the Constitution.

# 12. Scope and Exclusions

This Organizational Model owns:

- constitutional governance functions;
- responsibility-domain separation;
- authority allocation constraints;
- delegation boundaries;
- accountability relationships;
- decision authority boundaries;
- body-conformance conditions;
- the logical interaction of constitutional powers.

This Organizational Model does not define:

- specific individuals, AI systems, vendors, or tools;
- mandatory local job titles;
- a fixed committee, council, board, team, or reporting structure;
- staffing levels, quorum, voting, meeting, consultation, or communication
  mechanics;
- operational workflows or review procedures;
- the M7.4 Development Lifecycle;
- the M7.5 Autonomous Development Protocol;
- implementation methodology;
- repository, branch, pull-request, issue, or file mechanics;
- product, professional, domain, experience, presentation, or architecture
  meaning owned by canonical sources;
- M7.4–M7.7 authority or substantive work.

Later governance may assign these functions or define excluded mechanics only
within its authorized scope and in conformity with the canonical Constitution.
