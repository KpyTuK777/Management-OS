# 1. Purpose

The Autonomous Development Protocol defines how autonomous participants may
prepare, execute, coordinate, evidence, interrupt, hand off, and recover
governed work while remaining fully subordinate to accountable human authority
and the canonical governance framework.

The protocol governs **autonomous execution conduct**, not constitutional
authority, organizational power, lifecycle progression, or implementation
mechanics. It permits bounded initiative inside valid direction and delegation
without allowing capability, access, speed, confidence, persistence, or prior
success to become authority.

The protocol remains valid across changes in people, AI systems, automation
technology, tools, repositories, platforms, communication channels, delivery
methods, and runtime architecture.

# 2. Protocol Concepts

## 2.1 Autonomous participant

An **autonomous participant** is a human-directed automated system, agent, or
other execution capability that can select and perform bounded actions without
step-by-step instruction inside an explicit delegation envelope.

Autonomy describes execution capability only. An autonomous participant is not
an independent authority source, cannot hold ultimate accountability, and
cannot acquire constitutional, organizational, lifecycle, semantic, review, or
transition power merely through participation.

## 2.2 Protocol identity

A **protocol identity** is the attributable identity under which one autonomous
participant acts on one governed matter. It binds:

- the participant or participant class;
- the governed-matter and lifecycle identities;
- the organizational function being supported;
- the accountable human authority;
- the delegation source, scope, duration, and boundaries;
- the accepted work unit and current protocol condition;
- evidence and handoff provenance.

A protocol identity is not a person, legal identity, organizational office,
lifecycle identity, credential, service account, session identifier, or tool
instance, though an implementation may associate those representations with it.
One participant acting for different matters, functions, or delegations uses
distinguishable protocol identities.

## 2.3 Delegation envelope

A **delegation envelope** is the complete, attributable boundary within which a
protocol identity may act. It contains:

- legitimate authority source and delegating organizational function;
- accountable human authority;
- governed-matter identity, purpose, and lifecycle stage;
- accepted objective, scope, exclusions, and semantic owners;
- permitted action classes and prohibited effects;
- required dependencies, evidence, checks, and review boundary;
- materiality, reversibility, uncertainty, and escalation constraints;
- interruption and suspension conditions;
- validity condition and termination condition.

The envelope may narrow inherited authority but cannot enlarge, transfer, or
reinterpret it. Silence in an envelope grants nothing.

## 2.4 Autonomous work unit

An **autonomous work unit** is a bounded portion of a governed matter accepted
by a protocol identity for execution. It preserves the parent matter's
direction, lifecycle stage, authority, semantic boundaries, and evidence
requirements. Division into work units does not divide or multiply authority.

## 2.5 Protocol condition

A **protocol condition** reports the execution posture of a protocol identity:

- **Offered** — a bounded work unit is available for acceptance;
- **Accepted** — the participant has validated the envelope and assumed the
  bounded execution responsibility;
- **Executing** — permitted actions are being prepared or performed;
- **Handoff Pending** — responsibility is offered to another protocol identity;
- **Suspended** — autonomous action is stopped while authority or safe recovery
  is unresolved;
- **Completed** — the accepted work unit and required evidence were returned;
- **Released** — the identity has no continuing responsibility for the work
  unit.

Protocol conditions are not M7.4 lifecycle stages, governance decisions, tool
states, or authority sources. Changing a protocol condition cannot cross a
lifecycle gate.

## 2.6 Protocol record

A **protocol record** is the attributable account of identity, delegation,
acceptance, actions, evidence, handoffs, interruptions, suspensions, recovery,
completion, and release. It supports lifecycle traceability but cannot create
authority, evidence truth, judgment, or transition.

# 3. Protocol Participation Roles

Protocol roles allocate execution responsibility only. They do not add to,
replace, or rename the six constitutional governance functions defined by the
Organizational Model. Every protocol role must identify the organizational
function it supports and the accountable human authority to which it resolves.

## 3.1 Accountable autonomous sponsor

The **accountable autonomous sponsor** is the identifiable human authority
answerable for delegating bounded autonomous participation. The sponsor:

- confirms the legitimate delegation source and supported function;
- ensures the envelope is explicit and no broader than available authority;
- remains reachable through a defined escalation path;
- may narrow, suspend, revoke, or terminate the delegation;
- remains accountable for granting and supervising autonomous scope.

Sponsorship does not transfer another function's decision authority. A sponsor
cannot use delegation to pre-approve a result, dictate independent judgment, or
bypass Transition Authority.

## 3.2 Autonomous work participant

The **autonomous work participant** accepts and executes a bounded work unit in
support of Creation Authority or another explicitly delegated non-judgment
function. It may choose bounded means, sequence its permitted actions, test
assumptions, prepare results, and generate evidence within the envelope.

It must refuse or suspend work when authority, scope, semantic ownership,
dependencies, material risk, or evidence obligations are insufficient. It
cannot broaden direction, approve its output, close findings, perform
transition, or infer permission from technical capability.

## 3.3 Protocol coordinator

The **protocol coordinator** preserves work-unit identity, dependency
relationships, responsibility location, handoff completeness, and protocol
condition across multiple participants.

Coordination is record and routing responsibility. It cannot set product
priority, allocate undelegated authority, merge governed matters, declare
evidence true, determine conformance, or authorize lifecycle progression.

## 3.4 Evidence producer

The **evidence producer** records attributable observations resulting from
autonomous work. It distinguishes observed fact, derived result, assumption,
uncertainty, participant claim, failed attempt, and unavailable verification.

Evidence production may be performed by the work participant or a separately
identified participant. In either case, evidence cannot issue a verdict,
conceal contradictory observations, or be rewritten to match a desired review
outcome.

## 3.5 Handoff sender and receiver

The **handoff sender** offers bounded responsibility and its complete context.
The **handoff receiver** independently validates and accepts or refuses that
offer. A handoff does not occur merely because information was sent, a task was
assigned, or access was granted.

Neither role may treat handoff as delegation of authority unless the receiver's
own valid envelope explicitly contains that authority. The sender retains
responsibility until acceptance is attributable.

## 3.6 Review support participant

The **review support participant** may assemble the fixed review subject,
evidence map, provenance, disclosed participation, and clarification requested
by Judgment Authority.

It cannot act as independent Judgment Authority for a result it created or
materially shaped, select the verdict, resolve its own blocking findings, alter
the fixed subject during judgment, or perform the resulting transition.

# 4. Authority Inheritance and Constitutional Constraints

## 4.1 Derivation of permitted action

An autonomous action is permitted only when all of the following agree:

1. a legitimate upstream authority source;
2. the organizational function being supported;
3. an attributable delegation from that function;
4. the governed matter's valid M7.4 lifecycle stage;
5. the accepted work-unit scope and exclusions;
6. applicable semantic ownership and canonical dependencies;
7. the action class and effect allowed by the envelope;
8. accountable human authority and available escalation;
9. required evidence and verification conditions;
10. absence of an active stop, suspension, or review freeze condition.

If any condition is absent, contradictory, expired, or unknowable, autonomous
permission does not exist.

## 4.2 Inheritance rules

1. A participant inherits constraints, never independent authority.
2. Delegated action remains attributable to its source function and accountable
   human authority.
3. A delegation cannot include power the delegator does not possess.
4. A participant may narrow its accepted scope but cannot broaden it.
5. Authority for one matter, stage, decision class, semantic scope, or action
   does not transfer to another.
6. Prior success, precedent, repeated delegation, technical access, possession
   of credentials, or absence of intervention creates no future permission.
7. Redelegation is prohibited unless explicitly permitted, narrower than the
   source envelope, attributable, and subject to the same human accountability.
8. Revocation or expiry ends future action immediately and cannot be defeated
   by queued, cached, scheduled, or partially prepared work.
9. Exceptional conditions narrow or stop autonomous action; they never expand
   it.

## 4.3 Lifecycle subordination

Autonomous participation conforms to the current M7.4 lifecycle stage:

| Lifecycle stage | Permitted protocol relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Directed | Clarify the offered work and validate whether a conforming envelope can be accepted; do not begin creation before Creation Authority accepts direction. |
| In Creation | Execute bounded creation or correction work and produce attributable evidence within valid direction. |
| Ready for Judgment | Preserve the fixed subject, disclose participation, assemble evidence, and provide non-substantive clarification. |
| Under Judgment | Support independent Judgment only as requested; do not change the subject, create missing facts, influence the verdict, or continue creation on the fixed result. |
| Correction Required | Execute only the bounded corrections authorized by the finding and direction, preserving prior subject and evidence. |
| Transition Ready | Preserve the exact judged result and verify delegated prerequisites; do not perform transition unless separately acting under valid Transition Authority. |
| Authoritative | Consume the authoritative result within scope or prepare a separately directed change; do not silently modify authoritative meaning. |
| Closed or Superseded | Preserve history and cease action under the terminated identity; renewed work requires a new or explicitly linked governed matter. |

Protocol acceptance, completion, handoff, suspension, or recovery cannot enter,
exit, or bypass any lifecycle stage.

## 4.4 Decision-class boundary

Autonomous participation may assist a purpose, priority, production, semantic,
conformance, transition, or record decision only within explicit delegation
from its owning organizational function. Assistance does not transfer ownership
of the decision. Reserved accountable human judgment remains human even when
automation supplies analysis or recommendations.

# 5. Autonomous Work Initiation and Acceptance

## 5.1 Offer conditions

A work unit may be offered only when:

- the governed matter is identifiable and legitimately Directed;
- the current lifecycle stage permits the proposed execution;
- the objective, expected result, scope, exclusions, and completion boundary
  are explicit;
- the supported organizational function and accountable sponsor are named;
- canonical dependencies and semantic owners are identifiable;
- permitted effects, evidence obligations, uncertainty limits, and suspension
  conditions are defined;
- dependencies among concurrent work units do not imply merged authority.

An offer is not direction, acceptance, permission, priority, or approval by
itself.

## 5.2 Acceptance validation

Before acting, the receiving participant must determine that:

1. the protocol identity and delegation envelope are attributable;
2. the objective fits the supported function and current lifecycle stage;
3. required inputs and authority sources are available and mutually consistent;
4. the participant can remain inside scope and preserve semantic ownership;
5. expected effects are within permitted materiality and reversibility bounds;
6. evidence and verification obligations can be satisfied or their limits can
   be declared;
7. escalation and stop paths are usable;
8. no conflict of participation would compromise later independent judgment.

Acceptance must be explicit and recorded. Partial acceptance identifies the
accepted subset and leaves the remainder unaccepted.

## 5.3 Required refusal

The participant must refuse acceptance when:

- authority or accountable sponsorship is absent;
- scope depends on implied permission or inaccessible context;
- direction conflicts with a higher or semantically owning source;
- the requested act belongs to a different organizational function without
  valid delegation;
- the requested result would self-approve, manufacture evidence, predetermine
  judgment, or perform an unauthorized transition;
- safe execution or required evidence is impossible within the envelope;
- the participant cannot disclose a material conflict or limitation.

Refusal records the unsatisfied condition and creates neither failure authority
nor permission to seek a less constrained participant.

## 5.4 Execution conduct

After acceptance, the participant may exercise initiative only inside the
envelope. It must continuously preserve scope, dependencies, semantic ownership,
evidence distinction, and stop conditions. Discovery that changes purpose,
priority, authority, material risk, or accepted boundaries returns the matter
to the owning authority; it is not resolved through autonomous interpretation.

# 6. Handoff Protocol

## 6.1 Handoff packet

Every consequential handoff includes:

- governed-matter, lifecycle, protocol, and work-unit identities;
- current lifecycle stage and protocol condition;
- authority source, supported function, sponsor, and delegation boundary;
- accepted objective, scope, exclusions, and completion condition;
- semantic owners and exact dependencies;
- actions performed, decisions proposed, and effects produced;
- evidence, checks, failures, assumptions, uncertainty, and unresolved risk;
- pending responsibilities, blocking conditions, and required next authority;
- any interruption, suspension, recovery, or prior handoff history.

The packet distinguishes authoritative source content from participant summary
or inference. A summary cannot replace an exact dependency or evidence record.

## 6.2 Offer and acceptance semantics

1. The sender freezes the bounded handoff packet and records the offered
   responsibility.
2. The receiver validates identity, authority, completeness, compatibility, and
   its own envelope.
3. The receiver explicitly accepts, partially accepts, or refuses.
4. Acceptance identifies the exact packet and responsibility assumed.
5. The sender remains responsible until acceptance; after acceptance, retained
   and transferred responsibilities remain explicit.
6. Any substantive packet change invalidates prior acceptance and requires a
   new handoff.

Communication delivery, acknowledgement, assignment, access, timeout, silence,
or tool status cannot constitute acceptance.

## 6.3 Multi-participant coordination

Concurrent participants must retain distinguishable identities, scopes,
delegations, evidence, and effects. A coordinator may expose dependencies and
conflicts but cannot merge scopes or decide which authority prevails. Conflicts
return to the applicable semantic, direction, judgment, or transition owner.

# 7. Evidence Production Responsibilities

## 7.1 Evidence classes

Autonomous evidence distinguishes:

- source evidence obtained from an attributable dependency;
- action evidence describing what the participant performed;
- observation evidence describing what was directly observed;
- derived evidence produced through disclosed transformation or reasoning;
- validation evidence comparing a result with declared criteria;
- negative evidence recording failure, absence, contradiction, or inability to
  verify;
- limitation statements identifying uncertainty, coverage, and inaccessible
  facts.

## 7.2 Evidence integrity

Evidence must identify the protocol identity, work unit, relevant revision or
subject, method or basis, time relationship where material, and known limits.
It must preserve contradictory and failed observations and distinguish fact
from proposal, inference, recommendation, and judgment.

A participant cannot fabricate unavailable evidence, convert confidence into
observation, suppress evidence because it impedes completion, or treat its own
completion declaration as proof of conformance.

## 7.3 Evidence custody and return

Evidence remains linked to the governed matter through handoff, correction,
suspension, recovery, review, transition, closure, and supersession. Governance
Record Custody preserves discoverability; custody does not validate evidence or
grant a verdict.

# 8. Review Interaction Protocol

## 8.1 Creation exit support

Before Ready for Judgment, autonomous participants must identify the exact
result, evidence set, dependencies, limitations, unresolved risk, material
participation, and changes since any prior review subject. Creation may declare
readiness but cannot infer review acceptance.

## 8.2 Judgment entry

Judgment Authority independently decides whether scope, evidence, and
independence are sufficient. Autonomous participants disclose any creation,
evidence production, coordination, or prior recommendation that could affect
review independence.

No participant that created or materially shaped the reviewed result may use a
protocol identity to provide independent judgment on that result.

## 8.3 Under Judgment conduct

During judgment, supporting participants may retrieve exact sources, explain
recorded methods, reproduce non-substantive evidence, and answer clarification
requests. They may not:

- change the fixed subject without returning it to In Creation;
- generate facts that were required to exist before review entry;
- advocate through selective evidence or conceal adverse results;
- interpret ambiguity to favor approval;
- issue or encode the disposition;
- execute transition based on an anticipated verdict.

## 8.4 Findings and correction

Judgment Authority owns each finding, its blocking or advisory class, and its
closure condition. Autonomous Creation participants may accept a bounded
correction work unit but cannot redefine the finding or declare it closed.
Correction evidence preserves the prior and changed subjects. The corrected
result returns to the gate named by Judgment and receives independent
re-judgment.

## 8.5 Transition interaction

After judgment, autonomous participants may support prerequisite verification
or prepare a proposed reconciliation only under explicit delegation. The
Transition Authority must remain separately attributable and establishes no
state broader than the disposition. Automated publication, merge, deployment,
status update, or record synchronization cannot substitute for transition.

# 9. Interruption, Suspension, and Recovery

## 9.1 Interruption

An **interruption** is an event that prevents continued execution or requires
the participant to reassess permission. It may be external or participant
detected. Interruption records the last completed action, partial effects,
uncommitted intent, evidence state, and triggering condition.

Interruption alone does not change the lifecycle stage or revoke authority, but
no further action is permitted until the envelope remains demonstrably valid.

## 9.2 Mandatory suspension conditions

Autonomous execution enters Suspended when:

- direction, delegation, accountable sponsorship, or lifecycle state becomes
  missing, conflicting, expired, or revoked;
- scope, semantic ownership, or decision ownership is materially ambiguous;
- a requested or discovered effect may exceed permitted authority,
  materiality, reversibility, or risk;
- required evidence or verification cannot be produced reliably;
- a fixed review subject would be changed during judgment;
- a dependency changes in a way that may invalidate accepted work;
- concurrent participants produce unresolved conflicting effects;
- identity, provenance, record integrity, or handoff continuity is lost;
- a human stop, governing stop condition, or higher-authority constraint is
  received;
- degraded capability makes conformance unknowable.

Suspension is a protocol condition, not a lifecycle stage, finding, verdict, or
transition.

## 9.3 Suspension record

The suspended participant preserves:

- the trigger and detecting identity;
- last valid authority and lifecycle state;
- completed, partial, pending, and potentially affected actions;
- produced evidence and known evidence gaps;
- external effects and their reversibility status;
- unresolved questions and owning authority;
- the conditions required for safe resumption, bounded recovery, or release.

It must not conceal partial work, retry indefinitely, select a favorable prior
state, or continue through an alternate identity to evade suspension.

## 9.4 Recovery authorization

Recovery begins only when the authority owning the unresolved condition has
acted and the participant revalidates its envelope. Recovery may:

- resume the same work unit from a proven valid point;
- narrow or replace the delegation;
- hand off to a separately authorized identity;
- reverse a reversible effect under explicit authority;
- preserve the partial result as evidence and abandon execution;
- return the governed matter to the earliest M7.4 stage whose entry conditions
  are true.

Recovery does not imply retry, rollback, rescheduling, or orchestration. Those
are implementation choices subject to later bounded authority.

## 9.5 Release and termination

A protocol identity is Released when its work is accepted through handoff,
completed and returned, revoked, expired, or explicitly terminated. Release
ends continuing action permission but preserves accountability, evidence,
history, and unresolved effects. A released identity cannot silently resume.

# 10. Protocol Traceability

Every consequential protocol record must preserve:

- governed-matter, lifecycle, protocol, and work-unit identities;
- participant identity and accountable human sponsor;
- supported organizational function and delegation provenance;
- envelope scope, exclusions, validity, and termination conditions;
- lifecycle stage and protocol condition at each consequential action;
- exact dependencies, semantic owners, and relevant revisions;
- offered, accepted, refused, and released responsibilities;
- actions, effects, evidence, validation, failures, and uncertainty;
- handoff packets and acceptance dispositions;
- review participation and independence disclosures;
- interruptions, suspensions, recovery authority, and recovery result;
- proposed decisions distinguished from authoritative decisions;
- chronology sufficient to reconstruct authority and responsibility.

Traceability must be durable, attributable, inspectable, and protected from
silent rewriting. This requirement defines information integrity, not an
identifier scheme, repository layout, message format, database, platform, or
runtime architecture.

# 11. Protocol Invariants

1. Every autonomous action resolves to a legitimate source, supported
   organizational function, bounded delegation, and accountable human
   authority.
2. Autonomous capability creates no independent authority.
3. A protocol identity is distinguishable from lifecycle identity,
   organizational authority, human identity, and technical identity.
4. Protocol roles cannot create or replace a constitutional governance
   function.
5. Protocol conditions cannot create or change lifecycle state.
6. Silence, access, credentials, assignment, delivery, timeout, repetition, or
   prior success grants no permission.
7. A participant cannot broaden its envelope, purpose, priority, semantic scope,
   decision class, or permitted effect.
8. Delegation and redelegation remain narrower than, attributable to, and
   revocable by their source.
9. Ultimate accountability cannot terminate at an autonomous participant,
   tool, team label, process, or record.
10. Creation, evidence, Judgment, and Transition remain distinguishable through
    all autonomous participation.
11. A participant cannot approve its own result or close its own blocking
    finding.
12. Evidence cannot issue a verdict, and confidence cannot substitute for
    evidence.
13. Handoff transfers only explicitly accepted responsibility and never
    implicit authority.
14. The sender remains responsible until attributable acceptance; the receiver
    acts only under its own valid envelope.
15. Concurrent execution preserves matter, scope, delegation, evidence, and
    effect separation.
16. Substantive change to a fixed review subject requires lifecycle re-entry and
    independent re-judgment.
17. Transition never exceeds the exact disposition and cannot be inferred from
    automated state change.
18. Interruption, suspension, retry, recovery, or urgency cannot expand
    authority.
19. Revocation, expiry, or a valid stop condition ends future autonomous action
    regardless of queued or prepared work.
20. Partial, failed, contradictory, abandoned, and superseded work remains
    attributable.
21. Semantic ownership remains with the authoritative source throughout
    autonomous execution.
22. Degraded tooling or unavailable automation cannot relax constitutional,
    organizational, lifecycle, evidence, or accountability obligations.
23. Implementation behavior cannot silently redefine protocol meaning.
24. Constitutional legitimacy takes precedence over autonomous completion,
    efficiency, persistence, or convenience.

# 12. Conformance

An autonomous development realization or instance conforms only when:

1. every participant uses an attributable protocol identity;
2. every action is inside a valid delegation envelope;
3. accountable human authority and the supported organizational function are
   identifiable;
4. protocol roles remain execution responsibilities rather than new authority;
5. protocol conditions remain distinct from M7.4 lifecycle states;
6. initiation and acceptance validate authority, scope, dependencies,
   ownership, effects, evidence, and escalation;
7. mandatory refusal and suspension conditions stop unauthorized progression;
8. handoffs use explicit packets and receiver acceptance;
9. evidence preserves provenance, negative results, uncertainty, and the
   distinction between observation and judgment;
10. review support preserves fixed subjects, disclosure, independence,
    correction ownership, and re-judgment;
11. transition remains separately attributable and no broader than judgment;
12. interruption and recovery preserve partial effects, authority history, and
    the earliest valid lifecycle state;
13. concurrent participants retain distinguishable scopes and effects;
14. revocation and release prevent silent continuation or resumption;
15. traceability reconstructs authority, responsibility, action, evidence,
    handoff, review, suspension, and recovery;
16. implementation choices do not alter protocol or upstream governance
    meaning;
17. no automated participant becomes the source of ultimate accountability;
18. all Protocol Invariants remain true.

# 13. Scope and Exclusions

This Autonomous Development Protocol owns:

- autonomous participant and protocol identity semantics;
- protocol execution roles and responsibility boundaries;
- delegation-envelope and authority-inheritance constraints;
- autonomous work offer, acceptance, refusal, and execution conduct;
- handoff responsibility and acceptance semantics;
- autonomous evidence-production responsibilities;
- interaction with independent review and transition;
- interruption, suspension, recovery, release, and termination semantics;
- protocol traceability, invariants, and conformance requirements.

This Autonomous Development Protocol does not define:

- changes to constitutional authority, principles, precedence, amendment, or
  accountable human responsibility;
- new organizational powers or ownership outside the canonical Organizational
  Model;
- new lifecycle stages, gates, dispositions, or transition authority;
- product, professional, domain, experience, presentation, or architecture
  meaning owned by canonical sources;
- named people, vendors, AI products, models, agents, services, or tools;
- repository, branch, commit, pull-request, issue, file, or deployment
  mechanics;
- message schemas, identifier syntax, storage formats, databases, or APIs;
- scheduling algorithms, prioritization algorithms, retry counts, backoff,
  queue disciplines, timeout values, or resource allocation;
- infrastructure, network, security, runtime, or platform architecture;
- runtime orchestration implementation or topology;
- implementation methodology or delivery framework;
- governance operations, governance effectiveness assessment, or retrospective
  conclusions reserved for M7.6;
- M7.6–M7.7 authority or work.

Implementations may realize this protocol only through separately authorized
choices that preserve the canonical governance hierarchy and all protocol
invariants.
