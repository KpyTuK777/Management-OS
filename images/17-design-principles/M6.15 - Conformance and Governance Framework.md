# M6.15 — Conformance and Governance Framework

## Status

```text
Ready for Architecture Review
Milestone M6 — Active
```

## Authority

This document is the canonical architecture definition of conformance
qualification and governance for the M6 Presentation Architecture. It
consolidates the M6 Charter responsibilities named **Conformance and Validation
Framework** and **Design System Governance Model** while keeping their
contracts, owners, lifecycles, evidence, decisions, and review criteria
explicit, traceable, and independently reviewable.

It consumes all approved M6.1–M6.14 architecture without changing any source
meaning, identity, state, composition, interaction, navigation, rendering,
accessibility, motion, conversational, mapping, or adapter ownership.

Conformance determines whether an identified realization preserves an exact set
of approved obligations at exact revisions. Governance controls how M6
contracts may change. Neither creates professional meaning.

---

# 1. Purpose

M6 requires more than individually well-formed contracts. A realization must be
qualified against the complete applicable architecture, and changes to that
architecture must remain attributable, compatible, and under the authority of
their canonical owners.

This framework:

- makes conformance an exact-revision, evidence-backed qualification;
- prevents declarations, popularity, or implementation behavior from becoming
  proof;
- distinguishes model-level findings from aggregate M6 equivalence;
- makes omissions, limitations, degradation, and expired evidence visible;
- requires correction and requalification after invalidating change;
- separates proposal, realization, evidence production, independent review,
  and canonical approval responsibilities;
- governs M6 change, extension, versioning, compatibility, deprecation,
  migration, withdrawal, exception, traceability, and history;
- prepares the M6 Architecture Review and Final Review gates without executing
  or pre-approving either.

The framework is not a test system, workflow product, repository process, or
implementation quality model.

---

# 2. Framework Principles

1. **Source Authority Is Retained.** Every obligation remains owned by its
   approved canonical source.
2. **Exact Revision Qualification.** A verdict applies only to the identified
   source set and realization revision.
3. **Complete Applicable Coverage.** Unexamined mandatory obligations cannot
   receive a positive verdict.
4. **Evidence Before Verdict.** Assertion, intent, or observed popularity is not
   conformance evidence.
5. **Independent Judgment.** The artifact, adapter, or evidence producer under
   review cannot issue its own qualification.
6. **Binary Qualification.** Where qualification is required, the verdict is
   Conformant or Non-conformant; uncertainty prevents Conformant.
7. **No Averaging of Meaning.** Success in one obligation or modality cannot
   compensate for failure in another.
8. **Negative Paths Are First Class.** Invalid, unavailable, unauthorized,
   degraded, interrupted, and recovery conditions require evidence.
9. **Governance Coordinates; Owners Decide.** Governance routes and records
   change but cannot invent or reinterpret canonical meaning.
10. **Roles Remain Separable.** Proposal, implementation/evidence production,
    independent review, and authority disposition are distinct
    responsibilities.
11. **Exceptions Never Weaken Meaning.** An exception may constrain
    applicability or publication but cannot waive a mandatory semantic
    obligation.
12. **Atomic Qualification.** A published qualification exposes one complete
    verdict and evidence set, never a partial or mixed state.
13. **History Is Preserved.** Supersession and withdrawal do not rewrite prior
    sources, evidence, verdicts, or decisions.
14. **Implementation Is Replaceable.** Tools, platforms, formats, and mechanics
    remain outside canonical authority.
15. **Final Approval Is Separate.** Model conformance and M6 Architecture Review
    readiness do not equal the M6 Final Review decision.

# 3. Conformance Contract

## 3.1 Definition and ownership

A **Conformance Qualification** is the bounded determination that one identified
realization revision preserves every applicable obligation from one exact,
approved source set within a declared scope.

This framework owns:

- the qualification descriptor and lifecycle;
- CF-01–CF-16 conformance categories;
- evidence sufficiency, independence, expiry, and supersession rules;
- deterministic verdict and publication rules;
- correction and requalification obligations;
- aggregate qualification rules.

It does not own the obligations it qualifies. M6.1–M6.14 and their canonical
inputs retain their ID families and meanings. It does not own realization
mechanics or approve changes to source contracts.

## 3.2 Qualification descriptor

Every qualification contains:

| Field | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Qualification identity** | Stable identity of this qualification record. |
| **Realization identity** | Exact artifact, adapter, composition, or aggregate realization under review. |
| **Realization revision** | Immutable revision boundary actually examined. |
| **Source set** | Every applicable canonical source identity and exact approved revision. |
| **Scope** | Included contexts, modalities, capabilities, states, and boundaries. |
| **Applicability ledger** | CF-01–CF-16 applicability with every included and inapplicable obligation justified. |
| **Evidence set** | Positive and negative evidence, provenance, limitations, and reproduction conditions. |
| **Independence declaration** | Separation of realization/evidence production from qualification review. |
| **Findings** | Obligation-level results and attributable failures. |
| **Verdict** | Exactly Conformant or Non-conformant when qualification completes. |
| **Validity conditions** | Source, realization, capability, scope, and evidence conditions under which the verdict remains current. |
| **Publication state** | Draft, Under Independent Review, Qualified, Superseded, Expired, or Withdrawn. |
| **History references** | Predecessor, correction, requalification, exception, and supersession relationships. |

Scope cannot conceal an obligation required by the declared realization.
Inapplicability requires source-based evidence; unsupported capability is a
degradation fact, not automatic inapplicability.

## 3.3 Responsibilities

The Conformance Contract must:

- resolve the complete applicable source set before examination;
- preserve each source ID and revision without substitution;
- test both required presence and prohibited behavior;
- distinguish source failure, realization failure, evidence insufficiency, and
  scope error;
- expose limitations and validity conditions with the verdict;
- invalidate qualification when an identified condition changes;
- make correction traceable to failed obligations;
- require a new qualification after correction or invalidation;
- keep model-level and aggregate verdicts independently inspectable.

## 3.4 Lifecycle

```text
Scoped
  ↓
Evidence Prepared
  ↓
Under Independent Review
  ├─ insufficient / failed → Non-conformant
  └─ complete pass         → Conformant
                                ↓
              source, realization, capability,
              scope, or evidence validity changes
                                ↓
                    Expired or Superseded
                                ↓
                     correction / requalification
```

- **Scoped** identifies realization, revisions, source set, and applicability.
- **Evidence Prepared** contains complete positive and negative evidence.
- **Under Independent Review** permits no published positive qualification.
- **Conformant** means every applicable obligation passed with sufficient
  evidence.
- **Non-conformant** means at least one obligation failed, evidence is
  insufficient, independence is absent, or scope is invalid.
- **Expired** means validity can no longer be established.
- **Superseded** preserves a prior verdict while a later qualification replaces
  its current use.
- **Withdrawn** ends current reliance without erasing history.

Correction never edits a completed verdict. It produces a new realization
revision or evidence set and a new qualification identity.

## 3.5 Conformance invariants

1. A qualification identifies exactly one realization revision.
2. A qualification identifies exact revisions of every applicable source.
3. A verdict does not apply to an unnamed source, realization, context,
   capability, or revision.
4. Every mandatory applicable obligation has an explicit result.
5. Inapplicability is justified from canonical scope, not implementation
   convenience.
6. Unsupported capability is evaluated through declared degradation and
   recovery obligations.
7. Positive evidence proves required behavior.
8. Negative evidence proves prohibited behavior is rejected or safely bounded.
9. Missing evidence cannot yield Conformant.
10. Conflicting evidence cannot yield Conformant until resolved.
11. Evidence identifies provenance, production conditions, and limitations.
12. Evidence remains reproducible by an independent reviewer within its
    declared validity conditions.
13. A realization cannot certify itself.
14. An adapter cannot certify itself.
15. An evidence producer cannot be the sole independent reviewer of that
    evidence.
16. Reviewer fluency, confidence, or authority claims do not replace evidence.
17. One failed mandatory obligation yields Non-conformant.
18. Passing obligations cannot offset a failed obligation.
19. Each completed required qualification has exactly one binary verdict.
20. A Draft or Under Independent Review record has no qualification verdict.
21. Qualification publication is atomic.
22. Partial evidence cannot be published as an aggregate positive verdict.
23. Model-level qualification does not imply aggregate M6 conformance.
24. Aggregate qualification requires every applicable model-level obligation
    and cross-model relationship to pass.
25. Platform-specific success does not establish cross-platform equivalence.
26. Equivalence is preservation of obligations, not sameness of mechanics.
27. Exact-revision source change invalidates reliance until applicability and
    compatibility are reassessed.
28. Realization change invalidates reliance for the changed realization.
29. Capability change invalidates affected scope.
30. Expired evidence cannot support Conformant.
31. Correction preserves the failed verdict and its provenance.
32. Requalification creates a new attributable verdict.
33. Supersession never rewrites a predecessor.
34. Withdrawal never converts Non-conformant into Conformant.
35. An exception cannot erase a failed mandatory obligation.
36. A limitation is visible wherever its verdict is relied upon.
37. Degradation must be explicit, deterministic, semantically safe, and
    reviewable.
38. Recovery evidence proves restoration or safe continuation without invented
    state.
39. Qualification cannot grant canonical ownership to a realization.
40. Qualification cannot amend the source being qualified.

## 4.2 Taxonomy rules

1. CF-01 establishes semantic and identity integrity but does not replace
   model-specific checks.
2. CF-02–CF-12 qualify their source-owned model families without restating or
   renumbering them.
3. CF-13 and CF-14 apply across every category where limitation or recovery is
   possible.
4. CF-15 evaluates relationships that cannot be proven inside one model alone.
5. CF-16 is required only for an aggregate cross-platform claim, but such a
   claim cannot be made from a single platform or modality.
6. A qualification may be scoped to one category or model only when it does
   not claim aggregate M6 conformance.

# 5. Evidence Architecture

## 5.1 Evidence-set descriptor

An evidence set contains:

- qualification, realization, and exact realization revision identities;
- every source identity and exact source revision;
- an obligation ledger mapping each applicable source obligation to evidence;
- applicability and inapplicability rationale;
- normal-path positive evidence;
- negative-path and prohibited-behavior evidence;
- degraded, unavailable, interrupted, recovery, and changed-capability
  evidence where applicable;
- production conditions sufficient for independent reproduction;
- evidence producer provenance;
- independent reviewer identity and separation declaration;
- known limitations, unresolved conflicts, and validity bounds;
- retention, supersession, expiry, and predecessor relationships.

Evidence may be shared across obligations only when the ledger proves that it
independently supports each result. Evidence volume is not evidence coverage.

## 5.2 Sufficiency and reproducibility

Evidence is sufficient only when an independent reviewer can:

1. reconstruct the exact source and realization boundary;
2. determine the complete applicable obligation set;
3. reproduce the material observation within declared conditions;
4. distinguish observed facts from interpretation;
5. inspect both required outcomes and forbidden outcomes;
6. identify limitations and contradictory evidence;
7. derive the same verdict under this contract.

Where reproduction is impossible because validity conditions have expired, the
evidence is expired and cannot support current Conformant status.

## 5.3 Independence

The following responsibilities are separate:

| Responsibility | Permitted contribution | Prohibited contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Realization producer** | Identify revision, scope, capabilities, and supply attributable observations. | Issue the independent verdict for its own realization. |
| **Evidence producer** | Produce reproducible positive and negative evidence. | Be the sole independent reviewer of that evidence. |
| **Independent reviewer** | Evaluate coverage, sufficiency, conflicts, and derive the verdict. | Invent source meaning or approve source change. |
| **Canonical authority holder** | Approve changes to sources it owns and resolve source meaning. | Substitute authority claims for qualification evidence. |

One participant may perform multiple responsibilities only when independence is
not required between those responsibilities. The realization or adapter under
review is never a reviewer.

## 5.4 Retention, expiry, and supersession

- Evidence is retained with the verdict it supported.
- New evidence does not silently alter a completed verdict.
- Evidence expires when its source revision, realization revision, capability
  conditions, reproduction conditions, or declared validity cease to hold.
- A newer qualification supersedes current reliance but preserves predecessor
  history.
- Limitations remain attached to retained evidence and cannot be removed by
  summary.

# 6. Verdict, Failure, Correction, and Requalification

## 6.1 Deterministic verdict

For a completed required qualification:

```text
Conformant
```

is permitted only when every applicable obligation has sufficient,
non-conflicting, independently reviewed evidence and every boundary,
degradation, recovery, and validity condition passes.

Otherwise the verdict is:

```text
Non-conformant
```

“Partially conformant,” “provisionally conformant,” and equivalent blended
verdicts are not qualification outcomes. Progress may be recorded in Draft
findings but carries no positive verdict.

## 6.2 Failure record

Each failure identifies:

- failed obligation and owning source revision;
- affected realization, context, modality, and capability;
- observed divergence or evidence insufficiency;
- professional interpretation or boundary at risk;
- required correction condition;
- evidence needed for requalification;
- related failures, exceptions, and history.

Required correction describes the condition to restore, not implementation
mechanics.

## 6.3 Invalidation and requalification

Requalification is mandatory after:

- an applicable canonical source revision changes;
- the qualified realization changes;
- a relied-upon capability or adapter boundary changes;
- evidence expires or material contradictory evidence appears;
- scope expands;
- a correction is made;
- a governance decision alters applicability or compatibility.

Requalification begins from a new Scoped state. Prior evidence may be reused
only when its exact conditions remain valid and the new obligation ledger
records that determination.

## 6.4 Exceptions

An exception is a governed, attributable disposition for a bounded conflict,
transition, or inability to publish. It records scope, rationale, authority,
duration/termination condition, affected obligations, risk, and recovery path.

An exception:

- cannot make failed evidence pass;
- cannot redefine Conformant;
- cannot waive identity, meaning, authority, consequence, accessibility,
  navigation continuity, degradation, recovery, or source ownership;
- cannot become precedent outside its recorded scope;
- cannot remain implicit;
- ends in correction, withdrawal, supersession, or renewed governed
  disposition.

# 7. Governance Contract

## 7.1 Definition and ownership

The **Governance Contract** is the architecture-level control over admission,
change, review, approval, publication, compatibility, deprecation, migration,
withdrawal, exception, traceability, and historical preservation of M6
contracts.

Governance owns process semantics and decision traceability. It does not own the
professional or presentation meaning inside a governed source. The canonical
owner of each source alone may approve changes to that source.

## 7.2 Governance descriptor

Every governed change contains:

| Field | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Change identity** | Stable identity of the proposal and decision chain. |
| **Affected sources** | Exact governed source identities and revisions. |
| **Proposer** | Responsibility that states the need and evidence. |
| **Rationale** | Problem, evidence, intended outcome, and non-goals. |
| **Change class** | Clarification, compatible extension, incompatible change, deprecation, migration, withdrawal, or correction. |
| **Ownership map** | Canonical authority holder for each affected source. |
| **Impact analysis** | Meaning, compatibility, adapters, mappings, evidence, migration, and dependent-contract effects. |
| **Review record** | Independent findings, conflicts, limitations, and required corrections. |
| **Authority disposition** | Approved, Rejected, or Requires Correction by the proper source owner. |
| **Version disposition** | Resulting revision identity and compatibility relationship. |
| **Transition obligations** | Deprecation, migration, requalification, withdrawal, and recovery requirements. |
| **History** | Predecessor, successor, supersession, exception, and decision references. |

## 7.3 Abstract responsibilities

| Responsibility | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Proposer** | Problem statement, evidence, requested scope, and impact claim. | Approval or canonical meaning. |
| **Implementer / evidence producer** | Realization of an approved definition and attributable evidence. | Architecture change, self-qualification, or source approval. |
| **Independent reviewer** | Evidence-based assessment, findings, and review disposition within the assigned gate. | Canonical source meaning or implementation authority. |
| **Canonical authority holder** | Approval or rejection of changes to the source it owns. | Other owners' sources or conformance by assertion. |
| **Governance custodian** | Routing, gate integrity, records, version relationships, conflict escalation, and history. | Inventing meaning, overriding owners, or implementing change. |

Responsibility names are architectural roles, not organization titles, product
names, or staffing requirements.

## 7.4 Governance lifecycle

```text
Proposed
  ↓
Ownership and Scope Validated
  ↓
Impact Assessed
  ↓
Independent Architecture Review
  ├─ Rejected
  ├─ Requires Correction → revised proposal
  └─ Recommended
         ↓
Canonical Authority Disposition
  ├─ Rejected
  └─ Approved
         ↓
Versioned Publication
         ↓
Migration / Requalification / Deprecation
         ↓
Superseded or Withdrawn
```

No later state may be inferred from completion of an earlier gate.
Publication occurs only after every affected canonical authority has issued the
required disposition.

## 7.5 Change, versioning, and compatibility

- A clarification changes no obligation and requires evidence that all prior
  valid interpretations remain unchanged.
- A compatible extension adds an optional or newly bounded responsibility
  without changing existing mandatory meaning.
- An incompatible change alters applicability, obligation, interpretation, or
  valid realization and requires explicit migration and requalification.
- Every publication has a distinct revision identity.
- Compatibility is assessed against exact predecessor revisions and affected
  dependents; it is never inferred from labels.
- Extensions occupy declared extension points, cite canonical basis, preserve
  existing IDs, and cannot overload an existing category.
- Local extensions remain non-canonical until admitted through governance.

## 7.6 Deprecation, migration, and withdrawal

Deprecation:

- marks a still-identifiable contract or revision as scheduled for replacement;
- names its successor or safe withdrawal path;
- preserves current meaning during the transition;
- identifies affected mappings, realizations, evidence, and requalification.

Migration:

- maps every affected obligation and identity from predecessor to successor;
- identifies incompatibilities and safe intermediate dispositions;
- preserves canonical history and prevents mixed-revision publication;
- completes only when required dependent qualifications are current.

Withdrawal:

- ends permitted current reliance;
- preserves the withdrawn source, rationale, evidence, and prior decisions;
- cannot erase identity or make historical evidence current.

## 7.7 Conflict escalation

A conflict exists when:

- two approved obligations appear mutually unsatisfiable;
- ownership is ambiguous or contested;
- a proposed change crosses canonical owners without complete dispositions;
- evidence supports incompatible interpretations;
- compatibility cannot be established without changing meaning.

Governance records the exact sources, revisions, owners, evidence, and blocked
decision, then returns the conflict to the relevant canonical authority
holders. Governance cannot resolve it by weakening, averaging, silently
prioritizing, or reinterpreting obligations. Dependent publication and positive
qualification remain blocked until an attributable disposition exists.

## 7.8 Governance invariants

1. Every governed source has one explicit canonical authority holder.
2. Governance custody is not canonical ownership.
3. A proposer gains no approval authority by proposing.
4. An implementer gains no source authority by realizing a contract.
5. An evidence producer gains no source authority by observing behavior.
6. An independent reviewer gains no source authority by issuing findings.
7. Only the canonical owner may approve a change to its source.
8. Cross-owner changes require every affected owner's disposition.
9. Every change begins from identified source revisions.
10. Every approved change produces a distinct revision.
11. Published revisions are immutable historical facts.
12. A clarification cannot change valid meaning or obligation.
13. A compatible extension cannot invalidate a conformant predecessor
    realization within its prior scope.
14. An incompatible change requires migration and requalification.
15. An extension cannot overload an existing canonical ID.
16. A local extension cannot claim canonical status before admission.
17. Deprecation does not remove current obligations during its declared
    transition.
18. Migration preserves identity and maps every affected obligation.
19. Mixed source revisions cannot be published as one governed contract set.
20. Withdrawal preserves history and provenance.
21. An exception is explicit, bounded, attributable, and terminable.
22. An exception cannot weaken mandatory canonical meaning.
23. A governance record distinguishes proposal, review, authority disposition,
    and publication.
24. Review recommendation is not authority approval.
25. Authority approval is not conformance evidence.
26. Conformance evidence is not architecture approval.
27. Governance cannot convert implementation convention into canonical
    meaning.
28. Governance cannot rewrite a prior decision or verdict.
29. Supersession links predecessor and successor without erasure.
30. Every dependent contract affected by change is identified.
31. Every invalidated qualification is identified for requalification.
32. Compatibility is evidence-backed and revision-specific.
33. Unresolved ownership conflict blocks publication.
34. Unresolved semantic contradiction returns to canonical authority.
35. History remains reconstructable from proposal through terminal
    disposition.
36. Governance remains independent of organization structure, tools, cadence,
    and platform mechanics.

# 8. Boundary and Ownership Separation

```text
Canonical Sources
        ↓ approved meaning and exact revisions
Governance-controlled M6 Contracts
        ↓ traceable obligations and change control
Independent Conformance Qualification
        ↓ atomic verdict plus evidence
Qualified Realizations and Retained Evidence
```

| Layer | Owns | Must not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Canonical Sources** | Professional and source-specific meaning, identity, authority, and obligations. | Implementation mechanics or evidence outcomes. |
| **Governance-controlled M6 Contracts** | Controlled translation obligations, revision relationships, gates, and history. | Upstream meaning or realization behavior. |
| **Independent Conformance Qualification** | Scope, evidence assessment, findings, validity, and deterministic verdict. | Canonical meaning, architecture change, or implementation. |
| **Qualified Realizations and Evidence** | Replaceable mechanics and attributable observations. | Canonical authority, self-approval, or reverse definition. |

No downward layer may become authoritative for an upward layer. Upward layers
do not prescribe downstream mechanics. A qualification describes preservation;
it does not promote a realization into architecture.

# 9. Final M6 Gates

## 9.1 Model-level conformance gate

Each M6 model must have:

- an approved architecture definition;
- complete source and ownership traceability;
- deterministic obligations and conformance criteria;
- no unresolved required correction;
- evidence appropriate to its architecture-level review.

Passing this gate establishes model readiness only.

## 9.2 Aggregate Architecture Review readiness gate

M6 is ready for aggregate Architecture Review only when:

- M6.1–M6.15 definitions and individual reviews are present;
- their approved revisions form one consistent dependency set;
- CF-01–CF-15 coverage is complete at architecture level;
- cross-model identity, authority, state, navigation, accessibility,
  interaction, degradation, recovery, and mapping relationships have no
  unresolved contradiction;
- conformance and governance responsibilities remain independently reviewable;
- repository records consistently identify the same active milestone and gate.

Readiness is not an aggregate verdict.

## 9.3 Separate M6 Final Review

The M6 Final Review is a separate canonical decision. It:

- evaluates the complete M6 contract set and Charter completion criteria;
- inspects aggregate and cross-platform/cross-canonical evidence;
- records repository consistency and unresolved risk;
- returns only the Charter-authorized final verdict;
- alone may authorize M6 milestone closure and roadmap completion.

This M6.15 definition does not perform, approve, predict, or replace that
review.

## 9.4 Repository consistency

Before either review gate can complete, canonical documents, review records,
the Architecture Journal, backlog, roadmap, and cited revision history must
agree on:

- document identities and dispositions;
- exact approved dependency revisions;
- active milestone and next gate;
- unresolved corrections and exceptions;
- absence of implicit approval or closure.

Inconsistency blocks a positive gate until resolved. Repository consistency is
evidence of control, not proof of semantic conformance.

# 10. Architecture Conformance Criteria

This architecture definition is conformant only when:

1. Conformance and Governance Contracts are explicit and independently
   reviewable;
2. CF-01–CF-16 are distinct, complete, and traceable to M6.1–M6.14;
3. exact source and realization revisions bound every qualification;
4. applicability, positive evidence, negative evidence, limitations,
   provenance, reproducibility, retention, and expiry are mandatory;
5. no realization, adapter, or evidence producer can self-certify;
6. completed qualification verdicts are deterministic and atomic;
7. failure, correction, invalidation, expiry, supersession, withdrawal, and
   requalification preserve history;
8. exceptions cannot weaken mandatory meaning;
9. governance separates proposal, implementation/evidence production,
   independent review, custody, and canonical authority;
10. canonical owners retain approval of their sources;
11. change, versioning, extension, compatibility, deprecation, migration, and
    withdrawal are revision-specific and traceable;
12. conflicts return to canonical authority rather than being reinterpreted;
13. model, aggregate readiness, and Final Review gates remain distinct;
14. M6 closure cannot be inferred from this definition;
15. no implementation technology or organization-specific mechanism is
    selected.

# 11. Completion Criteria

M6.15 is ready for Architecture Review when:

- the Conformance Contract, descriptor, lifecycle, responsibilities, verdicts,
  correction, expiry, and requalification are complete;
- CF-01–CF-16 cover every required M6 concern and aggregate equivalence;
- evidence architecture is exact-revision-bound, complete, reproducible,
  independent, limitation-aware, and historically retained;
- the Governance Contract, descriptor, abstract responsibilities, lifecycle,
  change, versioning, extension, compatibility, deprecation, migration,
  withdrawal, exceptions, traceability, history, and conflict escalation are
  complete;
- all 40 conformance invariants and 36 governance invariants are sequential and
  reviewable;
- canonical authority and implementation non-authority are preserved;
- final M6 gates are prepared but not executed or pre-approved;
- the boundary is implementation-independent;
- repository state records M6 as Active and M6.15 as Ready for Architecture
  Review.
