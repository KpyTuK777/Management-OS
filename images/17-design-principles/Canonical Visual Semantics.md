# Canonical Visual Semantics

## Status

```text
Architecture & Design Language
Milestone M5
Canonical Review Candidate
```

## Purpose

This document defines how professional meaning becomes visual representation in
Management OS. It is a semantic language, not a design, UI Kit, Design System,
component library, or implementation specification.

It governs what a visual distinction may mean, which meanings must remain
independent, how attention follows professional importance, and which ambiguity
is forbidden. It does not prescribe a palette, color, typeface, typography
scale, spacing value, icon, SVG, component, layout, Figma artifact, CSS, motion
duration, or implementation technology.

Visual Semantics is downstream of canonical domain, experience, navigation, and
governance models. It may represent their meaning; it cannot create or alter
domain truth, lifecycle, authority, Evidence, Attention Priority, Focus,
Relationship, Decision, Outcome, Operational Memory, or Context Envelope.

# 1. Visual Philosophy

## 1.1 What it means to see a Matter

To see a Matter is to recognize its professional condition before reading every
detail. A manager must be able to perceive:

1. what Matter this is and whether the context is current, historical, or
   simulated;
2. what accepted understanding exists and where its uncertainty lies;
3. what currently deserves attention and what may safely remain peripheral;
4. which claims are observed, proposed, supported, challenged, confirmed,
   rejected, or unresolved;
5. who holds authority and which acts remain proposals;
6. what consequence, dependency, contradiction, or guardrail could change the
   work;
7. what changed, what remains unfinished, and how the context can be recovered.

Management OS does not merely display information. It makes professional state,
confidence, context, and attention legible while preserving the distinctions
that make judgment accountable.

## 1.2 Four visible professional questions

Every consequential representation must help answer:

- **State —** What is this, and what canonical condition is it in?
- **Confidence —** What is known, proposed, uncertain, supported, contradicted,
  or accepted, and on what basis?
- **Context —** Which Matter, intent, authority, time, source, and history make
  this meaningful?
- **Attention —** What is primary now, what supports it, what is material at the
  periphery, and what can wait?

No single visual signal may answer all four. The representation composes
independent signals whose meanings remain inspectable.

## 1.3 Semantic honesty

Visual treatment is a claim. Greater prominence claims greater current
attention relevance. A connector claims a relationship. A persistent mark
claims stable identity or state. Motion claims change in time. Grouping claims a
shared boundary or purpose.

Such claims are permitted only when canonical inputs support them. Decoration
cannot impersonate causality, confidence, authority, urgency, completion, or
progression.

## 1.4 Meaning before style

The same canonical input must communicate the same professional meaning across
different visual styles. A style may vary its aesthetic expression only when:

- the semantic distinction remains recognizable;
- the same dimension is not reassigned to another meaning;
- meaning survives monochrome, reduced motion, zoom, and non-visual access;
- labels and inspectable context resolve any unfamiliar representation;
- the style introduces no stronger claim than the canonical state.

# 2. Canonical Semantic Inventory

## 2.1 Inventory rules

A concept belongs in the inventory only when it:

- is a canonical domain object or professional context;
- is an attributable professional act, condition, or state dimension;
- materially changes interpretation, authority, attention, continuity, or
  recoverability;
- can be grounded in an owning canonical model;
- must remain distinguishable to prevent a professional error.

The inventory does not create new entities. “Semantic object” below means a
canonical subject or condition eligible for representation.

# 3. Independent Semantic Dimensions

## 3.1 Orthogonality rule

A semantic object may carry several dimensions at once, but each dimension
answers a different professional question. No change in one dimension silently
changes another. Every surface must maintain a local signal registry in which
one primary visual signal has one professional meaning.

Redundancy is permitted and often required: a semantic dimension may be
communicated by a primary signal plus text, accessible language, or inspectable
context. Overloading is prohibited: the same signal cannot mean two dimensions
in the same context.

## 3.2 Dimension registry

| Dimension | Professional question | Canonical values or range | Independent from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D-01 Identity and type** | What canonical subject is this? | Matter, Artifact, Relationship, Hypothesis, Decision, Event, and other inventory types; stable identity. | State, importance, authority, currentness. |
| **D-02 Provenance** | Where did it come from, and is the source intact? | Unattributed, attributed, verified source, source challenged; actor/source/time lineage. | Epistemic truth, authority, attention. |
| **D-03 Epistemic condition** | How is this claim currently understood? | Unexamined, proposed, supported, challenged, contradictory, confirmed within scope, rejected, unresolved. | Review, provenance, lifecycle, operational participation. |
| **D-04 Review condition** | Has the accountable person acknowledged it sufficiently? | New, reviewed. | Verification, acceptance, truth, urgency. |
| **D-05 Authority** | Who may perform which consequential act? | None, viewer, contributor, scoped delegate, owner/decision authority, system custodial power; explicit source/scope/time. | Visibility, access, prominence, ownership, activity. |
| **D-06 Ownership and responsibility** | Who is accountable or committed? | Owner, delegate, contributor, source owner, action owner, interpretation authority. | Authority scope, authorship, presence. |
| **D-07 Attention** | How does this relate to current professional Focus? | Primary Focus, supporting, inspected, peripheral, available, suspended. | Priority class, lifecycle, selection. |
| **D-08 Attention Priority** | How strong is the justified current claim on attention? | P0 Protective through P4 Dormant, with factors, time, uncertainty, and policy. | Severity, notification, lifecycle, preference. |
| **D-09 Consequence and materiality** | What happens if this is ignored or wrong? | Kind, scale, reach, reversibility, likelihood, dependency, guardrail exposure. | Urgency, epistemic confidence, activity volume. |
| **D-10 Temporal urgency** | When does consequence become unavoidable? | Time boundary, decision window, expiry, review/return condition. | Severity, recency, notification frequency. |
| **D-11 Lifecycle posture** | Where is the owning subject in its canonical lifecycle? | Model-owned states such as Reported, Under Assessment, Under Investigation, Decision Ready, Decided, In Execution, Outcome Assessed, Closed. | Navigation, attention, epistemic state, command availability. |
| **D-12 Operational participation** | Does it currently participate in work? | Active, peripheral, settled, archived; Working Set membership where applicable. | Deletion, truth, Focus, historical importance. |
| **D-13 Activity and execution** | What action or processing has occurred? | Planned, authorized, reported, running/indeterminate, completed, failed, paused, stopped—only when source-owned. | Decision, adoption, Outcome, stabilization, lifecycle. |
| **D-14 Stability and deviation** | Is observed operation within intended boundaries over time? | Baseline, within guardrails, drift, deviation, adverse effect, stabilized, recurrence. | Completion, certainty, closure, priority. |
| **D-15 Temporal validity and currentness** | Is this current, historical, superseded, stale, or unavailable? | Current, prior, superseded, corrected, stale, unavailable, simulated. | Epistemic status, lifecycle, access. |
| **D-16 Relationship condition** | What semantic connection exists and with what governance? | Type, direction, proposed/accepted/rejected, confidence, lifecycle, provenance. | Spatial proximity, grouping, sequence. |
| **D-17 System availability** | Can required data or capability be used now? | Available, loading, degraded, stale, offline/unavailable, access-denied. | Domain truth, uncertainty, authority, lifecycle. |
| **D-18 Interruption classification** | How may this event affect current Focus? | Interrupt Immediately, Queue Until Safe, Passive Awareness, Never Interrupt. | Notification delivery, priority class, UI modality. |

## 3.3 Compound state

A representation may compose dimensions only when each remains recoverable. For
example, a historical proposed Relationship from a challenged source may be:

```text
type: Relationship
relationship condition: proposed
provenance: source challenged
currentness: historical
attention: available context
```

It cannot be compressed into one generic “warning” treatment.

# 4. Visual Grammar

## 4.1 Grammar rule

Visual grammar is a set of signal types. It does not preassign a concrete style.
A future Canonical Visual System must register, for every use of a signal:

- the one semantic dimension it primarily encodes;
- its allowed values and contexts;
- its textual and accessible equivalent;
- conflicts with other signals;
- behavior under monochrome, reduced motion, zoom, and degradation;
- what the signal explicitly does not mean.

## 4.2 Signal types

| Signal | Permitted semantic work | Prohibited claim when used alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | Establish reading order, primary context, origin/destination, or stable orientation. | Truth, authority, lifecycle, causality, or priority merely from being first/top/left. |
| **Grouping** | Express shared Matter scope, purpose, Working Set participation, or explicitly named collection. | Accepted Relationship, shared ownership, or epistemic agreement. |
| **Containment** | Express canonical scope or a bounded professional context when the domain supports containment. | Copies, ownership transfer, or lifecycle nesting. |
| **Hierarchy** | Express Focus, support, periphery, available context, and deliberate depth. | Authority or certainty merely from visual dominance. |
| **Scale** | Modulate current attention gravity among already identified objects. | Severity, truth, seniority, or priority class without explicit semantics. |
| **Contrast** | Distinguish active from peripheral, current from historical, or a material exception from its field. | A complete status vocabulary or any color-only meaning. |
| **Whitespace** | Preserve cognitive boundaries, calm periphery, and separation between unlike judgments. | Absence, deletion, inactivity, or low importance. |
| **Rhythm and density** | Communicate pace, repeated structure, corpus scale, or compression/expansion of detail. | Urgency, consequence, confidence, or activity correctness. |
| **Persistence** | Keep identity, Focus, authority context, return, and unresolved material conditions recognizable. | Permanence of truth or lifecycle. |
| **Sequence** | Express attributable temporal order or a deliberate reasoning/navigation transition path. | Mandatory progression or causality without a canonical relation. |
| **Connector** | Represent an explicit Relationship, dependency, lineage, or return link with named semantics. | Decorative causality or inferred Relationship. |
| **Boundary** | Distinguish Matter, production/practice, source, authority, or historical/current scope. | Isolation of truth or denial of permitted reference. |
| **Material role** | Differentiate authored work, structural frame, tool/control, source material, and historical record when role is explicit. | Epistemic or authority status through texture alone. |
| **Label and language** | State exact type, condition, actor, time, scope, uncertainty, and action. | Hidden correction for an otherwise contradictory signal. |
| **Symbol** | Aid recognition of one registered semantic token. | State, authority, causality, or compound meaning without governed modifiers and labels. |
| **Motion** | Communicate a presentation transition, arrival, change, loading, temporal update, or preserved origin/return. | Intelligence, urgency, authority, uncertainty, success, or mandatory attention. |
| **Emphasis** | Direct attention according to current professional importance. | Creation of Attention Priority, lifecycle, acceptance, or judgment. |
| **Repetition** | Expose recurrence or a repeated comparable structure. | Escalation, urgency, materiality, or truth from volume alone. |

## 4.3 Composition syntax

Every consequential visual statement follows this order:

```text
identity and scope
→ current professional context
→ dominant object / Focus
→ semantic type and state dimensions
→ authority and provenance
→ consequence or uncertainty
→ available action and return
```

The order is semantic, not a required layout.

Composition rules:

1. Identify the object before encoding its state.
2. Establish Matter and production/historical/simulated boundary before showing
   cross-context references.
3. Separate accepted present understanding from proposals and historical
   versions.
4. Direct attention to one dominant object; supporting context cannot form an
   equal competing center.
5. Make authority inspectable at the point of consequential judgment or action.
6. Keep provenance and uncertainty attached to the claim they qualify.
7. Pair consequential exception with basis, scope, and next professional
   question.
8. Preserve the origin and return relation during movement or inspection.

# 5. Semantic Priority

## 5.1 Governing principle

Visual gravity follows current professional importance, not decoration,
component type, activity, data volume, recency, or technical convenience.

## 5.2 Priority order

The following order applies when the relevant conditions coexist:

1. **Non-waivable protective consequence** may temporarily challenge current
   Focus only under canonical interruption governance.
2. **Current Focus and dominant operational object** receive the strongest
   stable attention.
3. **Authority limitation, material contradiction, invalid source, guardrail
   breach, or irreversible uncertainty** receives enough emphasis to prevent an
   invalid act.
4. **Accepted Current Situation and its material basis** anchor orientation.
5. **Pending consequential judgment, Decision, or return condition** follows.
6. **Supporting evidence, dependencies, alternatives, and active context**
   remain clear but subordinate.
7. **Historical, peripheral, settled, and available context** remains
   retrievable and quiet.
8. **Navigation controls, Watson, metadata, administration, decoration, and
   system mechanics** recede until professionally needed.

This is not a rank formula. The governing context, authority, materiality, and
interruption laws decide the professional claim.

## 5.3 Canonical precedence statements

- Current Focus is stronger than decoration.
- Professional consequence is stronger than activity volume.
- Explicit authority is stronger than iconography or prominence.
- Accepted current meaning is stronger than proposal, without erasing proposal
  provenance.
- Material contradiction is stronger than routine completion.
- Source invalidation is stronger than visual confidence.
- Current truth is stronger than historical context; history remains reachable.
- Matter identity and return are stronger than transient navigation mechanics.
- Human judgment is stronger than Watson confidence.
- Uncertainty is stronger than a desire for aesthetic completeness.

# 6. Forbidden Ambiguity

## 6.1 General law

A visual signal has one primary professional meaning within a surface and
interaction context. If a second meaning is required, it receives a distinct
signal and explicit language. Meaning cannot depend on guessing a visual
designer's intent.

## 6.2 Prohibited conflations

1. One color or tonal treatment cannot simultaneously encode urgency, lifecycle,
   authority, epistemic confidence, and notification level.
2. One symbol cannot mean both confirmed claim and Hypothesis.
3. One checkmark cannot ambiguously mean reviewed, verified source, accepted,
   completed, correct, stabilized, or closed.
4. One warning mark cannot collapse contradiction, risk, deviation, source
   challenge, error, and uncertainty.
5. One animation cannot mean both loading and epistemic uncertainty.
6. Motion cannot manufacture urgency, authority, intelligence, or acceptance.
7. Fill cannot turn a proposal into accepted meaning.
8. Scale cannot turn seniority, activity, or popularity into professional
   importance.
9. Visual position cannot create authority, causality, sequence, or
   Relationship.
10. Grouping cannot create a Relationship, common ownership, shared truth, or
    Working Set membership.
11. A connector cannot imply causality unless an accepted causal Relationship
    exists.
12. An arrow cannot collapse navigation direction, dependency, causality,
    transfer, and lifecycle progression.
13. Absence from the active surface cannot mean deleted, rejected, resolved,
    archived, or unimportant.
14. Reduced contrast cannot mean both historical and disabled.
15. Disabled cannot mean unauthorized, unavailable by lifecycle, loading,
    source-degraded, or irrelevant without stating the actual condition.
16. Selected cannot mean accepted, active in the domain, primary Focus, or
    current truth.
17. New cannot mean urgent, unverified, important, or unread interchangeably.
18. Verified source cannot mean confirmed claim.
19. Supported cannot mean proven; confirmed within scope cannot mean universal
    fact.
20. Rejected cannot mean erased, false in every scope, or unavailable.
21. Uncertainty cannot be shown as conflict, loading, error, or low confidence
    without naming its actual kind.
22. Activity completion cannot mean Decision fulfillment, Outcome,
    stabilization, or closure.
23. Archived cannot mean historical truth is obsolete or inaccessible.
24. Closed cannot mean deleted or permanently irrelevant.
25. Reopened cannot look like a new Matter or erase prior closure context.
26. Watson origin cannot be encoded as authority or superior confidence.
27. A badge count cannot manufacture priority or interruption.
28. Repetition cannot escalate weak signals without new material consequence.
29. Decorative material cannot imply authorship, source integrity, precision, or
    operational reality.
30. A visual fallback cannot replace an unknown semantic token with a generic
    dot, sparkle, document, or warning.
31. Practice or simulated content cannot resemble production truth without an
    explicit persistent boundary and provenance.
32. Historical inspection cannot resemble current adoption or reactivated
    lifecycle.
33. Context recovery cannot imply that stale or unavailable information is
    current.
34. Relationship confidence and Relationship acceptance cannot share one
    undifferentiated signal.
35. Access, visibility, discoverability, Zone existence, command availability,
    and historical inspectability cannot collapse into “enabled.”

## 6.3 Compound representation test

A compound representation passes only when a reviewer can independently answer:

- What is the subject?
- Which Matter and time does it belong to?
- Is it current, historical, or simulated?
- What is its epistemic condition?
- What is known about provenance?
- Who has authority?
- How does it relate to Focus?
- What consequence or urgency exists?
- What action is available, and why?

If any answer depends on interpreting the same signal differently, the
representation fails.

# 7. Semantic Persistence

## 7.1 Stable meaning

The following must retain recognizable identity and semantic meaning across
navigation, interruption, reopening, recovery, restart, visual restyling, and
surface change:

- Matter identity and Matter boundary;
- canonical semantic type;
- Artifact, Relationship, Decision, Outcome, Event, and source identities;
- provenance, authorship, and source lineage;
- accepted-versus-proposed authority boundary;
- Relationship semantics and endpoints;
- Decision rationale and intended condition;
- historical/current/simulated distinction;
- delegation source and historical owner context;
- unresolved contradiction, uncertainty, residual risk, and return trigger;
- Context Envelope origin and reliable return relationship;
- rejection, correction, supersession, and append-only historical meaning.

Persistence does not require identical presentation. It requires semantic
continuity and non-reassignment of signals.

## 7.2 Meaning that must change

Representation must change when—and only when—an attributable canonical input
changes the represented dimension, including:

- review condition changes from New to Reviewed;
- source becomes attributed, verified, challenged, stale, or unavailable;
- epistemic condition gains support, is challenged, confirmed within scope,
  rejected, or becomes unresolved;
- owner accepts or rejects a proposal;
- authority or delegation validly changes;
- Focus is adopted, suspended, inspected, transferred, resumed, or abandoned;
- Attention Priority or interruption class changes after material input change;
- Working Set participation changes by valid intent;
- lifecycle changes through an authorized act;
- Decision, Operational Transition, observation, Outcome, stabilization, closure, or reopen
  condition is canonically recorded;
- a correction or supersession extends history;
- recovery validation distinguishes prior context from current truth.

The representation records why it changed and never retroactively restyles
history as though the later state always existed.

## 7.3 Restart and recovery

After restart or recovery:

1. stable identities and historical states remain unchanged;
2. currentness, authority, source availability, and freshness are revalidated;
3. indeterminate execution remains indeterminate;
4. prior Focus and intent return only when the Context Envelope remains valid;
5. changes since departure are presented as an attributable delta;
6. the new representation cannot imply success, consent, or continuity that the
   canonical sources cannot establish.

# 8. Relationship to Navigation

Visual Semantics does not define Navigation. Canonical Operational Navigation
defines professional contexts, layers, navigation transitions, Rail semantics, projected
navigation state, and recovery. Operational Navigation Governance defines
priority, interruption, provenance, authority, and degradation laws.

Navigation consumes Visual Semantics to represent:

- active, previous, historical, and suspended contexts;
- Focus, support, periphery, and available context;
- Matter boundaries and stable orientation;
- valid navigation transitions and reliable return;
- authority, degradation, priority basis, and interruption classification;
- Dynamic Operational Rail emphasis.

A visual change cannot create a navigation transition. A route or navigation
selection cannot create semantic state. Both remain projections over canonical
inputs.

| **Workbench Canonical Domain Model / Workbench Laws** | Matter, Current Situation, Artifact, Relationship, Hypothesis, Contradiction, Collection, Working Set, Memory, Proposal, Inspection, the owned Context Envelope, Focus, Event, and authority. | The inventory maps to canonical subjects and preserves orthogonal review, provenance, epistemic, participation, attention, and lifecycle dimensions. |

Visual Semantics is a representation contract only. It creates no lifecycle,
authority, priority, domain truth, Evidence, Relationship, navigation state, or
professional judgment.

# 10. Experience Validation

Each example describes required semantic difference without prescribing a
layout, component, icon, or color.

## EV-01 — Confirmed claim and assumption

A confirmed claim identifies its approved scope, supporting basis, authority,
and residual limits. An assumption identifies the unconfirmed premise and where
it is being relied upon. They differ by epistemic condition and basis, not by
decorative strength.

## EV-02 — Evidence and observation

An observation states what was perceived or measured, by whom/source and when.
Evidence additionally states how that observation supports a specific claim in
scope. Observation does not become Evidence merely through placement.

## EV-03 — Evidence and proof

Evidence exposes relevance, source, limitations, and the claim it supports.
“Proof” is not a generic visual state; confirmation remains scoped and
authorized. Stronger emphasis cannot remove uncertainty.

## EV-04 — Hypothesis and Current Situation

A Hypothesis remains a testable proposed explanation with evolving support.
Current Situation is the one owner-accepted synthesis. Both may cite the same
Artifacts, but acceptance, authority, and role remain visibly distinct.

## EV-05 — Decision and proposal

A Decision carries authorized actor, time, rationale, alternatives, accepted
uncertainty, and intended condition. A proposal carries proposer, basis, and
requested judgment. Visual prominence or fill cannot convert proposal to
Decision.

## EV-06 — Accepted and proposed Relationship

Both preserve endpoints and named semantics. The proposed Relationship exposes
its proposer and pending authority; the accepted Relationship exposes the
authorized acceptance event. Confidence is shown independently from acceptance.

## EV-07 — Uncertainty and contradiction

Uncertainty states a limit in knowledge or prediction. Contradiction states a
material incompatibility between identifiable claims or observations. A generic
warning treatment cannot substitute for either explanation.

## EV-08 — Risk and guardrail breach

Risk is an exposure with consequence and likelihood. A guardrail breach is an
observed or reported boundary violation with threshold, time, source, and
current applicability. Neither automatically determines priority or
interruption.

## EV-09 — Active and archived

Active means participating in current work. Archived means outside the active
surface but retained in Memory. Archived material preserves identity,
provenance, prior importance, and retrieval; quietness cannot mean deletion.

## EV-10 — Historical and current

Current identifies the applicable canonical object now. Historical identifies a
prior version or event with its own time and lineage. Historical inspection
cannot resemble current adoption, and current truth cannot erase its history.

## EV-11 — Primary Focus and inspected subject

Primary Focus is the owner-adopted center of work. Inspection is temporary depth
into a subject with preserved surrounding context and return. Visual depth
cannot create a second primary center.

## EV-12 — Focus and passive context

Focus receives stable gravity. Passive context remains retrievable and legible
without demanding acknowledgment. Lower prominence cannot mean low truth,
deletion, or irrelevance.

## EV-13 — Priority and severity

Severity describes magnitude. Attention Priority also depends on authority,
time, unresolved consequence, dependencies, stabilization, and intent. The
representation states the priority basis and cannot use “more severe-looking”
as a substitute.

## EV-14 — Priority and notification

Priority is a governance classification; notification is delivery. A dismissed
notification does not lower priority, and repeated delivery does not increase
it. Their signals and histories remain separate.

## EV-15 — Authority and activity

An active participant may have no consequential authority. The authority holder
is represented through explicit source, scope, time, and role; volume of
activity, location, and prominence confer nothing.

## EV-16 — Owner and delegate

The current delegate is distinguishable from historical owner context.
Delegation exposes source, scope, duration, effective time, and limitations.
Presence cannot imply delegation or permanent ownership.

## EV-17 — Action completion and Outcome

Completion is a source-owned execution condition. Outcome is an authorized
interpretation of observed operation against intent. Completion may coexist with
adverse or inconclusive Outcome and therefore cannot share its signal.

## EV-18 — Outcome and stabilization

Outcome Assessment interprets an observation interval. Stabilization judges
sustainability across boundaries, time, guardrails, and residual risk. A
favorable Outcome does not automatically appear stabilized or closed.

## EV-19 — Closed and reopened Matter

Closed quiets active-work claims while preserving identity, disposition,
residuals, history, and return trigger. Reopened restores the same identity under
a new attributable trigger and current authority; it never looks like a newly
created Matter.

## EV-20 — Loading and uncertainty

Loading states that requested system data is not yet available. Epistemic
uncertainty states that available knowledge cannot support a stronger judgment.
Loading may resolve with delivery; uncertainty requires evidence or judgment.
They cannot share motion or language as their only distinction.

## EV-21 — Source unavailable and claim rejected

Source unavailable limits current verification or retrieval. Rejected records
an authorized epistemic disposition with reason. Unavailability cannot visually
rewrite the claim as rejected, and rejection does not remove its source history.

## EV-22 — Watson proposal and human judgment

Watson contribution remains visibly attributable and proposed. Human judgment
exposes the authorized actor and disposition. “AI” novelty, animation, or
confidence cannot give Watson stronger semantic authority.

## EV-23 — Production and practice

Practice content carries persistent simulation provenance and non-production
authority across every context. Similar subject matter cannot make it resemble
production Evidence, Decision, or Outcome without a separate governed admission.

## EV-24 — Relationship and proximity

Two nearby objects remain unrelated unless an explicit canonical Relationship
exists. Grouping may express shared Matter or purpose, but no connector or
spatial arrangement may silently assert semantic connection.

# 11. Readiness for Canonical Visual System

## 11.1 Design Tokens

**Ready at semantic level.** Tokens may now be organized by one registered
dimension and value rather than by arbitrary component names. Each future token
must name its semantic purpose, allowed contexts, prohibited meanings, and
accessible equivalent.

## 11.2 Typography

**Ready for architecture.** Typography may express semantic hierarchy, reading
order, current versus supporting context, authored language, and exact labels.
It may not encode authority, truth, or lifecycle through style alone. Typeface,
scale, weight values, and metrics remain out of scope.

## 11.3 Color System

**Ready for architecture.** Color may supplement one registered semantic
dimension per context and must survive without color. No palette or color
assignment is defined here.

## 11.4 Surfaces and material

**Ready for architecture.** Surfaces may express scope, role, authorship,
structural frame, current/historical context, or inspection depth where
explicit. Material cannot decorate or manufacture truth, authority, precision,
or causality.

## 11.5 Elevation and depth

**Ready for architecture.** Depth may express inspection, temporary attention,
containment, or return relationships. It cannot mean importance, authority, or
epistemic superiority without separate semantics.

## 11.6 Dynamic Operational Rail and Sidebar

**Ready.** Navigation already owns context and movement; Visual Semantics now
defines how stable orientation, Focus, periphery, authority, degradation,
historical context, and return can remain distinguishable. Neither Rail nor
Sidebar may create navigation or lifecycle.

## 11.7 Cards and repeated semantic units

**Ready.** Repeated units may compose identity, type, state dimensions,
provenance, authority, attention role, and action. “Card” remains a possible
form, not a semantic object, and repetition cannot imply priority.

## 11.8 Workbench 2.0

**Ready at semantic architecture level.** The Workbench can represent Current
Situation, Working Set, Focus, Inspection, Artifacts, Evidence, Relationships,
Hypotheses, Contradictions, proposals, authority, history, and uncertainty
without collapsing their states.

## 11.9 Readiness gate

A future visual-system proposal receives:

- **Pass** when every signal maps to one registered semantic meaning, every
  object preserves independent dimensions, meaning survives non-color and
  reduced-motion conditions, and all examples in §10 remain distinguishable;
- **Fail** when any signal creates or conflates domain truth, lifecycle,
  authority, priority, Evidence, Relationship, navigation, or professional
  judgment;
- **Not Applicable** only when the proposal contains no representation in the
  reviewed semantic scope.
