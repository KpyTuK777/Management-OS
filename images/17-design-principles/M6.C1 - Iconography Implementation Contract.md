## Authority

This document is the canonical architecture definition of symbol implementation
obligations within the M6 Presentation Architecture. It satisfies RC-01 from
the first M6 Final Review and is a correction-stage artifact, not a new
strategic roadmap milestone.

Canonical Visual Semantics remains the sole authority for visual meaning.
Canonical Design Semantics owns Design Object roles and qualifiers. Semantic
Token Architecture owns governed symbolic design variables. All other approved
M6 models retain their existing ownership.

This contract owns only the traceable, implementation-independent obligations
by which an approved meaning may be realized as a symbol. It does not define,
select, or approve any icon, glyph, asset, icon set, visual style, platform
mechanism, or implementation.

`Canonical Iconography.md` remains Research Only — Advisory. Its observations,
proposals, inventories, construction guidance, and recommendations create no
canonical meaning or implementation obligation unless separately admitted
through M6.15 governance into an approved canonical contract.

# 1. Purpose

Symbols can make a professional meaning compactly discoverable, but their
familiarity, visual form, or platform convention can also create ambiguity,
collapse independent dimensions, or appear to grant authority. This contract
prevents those failures while allowing materially different platforms and
modalities to realize the same approved Presentation Architecture.

The contract:

- binds every semantic symbol occurrence to exact approved source identities
  and revisions;
- separates semantic meaning from symbol definition, occurrence, and platform
  realization;
- governs qualifiers, modifiers, composition, disambiguation, equivalence,
  degradation, recovery, and lifecycle;
- preserves state, status, action, navigation, provenance, authority, and
  uncertainty as independent obligations;
- requires accessible and non-symbolic equivalents wherever professional
  meaning is communicated;
- supports multiple replaceable platform realizations without promoting any
  glyph or vendor identity into architecture;
- integrates symbol mapping and qualification with M6.14 and M6.15.

A symbol communicates approved meaning. It never creates, completes, infers,
strengthens, or substitutes for that meaning.

# 2. Iconography Implementation Contract

## 2.1 Definition

An **Iconography Implementation Contract** is the versioned canonical agreement
that binds an approved semantic source to the obligations for defining and
realizing a symbol, and binds each use of that definition to an unambiguous
symbol occurrence.

The contract answers:

- which approved meaning and exact revision a symbol represents;
- which independent qualifiers and modifiers apply;
- whether the occurrence identifies a subject, status, state, action,
  navigation reference, provenance, authority, uncertainty, or another
  approved semantic obligation;
- what surrounding context is required to prevent ambiguity;
- what accessible, textual, and non-visual equivalent preserves meaning;
- what happens when the preferred realization is unsupported or unavailable;
- which mapping, adapter, evidence, and governance records establish
  conformance.

It does not answer what a symbol looks like or how a platform renders it.

## 2.2 Ownership

This contract owns:

- symbol-definition identity and revision;
- binding from a symbol definition to exact approved semantic sources;
- symbol-occurrence identity and definition reference;
- taxonomy classification and applicable cross-category obligations;
- modifier, composition, disambiguation, equivalence, degradation, and recovery
  obligations;
- definition and occurrence lifecycle;
- symbol-specific compatibility, migration, and evidence requirements;
- the boundary presented to Platform Iconography Adapters.

This contract does not own:

- professional truth, identity, lifecycle, state, authority, priority,
  provenance, uncertainty, or relationship;
- visual meaning or Visual Semantics dimensions;
- Design Object identity, semantic role, or qualifier meaning;
- Semantic Token identity, category, or resolved meaning;
- Presentation State, component, composition, layout, interaction, navigation,
  rendering, accessibility, motion, conversational, or adapter semantics;
- platform glyph identity, asset identity, rendering behavior, or input
  mechanics.

The owning upstream model remains authoritative for every source obligation.

## 2.3 Contract descriptor

Every governed symbol definition contains, conceptually:

| Field | Obligation |
| --- | --- |
| **Definition ID** | Stable identity independent of any name, glyph, asset, library, or platform. |
| **Definition revision** | Immutable revision of the complete symbol obligation. |
| **Primary semantic source** | Exact approved source identity and revision whose meaning is communicated. |
| **Canonical owner** | Upstream model retaining authority for the primary meaning. |
| **Additional source bindings** | Exact approved identities and revisions for applicable independent dimensions. |
| **Primary taxonomy category** | Exactly one IS category identifying the definition's principal obligation. |
| **Cross-category obligations** | Explicit references to every additionally applicable category. |
| **Permitted contexts** | Bounded semantic contexts in which the definition remains unambiguous. |
| **Prohibited interpretations** | Meanings that must not be inferred from the symbol. |
| **Qualifier and modifier contract** | Permitted independent dimensions, their sources, precedence, and composition constraints. |
| **Equivalent expression** | Required textual, non-visual, and alternative-modality meaning. |
| **Context requirement** | Label, structure, relationship, or other approved context required for professional interpretation. |
| **Degradation and recovery** | Safe fallback, explicit limitation, recovery condition, and continuity obligation. |
| **Mapping references** | Exact M6.14 mapping identities and revisions required for realization. |
| **Evidence references** | M6.15 evidence obligations and retained qualification references. |
| **Lifecycle state** | Draft, validated, published, deprecated, superseded, or withdrawn. |

Every symbol occurrence contains, conceptually:

| Field | Obligation |
| --- | --- |
| **Occurrence ID** | Identity of this bounded presentation occurrence. |
| **Definition reference** | Exact published definition identity and revision. |
| **Subject reference** | Exact canonical or presentation subject being represented. |
| **Source snapshot** | Exact applicable semantic source revisions and current qualified values. |
| **Applied qualifiers/modifiers** | Explicit independent bindings; none may be inferred from glyph form. |
| **Representation role** | Approved primary, secondary, reference, or inspection role where applicable. |
| **Interaction/navigation reference** | Exact canonical contract reference when the occurrence participates in action or navigation. |
| **Equivalent expression** | Resolved accessible and alternative-modality expression for this occurrence. |
| **Realization disposition** | Equivalent, explicitly degraded, unavailable-safe, recovering, superseded, or withdrawn. |

These descriptors are logical obligations, not schemas, naming rules, storage
formats, or APIs.

## 2.4 Responsibilities

The contract must:

1. resolve every semantic symbol definition to exact approved sources and
   revisions;
2. resolve every occurrence to one definition revision and one represented
   subject or bounded abstract obligation;
3. preserve all independent semantic dimensions without implicit collapse;
4. expose permitted and prohibited interpretations;
5. make action, navigation, state, status, provenance, authority, and
   uncertainty explicit when applicable;
6. require equivalent professional interpretation without the preferred
   symbol;
7. fail safely when meaning, context, capability, or realization is unresolved;
8. keep platform realization replaceable and non-authoritative;
9. preserve traceability through change, migration, degradation, correction,
   and withdrawal;
10. require independent qualification under M6.15 before a realization claims
    conformance.

The contract must not:

- invent or infer meaning from a symbol metaphor;
- permit visual familiarity to stand in for approved semantic binding;
- use one symbol occurrence to silently encode multiple independent
  dimensions;
- treat a symbol's presence, absence, emphasis, or platform availability as
  canonical state;
- allow a platform glyph, asset, library label, or vendor identity to become a
  canonical definition.

## 2.5 Definition lifecycle

```text
Identify approved semantic sources and exact revisions
    ↓
Classify primary and cross-category obligations
    ↓
Define permitted contexts and prohibited interpretations
    ↓
Define qualifiers, modifiers, composition, equivalents, and fallback
    ↓
Assign stable definition identity and revision
    ↓
Map through M6.14 and collect M6.15 evidence
    ↓
Independent qualification
    ↓
Publish atomically
    ↓
Observe, extend, deprecate, supersede, correct, or withdraw
```

A definition cannot be published when a source is unapproved or unresolved,
independent dimensions are collapsed, prohibited interpretations are absent,
equivalence or fallback is incomplete, or qualification is non-conformant.

## 2.6 Occurrence and realization lifecycle

```text
Resolve published definition revision
    ↓
Resolve represented subject and exact source snapshot
    ↓
Resolve context, qualifiers, modifiers, and representation role
    ↓
Resolve qualified adapter capability and fallback
    ↓
Produce symbol and equivalent expressions together
    ↓
Expose explicit degradation before consequential interpretation
    ↓
Replace atomically on source, context, or capability change
    ↓
Recover, supersede, or withdraw without rewriting history
```

An occurrence is invalid when its subject, definition revision, source
snapshot, required context, or equivalent expression cannot be resolved.

# 3. Canonical Symbol Taxonomy

## 3.1 Admission and classification

A semantic symbol definition is admitted only when it communicates an approved
source obligation and can do so without ambiguity. Decoration and purely
mechanical marks are outside this canonical taxonomy and cannot acquire
semantic status through repeated use.

Every definition has exactly one primary category. All other applicable
categories are explicit cross-category obligations; classification never
duplicates or transfers upstream ownership.

## 3.2 IS-01 Semantic Symbol Identity and Binding

Defines the stable identity of a symbol obligation and its exact binding to an
approved semantic source and revision.

The binding records meaning, owner, permitted context, prohibited
interpretations, required equivalent, and definition revision. A metaphor,
name, platform convention, visual similarity, or implementation reuse cannot
establish identity or binding.

## 3.3 IS-02 Qualifiers and Modifiers

Defines how an independently owned qualifier may accompany a primary symbol
meaning without becoming part of, replacing, or changing that meaning.

Each modifier has its own exact source, applicability, precedence, equivalent
expression, and removal behavior. A modifier cannot manufacture state,
authority, urgency, confidence, acceptance, relationship, or priority.

## 3.4 IS-03 Symbol Composition

Defines a bounded occurrence in which multiple approved symbol obligations are
presented together.

Composition preserves each definition identity, source, relationship, semantic
order, and equivalent expression. Proximity, overlap, containment, sequence, or
visual attachment cannot create a canonical relationship. When composition
cannot remain unambiguous, the meanings must be expressed separately.

## 3.5 IS-04 Status and State Distinction

Preserves the difference between a subject's approved status and applicable
Presentation State dimensions.

A subject symbol does not acquire status from its form. A status symbol does
not become subject identity. Pending, proposed, accepted, rejected, completed,
unavailable, stale, historical, degraded, or other conditions remain bound to
their actual owners and cannot be collapsed into a generic positive, negative,
or emphasized symbol.

## 3.6 IS-05 Action and Interaction Distinction

Preserves the difference between identifying a subject and offering or
reporting a canonical interaction.

An action-bearing occurrence references the exact approved interaction intent,
eligibility, consequence, feedback, cancellation, retry, and recovery
obligations. A symbol never grants eligibility, commits an outcome, or converts
acknowledgment into approval. Input events and controls remain adapter
mechanics.

## 3.7 IS-06 Navigation and Reference Distinction

Preserves the difference between a navigation representation, a reference to a
destination or subject, and the represented subject itself.

A navigation-bearing occurrence references the exact Navigation
Representation contract, origin, destination, continuity, interruption, and
return obligations. It does not create movement, Focus, priority, route truth,
or Context Envelope state.

## 3.8 IS-07 Provenance, Authority, and Uncertainty

Preserves provenance, authority, and uncertainty as independent semantic
dimensions.

Authorship does not imply authority. Confidence does not imply acceptance.
Repetition, familiarity, emphasis, source brand, or symbol style does not
strengthen truth. Unknown, disputed, proposed, inferred, observed, authorized,
and unauthorized conditions remain separately attributable to their approved
owners.

## 3.9 IS-08 Accessibility Equivalent

Defines the equivalent professional meaning, structure, and operation available
without perceiving or interpreting the symbol.

The equivalent identifies the represented subject, meaning, applicable
qualifiers, state/status, provenance, authority, uncertainty, interaction or
navigation consequence, and degradation where each applies. It is part of the
same occurrence contract, not optional metadata or a downstream repair.

## 3.10 IS-09 Degradation and Recovery

Defines explicit behavior when symbol realization, required context,
capability, or equivalence cannot be preserved.

Safe degradation uses an approved alternative expression, preserves
professional operation where possible, exposes the limitation, and prevents
ambiguous or consequential interpretation. Recovery re-resolves the same
definition and source snapshot or declares supersession; it never guesses from
the failed glyph.

## 3.11 IS-10 Adapter Realization

Defines the replaceable mapping from a published symbol definition and
occurrence to a qualified Platform Iconography Adapter realization.

Adapter realizations may differ mechanically while preserving the same
meaning, qualifiers, context, equivalence, degradation, and recovery. Asset,
library, glyph, platform, or vendor identity remains local and
non-canonical.

## 3.12 Collective completeness

IS-01–IS-10 cover admission, identity, binding, independent dimensions,
composition, professional distinctions, equivalent expression, failure,
recovery, and realization. A definition or occurrence spanning categories must
reference every applicable category. No category is a substitute for an
upstream semantic owner.

# 4. Deterministic Preservation Rules

## 4.1 Exact binding and resolution

Given the same approved source revisions, published definition revision,
occurrence context, subject snapshot, and qualified adapter capability,
resolution must produce the same:

- primary meaning and owner;
- applicable qualifiers and modifiers;
- permitted and prohibited interpretations;
- required context and equivalent expression;
- interaction or navigation reference;
- realization, degradation, and recovery disposition;
- evidence obligations.

An unresolved or conflicting input fails closed. No default symbol, platform
convention, or advisory recommendation may fill the gap.

## 4.2 Meaning and authority preservation

1. A symbol communicates only meaning already supplied by exact approved
   sources.
2. A symbol's visual form, emphasis, familiarity, repetition, placement, or
   availability creates no meaning.
3. A symbol creates no truth, identity, authority, priority, state, status,
   relationship, causality, confidence, provenance, eligibility, consequence,
   or history.
4. A source change cannot be represented by silently retaining an incompatible
   definition revision.
5. An occurrence cannot claim stronger certainty or authority than its source.

## 4.3 Independent-dimension preservation

Subject identity, semantic role, representation role, state, status,
interaction, navigation, provenance, authority, uncertainty, currentness,
historicity, accessibility, and degradation remain independently sourced.

A single visual distinction may not be the sole carrier of two independent
dimensions. Equal platform treatment does not merge dimensions. Different
platform treatment does not create a new dimension. A modifier is omitted
rather than guessed when its source cannot be resolved.

## 4.4 Reuse and ambiguity

Reuse is permitted only when:

- the same definition revision communicates the same approved meaning;
- the occurrence context cannot produce a prohibited interpretation;
- required qualifiers and equivalents remain explicit;
- materially different meanings use independently identifiable definitions;
- negative ambiguity evidence confirms confusing pairs remain distinguishable.

One glyph realization may support multiple definitions only when the complete
occurrence context and equivalent expression make each binding deterministic
and qualification proves no professional ambiguity. Glyph equality never
establishes semantic equality.

If reuse becomes ambiguous, the occurrence must degrade to a non-symbolic
equivalent or remain unavailable-safe until a conformant replacement is
published.

## 4.5 Textual and non-visual equivalence

The absence of a symbol must preserve:

- the same represented subject and professional meaning;
- every consequential qualifier, state, status, provenance, authority, and
  uncertainty distinction;
- the same interaction eligibility and outcome;
- the same navigation destination, continuity, and return;
- the same degradation, limitation, and recovery information.

An equivalent need not imitate visual form. It must preserve professional
interpretation and operation. Symbol-only understanding cannot be required for
consequential use.

## 4.6 Absence, unavailability, and recovery

When no conformant realization is available:

1. resolve an approved textual or alternative-modality equivalent;
2. preserve structural placement and semantic order where applicable;
3. expose any lost compactness or modality capability as degradation;
4. block only the operation whose professional meaning or safe outcome cannot
   be preserved;
5. never substitute an unknown, generic, decorative, vendor, or platform glyph;
6. retain subject, source, occurrence, and continuity references;
7. retry only against an identified definition, mapping, and capability;
8. replace the degraded occurrence atomically after successful
   requalification.

Absence is not evidence that the represented subject or condition is absent.

## 4.7 Cross-platform equivalence

Cross-platform equivalence requires equal professional interpretation and
operation, not equal appearance. Platform realizations may vary only in
mechanics. Each realization must independently preserve the same exact source
bindings, prohibited interpretations, independent dimensions, equivalents,
degradation, and recovery.

A platform or vendor identity cannot serve as the canonical symbol identity,
source revision, or evidence of equivalence.

# 5. Lifecycle and Change Governance

## 5.1 Registration and publication

Registration proposes a stable definition identity, exact source bindings,
taxonomy, contexts, prohibited interpretations, qualifiers, equivalents,
fallback, mappings, and evidence scope. Registration creates no approval.

Publication requires:

- approved and exact source revisions;
- complete IS-category applicability;
- an unambiguous M6.14 mapping;
- sufficient positive and negative M6.15 evidence;
- independent review and a conformant qualification;
- atomic publication of one complete definition revision.

## 5.2 Versioning and compatibility

Every change to binding, permitted context, prohibited interpretation,
modifier, composition, equivalent, degradation, recovery, or mapping produces
a new definition revision.

A change is compatible only when all existing valid professional
interpretations, operations, independent distinctions, and fallback obligations
remain intact. Mechanical similarity is insufficient. Compatibility is
revision-specific and evidence-backed.

## 5.3 Extension and modifiers

An extension:

- occupies a declared extension point;
- cites exact approved semantic sources;
- receives a new definition identity or compatible revision as applicable;
- preserves existing identities and prohibited interpretations;
- cannot overload a definition with a new meaning;
- remains non-canonical until M6.15 governance admits it.

A new modifier is an extension of the modifier contract, not a visual
variation. It requires an independent source, applicability, equivalent,
composition rule, negative ambiguity evidence, and requalification.

## 5.4 Deprecation, migration, and withdrawal

Deprecation preserves the definition, source bindings, evidence, and current
obligations while identifying a successor or safe withdrawal path.

Migration:

- maps every affected definition and occurrence to exact successor revisions;
- preserves subject identity, meaning, qualifiers, context, and equivalents;
- identifies incompatibilities and explicit degradation;
- prevents mixed-revision interpretation;
- completes only after dependent mappings and realizations are requalified.

Withdrawal ends current permitted reliance but preserves identity, provenance,
evidence, predecessor/successor relationships, and historical occurrences.

## 5.5 Fallback, correction, and requalification

A fallback is a governed alternative expression of the same approved meaning.
It cannot broaden meaning, invent a generic substitute, or hide degradation.

A correction creates attributable findings and a new revision or realization;
it never rewrites a published definition, occurrence history, evidence, or
verdict.

Requalification under M6.15 is required after:

- any applicable source revision changes;
- definition binding, context, modifier, composition, equivalent, mapping, or
  fallback changes;
- a realization, adapter capability, or platform dependency changes;
- negative ambiguity evidence fails;
- accessibility or cross-platform equivalence changes;
- evidence expires or contradictory evidence appears;
- a correction, migration, deprecation, extension, or recovery is introduced.

# 6. Adapter Boundary

```text
Canonical Visual / Presentation Semantics
        ↓ approved meaning and exact revisions
Iconography Implementation Contract
        ↓ symbol obligations and occurrence contract
Platform Iconography Adapter
        ↓ qualified realization mechanics
Symbol Assets / Libraries / Platform Glyph APIs
```

| Layer | Owns | Must not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Canonical Visual / Presentation Semantics** | Meaning, roles, independent dimensions, state, interaction, navigation, accessibility, motion, and other source obligations within each existing owner's boundary. | Symbol implementation mechanics. |
| **Iconography Implementation Contract** | Definition/occurrence identity, semantic binding, modifiers, composition, disambiguation, equivalence, lifecycle, degradation, recovery, mapping, and evidence obligations. | Visual meaning, concrete symbol form, or platform mechanics. |
| **Platform Iconography Adapter** | Capability matching and replaceable realization of a published occurrence contract. | Canonical meaning, source state, authority, definition approval, or conformance verdict. |
| **Symbol Assets / Libraries / Platform Glyph APIs** | Local mechanical resources used by an adapter. | Canonical identity, meaning, mapping, lifecycle, equivalence, or authority. |

The boundary is strictly downward. Lower-layer observation may produce
evidence or identify drift, but cannot define, correct, or reinterpret an upper
layer. An upstream contract constrains outcomes without selecting downstream
mechanics.

# 7. Design-to-Code Mapping and Conformance

## 7.1 M6.14 integration

Every realized definition and occurrence must be addressable through exact
M6.14 mapping revisions:

- DCM-01 preserves Design Object and semantic-role references;
- DCM-02 preserves Rendering Node and occurrence identity;
- DCM-03 preserves applicable Semantic Token identity;
- DCM-04 preserves independent Presentation State dimensions;
- DCM-05 and DCM-06 preserve component, composition, semantic order, and
  structural context;
- DCM-07 and DCM-08 preserve interaction and navigation references;
- DCM-09 preserves rendering and adapter qualification;
- DCM-10 and DCM-11 preserve accessibility and motion/no-motion equivalence;
- DCM-12 preserves conversational and multimodal projection where applicable.

An implementation-facing alias, artifact, or generated output remains
non-authoritative. Mapping coverage is complete only when every applicable
symbol obligation has an explicit disposition.

## 7.2 Required M6.15 evidence

Qualification evidence must identify:

- exact semantic source, definition, occurrence, mapping, adapter, and
  realization revisions;
- complete applicable IS-01–IS-10 and upstream obligation ledgers;
- positive evidence that meaning and operation are preserved;
- negative evidence that prohibited interpretations and confusing pairs do not
  occur;
- modifier and composition independence;
- status/state, action/subject, navigation/reference, and
  provenance/authority/uncertainty distinctions;
- textual, non-visual, accessibility, and multimodal equivalence;
- cross-platform professional equivalence;
- absence, unsupported capability, degradation, interruption, retry, recovery,
  replacement, and withdrawal behavior;
- limitations, contradictory evidence, provenance, reproduction conditions,
  validity, retention, and expiry.

Visual resemblance, convention, review preference, or asset availability is not
conformance evidence.

## 7.3 Qualification and independence

The realization producer may supply attributable evidence but cannot issue the
independent verdict for its own realization. The adapter, asset, library, tool,
or generator cannot self-certify.

A positive verdict is permitted only when every applicable obligation has
sufficient, non-conflicting, independently reviewed evidence. Otherwise the
realization is Non-conformant and remains unpublished, explicitly degraded, or
withdrawn according to the applicable safety disposition.

## 7.4 Conformance scenarios

Evidence must cover, where applicable:

- normal, historical, proposed, accepted, rejected, uncertain, unauthorized,
  stale, unavailable, interrupted, indeterminate, degraded, and recovery
  conditions;
- primary, secondary, reference, and inspection representation roles;
- symbols with and without qualifiers or modifiers;
- composed and separated expressions;
- action versus subject and navigation versus reference pairs;
- confusing meanings that could share familiar metaphors;
- symbol-present, symbol-absent, non-visual, and alternative-modality use;
- materially different platform realizations and capability sets;
- migration between compatible and incompatible revisions.

# Explicit Exclusions

This architecture does not define:

- icon assets, icon sets, inventories, or concrete glyphs;
- Unicode characters, SVG, raster imagery, pictograms, emoji, or illustrations;
- geometry, grids, keylines, silhouettes, stroke, weight, fill, corners,
  optical treatment, or drawing grammar;
- color, material, typography, spacing, size, placement, or visual style;
- animation, transition timing, or motion mechanics;
