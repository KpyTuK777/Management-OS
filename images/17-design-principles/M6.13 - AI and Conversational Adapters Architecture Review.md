# 3. Conversational Contract Review

## 3.1 Purpose and projection-only authority

The Authority statement and §1 establish that conversation represents canonical
presentation in dialogue, language, voice, chat, AI-mediated explanation, and
multimodal forms. They explicitly reject source-of-truth status and distinguish
fluency from conformance.

The definition in §3.1 is complete and implementation-independent. It describes
observable preservation obligations and explicitly excludes transcripts,
sessions, inference results, domain objects, authority grants, and professional
history.

**Result: Pass.**

## 3.2 Ownership

Section 3.2 provides an exhaustive ownership table for professional truth, AI
authority, Design Objects, semantics, rendering, tokens, Presentation State,
components, composition, interaction, navigation, adapter aggregation,
accessibility, and motion.

M6.13 owns only the conversational descriptor, CA taxonomy, projection/turn
lifecycles, preservation resolution, dialogue-local continuity, degradation,
correction, recovery, boundary, and presentation conformance.

The AI / Conversational Adapter is limited to meaning-preserving projection.
Technical systems own mechanics only.

**Result: Pass.**

## 3.3 Descriptor completeness

The descriptor contains stable identity, immutable revision, exact sources,
actor and authority, projection scope, CA applicability, dialogue context,
semantic order, provenance, interaction, navigation, accessibility,
multimodality, correction, degradation, recovery, evidence, and limitations.

The fields are sufficient to determine one bounded projection without reaching
behind source contracts or inferring platform capability.

**Result: Pass.**

## 3.4 Responsibilities and prohibitions

The nine responsibilities require exact source identification, complete CA
coverage, ownership preservation, state separation, interaction/navigation
equivalence, accessibility, ambiguity handling, bounded generated expression,
and evidence.

The prohibitions exclude promotion of generated output to truth, creation of
authority/state/consequence, silent omission, acknowledgment-as-approval,
platform outcome redefinition, and implementation prescription.

**Result: Pass.**

## 3.5 Lifecycles

The immutable definition lifecycle is conventional and bounded. The projection
lifecycle permits only validated complete sets to become Current. The turn
lifecycle distinguishes input, bounded intent, resolved sources, preparation,
validation, presentation, acknowledgment, correction, supersession, and
deferral.

The explicit rule that turn progression cannot substitute for CIP progression
prevents lifecycle overlap. Neither lifecycle creates domain, navigation,
authority, interaction, or Memory consequences.

**Result: Pass.**

# 5. Preservation Review

## 5.1 Professional meaning

Section 6.1 requires exact traceability or explicit non-canonical
interpretation. It preserves epistemic classes, wording, qualification,
uncertainty, provenance, scope, independent dimensions, conflict, current versus
historical state, correction, and professional tone.

Generated connective language cannot introduce causality, relationship,
priority, certainty, or authority. This closes the principal AI-mediated
semantic risk.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.2 Authority

Section 6.2 denies inherited adapter authority and denies authority from
dialogue, confidence, initiative, repetition, urgency, placement, modality,
recommendation, or platform identity. Actor and source authority are revalidated
for every consequential interaction, with ambiguity failing closed.

This is consistent with Watson preparation authority and owner/domain approval
boundaries.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.3 Interaction equivalence

Section 6.3 binds every operation to one OI/CIP occurrence, requires bounded
intent and clarification for material ambiguity, preserves all guards, separates
prepared from applied content, names exact consequence at commitment, and
preserves cancellation, retry, modality transition, failure, and append-only
correction.

Acknowledgment is independently prohibited as approval in §§3.7, 4.5, 6.2 and
invariants 28–30.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.4 Navigation Representation

Section 6.4 expressly separates topic from context and mention/inspection from
adoption. It preserves current context, destination distinction, origin, return,
interruption class, suspended context, history boundaries, NT/NAV contracts,
invalid-return recovery, cross-Matter isolation, and non-automatic recovery.

CA-03 covers all NR-01–NR-08 responsibilities without owning any navigation
truth.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.5 Dialogue continuity

Sections 4.3, 5.2, and 6.5 make dialogue continuity bounded, revision-specific,
reference-based, attributable, invalidatable, reconstructable, and disposable.
They explicitly prohibit replacement of Current Situation, Context Envelope,
Operational Memory, Current Focus, canonical history, authority, durable
preference, and cross-Matter boundaries.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.6 Accessibility and multimodality

Sections 6.6 and 6.7 consume the exact Accessibility Contract, preserve
discoverability, perceivability, operability, structural navigation, safeguards,
modality alternatives, identity, state, focus distinctions, interaction
commitment, cross-modal references, staleness, replacement, and return.

No modality receives semantic or authority precedence.

**Result: Pass.**

## 5.7 Professional intent

Section 6.8 preserves calm, bounded, evidence-led, least-intrusive,
non-manipulative communication. It admits silence and deferral, prohibits
attention capture and pressure, limits clarification to material ambiguity, and
keeps recommendations and context classes explicit.

**Result: Pass.**

# 6. Determinism Review

The input contract in §7.1 contains all variables allowed to affect resolution:
canonical revisions, contract, actor/authority, input, dialogue context, current
M6 projections, Accessibility/Motion contracts, declared capabilities,
modality limitations, prior projection, and fallback order.

The pipeline in §7.2 resolves these inputs in a total order. Sections 7.3 and
7.4 provide mutually distinguishable intent and projection outcomes.

The same exact inputs must produce the same coverage, authority disposition,
semantic order, interaction/navigation bindings, ambiguity branch, fallback,
degradation, and verdict. Surface-language variation is permitted only inside an
unchanged validated semantic outcome.

This is deterministic at the architecture-obligation level without prescribing
implementation output.

**Result: Pass.**

# 7. Degradation, Correction, and Recovery Review

## 7.1 Degradation

The ordered fallback begins with equivalent current-modality expression, then
another modality, source-grounded serialization, source-authorized
summary/deferral, explicit essential-preserving degradation, and finally
Incompatible.

Fluency, engagement, speed, provider preference, and convenience cannot reorder
fallback. Every degradation record exposes the exact source, category, reason,
risk, preserved/unpreserved properties, blocked consequence, recovery, and
evidence.

Generated approximation is rejected unless explicitly source-authorized,
bounded, and labeled.

**Result: Pass.**

## 7.2 Correction

Correction makes the bad projection non-current, retains attribution, identifies
the failed basis, reloads current sources, separates projection error from
committed consequence, and invokes canonical correction only through its owner.
Replacement is atomic.

**Result: Pass.**

## 7.3 Recovery

Recovery validates actor/authority and current revisions, reconstructs from
canonical references rather than generated recall, reconciles consequences and
open interactions, restores Navigation Representation, recomputes every CA and
accessibility obligation, exposes unresolved loss, and atomically supersedes
obsolete dialogue state.

Recovery creates no canonical event merely because presentation is restored.

**Result: Pass.**

# 8. Adapter Boundary Review

## 8.1 Layer separation

| Layer | Permitted ownership | Prohibited ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical Presentation Architecture | Source meaning, identity, state, structure, interaction, navigation, accessibility, motion, degradation, recovery, and complete Conversational Contract. | Provider/model/runtime selection or technical mechanics. |
| AI / Conversational Adapter | Bounded intent, source/authority mapping, CA resolution, validation, ambiguity, degradation, correction, recovery, and conformance evidence. | New meaning, authority, consequence, Navigation State, fallback order, or conformance criteria. |
| LLMs, Voice Systems, Chat Interfaces | Technical generation, recognition, transformation, presentation, and delivery. | Canonical truth, identity, authority, state, relationship, consequence, navigation, history, accessibility disposition, degradation, or conformance. |

Technical output remains candidate presentation until adapter validation.
Provider confidence and completion have no canonical force.

## 8.2 Boundary-failure coverage

Section 9.5 explicitly fails generated truth, dialogue-as-domain-state,
confidence-as-authority, acknowledgment-as-commitment, topic-as-navigation,
invented source/relationship/priority/interruption, misleading summary,
contradictory modalities, hidden capability gaps, implementation leakage, and
output-based conformance.

**Result: Pass.**
