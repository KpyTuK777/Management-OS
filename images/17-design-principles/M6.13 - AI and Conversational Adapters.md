# M6.13 — AI and Conversational Adapters

## Status

```text
Approved for M6 dependency use
Milestone M6 — Active
```

## Authority

This document is the canonical architecture definition of conversational and
AI-mediated representation within the M6 Presentation Architecture. It owns the
Conversational Contract, conversational adapter taxonomy, deterministic
projection and preservation rules, dialogue continuity, conversational
degradation and recovery, the AI / Conversational Adapter boundary, and
presentation conformance for conversational environments.

It consumes all approved M6.1–M6.12 architecture documents without modifying
their meaning, objects, ownership, lifecycle, authority, state, composition,
interaction, navigation, rendering, accessibility, motion, or adapter contracts.

Conversational interfaces project canonical meaning. They never replace,
reinterpret, validate, or become the source of that meaning.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Presentation Architecture must remain coherent when professional meaning is
represented through dialogue, language, voice, chat, AI-mediated explanation,
or a combination of conversational and non-conversational modalities.

The model:

- defines a complete canonical handoff for conversational representation;
- preserves presentation meaning across turns and modality changes;
- separates dialogue continuity from professional context and history;
- preserves canonical interaction and navigation contracts;
- constrains AI-generated expression to attributable, non-authoritative
  projection;
- protects accessibility and multimodal equivalence;
- makes ambiguity, limitation, degradation, and recovery explicit;
- permits implementation freedom without semantic or authority freedom.

A fluent response is not necessarily a conformant response. Conformance depends
on preservation of meaning, provenance, authority, state, interaction,
navigation, accessibility, and professional intent.

---

# 2. Conversational Principles

1. **Projection, Never Replacement.** Conversation expresses canonical
   presentation and cannot become a competing source of truth.
2. **Truth Before Fluency.** A limitation, conflict, uncertainty, or missing
   source remains explicit even when a smoother answer is possible.
3. **No Conversational Authority.** Confidence, eloquence, repetition,
   initiative, personalization, or conversational position grants no authority.
4. **Source Attribution.** Facts, interpretations, recommendations,
   uncertainties, and unavailable information remain distinguishable and
   traceable.
5. **Dialogue Is Presentation State.** Dialogue continuity supports
   representation but does not become domain state, Current Situation,
   Operational Memory, Current Focus, or canonical navigation.
6. **Intent Is Confirmed, Not Invented.** The adapter may resolve unambiguous
   presentation intent but must expose material ambiguity before consequence.
7. **Interaction Contracts Survive.** Conversation may invoke canonical
   interactions only with identical eligibility, authority, validation,
   commitment, outcome, and recovery.
8. **Navigation Contracts Survive.** Conversational movement preserves current
   context, destination, origin, return, interruption, suspension, and history
   distinctions.
9. **One Canonical Consequence.** Multiple conversational turns or modalities
   cannot duplicate or imply a canonical outcome.
10. **Multimodal Coherence.** Simultaneous or sequential modalities express one
    compatible canonical presentation revision.
11. **Accessible Equivalence.** Meaning and operation remain equivalent across
    supported conversational modalities.
12. **Professional Restraint.** Conversation remains optional, bounded,
    evidence-led, correctable, and subordinate to owner judgment.
13. **Deterministic Preservation.** Identical canonical inputs, contract
    revisions, declared capabilities, and resolved intent produce the same
    obligations and disposition.
14. **Explicit Degradation.** Missing context, capability, provenance,
    modality, or continuity is never silently approximated.
15. **Fail Closed.** A consequential act with unresolved meaning, authority, or
    validation cannot proceed.
16. **Implementation Neutrality.** Canonical contracts favor no model, provider,
    speech system, chat surface, protocol, or runtime.

# 3. Conversational Contract

## 3.1 Definition

A **Conversational Contract** is a versioned, implementation-independent
statement of the canonical sources, conversational scope, projection
obligations, dialogue continuity, navigation representation, interaction
equivalence, semantic preservation, accessibility, multimodal coordination,
degradation, recovery, and conformance evidence required for a conversational
presentation.

It defines what a conversational environment must preserve. It does not define
how language or other conversational output is generated.

A Conversational Contract is not a prompt, conversation transcript, model
context, message format, inference result, speech representation, chat session,
domain object, authority grant, or persistent professional history.

## 3.2 Ownership

This model owns:

- the Conversational Contract descriptor and definition lifecycle;
- CA-01–CA-08 adapter-contract categories;
- conversational projection-set and turn lifecycles;
- deterministic preservation and resolution rules;
- dialogue-local continuity and its strict boundary;
- conversational degradation, correction, and recovery;
- the three-layer AI / Conversational Adapter boundary;
- conversational presentation conformance evidence and verdicts.

Existing owners remain authoritative:

| Concern | Canonical owner |
| --- | --- |
| Professional and domain truth | Canonical Management Model and owning domain models |
| Watson capability, responsibility, and authority | AI Assistant Architecture and approved AI product policies |
| Design Objects and relationships | Canonical Design Object Model |
| Semantic Roles and semantic dimensions | Canonical Design Semantics |
| Rendering Nodes, tree identity, projection, and replacement | Canonical Rendering Model |
| Symbolic presentation meaning | Semantic Token Architecture |
| Presentation State | Presentation State Model |
| Component responsibility, composition, regions, and layout intent | Canonical Component Model and Composition and Layout System |
| Professional interaction intent, progression, commitment, and consequence | Canonical Operational Interaction Language and Canonical Interaction Patterns |
| Navigation truth, governance, and representation | Canonical Operational Navigation, Navigation Governance, and Navigation Representation Model |
| Aggregate platform handoff and qualification | Rendering Contracts and Platform Adapters |
| Accessibility and modality equivalence | Accessibility Model |
| Motion communication | Motion Semantics |

The AI / Conversational Adapter owns only meaning-preserving projection into a
declared conversational capability. LLMs, Voice Systems, and Chat Interfaces own
only technical generation, recognition, delivery, and interaction mechanics.

## 3.3 Contract descriptor

Every Conversational Contract contains:

| Field | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Contract ID** | Stable conversational-contract identity. |
| **Revision** | Immutable contract revision. |
| **Canonical sources** | Exact source contracts, occurrences, and revisions. |
| **Actor and authority scope** | Current actor, attributable authority sources, and limits. |
| **Projection scope** | Exact Design Objects, Rendering Nodes, Components, regions, state, interactions, and navigation representations in scope. |
| **Applicable categories** | Required CA-01–CA-08 obligations. |
| **Dialogue context** | Bounded presentation-local continuity needed for the current exchange. |
| **Semantic order** | Source-derived order for sequential expression. |
| **Provenance obligations** | Required distinction among source fact, interpretation, proposal, uncertainty, and generated connective expression. |
| **Interaction obligations** | Applicable intent, preconditions, validation, commitment, feedback, cancellation, retry, and recovery. |
| **Navigation obligations** | Current context, destination, origin, return, interruption, suspension, history, and recovery representations. |
| **Accessibility obligations** | Exact Accessibility Contract and conversational equivalents. |
| **Multimodal obligations** | Identity, revision, state, order, and focus coordination across modalities. |
| **Correction policy** | How detected misprojection is withdrawn and replaced without rewriting canonical history. |
| **Degradation policy** | Ordered source-authorized fallback and prohibited approximation. |
| **Recovery policy** | Deterministic restoration from current canonical sources. |
| **Evidence obligations** | Proof required for a conformance verdict. |
| **Known limitations** | Explicit capability and continuity boundaries. |

An incomplete descriptor is not a valid Conversational Contract.

## 3.4 Responsibilities

The Conversational Contract must:

1. identify exact canonical sources before projection;
2. enumerate every applicable CA obligation;
3. preserve source identity, meaning, state, order, relationship, provenance,
   authority, and consequence;
4. distinguish conversational continuity from professional truth;
5. preserve canonical interaction and navigation contracts;
6. provide equivalent accessible and multimodal representation;
7. define material-ambiguity, correction, degradation, and recovery behavior;
8. bound every proposal, interpretation, summary, and generated expression;
9. provide deterministic, implementation-independent evidence.

The contract must not:

- promote dialogue text, voice output, generated structure, or inferred intent
  to canonical truth;
- create authority, professional priority, interruption class, state,
  relationship, navigation eligibility, or domain consequence;
- silently omit material source conflict, uncertainty, limitation, or
  provenance;
- treat conversational acknowledgment as approval or commitment;
- allow provider or platform behavior to redefine canonical outcomes;
- prescribe generation, recognition, transport, storage, or runtime mechanics.

## 3.5 Definition lifecycle

Conversational Contract definitions follow:

```text
Proposed → Registered → Active → Deprecated → Retired
```

- **Proposed** may be reviewed but cannot qualify a conversational adapter.
- **Registered** has a stable identity, complete descriptor, and source
  traceability.
- **Active** is mandatory for new conversational projections.
- **Deprecated** remains interpretable only for a declared transition period.
- **Retired** is historical and cannot govern a current projection.

Contract revisions are immutable. Changed sources, obligations, authority,
continuity, modality, fallback, correction, or evidence create a new revision.

## 3.6 Conversational projection lifecycle

```text
Required
    ↓
Derived
    ↓
Validated
    ↓
Current
    ↓ source or contract revision changes
Obsolete

Derivation or validation failure
    ↓
Invalid — Never Current
```

- **Required** identifies a valid need for conversational representation.
- **Derived** contains candidate CA mappings for exact sources.
- **Validated** satisfies complete preservation and evidence obligations.
- **Current** represents one exact compatible canonical revision set.
- **Obsolete** cannot represent current professional meaning.
- **Invalid** never becomes available as a conformant projection.

Projection lifecycle creates no domain event, interaction consequence,
navigation transition, authority change, or Operational Memory entry.

## 3.7 Conversational turn lifecycle

```text
Input Presented
    ↓
Intent Bounded
    ↓
Sources Resolved
    ↓
Response Prepared
    ↓
Response Validated
    ↓
Presented
    ↓
Acknowledged | Corrected | Superseded | Deferred
```

- **Input Presented** is conversational input, not accepted professional truth.
- **Intent Bounded** distinguishes known intent, safe assumption, material
  ambiguity, and unavailable interpretation.
- **Sources Resolved** binds exact current canonical sources and authority.
- **Response Prepared** is non-canonical until validated and presented.
- **Response Validated** satisfies the active contract.
- **Presented** communicates a projection and creates no consequence by itself.
- **Acknowledged** means receipt only, never approval unless the owning
  interaction contract explicitly validates approval.
- **Corrected** replaces the conversational projection and preserves
  attributable correction.
- **Superseded** cannot continue to represent current context.
- **Deferred** exposes the limitation and safe next step.

Turn progression cannot substitute for a Canonical Interaction Pattern
lifecycle.

# 4. Adapter Taxonomy

## 4.1 Admission and exclusivity

A category is canonical only when it owns a distinct conversational
preservation responsibility, remains independent of implementation mechanics,
produces observable obligations, and cannot be fully represented by another
category.

Every obligation has one primary CA owner. Cross-category references do not
transfer or duplicate ownership.

## 4.2 CA-01 Conversational Projection Contract

Defines how a complete canonical presentation scope is expressed through
bounded conversational turns.

It requires:

- exact projection identity and canonical revision set;
- source-owned identity, role, state, relationships, qualification, provenance,
  and available action;
- explicit selection of conversational scope;
- source-derived semantic order for sequential expression;
- distinction among complete expression, summary, inspection, explanation, and
  proposal;
- reliable access to material detail omitted from a bounded turn;
- stable correction and replacement.

Conversational projection may sequence simultaneous presentation and summarize
only when the complete meaning remains discoverable. It cannot make the latest
utterance the sole source of professional truth.

## 4.3 CA-02 Dialogue Continuity Contract

Defines bounded presentation continuity across conversational turns.

It preserves:

- current projection and contract revisions;
- active conversational scope and resolved intent;
- referenced canonical identities;
- open clarification, inspection, and uncommitted interaction occurrences;
- prior material assumptions, limitations, corrections, and unresolved
  ambiguity;
- relationship to current professional context and return.

Dialogue continuity is not Current Situation, Current Focus, Context Envelope,
Operational Memory, domain history, durable preference, or authority. It may be
discarded and reconstructed from current canonical sources without loss of
professional truth.

## 4.4 CA-03 Conversational Navigation Contract

Defines conversational representation of the approved Navigation
Representation Model.

It preserves:

- the one current professional context;
- available destinations distinct from adopted context;
- origin and qualified return;
- active interruption and its exclusive class;
- suspended contexts and unfinished intent;
- contextual navigation and authorized history inspection;
- source-owned adoption, return, interruption, and recovery interactions.

Changing conversational topic, asking about another object, following a
reference, or producing an answer does not itself navigate. Conversational
navigation cannot create a destination, Context Envelope, Navigation State, or
history entry.

## 4.5 CA-04 Interaction Equivalence Contract

Defines how canonical professional interactions are represented and invoked
through conversation.

It preserves:

- exact OI and CIP identity and participant roles;
- actor intent and material ambiguity threshold;
- eligibility, authority, freshness, and validation;
- information available before commitment;
- explicit commitment boundary and exact consequence;
- confirmation, cancellation, retry, interruption, correction, and recovery;
- feedback distinguishing prepared, proposed, committed, failed, and deferred.

Natural-language agreement, politeness, conversational acknowledgment, repeated
request, or inferred preference is not approval. A consequential interaction
proceeds only through the owning contract with bounded, attributable approval.

## 4.6 CA-05 Semantic Preservation Contract

Defines preservation of canonical meaning in generated, selected, summarized,
or transformed conversational expression.

It preserves:

- Design Object and Rendering Node identity;
- Semantic Role and every independent semantic dimension;
- current versus historical, fact versus interpretation, observation versus
  recommendation, and proposal versus consequence;
- provenance, uncertainty, authority, scope, and known limitation;
- relationships, hierarchy, order, boundaries, and professional emphasis;
- correction without silent historical rewrite.

Plausibility, fluency, confidence, conversational coherence, or repetition
cannot fill a missing canonical source. Unsupported content is explicitly
generated interpretation or is excluded from canonical projection.

## 4.7 CA-06 Accessibility Equivalence Contract

Defines application of the Accessibility Model to conversational presentation.

It preserves:

- semantic discoverability and perceivability;
- operation without dependence on one sensory channel or conversational mode;
- structural navigation through sequential and retrievable expression;
- interaction and presentation equivalence;
- explicit speaker, source, state, context, and change distinctions;
- interruption, correction, degradation, and recovery;
- alternatives when speech, text, or another modality is unavailable.

Conversational naturalness cannot override clarity, source distinction,
operability, or reliable retrieval.

## 4.8 CA-07 Multimodal Representation Contract

Defines one coherent presentation across conversation, voice, text, visual,
spatial, static, or other supported modalities.

It preserves:

- one compatible canonical source revision set;
- shared occurrence identity and state;
- semantic order and cross-modal references;
- active interaction occurrence and commitment status;
- current Navigation Representation and presentation focus;
- modality-specific scope without contradictory completeness claims;
- atomic replacement and recovery when one modality becomes stale.

No modality is automatically primary or canonical. A modality may specialize
expression, but it cannot introduce meaning, operation, authority, or state
absent from the shared contract.

## 4.9 CA-08 Degradation and Recovery Contract

Defines the disposition of missing context, ambiguous intent, unsupported
capability, unavailable modality, stale source, broken continuity, conflicting
projection, or failed conversational expression.

It requires:

- exact affected source, occurrence, category, and obligation;
- reason and attributable capability boundary;
- preserved and unpreserved properties;
- ordered source-authorized fallback;
- explicit limitation and blocked consequence;
- correction or recovery target;
- deterministic disposition and evidence.

Degradation cannot be hidden by confident language, fabricated context,
unattributed approximation, or conversational redirection.

# 5. Projection and Dialogue Model

## 5.1 Conversational Projection Set

A **Conversational Projection Set** is the atomic, versioned aggregate of all
applicable CA-01–CA-08 obligations for one actor, one bounded conversational
scope, and one compatible canonical source revision set.

```text
Conversational Projection Set
  ├─ canonical source and occurrence references
  ├─ actor and authority scope
  ├─ dialogue-local continuity
  ├─ semantic order and projection coverage
  ├─ interaction and navigation bindings
  ├─ accessibility and multimodal bindings
  ├─ provenance, uncertainty, and limitations
  ├─ degradation and recovery disposition
  └─ conformance evidence
```

The set is not a domain object, transcript, professional record, Current
Situation, Context Envelope, model context, or canonical history.

## 5.2 Dialogue context boundary

Dialogue context may retain only what is necessary to preserve the active
presentation exchange:

- exact canonical references rather than copied authority;
- the current bounded question or presentation intent;
- explicit assumptions and unresolved ambiguity;
- open non-committed interaction occurrence;
- source-owned navigation context and return reference;
- the latest validated projection revision;
- corrections, limitations, and recovery state.

Dialogue context cannot:

- become the authoritative copy of canonical content;
- survive source invalidation as current truth;
- infer durable owner preference from conversational behavior;
- merge contexts from separate Matters or actors;
- elevate frequently mentioned content to priority;
- retain attention for conversational convenience;
- replace Operational Memory or canonical audit history.

## 5.3 Sequentialization

When conversation serializes a presentation that may otherwise be simultaneous:

1. semantic order derives from the Rendering Tree, Composition Plan, Structural
   Regions, Layout Intent, Presentation State, and source-owned professional
   priority;
2. material context required to interpret a later element is introduced before
   it;
3. independent dimensions remain independently expressed;
4. summary never impersonates complete inspection;
5. omitted detail remains reliably retrievable;
6. related actions remain attached to their exact object and consequence;
7. navigation and return remain explicit;
8. no conversational ordering creates authority or priority.

## 5.4 Conversational acts

The adapter may represent only source-bounded conversational acts:

| Act | Presentation responsibility | Prohibited implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | Express source-owned content and state. | Does not create or endorse truth. |
| **Summarize** | Compress while preserving material distinctions and access to complete sources. | Does not replace complete evidence or history. |
| **Explain** | Make source relationships, basis, qualification, or consequence understandable. | Does not add unsupported rationale. |
| **Clarify** | Resolve one material ambiguity before projection or action. | Does not transfer organizational work or presume intent. |
| **Inspect** | Expose authorized detail and provenance. | Does not adopt a navigation context automatically. |
| **Compare** | Present source-defined differences on explicit dimensions. | Does not manufacture ranking or recommendation. |
| **Propose** | Express an attributable, bounded, non-authoritative option. | Does not approve or apply it. |
| **Confirm** | Invoke an owning confirmation interaction with exact consequence. | Does not treat casual agreement as commitment. |
| **Correct** | Withdraw and replace a misprojection while preserving attribution. | Does not rewrite canonical history. |
| **Defer** | State why a conformant result is unavailable and the safe next step. | Does not simulate capability or completion. |

These are projection responsibilities, not a new professional interaction
language.

# 6. Preservation Rules

## 6.1 Professional meaning preservation

1. Every conversational claim in canonical scope traces to an exact current
   source or is explicitly identified as non-canonical interpretation.
2. Facts, observations, interpretations, hypotheses, recommendations,
   decisions, and outcomes remain distinct.
3. Source wording, qualification, uncertainty, provenance, and scope survive
   summarization.
4. Independent semantic dimensions cannot be collapsed into conversational
   sentiment or a single confidence expression.
5. Generated connective language cannot introduce causality, relationship,
   priority, certainty, or authority.
6. Missing or conflicting source meaning is stated rather than completed
   conversationally.
7. Current and historical presentation remain distinct.
8. Correction replaces only the projection; it does not rewrite owning source
   history.
9. Professional tone cannot conceal limitation or make a proposal appear
   settled.
10. Equivalent canonical inputs produce equivalent semantic obligations even
    when surface phrasing differs.

## 6.2 Authority preservation

1. The adapter inherits no authority from access to canonical sources.
2. Dialogue never grants the AI, voice system, chat interface, or adapter
   decision authority.
3. Confidence changes neither action eligibility nor approval requirements.
4. Conversational initiative cannot create interruption or priority.
5. Repetition, urgency language, placement, or modality cannot strengthen an
   authority claim.
6. The actor and authority source are resolved for each consequential
   interaction.
7. Ambiguous identity, delegation, or approval fails closed.
8. A generated recommendation remains attributable, optional, rejectable, and
   non-authoritative.
9. Conversational acknowledgment is not approval.
10. Platform identity or account state cannot substitute for canonical
    authority validation.

## 6.3 Interaction preservation

1. Every conversational operation binds to one exact OI and CIP occurrence.
2. Intent must be sufficiently bounded for the exact target and consequence.
3. Material ambiguity triggers clarification or deferral, never guessed action.
4. Preconditions, freshness, concurrency, validation, and authority remain
   unchanged.
5. Prepared content remains distinct from applied consequence.
6. The commitment request identifies action, target, destination, content, and
   consequence.
7. Commitment occurs only through the owning canonical boundary.
8. Cancellation before commitment produces no canonical consequence.
9. Retry cannot duplicate a consequence.
10. Modality change cannot create a second interaction occurrence.
11. Failure, partial result, deferral, and completion remain distinguishable.
12. Conversational correction after commitment follows canonical correction or
    reversal and never rewrites history.

## 6.4 Navigation preservation

1. Conversational topic is distinct from professional context.
2. Mention, inspection, reference-following, and answer generation do not adopt
   a destination.
3. The current Navigation Representation remains explicit where needed for
   orientation or consequence.
4. Available destination remains distinct from current context.
5. Origin and return are preserved across conversational inspection.
6. Interruption class remains source-owned and exclusive.
7. Dialogue cannot suspend, abandon, or restore professional context by itself.
8. Conversational history is not navigation history or Operational Memory.
9. Topic change cannot bypass an NT or NAV contract.
10. Invalid return produces an explicit qualified recovery target.
11. Cross-Matter dialogue never merges Focus, Working Set, authority, context,
    or history.
12. Recovery never adopts a context automatically.

## 6.5 Dialogue continuity preservation

1. Each current projection set binds to one exact compatible source revision
   set.
2. Dialogue context retains references, not replacement truth.
3. Material assumptions remain explicit until resolved or superseded.
4. Pronoun, shorthand, or implicit reference resolution cannot cross an
   ambiguous identity boundary.
5. Open interaction state remains attributable and uncommitted until canonical
   commitment.
6. Source changes invalidate affected conversational projections.
7. Obsolete turns cannot continue to govern current action.
8. Correction is visible within the active exchange.
9. Lost dialogue context triggers reconstruction from canonical sources, not
   invention.
10. Reliable professional work can continue outside the conversation.

## 6.6 Accessibility preservation

1. Every projection consumes the applicable Accessibility Contract.
2. Speaker, source, object, state, context, action, and consequence remain
   discoverable.
3. Sequential conversation preserves structural navigation and reliable
   retrieval.
4. Speech, text, and other supported modalities offer equivalent meaning and
   operation.
5. No essential meaning depends on tone, voice character, conversational pace,
   or transient delivery.
6. Clarification, correction, interruption, and recovery remain operable in
   every supported modality.
7. Conversational brevity cannot remove material context or safeguards.
8. Modality loss resolves to an equivalent supported presentation or explicit
   degradation.
9. Accessibility preference or modality creates no professional state.
10. Failure of accessibility equivalence makes the affected projection
    non-conformant.

## 6.7 Multimodal preservation

1. All active modalities consume one compatible canonical revision set.
2. The same canonical occurrence retains identity across modalities.
3. State, selection, presentation focus, Current Focus, and navigation context
   remain distinct and synchronized by source ownership.
4. One modality cannot commit an operation that another reports as merely
   prepared.
5. A modality-specific summary cannot make the full presentation appear
   complete.
6. Cross-modal references identify exact occurrences and context.
7. Stale modality output becomes explicitly obsolete.
8. Replacement of a multimodal projection set is atomic.
9. Modality transition preserves open interaction and valid return.
10. No modality has implicit semantic or authority precedence.

## 6.8 Professional-intent preservation

1. Conversation remains calm, precise, bounded, evidence-led, and
   non-manipulative.
2. The adapter uses the least intrusive expression consistent with canonical
   obligations.
3. Silence or deferral is valid when intervention lacks source basis or value.
4. Conversation cannot capture attention for continuity or engagement.
5. Questions are limited to material ambiguity that cannot be resolved safely
   from authorized context.
6. The adapter cannot reward agreement, pressure response, or imply deficiency
   for rejection.
7. Recommendations distinguish evidence, interpretation, confidence,
   limitation, and proposed action.
8. Production, learning, simulation, history, and hypothetical content remain
   unmistakably distinct.

# 7. Deterministic Resolution

## 7.1 Input contract

Resolution accepts:

- exact canonical presentation inputs and revisions;
- active Conversational Contract revision;
- actor identity, authority, and access;
- bounded presented input and current dialogue-local context;
- current Rendering Tree, Semantic Tokens, Presentation State, Components,
  Composition Plan, Interaction Patterns, and Navigation Representation;
- applicable Accessibility and Motion Contracts;
- declared abstract conversational, language, voice, chat, and multimodal
  capabilities;
- current modality availability and limitations;
- prior valid projection set where continuity applies;
- source-authorized fallback order.

## 7.2 Resolution pipeline

```text
Validate Actor, Contract, and Canonical Revisions
        ↓
Bound Conversational Scope and Intent
        ↓
Resolve CA-01 Projection and CA-02 Continuity
        ↓
Resolve CA-03 Navigation and CA-04 Interaction
        ↓
Validate CA-05 Semantic Preservation
        ↓
Resolve CA-06 Accessibility and CA-07 Multimodality
        ↓
Apply CA-08 Degradation or Recovery
        ↓
Validate and Publish Complete Projection Set
```

Identical exact inputs produce the same source coverage, applicable
obligations, authority disposition, semantic order, interaction and navigation
bindings, ambiguity branch, fallback, degradation, and conformance outcome.

Surface phrasing may vary only within the same validated semantic obligations.
Variation cannot change facts, distinctions, omissions, authority, interaction,
navigation, accessibility, or outcome.

## 7.3 Intent disposition

- **Resolved** — one interpretation is fully supported and changes no material
  target, meaning, authority, or consequence.
- **Resolved with Disclosed Assumption** — a non-material ambiguity permits one
  bounded assumption that is exposed and easily corrected.
- **Clarification Required** — one concise distinction is necessary because
  alternatives change material meaning, target, authority, or consequence.
- **Deferred** — available context, capability, authority, or evidence cannot
  support a conformant interpretation.

Consequential interaction requires Resolved intent. A disclosed assumption
cannot carry a material consequence.

## 7.4 Projection outcomes

- **Complete** — every applicable CA obligation has a direct,
  meaning-preserving conversational realization.
- **Equivalent Multimodal** — obligations are distributed across supported
  modalities with complete coherent access.
- **Explicitly Degraded** — an authorized fallback preserves all essential
  meaning and operation while a bounded limitation is exposed.
- **Incompatible** — any essential meaning, authority, operation, navigation,
  accessibility, continuity, or recovery obligation cannot be preserved.

Only Complete, Equivalent Multimodal, or valid Explicitly Degraded projections
may become current.

# 8. Degradation, Correction, and Recovery

## 8.1 Ordered fallback policy

When a direct conversational capability is unavailable, evaluate:

1. an equivalent expression in the current conversational modality;
2. an equivalent expression in another supported modality;
3. a source-grounded serialized presentation preserving identity, semantic
   order, provenance, state, interaction, and navigation;
4. a source-authorized summary or deferral preserving reliable access to
   complete canonical sources;
5. an explicit degradation preserving every essential obligation and exposing
   the limitation;
6. Incompatible.

The first conformant option is selected. Fluency, engagement, speed, provider
preference, or conversational convenience cannot reorder fallback.

## 8.2 Degradation record

Every degradation records:

- affected contract, source revisions, occurrence, and CA obligation;
- unavailable context, provenance, authority, capability, modality, or
  continuity;
- meaning, operation, navigation, or accessibility at risk;
- selected authorized fallback;
- preserved and unpreserved properties;
- blocked consequence;
- correction or recovery condition;
- evidence and disposition.

Generated approximation is not a fallback unless a canonical source explicitly
permits bounded interpretation and the result remains labeled as such.

## 8.3 Correction

When a misprojection is detected:

1. mark the affected conversational expression non-current;
2. preserve its attribution and relationship to the correction;
3. identify the exact source, assumption, or transformation that failed;
4. resolve current canonical sources and authority;
5. distinguish presentation error from any already committed canonical
   consequence;
6. prepare and validate a replacement projection;
7. invoke canonical correction or reversal only if an owning consequence
   requires it;
8. replace the projection atomically.

Correction never silently edits canonical history or represents an uncorrected
source as corrected.

## 8.4 Recovery

Conversational recovery:

1. validates actor, authority, sources, contract, and capabilities;
2. obtains the latest canonical presentation revision set;
3. reconstructs context from canonical references rather than generated recall;
4. reconciles completed consequences and open uncommitted interactions;
5. restores current Navigation Representation, origin, return, interruption,
   and suspended context;
6. recomputes all CA obligations, accessibility, and modality bindings;
7. exposes missing, changed, stale, conflicting, or inaccessible meaning;
8. validates the complete replacement projection;
9. atomically supersedes obsolete dialogue state.

Recovery creates no domain event merely because conversational presentation is

# 9. Adapter Boundary

## 9.1 Boundary

```text
Canonical Presentation Architecture
        ↓  Conversational Contract
AI / Conversational Adapter
        ↓  Meaning-preserving bounded technical request
LLMs, Voice Systems, Chat Interfaces
```

No lower layer may bypass the layer immediately above it, read platform output
as canonical truth, or reinterpret an upstream source.

## 9.2 Canonical Presentation Architecture

The canonical layer:

- owns all source meaning, identity, state, structure, interaction, navigation,
  accessibility, motion, degradation, and recovery obligations;
- produces the exact compatible source revision set and Conversational Contract;
- defines allowable interpretation, summary, proposal, and fallback boundaries;
- remains independent of generation and delivery capability;
- never selects a provider, model, voice, chat mechanic, or technical format.

## 9.3 AI / Conversational Adapter

The adapter:

- consumes one valid current Conversational Contract;
- declares abstract capabilities and limitations;
- bounds intent, scope, sources, authority, and permitted conversational acts;
- maps all applicable CA-01–CA-08 obligations;
- validates semantic, interaction, navigation, accessibility, and multimodal
  preservation;
- rejects unsupported generated claims and prohibited implications;
- isolates dialogue-local state from canonical state;
- exposes ambiguity, degradation, correction, and recovery;
- supplies complete reproducible conformance evidence;
- rejects any essential obligation it cannot preserve.

The adapter may select technical mechanics but cannot select different
canonical meaning, authority, interaction consequence, navigation state,
fallback order, or conformance criteria.

## 9.4 LLMs, Voice Systems, and Chat Interfaces

These systems:

- provide technical generation, recognition, transformation, presentation, and
  turn-delivery capability;
- operate only within adapter-bounded requests;
- return technical output, capability, and failure information;
- may vary surface expression within validated semantic bounds;
- establish no canonical truth, identity, authority, state, relationship,
  interaction consequence, navigation, history, accessibility, degradation, or
  conformance.

Technical output is candidate presentation until the adapter validates it.
Provider confidence or completion has no canonical authority.

## 9.5 Boundary failures

The boundary fails if:

- generated output becomes a source of canonical truth;
- dialogue state replaces Current Situation, Context Envelope, or Operational
  Memory;
- model confidence changes authority or approval;
- conversational acknowledgment commits an action;
- topic change becomes navigation;
- a system invents a source, relationship, priority, or interruption;
- a summary conceals material uncertainty or missing evidence;
- modalities present contradictory state or consequence;
- a capability gap is hidden by plausible expression;
- provider-specific concepts enter the canonical contract;
- output availability alone is treated as conformance.
