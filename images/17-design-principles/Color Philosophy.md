# Color Philosophy

Color carries restrained operational meaning.

Neutral dark and paper tones create the office. Warm amber supports orientation. Green may indicate supporting evidence or accepted relation. Red identifies material risk or contradiction. Proposed and uncertain states require form, line, label, or texture in addition to color.

Color should reduce reading, not replace it. It must remain accessible on every surface and should become quieter as information becomes less important.

Reject decorative palettes, category rainbows, excessive saturation, and color that implies confidence the evidence does not support.
