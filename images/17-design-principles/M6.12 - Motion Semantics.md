# M6.12 — Motion Semantics

## Status

```text
Ready for Architecture Review
Milestone M6 — Active
```

## Authority

This document is the canonical architecture definition of motion semantics
within the M6 Presentation Architecture. It owns the Motion Contract, canonical
motion taxonomy, motion applicability, deterministic preservation and
degradation rules, the Platform Motion Adapter boundary, and motion conformance.

It consumes all approved M6.1–M6.11 architecture documents without modifying
their meaning, objects, ownership, lifecycle, authority, state, composition,
interaction, navigation, rendering, accessibility, or adapter contracts.

Motion is semantic communication rather than visual animation. Motion may
communicate canonical meaning that already exists; it never creates, changes,
completes, or proves that meaning.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Motion Semantics model defines when change over presentation progression may
communicate canonical appearance, disappearance, transition, continuity,
emphasis, attention guidance, state transition, interruption, or recovery.

The model:

- gives motion an explicit, bounded semantic role;
- traces every motion occurrence to existing canonical meaning;
- distinguishes motion meaning from platform animation mechanics;
- preserves professional interpretation when motion is reduced or absent;
- prevents movement, duration, sequence, or spectacle from becoming hidden
  domain truth;
- requires accessibility-equivalent and deterministic alternatives;
- makes degradation, omission, and recovery explicit and reviewable.

Motion supplements a complete static and structural interpretation. A person
must never need motion in order to discover essential meaning, perform an
operation, understand an outcome, or recover professional context.

---

# 2. Motion Principles

1. **Communication, Never Creation.** Motion expresses source-owned meaning and
   creates none.
2. **Static Truth Is Complete.** Canonical professional interpretation remains
   complete at every stable presentation state.
3. **Motion Is Optional.** Absence of motion cannot remove meaning, operation,
   consequence, context, or recovery.
4. **Source Before Movement.** Every motion occurrence requires an exact
   canonical source, applicable category, and communication purpose.
5. **One Primary Meaning.** Each occurrence has one primary motion category;
   supporting effects cannot introduce another meaning implicitly.
6. **State Is Authoritative.** Motion may communicate a valid state transition
   but cannot initiate, authorize, delay, complete, or validate it.
7. **Continuity Without Fabrication.** Motion may preserve identity across
   presentation change but cannot claim identity not established upstream.
8. **Emphasis Without Priority Creation.** Motion may express source-owned
   emphasis or attention obligations but cannot manufacture urgency, authority,
   certainty, or importance.
9. **Restraint.** Motion is admissible only where it materially improves
   orientation, continuity, feedback, or comprehension.
10. **No Manipulation.** Repetition, persistence, surprise, or movement cannot
    coerce attention beyond canonical professional priority.
11. **Accessible Equivalence.** Reduced-motion, no-motion, and alternative
    modalities preserve the same professional interpretation and outcomes.
12. **Deterministic Resolution.** Identical canonical inputs, contract
    revisions, capabilities, and preferences produce the same motion
    disposition.
13. **Explicit Degradation.** Unsupported or suppressed motion has a declared
    equivalent and is never silently relied upon.
14. **Adapter Neutrality.** Canonical semantics favor no animation system,
    rendering technology, sensory channel, or platform convention.

# 3. Motion Contract

## 3.1 Definition

A **Motion Contract** is a versioned, implementation-independent statement of
the canonical source meaning, motion category, communication purpose,
participants, start and end presentation conditions, continuity obligations,
optional no-motion equivalent, accessibility obligations, degradation policy,
and evidence required for a motion occurrence.

It specifies what a change-oriented expression may communicate. It does not
specify how movement is produced.

A Motion Contract is not an animation, transition effect, timeline, duration,
velocity, choreography, rendering instruction, interaction consequence, or
domain event.

## 3.2 Ownership

This model owns:

- the Motion Contract descriptor and definition lifecycle;
- MT-01–MT-09 category definitions;
- motion applicability and admissibility rules;
- occurrence resolution and lifecycle;
- no-motion, accessibility, degradation, and recovery obligations;
- the three-layer motion adapter boundary;
- motion conformance evidence and verdicts.

Upstream owners remain authoritative:

| Concern | Canonical owner |
| --- | --- |
| Professional and domain truth | Canonical Management Model and owning domain models |
| Design Objects and relationships | Canonical Design Object Model |
| Semantic Roles and semantic dimensions | Canonical Design Semantics |
| Rendering Nodes, tree identity, projection, and replacement | Canonical Rendering Model |
| Symbolic presentation meaning | Semantic Token Architecture |
| Presentation State and valid state transitions | Presentation State Model |
| Component responsibility and composition | Canonical Component Model and Composition and Layout System |
| Professional interaction intent, progression, and consequence | Canonical Operational Interaction Language and Canonical Interaction Patterns |
| Navigation truth, continuity, and representation | Canonical Operational Navigation, Navigation Governance, and Navigation Representation Model |
| Platform contract aggregation and qualification | Rendering Contracts and Platform Adapters |
| Accessibility obligations and modality equivalence | Accessibility Model |

The Platform Motion Adapter owns only translation of a valid Motion Contract
into declared platform capabilities. Platform Animation / Rendering APIs own
only technical mechanics.

## 3.3 Contract descriptor

Every Motion Contract contains:

| Field | Required meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Contract ID** | Stable motion-contract identity. |
| **Revision** | Immutable contract revision. |
| **Canonical sources** | Exact source contracts, occurrences, and revisions. |
| **Category** | Exactly one MT-01–MT-09 primary category. |
| **Communication purpose** | Existing meaning the motion may reinforce. |
| **Participants** | Exact Rendering Nodes, components, regions, interaction occurrences, or navigation representations involved. |
| **Source condition** | Valid stable presentation condition before motion. |
| **Target condition** | Valid stable presentation condition after motion. |
| **Trigger evidence** | Source-owned change that makes communication applicable. |
| **Semantic order** | Required order of communicated change where order carries source meaning. |
| **Continuity obligations** | Identity, context, focus, relationship, and progress that must survive. |
| **Static equivalent** | Complete no-motion expression of the same meaning. |
| **Accessibility obligations** | Applicable Accessibility Contract and equivalent alternatives. |
| **Interruption policy** | Valid disposition if the expression is interrupted or superseded. |
| **Degradation policy** | Ordered equivalent, reduced, absent, or incompatible disposition. |
| **Prohibited implications** | Meaning the occurrence must never communicate. |
| **Evidence obligations** | Proof required for conformance. |

An incomplete descriptor is not a Motion Contract.

## 3.4 Responsibilities

The Motion Contract must:

1. trace each occurrence to exact canonical source meaning;
2. identify one primary category and communication purpose;
3. prove both source and target presentation conditions are independently
   complete and valid;
4. preserve participants, identity, state, order, interaction, navigation, and
   accessibility ownership;
5. define a complete static equivalent before motion is admitted;
6. define interruption, supersession, degradation, and recovery behavior;
7. prohibit implications not supported by canonical sources;
8. provide deterministic, implementation-independent evidence.

The Motion Contract must not:

- create professional priority, urgency, authority, certainty, currentness,
  state, relationship, or consequence;
- make motion completion a canonical commitment signal;
- use movement to conceal missing content, operation, provenance, or feedback;
- infer source meaning from platform animation conventions;
- require a particular sensory capability;
- prescribe physical timing, trajectory, transformation, or implementation.

## 3.5 Definition lifecycle

Motion Contract definitions follow:

```text
Proposed → Registered → Active → Deprecated → Retired
```

- **Proposed** is reviewable but cannot govern a current occurrence.
- **Registered** has a stable ID, complete descriptor, and source traceability.
- **Active** is admissible for new occurrence resolution.
- **Deprecated** remains interpretable during an explicit transition period but
  cannot be selected for new use.
- **Retired** is historical and cannot govern a current realization.

A revision is immutable. Changed source meaning, category, applicability,
static equivalent, accessibility obligation, degradation, or evidence creates a
new revision.

## 3.6 Motion occurrence lifecycle

```text
Potential
    ↓ source trigger and applicability validated
Required
    ↓ contract and capability resolution
Resolved
    ↓ source and target states validated
Communicating
    ↓ target stable state is current
Complete

Required → Suppressed → Static Equivalent Current
Communicating → Interrupted → Resolve Current Canonical State
Any non-current occurrence → Superseded
Invalid resolution → Rejected
```

- **Potential** identifies no obligation until its source trigger exists.
- **Required** means a canonical change is eligible for motion communication;
  it does not mean motion mechanics are mandatory.
- **Resolved** has one valid motion or static-equivalent disposition.
- **Communicating** is a transient presentation condition only.
- **Complete** means the target presentation condition is current; it does not
  prove a domain or interaction outcome.
- **Suppressed** uses the static equivalent without semantic loss.
- **Interrupted** resolves from current canonical state rather than assuming an
  endpoint.
- **Superseded** cannot continue to communicate obsolete meaning.
- **Rejected** never becomes current.

Occurrence lifecycle creates no domain event, state transition, interaction
commitment, navigation transition, authority change, or history entry.

# 4. Motion Taxonomy

## 4.1 Admission and exclusivity

A category is canonical only when it communicates a distinct class of
source-owned presentation change, remains meaningful without prescribing
mechanics, has a complete static equivalent, and cannot be fully represented by
another category.

Each occurrence has exactly one primary MT category. A contract may reference
supporting obligations from other categories, but duplicated ownership is not
permitted.

## 4.2 MT-01 Appearance

Communicates that a canonical presentation occurrence has become validly
present in the current Rendering Tree or exposure state.

Required source:

- exact occurrence identity and source revision;
- valid Rendering Node projection and parentage;
- source-owned exposure or Presentation State change;
- semantic order and relevant context;
- distinction between newly available, newly current, restored, and merely
  newly exposed.

Appearance cannot imply creation of the underlying Design Object, domain
lifecycle entry, successful operation, new authority, or increased priority.
Its static equivalent is the complete target state with explicit presence and
applicable state.

## 4.3 MT-02 Disappearance

Communicates that a presentation occurrence is no longer exposed or has been
validly removed from the current Rendering Tree.

Required source:

- exact occurrence identity and prior state;
- source-owned removal, replacement, or exposure change;
- continuity or recovery destination;
- distinction between hidden, unavailable, obsolete, replaced, resolved, and
  deleted;
- unfinished interaction or focus disposition.

Disappearance cannot imply domain deletion, completion, abandonment, loss of
history, or loss of authority unless an owning source states it. Its static
equivalent omits the occurrence while preserving explanation and recovery where
professionally required.

## 4.4 MT-03 Transition

Communicates a valid change between two complete presentation configurations.

Required source:

- valid source and target configurations;
- exact changed and unchanged participants;
- source-owned transition basis;
- semantic order where change order matters;
- interaction and navigation continuity;
- interruption and supersession policy.

Transition cannot make an invalid intermediate condition canonical, turn
presentation change into domain change, or use physical travel to assert a
relationship. Its static equivalent is an atomic target replacement with the
change communicated by non-motion means.

## 4.5 MT-04 Continuity

Communicates that identity, context, relationship, or professional intent
persists across a valid presentation replacement or re-composition.

Required source:

- stable canonical and occurrence identities;
- exact source and target positions in their respective structures;
- preserved context, relationship, and state;
- changed properties and unchanged properties;
- semantic continuation and recovery target.

Continuity cannot infer sameness from visual resemblance, proximity, trajectory,
or platform object reuse. Its static equivalent preserves stable identity,
references, context, and semantic continuation explicitly.

## 4.6 MT-05 Emphasis

Communicates a source-owned change in presentation emphasis for an already
present occurrence.

Required source:

- exact Semantic Role, token, or Presentation State basis;
- affected semantic dimension;
- reason and validity interval;
- relationship to other independent dimensions;
- cessation or supersession condition.

Emphasis cannot create urgency, priority, authority, certainty, selection,
Current Focus, or importance. Repetition cannot amplify source meaning. Its
static equivalent expresses the same source-owned emphasis without motion.

## 4.7 MT-06 Attention Guidance

Communicates where professional attention may be productively directed after a
material source-owned change while preserving deliberate control.

Required source:

- attributable change or navigation-governance basis;
- target occurrence and current context;
- attention class and relative obligation;
- actor authority and available response;
- origin, return, and dismissal or acknowledgment conditions.

Attention guidance cannot seize Current Focus, perform navigation, select an
object, classify an interruption, aggregate weak signals, or compel action.
Its static equivalent exposes the same target, basis, class, and available
response through accessible structure.

## 4.8 MT-07 State Transition

Communicates a valid change in Presentation State or a source-owned domain state
already projected into presentation.

Required source:

- prior and current canonical state revisions;
- valid state-transition authority and evidence;
- affected occurrence and independent state dimension;
- commitment or non-commitment boundary;
- completed, pending, failed, reversed, or indeterminate disposition.

Motion never initiates or completes the state transition. Motion completion is
not state evidence. Its static equivalent presents the current valid state and,
where material, an explicit change indication.

## 4.9 MT-08 Interruption

Communicates an interruption already classified and governed by Canonical
Operational Navigation, Navigation Governance, and Canonical Interaction
Patterns.

Required source:

- exactly one source-owned interruption class;
- material basis, provenance, and current disposition;
- affected context and displaced professional intent;
- authority, available action, and reliable return;
- escalation, reclassification, and expiry evidence where applicable.

Motion cannot classify, escalate, aggregate, or strengthen an interruption.
Delivery intensity cannot change professional priority. Its static equivalent
preserves class, basis, affected context, response, suspension, and return.

## 4.10 MT-09 Recovery

Communicates restoration from a degraded, interrupted, stale, failed, or
incomplete presentation condition to a valid current presentation.

Required source:

- last valid and current canonical revisions;
- affected occurrences and obligations;
- completed versus uncommitted consequences;
- restored, changed, missing, or still degraded properties;
- context, focus, origin, return, and next valid action.

Recovery motion cannot claim successful domain recovery, replay an interaction,
hide residual degradation, or restore obsolete state. Its static equivalent
presents the reconciled current state, changes, remaining limitations, and
recovery path explicitly.

# 5. Motion Applicability and Mapping

## 5.1 Applicability rule

Motion is admissible only when all conditions hold:

1. an exact canonical source change exists;
2. one MT category materially improves communication of that change;
3. source and target stable states are independently valid and complete;
4. a static equivalent preserves the entire professional interpretation;
5. motion adds no semantic distinction absent from the stable states;
6. interaction and navigation remain operable before, during, and after the
   occurrence;
7. accessibility equivalence is established;
8. interruption, supersession, degradation, and recovery are deterministic;
9. required evidence can be produced.

If motion is merely decorative or provides no material semantic benefit, it is
outside this model and cannot carry canonical meaning.

## 5.2 Source mapping

| Motion category | Primary source ownership | Required supporting contracts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MT-01 Appearance** | Rendering Model exposure or Presentation State | Design Semantics; Components; Composition; Accessibility |
| **MT-02 Disappearance** | Rendering replacement or Presentation State | Interaction continuity; Navigation Representation; Accessibility |
| **MT-03 Transition** | Valid presentation configuration change | Rendering; Composition; Interaction; Navigation; Accessibility |
| **MT-04 Continuity** | Stable upstream identity and Context Envelope | Rendering identity; Composition; Navigation Representation; Accessibility |
| **MT-05 Emphasis** | Semantic Tokens or Presentation State | Design Semantics; Visual Semantics; Accessibility |
| **MT-06 Attention Guidance** | Source-owned change and Navigation Governance | Interaction Patterns; Navigation Representation; Accessibility |
| **MT-07 State Transition** | Presentation State or projected owning-domain state | Rendering Contracts; Interaction Patterns; Accessibility |
| **MT-08 Interruption** | Navigation Governance and Interaction Patterns | Navigation Representation; Presentation State; Accessibility |
| **MT-09 Recovery** | Owning source recovery and current canonical state | Rendering; Interaction; Navigation; Accessibility |

Mapping references source ownership; it transfers none.

## 5.3 Stable-state completeness

Before and after every occurrence:

- all essential meaning is discoverable without motion history;
- current state is explicit;
- available operations are fully operable;
- semantic order and structure are complete;
- focus and navigation context are valid;
- feedback and consequence do not depend on observing movement;
- accessibility obligations are satisfied.

A transient expression cannot be the only carrier of a fact, warning,
qualification, provenance, relationship, state, or recovery path.

# 6. Motion Preservation Rules

## 6.1 Semantic preservation

1. Motion communicates only meaning present in exact canonical source and target
   states.
2. Motion direction, path, scale, intensity, repetition, duration, and sequence
   have no implicit canonical meaning.
3. A physical transformation cannot add a relationship, hierarchy, priority,
   authority, certainty, causality, or lifecycle implication.
4. Motion cannot merge independent semantic dimensions.
5. Motion cannot change Semantic Role, Design Object identity, or canonical
   state.
6. Platform conventions cannot supply missing motion meaning.
7. Every interpreted change remains attributable without observing motion.
8. Motion completion cannot prove interaction or domain completion.

## 6.2 Optionality and no-motion preservation

1. Every Motion Contract defines a complete static equivalent.
2. Motion may be suppressed before or during an occurrence without changing
   canonical outcome.
3. Suppression resolves directly to the current valid stable state.
4. Absence of motion preserves appearance, disappearance, transition,
   continuity, emphasis, attention, state, interruption, and recovery meaning
   through static structure, state, and feedback.
5. No operation requires observation or completion of motion.
6. No navigation return or recovery depends on remembered movement.
7. No-motion presentation is first-class and cannot be labeled incomplete.
8. Decorative motion cannot substitute for a required static indication.

## 6.3 Interaction and state preservation

1. Motion lifecycle remains separate from Interaction Pattern progression.
2. Interaction eligibility is identical with motion present, reduced, absent,
   interrupted, or failed.
3. Commitment occurs only at the owning canonical boundary.
4. Input received during motion is evaluated against current canonical state,
   not a presumed visual endpoint.
5. Cancellation of motion does not cancel or reverse a canonical consequence.
6. Cancellation of an uncommitted interaction follows the Interaction Pattern,
   not the motion occurrence.
7. Motion failure cannot leave state indeterminate; current canonical state is
   re-resolved.
8. Retry of motion never retries the canonical operation.

## 6.4 Continuity and navigation preservation

1. Continuity motion uses only upstream-established stable identity.
2. Semantic order remains authoritative over movement order.
3. Presentation focus remains valid and distinguishable from selection and
   Current Focus.
4. Motion cannot adopt, abandon, suspend, or return to professional context.
5. Origin, return, interruption, and recovery remain explicit without spatial
   travel.
6. Atomic replacement remains authoritative even when motion communicates the
   replacement.
7. Superseded motion stops communicating obsolete navigation or state.
8. Interrupted motion resolves from current sources and preserves the nearest
   valid semantic continuation.

## 6.5 Accessibility preservation

1. Every occurrence consumes the applicable Accessibility Contract.
2. Equivalent meaning and operation exist in reduced-motion, no-motion, and
   alternative supported modalities.
3. Motion is never the sole signal for content, state, relationship, attention,
   warning, completion, or error.
4. Suppression preserves semantic discoverability, perceivability, structural
   navigation, and interaction equivalence.
5. Attention guidance cannot force focus or repeated interruption.
6. A modality transition during motion resolves to a complete stable state.
7. Accessibility preference is a capability input, not professional state.
8. Failure of accessibility equivalence makes the occurrence inadmissible.

## 6.6 Professional-intent preservation

1. Motion remains calm, purposeful, restrained, and proportional to source
   meaning.
2. Motion cannot manufacture urgency through spectacle, repetition, or
   persistence.
3. Evidence, uncertainty, provenance, and authority remain available before
   consequential action.
4. Historical, current, recommended, interrupted, and recovered conditions
   remain distinct.
5. Motion cannot obscure professional content or defer access to it.
6. Efficiency of presentation cannot remove deliberate review or confirmation.
7. Motion cannot reward, pressure, entertain, or distract in ways that alter
   professional judgment.

# 7. Deterministic Resolution

## 7.1 Input contract

Resolution accepts:

- exact canonical source and target revisions;
- active Motion Contract revision;
- applicable MT category and source trigger;
- current Rendering Tree, Semantic Token, Presentation State, Component,
  Composition Plan, Interaction Pattern, and Navigation Representation;
- applicable Accessibility Contract;
- declared abstract platform motion and rendering capabilities;
- declared accessibility and motion-preference constraints;
- current or prior motion occurrence where interruption applies;
- source-authorized fallback order.

## 7.2 Resolution pipeline

```text
Validate Source Meaning and Trigger
        ↓
Validate Stable Source and Target States
        ↓
Resolve One Primary MT Category
        ↓
Validate Static and Accessibility Equivalents
        ↓
Resolve Continuity, Interaction, and Navigation
        ↓
Match Declared Motion Capability
        ↓
Apply Ordered Fallback or Suppression
        ↓
Validate Complete Disposition
```

Identical inputs produce the same applicability, category, participants,
semantic order, continuity, suppression, fallback, degradation, and conformance
outcome.

## 7.3 Outcomes

- **Motion Equivalent** — an admissible motion expression and its static
  equivalent preserve every obligation.
- **Reduced Motion Equivalent** — a bounded change-oriented expression preserves
  every obligation under declared constraints.
- **No-Motion Equivalent** — the static equivalent preserves every obligation;
  this is conformant and not degradation.
- **Explicitly Degraded** — an authorized fallback preserves essential meaning
  and operation while a bounded non-essential limitation is exposed.
- **Incompatible** — any essential meaning, operation, continuity,
  accessibility, or recovery obligation cannot be preserved.

Motion Equivalent has no higher canonical status than No-Motion Equivalent.

# 8. Degradation, Interruption, and Recovery

## 8.1 Ordered fallback policy

When direct motion expression is unavailable or inappropriate, evaluate:

1. a semantically equivalent reduced-motion expression;
2. the complete no-motion static equivalent;
3. an equivalent expression through another supported modality;
4. a source-authorized serialized explanation of change preserving identity,
   order, state, operation, and continuity;
5. an explicit degradation preserving every essential obligation and exposing
   the limitation;
6. Incompatible.

The first conformant option is selected. Platform preference, visual effect,
performance convenience, or implementation cost cannot reorder fallback.

## 8.2 Explicit degradation

Every degradation records:

- affected Motion Contract and canonical sources;
- unsupported capability or conflicting constraint;
- affected participants and MT category;
- selected fallback and preserved properties;
- any unpreserved non-essential communication benefit;
- static and accessibility-equivalence result;
- interruption and recovery disposition;
- evidence and final outcome.

Silent omission is prohibited only when motion was the declared communication
path and its required equivalent was not supplied. Choosing a valid No-Motion
Equivalent is not degradation.

## 8.3 Interruption and supersession

On interruption or supersession:

1. stop relying on the motion occurrence as current communication;
2. obtain current canonical source and presentation revisions;
3. distinguish committed consequences from uncommitted interaction;
4. invalidate obsolete source or target assumptions;
5. resolve the complete current stable state;
6. preserve valid identity, focus, context, origin, and return;
7. apply the current accessibility and fallback obligations;
8. publish only a validated replacement disposition.

An interrupted occurrence never assumes its source or target state is current
based on visual progress.

## 8.4 Recovery

Motion recovery:

1. validates current sources, actor, authority, state, and capabilities;
2. identifies the last valid stable presentation state;
3. reconciles source changes and canonical consequences;
4. recomputes applicability from current meaning;
5. restores the current state through motion, reduced motion, or no motion with
   equal authority;
6. exposes remaining change, limitation, or uncertainty;
7. preserves interaction and navigation recovery;
8. replaces obsolete motion state atomically.

Recovery creates no canonical event merely because presentation communication
is restored.

# 9. Adapter Boundary

## 9.1 Boundary

```text
Canonical Motion Semantics
        ↓  Motion Contract
Platform Motion Adapter
        ↓  Meaning-preserving technical mapping
Platform Animation / Rendering APIs
```

No lower layer may bypass the layer immediately above it or reinterpret an
upstream canonical source.

## 9.2 Canonical Motion Semantics

The canonical layer:

- owns MT-01–MT-09, applicability, static equivalence, preservation,
  degradation, recovery, and evidence obligations;
- binds every contract to exact canonical sources;
- defines permitted communication and prohibited implication;
- remains independent of platform capability and mechanics;
- produces complete Motion Contracts for adapter qualification.

It does not select physical effects, duration, path, cadence, interpolation,
rendering operation, or technical interface.

## 9.3 Platform Motion Adapter

The adapter:

- consumes one valid current Motion Contract;
- declares abstract motion and rendering capabilities and limitations;
- maps obligations to an admissible motion, reduced-motion, no-motion, or
  authorized fallback disposition;
- preserves source and target states, identity, semantic order, interaction,
  navigation, accessibility, and professional intent;
- treats static equivalence as first-class;
- handles interruption and supersession from current canonical state;
- exposes degradation and supplies reproducible evidence;
- rejects obligations it cannot preserve.

The adapter may choose technical mechanics but cannot choose new meaning,
category, applicability, priority, outcome, fallback order, or conformance
criteria.

## 9.4 Platform Animation / Rendering APIs

Platform Animation / Rendering APIs:

- provide technical change-oriented and rendering mechanics;
- realize only adapter mappings;
- report technical capability, progress, interruption, and failure;
- may optimize mechanics without changing semantic disposition;
- establish no canonical meaning, state, continuity, interaction, navigation,
  accessibility, degradation, or conformance.

Technical completion is never evidence of canonical state or interaction
completion.

## 9.5 Boundary failures

The boundary fails if:

- movement mechanics become a source of canonical meaning;
- platform defaults choose category, order, emphasis, or priority;
- animation completion commits an interaction or domain transition;
- motion is the sole carrier of essential meaning;
- suppressed motion removes an operation or recovery path;
- platform focus or position becomes professional context;
- an unsupported capability is silently approximated;
- implementation vocabulary enters a canonical Motion Contract;
- conformance is claimed from API availability alone.

# 11. Conformance Evidence and Verdict

## 11.1 Required evidence

A motion qualification package contains:

- exact Motion Contract, canonical sources, capability declaration, and
  Accessibility Contract;
- MT-01–MT-09 applicability and coverage matrix;
- source and target stable-state completeness evidence;
- occurrence-to-source identity and trigger traceability;
- primary category and prohibited-implication evidence;
- semantic order, identity, continuity, interaction, and navigation evidence;
- static, reduced-motion, no-motion, and alternative-modality equivalence
  evidence;
- accessibility discoverability, perceivability, operability, structural
  navigation, and recovery evidence;
- interruption, supersession, failure, and modality-transition scenarios;
- Motion Equivalent, Reduced Motion Equivalent, No-Motion Equivalent,
  Explicitly Degraded, and Incompatible scenarios;
- deterministic rerun evidence;
- adapter-boundary audit, limitations, and final verdict.
