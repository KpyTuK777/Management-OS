# Canonical Matter Experience

## Status

```text
Canonical Matter Experience v1.0
Hardened after Architecture Review
Milestone M3.1
```

## Purpose

This document defines how an operational manager experiences one admitted Matter
from its preserved report through closure and possible return. It is a human experience
contract, independent of interface, device, layout, component, visual language,
or implementation.

The manager does not experience a sequence of system states. The manager
experiences one enduring operational concern while their intent, confidence,
attention, and responsibility change over time.

This document refines, and does not replace, the lifecycle, authority, and model
boundaries established by the
[Canonical Product Operating Model](Canonical%20Product%20Operating%20Model.md).
The experience journey is not a second lifecycle. Its stages describe the
manager's professional posture and may overlap, repeat, or be skipped when the
Matter warrants a proportionate path.

## Scope and interpretation

The canonical experience begins only after an admission decision creates a
`Reported` Matter and ends when no current operational work is justified. It includes assessment,
investigation, judgment, intervention, observation, stabilization, closure, and
reopening.

The canonical admission boundary is:

```text
Pre-admission observation
→ admission decision
→ Reported Matter
→ Canonical Matter Experience
```

Pre-admission observation is outside the Canonical Matter Experience. Before
admission there is no Matter identity or Matter history. Admission is an
attributable decision that a captured signal deserves accountable continuity;
capture alone never grants Matter authority. A rejected or non-admitted signal
may remain in attention history, but it does not become Matter history.

Three terms remain distinct:

- **Matter state** is the authoritative operational posture defined by the
  canonical lifecycle.
- **Experience stage** is the manager's current professional intent.
- **Focus** is the specific question, object, or condition receiving the
  manager's attention now.

An experience stage does not authorize a Matter lifecycle transition. A change of focus
does not change Matter truth. A system suggestion does not become a human
Decision.

## 1. Experience Principles

### Independent Experience Principles

1. **Attention always has a reason.** The manager can understand why this Matter
   deserves attention now, what changed, and what responsibility follows.
2. **The current professional intent is clear.** At any moment the manager can
   distinguish observing, assessing, investigating, deciding, intervening, and
   evaluating.
3. **Action follows authority.** The manager can distinguish proposed,
   authorized, delegated, underway, reported complete, and effective change.
4. **Correction is safer than concealment.** Contradiction, reversal,
    reconsideration, adverse outcome, and failure remain first-class parts of
    professional work.
5. **Proportionality governs effort.** The experience asks for no more
    investigation, ceremony, intervention, or monitoring than the Matter
    justifies.

### Derived Experience Constraints

These constraints translate canonical domain and product truth into required
human experience. They do not redefine their canonical sources.

1. **One Matter remains one concern.** Derived from Matter identity and
   reopening in the
   [Canonical Product Operating Model](Canonical%20Product%20Operating%20Model.md):
   the manager is never presented with fragments or a reopened Matter as though
   they were newly created concerns.
2. **One operational object dominates.** Derived from Product Experience
   Principle 13 and the canonical Focus laws: supporting evidence, history, and
   capabilities never compete as an equal operational center.
3. **Unknown remains unknown.** Derived from Product Experience Principle 15:
   missing, stale, disputed, inferred, and unverified information is visibly
   qualified rather than given visual confidence.
4. **Understanding is traceable and revisable.** Derived from
   [Operational Memory — Canonical](Operational%20Design%20Brief%2001%20-%20Operational%20Memory.md):
   the manager can inspect the basis, correction, and earlier accepted form
   without history being rewritten.
5. **Judgment is deliberate and attributable.** Derived from canonical authority:
   progression, recommendation, completion, and system events never appear as a
   human Decision.
6. **Continuity survives movement.** Derived from the canonical Context Envelope
   in the
   [Canonical Product Operating Model](Canonical%20Product%20Operating%20Model.md#navigation-invariant):
   movement, interruption, and reopening restore professional orientation rather
   than presenting restarted work.
7. **History supports the present.** Derived from Operational Memory and Product
   Experience Principle 13: prior reasoning is reachable and attributable but
   subordinate until relevant.
8. **Outcomes outrank activity.** Derived from Product Experience Principle 9:
   effort and completion never appear as observed effect, adoption, guardrail
   health, or stabilization.
9. **Closure is an accountable conclusion.** Derived from the canonical closure
   contract: the experience exposes why work may stop, residual uncertainty, and
   return conditions without implying perfect knowledge.
10. **The same professional standard applies in every context.** Derived from
    the Practice / Learning Context contract: production, learning, simulation,
    and retrospective work preserve one professional standard while exposing
    different evidence and consequence.

## 2. Experience Journey

The ordinary human journey is:

```text
Observe
  ↓
Assess
  ↓
Investigate
  ↓
Understand
  ↓
Decide
  ↓
Intervene
  ↓
Monitor
  ↓
Stabilize
  ↓
Close
```

Experience Journey ≠ Matter lifecycle. The Journey describes professional
intent, attention, judgment, and experience completion criteria. The canonical
Matter lifecycle describes state, authority, permitted Matter lifecycle transitions, and
historical consequences and is owned only by the Canonical Product Operating
Model.

This is an orientation model, not a mandatory sequence. Understand may return to
Investigate. Monitor may begin
during Intervene. An adverse or inconclusive result may return the manager to
Investigate, Decide, or Intervene. Reopening begins a new assessment under the
same Matter identity.

A Matter may compress, revisit, or omit experience stages where the canonical
lifecycle and authority contracts permit it. Not every Matter passes through all
nine stages. Stage completion never implies a Matter lifecycle transition,
experience stage omission or compression never creates a new Matter lifecycle
Matter lifecycle transition, and no Journey path may bypass an authority boundary.

### Observe

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Professionally process the preserved report of an already admitted Matter without premature interpretation |
| Dominant object | The admitted Matter's original report in its source context |
| Key questions | What happened or was noticed? Who or what is the source? When and where? What may be affected? Is immediate protective action required? |
| Typical decisions | Confirm faithful preservation; identify immediate risk; seek essential clarification; begin accountable assessment |
| Completion criterion | The report, source, time, context, known impact, and explicit unknowns are available for proportionate assessment without premature diagnosis |

The manager should feel that the report has been heard without being embellished.
Hypotheses, solutions, and broad history remain peripheral unless immediate risk
makes them material.

Observe occurs after admission and cannot dismiss or close the Matter. A
no-current-work disposition follows the canonical path:

```text
Reported
→ Under Assessment
→ Closed
```

### Assess

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Establish ownership, boundary, materiality, urgency, and the justified next posture |
| Dominant object | The bounded Matter and its current assessment |
| Key questions | What is inside and outside scope? Why does it matter now? Who is accountable? What is known versus assumed? What is the cost of delay? Is investigation or another response justified? |
| Typical decisions | Accept or delegate ownership; refine scope; set urgency; initiate investigation; take proportionate protective action; close with a no-current-work disposition |
| Completion criterion | An accountable owner has an inspectable assessment and can justify investigation, another proportionate response, or closure |

Assessment ends ambiguity about responsibility and next posture, not uncertainty
about cause.

### Investigate

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Reduce the most decision-relevant uncertainty through disciplined inquiry |
| Dominant object | The current Investigation focus supported by the active Working Set |
| Key questions | What do we need to know next? Which evidence can change judgment? What explanations compete? What contradicts the current view? What is stale, missing, or inaccessible? |
| Typical decisions | Prioritize an inquiry; admit or reject material; request evidence; test a hypothesis; narrow or broaden scope; involve a contributor; return to assessment |
| Completion criterion | The material uncertainty is reduced enough to form an attributable understanding or the Matter requires reassessment |

Collection volume is not progress. The manager should know the next uncertainty
worth reducing and why it matters.

### Understand

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Form a coherent, challengeable account of the current situation and its implications |
| Dominant object | The accepted Current Situation with its evidence, contradictions, and residual uncertainty |
| Key questions | What do we now believe is happening? On what basis? How strong is that basis? What remains contested? What changes if the understanding is wrong? Is a judgment required? |
| Typical decisions | Accept, correct, or reject a synthesis; preserve alternatives; resolve or acknowledge contradiction; identify the decision question; continue investigation |
| Completion criterion | The manager can explain the situation, its basis, limits, and operational implication without concealing material uncertainty |

Understanding is not certainty and does not automatically authorize action. It is
a governed synthesis that remains revisable.

### Decide

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Make a deliberate, attributable judgment on an inspectable basis |
| Dominant object | The decision question and Decision Readiness basis; after judgment, the authoritative Decision |
| Key questions | What must be decided? What alternatives exist? What happens if we defer or do nothing? What evidence, risk, uncertainty, guardrails, and authority apply? What intended condition defines success? |
| Typical decisions | Choose an alternative; choose no action; defer with conditions; request further investigation; reject readiness; define intended condition and accepted risk |
| Completion criterion | An authorized owner records the Decision, rationale, intended condition, assumptions, accepted uncertainty, and consequences, or explicitly returns the Matter for more work |

The manager must never feel pushed to decide merely because the system has
advanced, material has been completed, or a recommendation appears confident.

### Intervene

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Translate Decision intent into proportionate, governed change while preserving source ownership |
| Dominant object | The intended change and its current Operational Transition |
| Key questions | What must change? Who owns each commitment? Which dependencies and adaptations matter? What guardrails must hold? What would require pause, escalation, or reconsideration? |
| Typical decisions | Approve an adaptation; assign or confirm ownership; sequence commitments; authorize a checkpoint; pause or stop change; return for Decision reconsideration |
| Completion criterion | Approved change is underway or has a justified execution-free disposition, with ownership, dependencies, guardrails, and observable conditions explicit |

The Matter coordinates meaning and accountability. It does not manufacture task,
calendar, or external execution truth.

### Monitor

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Compare observed operation with Decision intent and detect meaningful deviation early |
| Dominant object | The intended-versus-observed condition |
| Key questions | What changed in reality? Is the expected effect appearing? Are guardrails holding? Is adoption sufficient? Is the evidence fresh and representative? What is signal versus ordinary variation? |
| Typical decisions | Continue observation; investigate deviation; adjust authorized execution; escalate; reassess the Decision; record an adverse, partial, unknown, or inconclusive Outcome |
| Completion criterion | Proportionate observation supports an honest Outcome disposition or shows that further investigation, judgment, or intervention is required |

Monitoring may overlap intervention. Task completion, elapsed time, or the absence
of alerts does not prove effect.

### Stabilize

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | Establish whether the intended condition is sustained and safe enough to end active change |
| Dominant object | The stabilization assessment within the Outcome |
| Key questions | Has intended behavior held for the required interval? Are guardrails and dependencies stable? Are adverse effects absent or accepted? Is recurrence risk understood? What monitoring must remain? |
| Typical decisions | Accept stabilization; extend observation; continue intervention; reopen investigation; accept bounded residual risk |
| Completion criterion | Proportionate evidence shows a sustained disposition, or the manager explicitly redirects the Matter because stabilization is not established |

Stabilization is an evidence-based judgment, not a new Matter state and not a
synonym for completed execution.

### Close

| Concern | Experience contract |
|---|---|
| Main user goal | End current operational work without losing truth, accountability, or the ability to return |
| Dominant object | The closure disposition and final accepted Current Situation |
| Key questions | Why is no current work justified? What was decided and observed? What remains uncertain or risky? What recurrence or review condition would justify reopening? Who owns any continuing obligation? |
| Typical decisions | Close; defer closure; define recurrence or review conditions; preserve residual monitoring; reopen when new information is already material |
| Completion criterion | The accountable owner records a supported final disposition, residual uncertainty and risk, continuing obligations, and explicit return conditions |

Closure makes the Matter quiet, not absent. Reopening preserves the same identity
and returns the manager to Assess with the prior closure and new trigger visible
together.

## 3. Attention Flow

Attention moves by professional relevance, not by navigation or data structure.
At every stage the manager experiences three deliberate layers:

- **Focus:** the one question or object that currently requires judgment or work.
- **Periphery:** the small set of conditions that could materially change that
  work, such as urgent risk, contradiction, authority, freshness, dependency, or
  guardrail status.
- **Available context:** complete relevant history, evidence, alternatives,
  source records, and adjacent capabilities, reachable without competing with
  the current task.

| Stage | In focus | Moves to the periphery | Remains available |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observe | Original signal, source, immediate consequence | Early interpretations and possible responses | Related history, operating context, prior Matters |
| Assess | Scope, ownership, materiality, urgency, next posture | Detailed causal inquiry and solution detail | Original report, immediate evidence, authority context |
| Investigate | Most valuable uncertainty and evidence needed next | Settled low-relevance material and execution concerns | Current Situation, inquiry history, hypotheses, contradictions, full provenance |
| Understand | Coherent account, evidence basis, limits, implication | Collection mechanics and superseded synthesis | Alternative explanations, source material, earlier versions |
| Decide | Decision question, alternatives, consequences, risk, authority | Raw material already represented faithfully in the basis | Traceable evidence, dissent, assumptions, history |
| Intervene | Intended change, owner, dependency, guardrail, deviation | Settled investigative detail | Decision rationale, impact basis, source-owned execution records |
| Monitor | Intended versus observed condition | Routine activity and non-material variation | Baseline, Decision, execution history, evidence provenance |
| Stabilize | Sustainability, guardrails, recurrence, residual risk | Individual completed actions | Outcome history, observation limits, unresolved conditions |
| Close | Final disposition, residual conditions, return trigger | Active-work prompts and obsolete plans | Complete immutable history, Decision, Outcome, sources |

Attention may be interrupted only by a concern that is more material than the
current focus: immediate safety or continuity risk, material contradiction,
authority failure, source invalidation, privacy concern, guardrail breach, or
consequential approval. The interruption must explain why it displaced the
previous focus and preserve a reliable return point.

When attention returns after interruption, the manager is reoriented through the
same Matter identity, the last accepted situation, what changed, why attention
returned, and the next unresolved question. The manager is never returned merely
to a location.

## 4. Cognitive Load

The experience reduces cognitive load without reducing professional truth:

1. Present one dominant operational object for the current intent.
2. Ask for the smallest consequential judgment that can responsibly advance the
   Matter.
3. Separate observation, interpretation, Decision, action, and Outcome so the
   manager does not have to untangle mixed claims.
4. Reveal detail progressively according to relevance, risk, and requested
   inspection.
5. Summarize only when the summary remains traceable to its basis and exposes
   uncertainty.
6. Keep stable identity, owner, current posture, and current focus recognizable
   throughout the journey.
7. Preserve earlier work and corrections so the manager does not have to remember
   or recreate them.
8. Keep history available but subordinate to the current concern unless prior
   context becomes decision-relevant.
9. Distinguish required decisions from optional exploration and informational
   material.
10. Do not demand completeness where proportionality permits an explicit unknown,
    deferral, or no-current-work disposition.
11. Avoid simultaneous calls for unrelated judgments; material safety,
    authority, and contradiction concerns are the exceptions.
12. Restore work using meaningful changes and unresolved questions, not a replay
    of everything that happened.

Reduced cognitive load never means hidden disagreement, collapsed provenance,
premature certainty, automatic consent, or removal of relevant risk.

## 5. Context Continuity

Context continuity means that after any navigation transition, interruption, return, or
reopening, the manager can continue reasoning rather than reconstruct the Matter.

The Context Envelope owned by the
[Workbench Canonical Domain Model](Workbench%20Canonical%20Domain%20Model.md#12-context-envelope)
is the single canonical context contract. The Canonical Product Operating Model
defines downstream cross-zone requirements only. This document creates no
parallel container, aggregate, store, payload owner, or implementation schema.

For experience continuity, the canonical Context Envelope restores the applicable
meaning from these categories:

- current Matter identity and authoritative posture;
- current professional intent and dominant operational object;
- accepted Current Situation reference and active Working Set reference;
- unresolved questions and material changed conditions;
- current Decision, pending judgment, responsible actor, and authority condition;
- interruption origin and reliable return point;
- relevant historical context, including contradictions and residual unknowns.

These are experience-level continuity requirements, not a fixed payload. The
exact required detail for each cross-zone navigation transition, recovery event, and
degraded-source condition remains a Navigation governance decision.

Continuity rules:

- entering a new stage carries forward relevant accepted context and does not ask
  the manager to restate known information;
- a new synthesis supersedes but never overwrites an earlier accepted version;
- a return to an earlier stage explains the new evidence, contradiction,
  adverse result, scope change, or reconsideration that caused the return;
- crossing into source-owned work preserves the Matter, Decision intent, and
  return relationship without copying source authority;
- interruption preserves unfinished reasoning and the reason for interruption;
- resuming after absence distinguishes new information from unchanged context;
- reopening joins the prior closure disposition and the new trigger under the
  same identity;
- degraded or unavailable sources are shown as a continuity limitation, not
  silently replaced by stale or inferred truth.

The continuity test is human: the manager can answer “What is this?”, “Why am I
here now?”, “What changed?”, “What did we already establish?”, and “What must I
do or judge next?” without rebuilding the case.

## 6. Operational Confidence

Operational confidence is justified trust in the integrity of the work. It does
not arise from visual polish, confident language, automation, or apparent
completeness.

The manager can trust the experience when:

- every material claim can be traced to a source, actor, observation, inference,
  or explicit assumption;
- known, unknown, disputed, stale, unavailable, simulated, and inferred material
  remain distinguishable;
- accepted summaries and Current Situation versions identify who accepted them
  and what changed;
- every Decision identifies its author, authority, rationale, basis, time,
  assumptions, and accepted uncertainty;
- proposals, recommendations, approvals, and authoritative changes are distinct;
- no system, integration, or assistant silently changes Matter truth;
- state and context changes are attributable, timestamped, explained, and
  historically inspectable;
- responsibility boundaries show who may recommend, decide, execute, attest, and
  close;
- source-owned execution and observation remain source-owned;
- any conclusion can be challenged by inspecting its supporting and conflicting
  material;
- stale, incomplete, conflicting, or unavailable inputs reduce expressed
  confidence rather than disappear;
- corrections append a new accountable interpretation instead of rewriting the
  past;
- adverse and inconclusive Outcomes are as representable as successful ones;
- system limitations and recovery conditions are explicit.

The manager does not need to trust an opaque conclusion. The experience makes
verification proportionate: the basis is understandable first, and deeper
inspection remains available when consequence, doubt, or accountability requires
it.

### Delegation and substitute authority

Before general implementation, every experience involving delegated or
substitute authority must satisfy these constraints:

- the current authority holder is identifiable;
- delegation is explicit and attributable;
- substitute authority has an inspectable scope and duration;
- transfer or substitution never rewrites prior authority history;
- activity, availability, or continued work never causes the system to presume
  authority.

These constraints do not create a new owner model. Lifecycle and authority remain
owned by the Canonical Product Operating Model and applicable domain contracts.

### Stabilization judgment

Before general implementation, stabilization experience must satisfy these
constraints:

- stabilization is distinct from task or execution completion;
- the observation window and its limits are explicit;
- the expected condition and applicable guardrails are known;
- an adverse or inconclusive Outcome cannot appear stabilized or be concealed by
  closure;
- an accountable actor makes and attributes the stabilization judgment.

Observation windows remain proportionate to the Matter class; this experience
contract does not prescribe universal numeric intervals.

## 7. Practice & Learning

Production, learning, simulation, and retrospective cases pass through the same
canonical Matter lifecycle and shared experience contract. They use the same
identity continuity, professional stages, epistemic discipline, attribution,
decision responsibility, history, closure, and reopening semantics.

Only context differs:

- **Production** concerns current operational reality and may create real
  organizational consequences through valid authority.
- **Simulation** uses declared assumptions and non-production consequences.
- **Learning** prioritizes coached practice and reflection while preserving the
  same professional reasoning standard.
- **Retrospective** reconstructs a past situation from bounded historical
  material and cannot present hindsight as information available at the time.

The context must remain visible and must qualify evidence, authority, consequence,
and permissible action. Simulated or retrospective material cannot silently
become production Evidence, execution truth, or organizational Knowledge.

Practice is not a demo, simplified lifecycle, separate UX, or lower-integrity
Matter. It is a full professional experience in which the manager can practice
attention, uncertainty management, judgment, intervention logic, monitoring,
reflection, and accountable closure. Learning derived from any Matter remains a
candidate until separately governed as Knowledge.

Before Practice or Retrospective implementation, four time meanings must remain
distinguishable: event time, observation time, retrospective reconstruction
time, and current interpretation time. Reconstructed or current knowledge may
not appear as knowledge available to the historical actor at the historical
decision point.

Learning and Simulation evidence admission must also satisfy these constraints:

- simulated evidence is explicitly marked and remains separate from production
  Evidence;
- provenance preserves the context class;
- facilitator-provided facts receive no production authority;
- promotion into production Evidence or governed Knowledge requires a separate,
  explicit admission under the owning canonical governance;
- reset or replay creates attributable attempt history and never rewrites an
  earlier attempt.

## 8. Experience Invariants

The following rules may never be violated:

1. Matter identity is stable from first report through closure and reopening.
2. The original report and its source context are never overwritten.
3. Context is carried forward; a navigation transition never silently resets the manager's
   work.
4. The manager can always determine why this Matter requires attention now.
5. Current focus never obscures the Matter's identity, accountable owner, or
   authoritative posture.
6. Observation, interpretation, assumption, Evidence, Decision, action, and
   Outcome never become indistinguishable.
7. Unknown, disputed, stale, unavailable, and inferred information is never
   represented as established fact.
8. Every accepted synthesis, Decision, Operational Transition, correction, Outcome, and
   closure is attributable.
9. A recommendation or prediction never impersonates an authorized human
   judgment.
10. No interface progression, task completion, or external event silently changes
    Matter state or authority.
11. History is append-only in meaning: later versions may supersede but never
    rewrite earlier accepted reality.
12. The manager can inspect the basis and contradiction set behind any material
    conclusion.
13. Returns and reversals preserve prior work and explain why the professional
    posture changed.
14. Source-owned execution and evidence remain source-owned.
15. Activity, completion, effect, Outcome, and stabilization remain distinct.
16. Closure records residual risk, uncertainty, responsibility, and explicit
    return conditions.
17. Reopening preserves identity and prior closure; it never creates a duplicate
    merely to represent recurrence.
18. Practice, simulation, retrospective, and production contexts never alter the
    canonical Matter lifecycle or shared experience contract and never conceal
    their evidentiary context.
19. Reduced cognitive load never removes material risk, dissent, provenance, or
    authority information.
20. After any movement, the manager can recover what changed and what requires
    attention next.

## 9. Acceptance Criteria

A future UX conforms to the Canonical Matter Experience only if every applicable
criterion below has the stated minimum observable evidence. “Partially” or
“implicitly” is not a pass. Results are `Pass`, `Fail`, or `Not Applicable`;
subjective scoring is forbidden. `Not Applicable` requires an explicit lifecycle
and authority reason.

### Matter orientation

- [ ] The manager can identify the Matter, its origin, accountable owner, and
  authoritative posture.
- [ ] The manager can explain why the Matter deserves attention now.
- [ ] The current professional intent and dominant operational object are clear
  without requiring knowledge of application structure.
- [ ] Related context remains available without competing equally with the
  current focus.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Matter identity, origin, current authority holder, and canonical state are
   distinguishable.
2. A source-backed reason, relevant change, pending judgment, risk, recurrence,
   or commitment is inspectable.
3. The intended professional act and one leading object can be identified
   without interpreting navigation structure.
4. Supporting context is reachable while only one object has primary operational
   emphasis.

### Epistemic integrity

- [ ] Reported observation, sourced Evidence, interpretation, assumption,
  contradiction, Decision, action, and Outcome are distinguishable.
- [ ] Unknown, disputed, stale, unavailable, inferred, and simulated material is
  explicitly qualified.
- [ ] Every material synthesis or conclusion has an inspectable basis and
  contradiction set.
- [ ] No summary, confidence expression, or automation conceals a material limit.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Representative applicable record classes retain visible semantic distinction.
2. Each applicable epistemic condition is marked where it could be mistaken for
   established fact.
3. A material conclusion traces to supporting material and its material
   contradiction set.
4. Inspection exposes basis, unknowns, freshness, conflict, and system limits
   where applicable.

### Judgment and authority

- [ ] The user can distinguish recommendation, proposal, approval, authoritative
  Decision, and execution status.
- [ ] Every consequential judgment has an identifiable author, authority, time,
  reason, and supporting basis.
- [ ] The experience permits correction, deferral, no action, return to
  investigation, and reconsideration without treating them as failure.
- [ ] No progression, completion, assistant output, or source event silently
  exercises human authority.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Each applicable authority condition is independently identifiable and does
   not inherit another condition through presentation.
2. The accepted record exposes author, authority, time, reason, and basis; the
   current authority holder is identifiable.
3. Each applicable correction or non-forward path preserves an attributable
   disposition and prior work.
4. Progression, completion, proposals, and source synchronization leave
   authoritative judgment unchanged until an authorized act occurs.

### Journey fitness

- [ ] The experience supports Observe, Assess, Investigate, Understand, Decide,
  Intervene, Monitor, Stabilize, and Close with the goal, dominant object,
  questions, decisions, and completion condition defined here.
- [ ] The journey supports proportionate omission, overlap, repetition, and
  non-forward movement without implying a mandatory wizard.
- [ ] No-action closure, execution-free Decision, adverse Outcome, inconclusive
  Outcome, reconsideration, interruption, and reopening remain intelligible.
- [ ] Monitoring evaluates observed effect against Decision intent rather than
  substituting activity or completion.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Each applicable professional posture can be performed without stage
   completion being treated as a Matter lifecycle transition.
2. A compressed or returning path preserves valid canonical states, authority,
   and orientation.
3. Each applicable exceptional path retains its basis, disposition, prior
   history, and next professional intent.
4. Intended condition, observed condition, evidence limits, and guardrails can
   be compared independently of task status.

### Continuity and recovery

- [ ] Every movement restores the applicable experience-level continuity
  requirements through the canonical Context Envelope.
- [ ] After a navigation transition or return, the manager can identify what changed, why,
  and what requires judgment next.
- [ ] Interruption and resumption preserve unfinished work and a meaningful return
  point.
- [ ] Reopening uses the same Matter identity and presents prior closure together
  with the new trigger.
- [ ] Source failure or degraded operation exposes the continuity limitation and
  never manufactures replacement truth.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Matter, origin, current intent, dominant object, relevant accepted context,
   changed condition, and return are recoverable where applicable.
2. What changed, why, and the next required judgment are recoverable without
   replaying the complete history.
3. Resumption restores prior professional intent, active Working Set reference,
   unresolved question, interruption reason, and return target.
4. Identity is unchanged and prior closure and the reopening trigger are jointly
   inspectable.
5. Source unavailability, last known time, freshness limit, and resulting
   uncertainty remain visible.

### Cognitive discipline

- [ ] One operational object dominates at a time.
- [ ] The manager is asked for the minimum number of simultaneous consequential
  judgments.
- [ ] Detail is disclosed according to relevance while complete inspectability is
  retained.
- [ ] History supports current work but does not dominate it by default.
- [ ] Any interruption of focus is justified by a more material concern and
  preserves return.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. One primary subject is identifiable and secondary subjects visibly support it.
2. Unrelated judgments are deferred; simultaneous exceptions expose their
   materiality reason.
3. Immediate basis is understandable while provenance, dissent, risk, and
   history remain inspectable.
4. Current concern leads and historical material is distinguishable from current
   truth.
5. The interrupting concern, priority reason, displaced Focus, unfinished work,
   and return condition are inspectable.

### Outcome, closure, and learning

- [ ] Completion, adoption, Outcome, stabilization, and closure are represented
  as distinct judgments.
- [ ] Closure states residual uncertainty, accepted risk, continuing
  responsibility, and reopening conditions.
- [ ] Successful, partial, adverse, unknown, and inconclusive dispositions are
  equally representable.
- [ ] Production, learning, simulation, and retrospective cases use the same
  Matter lifecycle and shared experience contract while their context and
  evidentiary consequences remain explicit.

Minimum observable evidence, in criterion order:

1. Changing completion, adoption, Outcome, stabilization, or closure does not
   silently change another judgment.
2. Closure exposes residual uncertainty, accepted risk, continuing
   responsibility, and return conditions where applicable.
3. Every applicable disposition can be recorded without coercion into success or
   stabilization.
4. Context class, evidence qualification, consequence, authority, and time
   boundary are explicit; simulated Evidence is visibly separated from
   production Evidence.

### Deterministic review rule

The answer to “Does this UX conform to the Canonical Matter Experience?” is:

- **Pass** only when every applicable criterion passes and no Experience Invariant
  is violated.
- **Fail** when any applicable criterion fails, any invariant is violated, or
  observable evidence for an applicable behavior is absent.
- **Not Applicable** only with an explicit lifecycle and authority reason.

Aesthetic quality, usability preference, or implementation completeness cannot
compensate for a failed invariant.

## Negative Conformance Scenarios

These scenarios are mandatory review evidence:

### Interruption

If a manager returns after interruption and cannot identify why the Matter was in
Focus, recover the current professional intent, or restore the active Working Set
without manual reconstruction, the result is `Fail`.

### Reopening

If a Closed Matter appears as a new Matter or loses its closure history when
reopened, the result is `Fail`.

### Adverse Outcome

If execution activity is complete but the expected condition is not achieved and
the experience presents success or stabilization, the result is `Fail`.

### Source degradation

If a source is unavailable but last-known information appears current and
confirmed without visible freshness and availability limits, the result is
`Fail`.

### Simulated Evidence leakage

If simulated Evidence from a Practice context can be understood as production
Evidence, the result is `Fail`.
