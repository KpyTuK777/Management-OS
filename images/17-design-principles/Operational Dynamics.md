# Operational Dynamics

## Purpose

Operational Dynamics defines how the Workbench lives.

It does not describe screens, components, styling, or implementation. It defines the behavioral model through which operational artifacts appear, gain meaning, attract attention, change, relate, remain remembered, and recede.

The Workbench is a shared thinking surface. Its dynamics must preserve the canonical laws:

1. One operational centre.
2. Quiet periphery.
3. Space communicates.
4. Light creates hierarchy.
5. Empty space is working capacity.
6. State remains visible.
7. Thinking stays fluid.
8. Detail never destroys context.
9. Material hierarchy over interface hierarchy.
10. The interface disappears.

The model rejects equal-weight cards, permanent grids, dashboard composition, decorative connector networks, sticky-note walls, crime-board metaphors, literal desk skeuomorphism, corporate meeting imagery, luxury-office symbolism, technology as the subject, and empty minimalism without evidence of reasoning.

## Core proposition

The Workbench is not a container of records.

It is the current, traceable state of an investigation.

Its life is produced by five continuous movements:

1. material enters;
2. attention selects;
3. inspection deepens;
4. interpretation changes;
5. memory preserves.

Every behavior must strengthen understanding of one current operational subject. Activity that does not change attention, evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, or memory does not belong to Operational Dynamics.

## Behavioral invariants

These conditions must remain true regardless of investigation size or interaction:

- There is one Current Situation.
- There is at most one primary focus.
- Detail never replaces the identity of the investigation.
- Every meaningful change has an origin, author, time, and reason.
- Source verification and claim confirmation are different.
- A proposal never impersonates an accepted interpretation.
- Rejection removes authority, not history.
- Relationships are interpretations with provenance, not decorative lines.
- New understanding extends or supersedes prior understanding; it does not silently rewrite it.
- Watson cannot accept its own proposals.
- Scaling changes what is immediately present, not what is remembered.
- The owner can always return to the same operational context.

# 1. Operational Artifact

## Definition

An Artifact is a durable unit of operational meaning with identity, provenance, content, history, and possible relationships.

It is not a card, block, file, or interface component. Those are possible representations.

An Artifact may represent:

- an observation;
- a symptom;
- a conversation;
- a claim;
- a question;
- a hypothesis;
- an assumption;
- a decision;
- a contradiction;
- a metric or measured change;
- a report, file, image, recording, or link;
- an accepted relationship;
- an operational outcome;
- a prior formulation of understanding.

An Artifact remains the same operational object when its representation changes. Editing wording creates a revision of its expression, not a new identity, unless the meaning itself is split.

## Artifact anatomy

Every Artifact has:

- **Identity** — what remains stable across revisions.
- **Type** — the role it currently plays in reasoning.
- **Content** — its present formulation or material.
- **Provenance** — who or what introduced it, when, and from where.
- **Lineage** — previous formulations, splits, merges, and derivations.
- **Epistemic state** — what is known about its reliability.
- **Operational state** — how it currently participates in the investigation.
- **Attention state** — whether it is peripheral, supporting, primary, or inspected.
- **Relationships** — explicit interpretations connecting it to other Artifacts.
- **History** — consequential events that changed its meaning or role.

No single status may collapse these dimensions.

## Birth

An Artifact is born when material obtains stable identity inside the investigation.

Before birth, material may exist as:

- raw owner contribution;
- imported source;
- Watson extraction;
- proposed relationship;
- unsaved working thought.

Birth occurs through one of three paths:

1. **Owner-authored** — the owner intentionally adds or accepts material.
2. **Source-derived** — an authorized source produces identifiable material with provenance.
3. **Proposed** — Watson or another process extracts a candidate that remains visibly proposed until owner acceptance or an approved automatic rule admits it.

Raw input must be preserved when interpretation creates derived Artifacts.

## Lifecycle

The general lifecycle is:

1. **Captured** — material exists with provenance but may not yet be interpreted.
2. **Proposed** — a possible type, meaning, or relationship has been suggested.
3. **Admitted** — the Artifact belongs to the investigation.
4. **Active** — it contributes to the current understanding or an open line of inquiry.
5. **Evaluated** — its reliability, relevance, or relationship has been examined.
6. **Settled** — its current operational role no longer requires active work.
7. **Archived** — it leaves the active working set but remains retrievable and historically connected.

Settled Artifacts may be:

- confirmed within a stated scope;
- rejected with a reason;
- superseded by a later formulation;
- resolved as a question;
- absorbed into Current Situation;
- retained as contextual memory.

Settled does not mean deleted.

## Importance

Importance is not a permanent decoration.

An Artifact becomes important when it materially affects:

- Current Situation;
- a consequential uncertainty;
- a leading hypothesis;
- a contradiction;
- a decision boundary;
- authority or authorization;
- a source failure;
- the next necessary judgment.

Importance may rise or fall as the investigation changes. A previously peripheral Artifact may become central after new evidence. A once-critical Artifact may recede after resolution while retaining its historical significance.

## Completion

An Artifact does not “complete” merely because it was viewed.

Its active work concludes when:

- its question is resolved;
- its claim receives an explicit scoped judgment;
- its contradiction is explained or accepted as residual uncertainty;
- its hypothesis is rejected, superseded, or incorporated into accepted understanding;
- its evidence has contributed all currently relevant meaning;
- it no longer affects the current investigation.

Deletion is reserved for duplicates, accidental capture, unauthorized material, or content that should not exist. Ordinary rejection, irrelevance, or obsolescence leads to historical retention, not erasure.

# 2. Focus

## Definition

Focus is the current allocation of operational attention.

Focus is not selection alone. It changes which subject governs interpretation, which context becomes supporting, and which questions or actions are relevant.

## Focus structure

The Workbench supports:

- **One primary focus** — the Artifact, relationship, contradiction, or Current Situation currently governing attention.
- **A bounded supporting context** — Artifacts directly needed to understand the primary focus.
- **A quiet periphery** — relevant material that remains available without demanding attention.

There cannot be two equal primary focuses. Comparison still has one primary focus: the comparison itself, with its subjects in supporting roles.

## Active

Active means that an Artifact participates in the live investigation.

It may affect Current Situation, an open question, a hypothesis, a contradiction, or the next judgment without being the primary focus.

Many Artifacts may be active.

## Focused

Focused means the Artifact currently organizes attention.

Only one subject may be primarily focused at a time.

## Inspected

Inspected means the owner has temporarily deepened attention into one Artifact while preserving its relationship to the investigation.

Inspection is a mode of focus, not a new destination.

## Secondary focus

There are no equal secondary focuses.

There is supporting context with explicit reasons for inclusion:

- supports;
- contradicts;
- precedes;
- derives from;
- is compared with;
- shares a source;
- affects the same uncertainty.

Calling these relationships “secondary focus” must not give them independent visual or behavioral gravity.

## Change of attention

When focus changes, the system must preserve:

- the previous focus;
- the reason for transition;
- the shared investigation center;
- the owner’s return path;
- the spatial neighborhood before the transition.

A focus change should feel like turning attention across one table, not leaving one page for another.

# 3. Inspection

## Definition

Inspection is a reversible deepening of attention into an Artifact.

Its purpose is to understand content, provenance, history, state, relationships, and implications without destroying the surrounding investigation.

## What remains present

During Inspection, the following context must remain logically present:

- Matter identity;
- Current Situation;
- identity and type of the inspected Artifact;
- its relationship to the primary investigation;
- the immediate supporting neighborhood;
- unresolved contradictions affecting it;
- the origin from which Inspection began;
- the means of returning.

Not all context must remain equally prominent, but none may become ambiguous.

## What may temporarily change

Inspection may:

- reduce the prominence of unrelated peripheral material;
- expand provenance, history, content, or attached sources;
- reveal alternate formulations;
- surface relationship semantics;
- expose available judgments;
- make comparison subjects temporarily co-present.

Inspection may not:

- replace the Workbench with an unrelated destination;
- silently change primary interpretation;
- move or reorder unrelated Artifacts;
- lose prior position, scale, or neighborhood;
- convert viewing into acceptance;
- hide consequential uncertainty.

## Entering Inspection

Inspection begins from an identifiable origin. The transition records:

- inspected Artifact;
- previous focus;
- spatial context;
- active filters or scope;
- open collection or line of inquiry.

## Leaving Inspection

Leaving Inspection restores the prior operational context unless the owner explicitly chooses a new focus.

If Inspection changed the investigation, restoration must make the change perceptible:

- what changed;
- which Artifact changed;
- whether Current Situation was affected;
- what remains unresolved.

Return is not merely closing detail. It is re-entry into an updated shared understanding.

# 4. Context Preservation

## Principle

After any action, the owner must immediately understand:

- where they are;
- what they opened;
- what changed;
- how to return.

Context is not a breadcrumb or navigation feature. It is continuity of operational meaning.

## Context envelope

Every meaningful interaction carries a Context Envelope:

- Matter;
- Current Situation version;
- primary focus;
- supporting neighborhood;
- spatial location;
- active scope;
- entry origin;
- unresolved work;
- last consequential change.

The Context Envelope follows inspection, comparison, collection exploration, Watson proposals, source review, and history review.

## Preservation rules

- New detail appears relative to a known subject.
- Temporary focus does not erase previous focus.
- Closing a temporary activity restores the previous neighborhood.
- Accepted changes return the owner to context with the change visible.
- Rejected or deferred proposals leave the original context intact.
- Refresh or re-entry restores the last meaningful working context, not merely the last opened screen.
- The system does not move the owner because background processing completed.

## Failure condition

Context is lost when the owner must reconstruct where they were, re-find the Artifact, remember why it mattered, or infer whether anything changed.

Any behavior producing that condition violates the Workbench.

# 5. State Visibility

## Principle

State must be inferable from behavior, language, provenance, available judgments, and relationship to Current Situation—not from color alone.

State visibility answers: what is this, what has happened to it, how much authority does it have, and what may happen next?

## Orthogonal state dimensions

### Review state

- **New** — admitted but not yet acknowledged by the owner.
- **Reviewed** — seen and understood enough to leave the new-material queue.

Review does not imply verification.

### Provenance state

- **Unattributed** — origin is not yet known.
- **Attributed** — a source or author is identified.
- **Verified source** — identity and integrity of the source have been checked.
- **Source challenged** — origin, integrity, or authorization is disputed.

A verified source may still contain a false claim.

### Epistemic state

- **Unexamined** — no reliability judgment has been made.
- **Proposed** — meaning or interpretation awaits owner governance.
- **Supported** — relevant evidence increases confidence.
- **Challenged** — evidence or reasoning materially weakens confidence.
- **Contradictory** — it conflicts with another material claim or observation.
- **Confirmed within scope** — sufficient approved evidence supports a precise claim under stated boundaries.
- **Rejected** — a claim or interpretation is not accepted, with reason preserved.
- **Unresolved** — available material cannot yet support a judgment.

### Operational participation

- **Active** — affects the current investigation.
- **Peripheral** — relevant but not currently governing work.
- **Settled** — no active judgment remains.
- **Archived** — outside the active surface but retained in memory.

### Attention state

- **Peripheral**
- **Supporting**
- **Primary focus**
- **Inspected**

## Logical visibility by state

- **New material** remains distinguishable until acknowledged; it cannot silently blend into reviewed material.
- **Reviewed material** loses novelty, not provenance or uncertainty.
- **Verified material** exposes what was verified: source, calculation, observation, or claim.
- **Confirmed material** names its scope and supporting basis.
- **Contradictory material** remains co-present with the conflict and does not collapse into a generic warning.
- **Rejected material** retains wording, reason, author of judgment, time, and affected relationships.
- **Active material** participates in Current Situation or open work.
- **Archived material** remains retrievable through lineage, source, relationship, time, or prior focus.

No state change may be inferred solely from disappearance.

# 6. Hypothesis Evolution

## Definition

A Hypothesis is a testable explanatory Artifact.

It is neither a guess label nor a provisional fact. It lives through explicit questions, evidence, challenges, comparisons, and judgments.

## Lifecycle

1. **Emergent** — an explanatory possibility is noticed.
2. **Formulated** — the claim becomes precise enough to challenge.
3. **Proposed** — it enters the investigation without authority.
4. **Testable** — required observations and possible disconfirming evidence are known.
5. **Under examination** — evidence and alternatives are being compared.
6. **Supported** — evidence increases plausibility without proving causality.
7. **Challenged** — contradiction, missing mechanism, or alternate explanation weakens it.
8. **Provisionally accepted** — it is the best current explanation within explicit limits.
9. **Rejected** — evidence or reasoning makes it unsuitable.
10. **Superseded** — a more precise explanation replaces its operational role.
11. **Incorporated** — a separately confirmed claim derived from it becomes part of Current Situation.

## Strengthening

A Hypothesis becomes stronger through:

- independent supporting evidence;
- verified provenance;
- successful predictions or discriminating tests;
- an explicit plausible mechanism;
- survival against serious alternatives;
- resolution of contradictions;
- consistency across relevant scopes.

Repetition of the same source does not create independent support.

## Weakening

A Hypothesis becomes weaker through:

- disconfirming evidence;
- source failure;
- contradiction with established observations;
- inability to distinguish itself from alternatives;
- expansion beyond the scope of its support;
- evidence that the proposed mechanism cannot operate.

## Becoming fact

A Hypothesis does not transform itself into a fact.

When a claim becomes sufficiently confirmed, the system creates or recognizes a confirmed operational understanding with:

- explicit wording;
- scope;
- evidence basis;
- residual uncertainty;
- lineage back to the Hypothesis.

The original Hypothesis remains in memory as part of the reasoning history.

## Rejection

Rejected Hypotheses remain available because they explain:

- what was considered;
- why it was plausible;
- what evidence rejected it;
- which future conditions might justify reconsideration.

Rejection reduces current authority. It does not erase learning.

# 7. Operational Memory

## Definition

Operational Memory is the durable continuity of what the Workbench learned, changed, considered, and rejected.

It is not a log of every interaction. It preserves consequential reasoning.

## What must be remembered

- original owner contributions;
- source provenance and authorization;
- Artifact birth and lineage;
- material revisions;
- splits and merges;
- accepted and rejected relationships;
- Hypothesis evolution;
- contradictions and their resolution;
- previous versions of Current Situation;
- judgments and their reasons;
- Watson proposals that materially affected reasoning;
- rejected alternatives;
- evidence that changed importance;
- transitions between investigation phases;
- residual uncertainty;
- the last meaningful working context.

## Relationship history

Relationships have their own lifecycle:

1. proposed;
2. accepted;
3. challenged;
4. revised;
5. rejected;
6. superseded;
7. archived.

Removing a relationship from the active map does not erase that it once influenced reasoning.

## Revision history

History must answer:

- what changed;
- from what;
- to what;
- by whom or what;
- when;
- why;
- what downstream understanding was affected.

History should emphasize turning points, not generate noise from inconsequential edits.

## Memory and present work

Memory remains quiet until relevant.

It returns when:

- the same source reappears;
- a rejected explanation becomes plausible again;
- a new contradiction touches a settled judgment;
- the current pattern resembles a prior Matter;
- the owner asks how understanding changed;
- provenance or authority is questioned.

Memory supports present reasoning. It does not compete with it.

# 8. Watson in Operational Dynamics

## Role

Watson is a participant in the dynamics, not the director of them.

It may observe, organize, propose, compare, warn, and wait. It may not govern owner attention, accept its own interpretation, or force conversation before work continues.

## Behavioral modes

### Silent observation

Watson remains silent when:

- new material can be organized without ambiguity or risk;
- the owner is actively contributing or inspecting;
- its thought would not materially change understanding;
- a proposal already awaits owner judgment;
- the owner deferred assistance;
- available evidence is insufficient for a useful intervention.

Silence is the default.

### Quiet organization

Without interrupting, Watson may:

- decompose natural contribution;
- identify possible Artifact types;
- link provenance;
- detect dates, entities, processes, or metrics;
- update candidate clusters;
- prepare proposed relationships;
- notice possible contradictions.

Organization remains proposed where interpretation is involved.

### Proposal

Watson proposes when it can state:

- what it noticed;
- why it may matter;
- what evidence supports the proposal;
- what remains uncertain;
- what owner judgment is requested.

After proposing, Watson waits.

It does not repeat, escalate for attention, or block unrelated contribution.

### Explicit assistance

When invited, Watson may:

- compare Artifacts or Hypotheses;
- find contradictions;
- identify missing evidence;
- trace provenance;
- summarize current understanding;
- explain why a relationship is proposed;
- identify what changed;
- suggest a useful test.

The invitation defines scope. Watson should not broaden it silently.

### Risk interruption

Watson may interrupt without invitation only for:

- material operational risk;
- serious contradiction affecting a consequential judgment;
- authority or authorization conflict;
- likely source failure;
- a decision conflicting with strong evidence;
- irreversible action based on unresolved provenance.

An interruption must be concise, specific, and dismissible. It preserves the owner’s current context and never seizes primary focus permanently.

### Withdrawal

Watson recedes when:

- its proposal is accepted, rejected, corrected, or deferred;
- the owner resumes contribution;
- the risk is acknowledged;
- its assistance no longer changes the next judgment.

It does not remain visible to prove that assistance occurred.

## Watson memory

Watson remembers consequential proposals and corrections so it can avoid repeating misunderstandings.

It does not treat rejected proposals as hidden truth, and it does not use its own prior output as independent evidence.

# 9. Scaling

## Principle

The active Workbench remains cognitively bounded even when Operational Memory becomes large.

Scaling is achieved through relevance, clustering, scope, lineage, and progressive presence—not by shrinking every Artifact, building a permanent grid, or showing everything at once.

## Working set

At any scale, the shared surface contains a Working Set:

- Current Situation;
- primary focus;
- supporting neighborhood;
- active contradictions;
- leading Hypotheses;
- newly relevant material;
- current questions and judgments.

The Working Set is not a fixed number. It is bounded by coherent reasoning.

## At approximately 10 Artifacts

- Artifacts may remain individually present.
- Relationships can be read directly.
- The full active investigation may fit within one spatial neighborhood.

## At approximately 50 Artifacts

- Natural clusters and lines of inquiry become necessary.
- Peripheral material recedes while remaining spatially anchored.
- The active neighborhood remains individually understandable.
- Collections support access without replacing the shared surface.

## At approximately 100 Artifacts

- The Workbench distinguishes the active model from the broader case corpus.
- Only relevant neighborhoods expand.
- Repeated sources, derived material, and settled lines compress into meaningful summaries without losing members.
- Navigation moves among operational neighborhoods, not pages.

## At approximately 300 Artifacts

- Most material belongs to Operational Memory rather than the immediate surface.
- The current map represents live understanding, open contradictions, important lineage, and retrieval paths.
- Source, time, relationship, uncertainty, and phase become essential retrieval dimensions.
- Changes in relevance can return archived material to the Working Set.

## At approximately 500 Artifacts and beyond

- The Workbench does not attempt total simultaneous visibility.
- Scale is handled by bounded context, semantic neighborhoods, history, and deliberate retrieval.
- The active surface remains calm.
- The owner can still trace any present conclusion to its underlying Artifacts.
- No summary may sever provenance or hide material contradiction.

## Scaling failures

The system has failed if scale produces:

- equal-weight miniature cards;
- a dense permanent grid;
- decorative connector noise;
- hidden provenance;
- untraceable summaries;
- modal fragmentation;
- search as the only way to recover context;
- archival disappearance;
- reduced text or targets below readable, usable thresholds.

# 10. Navigation Within the Workbench

## Definition

Navigation is movement of attention through one operational space.

It is not movement among application pages.

## Navigable dimensions

The owner may move:

- from Current Situation to supporting Artifact;
- from Artifact to source;
- from claim to evidence;
- from Hypothesis to challenge;
- from contradiction to affected understanding;
- from present interpretation to history;
- from summary to underlying material;
- from one operational neighborhood to another;
- from active work to relevant memory and back.

## Navigation contract

Every movement has:

- an origin;
- a destination;
- a reason;
- a preserved Context Envelope;
- a return path.

Movement should preserve the identity of the Matter and Current Situation.

## Spatial continuity

An Artifact’s location may change when its operational role changes, but not arbitrarily.

Movement must be attributable to:

- owner arrangement;
- accepted relationship;
- change of importance;
- entry into or departure from the Working Set;
- explicit regrouping;
- a changed Current Situation.

The system must not constantly rearrange the surface in response to background inference.

## Navigation and history

Moving into history temporarily changes temporal scope, not Matter identity.

Returning restores the present while making any comparison or insight available as supporting context.

## Navigation and focus

Navigation may change focus, inspect focus, or reveal supporting context.

It must not create competing centers. At every point the owner can answer:

- What investigation am I in?
- What is the primary subject?
- Why am I seeing this?
- How does it relate to Current Situation?
- How do I return?

# Relationship Dynamics

Relationships are operational interpretations connecting Artifacts.

Every Relationship has:

- source Artifact;
- target Artifact;
- semantic type;
- direction where meaningful;
- provenance;
- proposer;
- confidence or uncertainty;
- lifecycle state;
- history.

Valid semantic types include:

- supports;
- contradicts;
- influences;
- may influence;
- depends on;
- mutually depends on;
- derives from;
- precedes;
- follows;
- is part of;
- supersedes;
- explains;
- is evidence for.

Proximity alone does not create a Relationship. Temporal order alone does not create causality. Watson proposals remain proposed until accepted.

---

# Current Situation Dynamics

Current Situation is the authoritative working synthesis of the investigation.

It is not a static title and not an automatic summary.

It changes only when admitted material materially changes:

- what is happening;
- scope or period;
- the most important uncertainty;
- the leading operational explanation;
- a critical contradiction;
- readiness for judgment.

Every revision preserves:

- prior formulation;
- material that caused the change;
- author or approving authority;
- time;
- residual uncertainty.

Current Situation may be wrong. Its authority means it is the accepted current synthesis, not absolute truth.

---

# Event Model

Operational Dynamics is driven by consequential events:

- Artifact captured;
- Artifact admitted;
- Artifact revised;
- Artifact split or merged;
- source attributed or verified;
- focus changed;
- Inspection opened or closed;
- Relationship proposed, accepted, challenged, or rejected;
- Hypothesis strengthened, challenged, rejected, or incorporated;
- contradiction detected or resolved;
- Current Situation revised;
- owner judgment recorded;
- material archived or reactivated;
- Watson proposal corrected;
- Context Envelope restored.

Events produce state transitions. They do not directly dictate visual implementation.

Every consequential event must be traceable. Routine cursor movement, scrolling, or incidental opening need not become Operational Memory unless it changes reasoning.

---

# Behavioral acceptance tests

The model succeeds when:

- a new Artifact can enter without forcing classification or conversation;
- the owner can distinguish source verification from claim confirmation;
- one primary focus remains clear during comparison and Inspection;
- opening detail preserves the investigation center and return path;
- closing detail restores spatial context;
- changes remain visible without relying on color;
- rejected material remains historically traceable;
- a Hypothesis can evolve without being silently converted into fact;
- Watson can propose, wait, withdraw, and interrupt only under defined risk;
- the active Workbench remains calm with hundreds of remembered Artifacts;
- navigation preserves Matter, Current Situation, origin, and return;
- every present conclusion can be traced to evidence and reasoning history.

The model fails when:

- viewing becomes approval;
- disappearance is the only state signal;
- detail replaces context;
- rejected reasoning is erased;
- background inference rearranges attention;
- Watson becomes a mandatory conversation;
- scale creates a dashboard or grid;
- summaries sever provenance;
- multiple elements compete as equal centers;
- technology becomes more visible than the investigation.

---

# Final principle

The Workbench is alive when understanding can change without orientation being lost.

It thinks by preserving the relationship between present attention, accumulated evidence, evolving interpretation, and remembered history.

It grows by moving material between active presence and quiet memory.

It remains calm because only the current operational truth claims attention.

The owner drives the investigation.

Artifacts carry the evidence.

Relationships carry interpretation.

Memory carries continuity.

Watson carries proposals and warnings.

Current Situation carries the accepted present understanding.

Everything else should disappear.
