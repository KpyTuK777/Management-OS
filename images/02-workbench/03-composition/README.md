# Workbench Composition

## Purpose

Define hierarchy, balance, rhythm, visual gravity, and negative space across the Workbench.

## Questions answered

- What is dominant?
- What may remain peripheral?
- How does the eye move through the situation?
- Where should asymmetry create useful tension?

## Supports

Immediate orientation, meaningful priority, and quiet visual rhythm.

## Belongs here

Editorial layouts, spatial arrangements, focal compositions, and annotated hierarchy studies.

## Avoid

Template grids, symmetrical card matrices, equal visual weight, arbitrary overlap, and unused margins that steal working space.

## Accepted references

### [`workbench-composition-central-focus-01.jpg`](workbench-composition-central-focus-01.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This reference demonstrates how a workspace should organize attention around one primary operational subject supported by contextual tools. Its greatest value is the clarity of its visual hierarchy.

#### What it teaches

- One dominant focal point.
- Supporting objects should reinforce the primary subject.
- Visual hierarchy should be immediately understandable.
- The user’s eye should naturally return to the central investigation.
- Calm spacing improves comprehension.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Current Situation as the strongest visual anchor.
- Evidence, symptoms, hypotheses, and conversations orbiting the central subject.
- Auxiliary interface elements supporting understanding without competing for attention.
- Visual hierarchy that is obvious at first glance.

#### What should not be copied

- Architectural drawings.
- Technical drafting tools.
- Literal object arrangement.
- White-tabletop aesthetics.

Management OS should adopt the compositional hierarchy rather than the photographed materials.

#### Canonical candidacy

### [`workbench-composition-central-focus-04.jpg`](workbench-composition-central-focus-04.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This reference illustrates the concept of a shared thinking surface where operational reasoning unfolds around one central workspace rather than around interface controls.

#### What it teaches

- The Workbench should feel like one continuous thinking surface.
- Cards should appear as artifacts placed on a shared workspace, not isolated UI widgets.
- Supporting tools should surround the workspace instead of interrupting it.
- White space is essential: it creates clarity and makes every artifact feel intentional.
- The user’s attention should naturally return to the center of the investigation.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Design the Workbench as a single operational surface.
- Preserve generous spacing between evidence artifacts.
- Keep navigation and utilities visually subordinate to the investigation area.
- Make every panel reinforce the feeling of working around one evolving operational model.

#### What should not be copied

- The Business Model Canvas layout.
- Workshop materials.
- The round meeting table.
- Physical markers or a paper template.

Only the spatial composition and collaborative-thinking principles should influence the interface.

### [`workbench-composition-central-focus-05.jpg`](workbench-composition-central-focus-05.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This reference captures the emotional state of deep operational investigation. The user is immersed in evidence rather than interacting with software.

#### What it teaches

- The Workbench should encourage investigation, not data entry.
- Evidence should occupy the visual center of the workspace.
- The interface should feel calm, deliberate, and focused.
- Local warm lighting and restrained contrast reinforce concentration.
- Users should feel they are gradually uncovering understanding rather than completing forms.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Evidence cards dominating the workspace.
- Interaction encouraging review, connection, and reflection on artifacts.
- Visual hierarchy communicating that understanding emerges from evidence.
- A calm, distraction-free atmosphere.

#### What should not be copied

- Detective or crime aesthetics.
- Character imagery.
- Physical printed documents.
- Narrative props.

Only the investigative mindset and evidence-centered composition should influence the interface.
