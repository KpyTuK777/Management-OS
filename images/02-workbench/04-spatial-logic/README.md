# Workbench Spatial Logic

## Purpose

Define how operational meaning becomes position, proximity, grouping, and relationship.

## Questions answered

- Why is one item near another?
- Which relationships deserve connectors?
- How is stable spatial context preserved?
- How should a map grow without becoming cluttered?

## Supports

Spatial memory, causal discipline, comparison, and progressive understanding.

## Belongs here

Knowledge maps, evidence walls, systems diagrams, and spatial-navigation studies with explicit semantics.

## Avoid

Decorative connectors, automatic hub-and-spoke maps, accidental proximity, forced causality, and movement that destroys orientation.

## Accepted references

### [`workbench-spatial-logic-01.jpg`](workbench-spatial-logic-01.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This reference demonstrates how a large shared workspace supports simultaneous interaction with multiple operational artifacts without becoming visually crowded.

#### What it teaches

- The workspace should remain larger than the content placed upon it.
- Multiple artifacts can coexist without competing for attention.
- Spatial organization is more important than decorative styling.
- Empty space is an active design element that improves clarity.
- The Workbench should feel like a shared operational surface rather than isolated panels.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Preserve generous spacing around evidence cards.
- Avoid filling every available area with interface elements.
- Keep multiple operational artifacts visible at the same time.
- Let users build understanding by moving attention across a coherent workspace rather than through modal dialogs.

#### What should not be copied

- Workshop facilitation.
- Corporate-meeting aesthetics.
- Charts and presentation materials.
- Bright-office styling.

Only the spatial-organization principles should influence the interface.

### [`workbench-spatial-logic-03.jpg`](workbench-spatial-logic-03.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This is a strong reference for the spatial organization and interaction patterns of a Thinking Table. Its value lies in how the working surface supports active analysis rather than computer interaction.

#### What it teaches

- A large uninterrupted working surface preserves reasoning capacity.
- One central artifact dominates the workspace.
- Tools remain at the perimeter.
- A standing posture can encourage active reasoning.
- The workspace is organized for analysis rather than device use.

#### What should influence Management OS

- A dominant central operational artifact.
- Peripheral placement of tools and controls.
- A continuous surface large enough for evidence to expand.
- Interaction that encourages active review, comparison, and connection.

#### What should not be copied

- Architectural subject matter.
- Drafting-studio aesthetics.
- Specific physical tools.
- Literal standing-desk behavior.

Only the spatial logic and interaction pattern should influence Management OS.

### [`workbench-spatial-logic-04.jpg`](workbench-spatial-logic-04.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted

#### Purpose

This reference demonstrates how a workspace supports inspection and decision-making around tangible artifacts. Its value lies in the relationship among focused observation, active verification, and iterative refinement.

#### What it teaches

- A physical model can become the center of attention.
- Natural light can participate in the reasoning process.
- A visually quiet workspace reduces distraction.
- Standing interaction reinforces active thinking.
- The environment supports observation, verification, and refinement rather than passive computer work.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Selected artifacts becoming the clear center of inspection.
- Tools and surrounding context remaining visually quiet.
- Interaction that supports close observation, comparison, and verification.
- Decisions emerging through refinement of the operational artifact.

#### What should not be copied

- The physical model itself.
- Architectural-studio subject matter.
- Literal daylight simulation.
- A prescribed standing posture.

Only the artifact-inspection and decision-making principles should influence Management OS.

### [`workbench-spatial-logic-05.jpg`](workbench-spatial-logic-05.jpg)

**Status:** Accepted with limited scope

#### Purpose

This reference demonstrates collaborative work around a shared surface. Its value is limited to group posture, convergent attention, and interaction around common material.

#### What it teaches

- Multiple participants can focus on one shared workspace.
- Discussion remains centered around common documents.
- Standing and leaning interactions can support active collaboration.

#### What should influence Management OS

- Shared attention around the current operational artifact.
- Collaborative contributions that remain attached to common context.
- Interaction patterns that support active review and discussion.

#### Limitations

- The content represents business reporting and financial analysis rather than system design or deep reasoning.
- The displayed documents must not define Management OS artifacts.
- Reporting-dashboard logic must not enter the Workbench through this reference.

Use this image only for collaborative posture and group interaction—not for the nature, appearance, or organization of the artifacts displayed.
