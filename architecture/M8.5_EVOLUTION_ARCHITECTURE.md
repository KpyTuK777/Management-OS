## 1. Purpose

This document defines the implementation-independent architecture by which
Management OS may evolve from its reviewed baseline toward a coherent
Operational Intelligence Platform. It establishes how possible architectural
change is identified, bounded, related to dependencies, compared with
alternatives, and tested for coherence while canonical authority, semantic
ownership, human authority, and source ownership remain preserved.

This document does not select a target state, a delivery route, or work. It does
not make a future capability current, resolve an assessed Gap, accept or treat
an assessed Risk, or adopt an advisory Recommendation. It defines the
architectural rules within which separately authorized future change could be
considered.

## 2. Authority and Controlling Baseline

M8.5 is governed by the canonical M7 Governance Architecture and derives its
product-architecture baseline from:

- the Architecture Approved M8 Architecture Vision;
- canonical M8.1 Architectural Identity and Baseline Mapping;
- canonical M8.2 Capability and Ownership Coherence;
- canonical M8.3 Information, Evidence, and Handoff Architecture;
- canonical M8.4 Gap and Risk Assessment; and
- the scope-bounded M8.5 Evolution Architecture Order.

The controlling current architecture is M8.1–M8.3. M8.4 supplies an assessment
of that architecture and its source corpus; it does not itself authorize a
correction, treatment, or change. This document neither amends those sources
nor changes their authority conditions.

The M8 Architecture Vision's phrase **Capability Evolution Portfolio** denotes
a reviewable set of architectural possibilities. In M8.5 it does not mean a
funded portfolio, roadmap, priority list, delivery plan, backlog, or commitment.

## 3. Architectural Boundary

### 3.1 Product boundary

Management OS remains the management and operational-intelligence product
defined by M8.1. It is distinct from the real Operational System, external
systems of record, human authority, and technical realization. Evolution may
change the product architecture only through separately governed authority; it
cannot absorb those external realities merely because it represents, supports,
or reasons about them.

Product Purpose and System Identity remain architectural-object-level framing
constraints. Their M8.1 mapping to P1 and P2 remains a lookup aid and does not
make them ordinary capability responsibilities or locally evolvable ownership.

### 3.2 Layer and plane boundary

The six enduring M8.1 product responsibility layers remain responsibility
views, not implementation tiers, workflow steps, deployment locations, or an
authority hierarchy. Future capability growth may be situated against one
primary responsibility layer for coherence analysis, but that placement does
not transfer semantic ownership or prescribe realization.

The cross-cutting Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety, and Quality
plane remains distinct from the product layers. It constrains every evolution
subject without becoming a product capability or a universal semantic owner.

Canonical M7 Governance remains an external Governance and Evolution
constraint plane. It governs authorization, review, authority transition, and
canonical change; it is not a product layer, product capability, or internal
product mechanism.

## 4. Evolution Vocabulary and Non-Equivalence

| Term | Architectural meaning | What it does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current architecture | The canonical M8.1–M8.3 product meaning and canonical M8.4 assessment controlling M8.5 | A provisional baseline open to silent reinterpretation |
| Future architectural direction | A bounded description of a coherent kind of possible growth | A selected target, promise, priority, or authorization |
| Architectural option | One reviewable alternative for satisfying an identified architectural need or direction | A decision, recommendation adoption, or backlog item |
| Dependency | A meaning or authority relationship in which one architectural subject relies on another | A delivery schedule, runtime call, or organizational dependency |
| Prerequisite | A condition that must hold before a dependent architectural proposition can be coherent or eligible for authority | A task, date, implementation step, or automatic permission |
| Architectural increment | A bounded, internally coherent proposed change in architectural meaning that can be reviewed without requiring unrelated change | A release, sprint, work package, deployment, or delivery sequence |
| Recommendation | An advisory M8.4 or later assessment object proposing consideration | A decision, approval, treatment, or authorized work |
| Implementation | A technical realization of authorized architecture | An M8.5 subject or architectural conclusion |
| Product planning | Selection of outcomes, priorities, timing, investment, and delivery commitments | Architecture ownership or authority created by this document |

These terms are not promotion states. In particular, a direction does not
become an option merely through elaboration; an option does not become a
recommendation through comparison; a recommendation does not become a
decision through repetition; and an architectural increment does not become
authorized work through inclusion in an evolution route.

## 5. Evolution Objectives

M8.5 establishes the architecture needed to:

1. preserve one coherent Management OS identity while future capabilities are
   considered;
2. permit bounded growth without duplicating or silently transferring semantic
   ownership;
3. expose the architectural dependencies and prerequisites that constrain
   possible change;
4. allow alternative evolution routes to be compared without selecting or
   authorizing one;
5. retain provenance, uncertainty, traceability, and human authority across
   architectural change;
6. keep source authority explicit and prevent architectural synthesis from
   upgrading a source;
7. make fragmentation, semantic conflict, and hidden coupling reviewable before
   any authority transition; and
8. provide durable coherence criteria independent of technical realization.

## 6. Architectural Evolution Principles

### EP-01 — Baseline before change

Every evolution subject starts from an attributable current baseline. Proposed
meaning must be stated as a delta from that baseline, not as if it were already
part of the product.

### EP-02 — Responsibility before form

Evolution is described as a change in architectural responsibility, meaning,
constraint, or composition. Technical form cannot define the architectural
need.

### EP-03 — One primary semantic owner

Every current or proposed responsibility must have exactly one identifiable
primary semantic owner within its scope. Cooperation, orchestration,
presentation, automation, and composition never transfer ownership.

### EP-04 — Authority before normative dependence

A dependent proposition cannot treat a future option as normative until the
option has independently acquired the necessary authority. Description creates
no authority.

### EP-05 — Evidence before architectural claim

The reason for considering evolution must remain traceable to
repository-visible evidence or to an explicitly bounded architectural premise.
Inference cannot silently promote evidence or erase uncertainty.

### EP-06 — Preservation by default

Canonical meaning, source ownership, source authority, human authority, and
external governance remain unchanged unless separately authorized to change.

### EP-07 — Alternatives remain alternatives

Plausible routes may coexist. Comparison records consequences and constraints;
it does not imply ranking, selection, priority, or commitment.

### EP-08 — Residual exposure remains visible

Evolution reasoning must show which M8.4 conditions remain unaffected or
uncertain. A proposed architecture cannot claim resolution, acceptance, or
treatment merely by naming a relationship to a Gap or Risk.

### EP-09 — Smallest coherent architectural delta

An increment is bounded at the smallest scope that preserves identity,
ownership, dependencies, handoffs, and cross-cutting constraints. Smallness
does not justify an incoherent or authority-incomplete change.

### EP-10 — Implementation independence

Architectural validity cannot depend on a particular runtime, interface, data
representation, service boundary, user experience, infrastructure, or delivery
method.

## 7. Capability-Evolution Model

### 7.1 Evolution subject

An **evolution subject** is a bounded architectural proposition concerning a
current capability, a possible future capability, a composition relationship,
or a cross-cutting constraint. It is descriptive until separately reviewed and
authorized.

Every evolution subject must identify:

| Element | Required architectural statement |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | The canonical current meaning from which the subject differs |
| Need | The evidenced architectural condition that makes consideration legitimate |
| Scope | The responsibilities and boundaries potentially affected |
| Primary semantic owner | The existing owner, or the unresolved ownership question for genuinely new meaning |
| Preserved non-ownership | Meanings the subject must not acquire |
| Source relationship | The authoritative, supporting, and constraining sources and their unchanged authority conditions |
| Dependencies | Existing meaning on which coherence relies |
| Prerequisites | Conditions required before the proposition can become coherent or eligible for authority |
| Handoff impact | Whether canonical M8.3 semantic movement remains sufficient or requires a future architectural proposition |
| Trust impact | Applicable cross-cutting constraints and preserved human authority |
| M8.4 relationship | Relevant Gap, Risk, Observation, or Recommendation without resolution, acceptance, treatment, or adoption |
| Alternatives | Materially distinct architectural options, including preservation of the current state where valid |
| Uncertainty | Unknowns and assumptions that remain open |
| Non-authority | Explicit statement that the subject is not approval, canonical meaning, implementation, or work |

An evolution subject missing any applicable element is incomplete. Incomplete
subjects may remain observations or questions; they cannot be treated as
architectural increments.

### 7.2 Modes of capability evolution

| Mode | Architectural purpose | Ownership rule | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clarification | Make an existing responsibility boundary more explicit | Existing owner remains primary | Cannot add responsibility by interpretation |
| Refinement | Divide an owned responsibility into more precise sub-responsibilities | One primary owner remains identifiable for each scope | Cannot create duplicate ownership |
| Extension | Add bounded meaning adjacent to an existing capability | Ownership must be demonstrated, not inferred from adjacency | Cannot absorb another capability's meaning |
| Composition evolution | Change how capabilities cooperate while their meanings remain owned | All participating owners remain unchanged | Cannot become orchestration-based ownership transfer |
| New capability emergence | Introduce a genuinely new stable product responsibility | Unique ownership must be established through separate authority | Cannot be declared merely because a source or implementation exists |
| Cross-cutting constraint evolution | Propose a changed product-wide trust, identity, provenance, privacy, safety, or quality constraint | Constraint ownership does not replace product semantic ownership | Cannot turn the cross-cutting plane into a product layer |
| Retirement or supersession | Propose removal or replacement of architectural meaning | Existing authority remains until a governed transition changes it | Cannot be achieved by omission or non-use |

These modes classify possible architectural deltas. They neither assert that a
delta is needed nor authorize one.

### 7.3 Conditions for future capability emergence

A future capability may be proposed only when all of the following are made
explicit:

1. a stable product responsibility exists that is not already completely owned
   by a current M8.2 capability;
2. the responsibility belongs inside the M8.1 Management OS product boundary;
3. treating it as a refinement or composition of current capabilities has been
   considered and would not preserve a coherent owner;
4. its primary semantic ownership can be uniquely stated or is explicitly
   recorded as an unresolved prerequisite;
5. its source-authority basis is attributable without authority upgrade;
6. its relationships to current capabilities preserve their non-ownership;
7. any semantic movement can be expressed using the M8.3 distinctions and
   authority-preserving handoff model, or the missing architectural meaning is
   identified without being invented;
8. cross-cutting constraints and human decision authority remain preserved;
   and
9. relevant M8.4 Gaps and Risks remain visibly unresolved and unaccepted unless
   changed by separate authority.

A proposed future capability is therefore an architectural option. It is not a
current capability, product commitment, implementation boundary, or work item.

## 8. Dependency-Respecting Architectural Increments

### 8.1 Increment identity

An architectural increment is independently reviewable when it contains one
coherent change thesis, a bounded semantic delta, preserved ownership, explicit
dependencies, satisfied or open prerequisites, and a stated relationship to
the current baseline. Its boundary is architectural, not temporal.

An increment is not coherent if it:

- depends normatively on an unauthorized future option;
- leaves two owners for the same meaning or no identifiable owner;
- changes a handoff while leaving semantic kind, provenance, uncertainty, or
  authority effects unstated;
- changes a source's authority by mapping or convenience;
- relies on an implementation choice for its meaning;
- hides a required governance decision inside architecture; or
- claims a Gap resolved or a Risk accepted or treated without the corresponding
  separate authority.

### 8.2 Dependency classes

| Dependency class | Meaning | Required preservation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity dependency | The proposition relies on Management OS purpose, boundary, or system identity | M8.1 framing remains controlling |
| Responsibility dependency | One responsibility presupposes another product responsibility | M8.1 layer placement and non-ownership remain explicit |
| Capability dependency | One capability relies on meaning owned by another | M8.2 unique ownership remains unchanged by reliance |
| Semantic dependency | A proposition relies on a defined information kind or transformation boundary | M8.3 semantic distinctions remain explicit |
| Handoff dependency | Cooperation relies on an authority-preserving movement between capabilities | Provenance, uncertainty, authority, and traceability remain attached |
| Trust dependency | Coherence relies on a cross-cutting constraint | The cross-cutting plane constrains but does not own product meaning |
| Source dependency | Meaning relies on an authoritative, supporting, or constraining source | Recorded authority condition and scope remain unchanged |
| Governance dependency | Normative effect relies on review, approval, canonicalization, or authority transition | Canonical M7 remains external and controlling |
| Assessment dependency | Consideration relies on an M8.4 assessment object | The object retains its type and non-authority |

Dependencies form a partial order of architectural validity. They do not define
delivery order, team order, runtime order, or a roadmap.

### 8.3 Prerequisite tests

Before an increment can be considered coherent, reviewers must be able to
answer:

- Is every meaning it relies on currently authoritative or clearly conditional?
- Is every semantic owner unique and attributable?
- Are all affected non-ownership boundaries preserved?
- Are relevant information kinds and handoffs distinct?
- Are provenance, uncertainty, and human authority retained?
- Are source conditions unchanged or separately governed?
- Are unresolved M8.4 conditions still visible?
- Can the architectural proposition stand without prescribing realization?

A failed test is an open prerequisite or finding. It is not permission to fill
the gap by assumption.

## 9. Evolution Routes and Alternative Paths

An **evolution route** is a logical arrangement of architectural options that
could connect the current architecture to a future direction. Routes express
compatibility and prerequisites only. They are not delivery sequences,
priorities, recommendations, or plans.

### Route A — Preserve and clarify

Retain the current capability set and clarify ambiguous responsibility,
terminology, source, or handoff boundaries through separately governed
architectural propositions. This route is available where new product meaning
is not demonstrated.

### Route B — Refine existing capability meaning

Express greater precision within an existing capability's owned responsibility
while keeping its owner and non-ownership intact. This route is available where
the need is already inside a current ownership boundary.

### Route C — Evolve composition

Change the architectural cooperation among existing capabilities while no
capability relinquishes or acquires another's meaning. This route is available
where current capabilities are sufficient but their coherent composition needs
future architectural treatment.

### Route D — Establish a future capability option

Describe a genuinely unowned stable responsibility as a candidate capability,
with unique ownership and all emergence conditions stated. This route requires
separate architectural and governance authority before the candidate can become
part of the current architecture.

### Route E — Evolve a cross-cutting constraint

Propose a product-wide change to Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety,
or Quality without relocating product responsibilities into that plane. A
change to canonical authority or governance meaning is outside this route and
requires governance.

### Route F — Retain the current state with explicit residual exposure

Make no architectural change and continue to expose the relevant uncertainty,
Gap, or Risk. This route is architecturally legitimate where evidence is
insufficient, dependencies are unsatisfied, or no authorized change exists. It
does not constitute Risk acceptance.

Routes may be combined only when the resulting evolution subject remains
bounded and all dependencies are explicit. No route is selected or preferred
by this document.

## 10. Architectural Sequencing Constraints

Architectural sequencing is the logical precedence required for coherent
meaning. It is distinct from delivery sequencing.

| Constraint | Required logical precedence | Prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline precedence | Current authoritative meaning is identified before a delta is evaluated | Identification authorizes change |
| Ownership precedence | Primary semantic ownership is known before new composition is treated as coherent | Participation creates ownership |
| Semantic precedence | Information kind and meaning are explicit before a handoff proposition is evaluated | Movement changes kind or authority |
| Evidence precedence | Evidence and uncertainty are attributable before an architectural claim relies on them | Repetition promotes evidence |
| Dependency precedence | Required architectural dependencies are authoritative or explicitly conditional before a dependent option is coherent | Dependency creates permission |
| Trust precedence | Applicable cross-cutting constraints are stated before a proposition is considered complete | Trust plane owns the proposition's meaning |
| Governance precedence | Required review and authority transition occur before proposed meaning becomes normative | Architecture definition is approval |
| Canonical precedence | A separately canonicalized change exists before another canonical subject treats it as controlling | Review disposition alone performs transition |

These constraints impose no dates, releases, team assignments, or work order.

## 11. Architectural Layering of Future Growth

Future growth is analyzed through the enduring responsibility model:

1. identify the primary M8.1 product responsibility layer to which the proposed
   responsibility belongs;
2. identify the M8.2 capability that currently owns adjacent or prerequisite
   meaning;
3. determine whether growth is clarification, refinement, extension,
   composition, emergence, constraint evolution, or retirement;
4. preserve M8.3 semantic and handoff distinctions across every affected
   capability boundary;
5. apply the cross-cutting plane to the complete proposition; and
6. leave governance external to authorize any normative change.

This is architectural layering by responsibility. It is not a stack. A future
capability does not need to be built “above” or “after” another layer, and a
dependency does not imply control, execution order, or authority rank.

Growth spanning multiple layers must still identify one primary responsibility
location for each meaning. Secondary participation is composition, not shared
semantic ownership.

## 12. Preservation of Composition, Sources, and Semantics

### 12.1 Composition preservation

Evolutionary composition is coherent only when:

- each participating capability retains its M8.2 semantic owner;
- requested and returned meaning is explicit without defining a protocol;
- presentation, automation, orchestration, and aggregation remain non-owning;
- a composite result does not conceal the meanings and authorities from which
  it was derived; and
- interruption or absence of one participant does not silently reassign its
  responsibility.

### 12.2 Source-ownership preservation

An evolution subject must distinguish primary authoritative sources from
supporting and constraining sources. It may map, compare, or depend on them only
within their recorded scope and authority condition. It cannot consolidate
sources, normalize their status, select a new semantic owner, or treat a future
source as current authority.

Where source authority is insufficient or conflicting, the condition remains a
finding or prerequisite. Architecture may expose the need; governance must
authorize any authority transition.

### 12.3 Semantic-consistency preservation

The M8.3 distinctions among Information, Evidence, Interpretation, Hypothesis,
Recommendation, Decision, Execution intent, Execution outcome, Learning, and
Knowledge remain controlling. Evolution cannot collapse these kinds, imply
promotion between them, or use a future composition to bypass their authority,
provenance, uncertainty, and traceability conditions.

## 13. Architectural Decision Boundaries

### 13.1 Architecture may determine

Subject to review and authority, architecture may determine:

- whether a proposed responsibility belongs within the Management OS boundary;
- whether current ownership is coherent or a future owner must be established;
- whether options preserve product layers, cross-cutting constraints, and
  external governance;
- the architectural dependencies and prerequisites among options;
- whether a proposed increment is semantically complete and
  implementation-independent; and
- whether alternative routes are mutually compatible, exclusive, or
  conditional.

M8.5 itself supplies the model for those determinations; it does not make a
decision about any particular future option.

### 13.2 Governance is required when

Evolution requires governance rather than architecture alone when it would:

- approve, reject, canonicalize, supersede, or close architectural meaning;
- change a source's authority condition, scope, or primary semantic ownership;
- amend canonical M7 meaning or alter an authority-transition rule;
- reassign human decision authority or organizational authority;
- authorize a stage, successor work, implementation, or backlog change;
- accept a Risk, approve treatment, resolve a Gap, or adopt a Recommendation;
- waive a canonical invariant or approve an exception; or
- make a future option normative for dependent work.

Architecture may identify that one of these decisions is necessary. It cannot
perform or presume the decision.

### 13.3 Product planning and implementation boundaries

Product planning decides priorities, investment, timing, commitments, and
delivery sequencing under its own authority. Implementation determines
technical realization under authorized architecture and delivery authority.
Neither is defined, selected, or authorized here, and neither can retroactively
change architectural ownership or canonical meaning.

## 15. Avoiding Architectural Fragmentation

Fragmentation exists when independently plausible changes no longer compose as
one product architecture. Every evolution subject must therefore be tested for:

1. **identity coherence** — one Management OS boundary and purpose;
2. **responsibility coherence** — one primary layer location for each meaning;
3. **ownership coherence** — one primary semantic owner per scope;
4. **semantic coherence** — common distinctions without silent mutation;
5. **handoff coherence** — authority, provenance, uncertainty, and traceability
   survive composition;
6. **source coherence** — recorded source authority remains attributable;
7. **trust coherence** — cross-cutting constraints apply consistently;
8. **governance coherence** — normative change occurs only through external
   governance; and
9. **option coherence** — alternatives declare conflicts and shared
   prerequisites rather than being combined implicitly.

Failure of any test is an architectural finding or open prerequisite. It cannot
be hidden by naming the result a platform, shared service, common layer,
automation, or integration.

## 16. Long-Term Architectural Sustainability Criteria

The architecture remains sustainable when, across separately authorized
change:

- product identity and external boundaries remain intelligible;
- every responsibility can be traced to one semantic owner and authoritative
  source scope;
- capabilities can be extended or replaced without covert ownership transfer;
- dependencies remain explicit and do not become cycles of undefined meaning;
- information kinds retain their distinctions across old and new capabilities;
- provenance and uncertainty remain inspectable across derived meaning;
- human authority remains visible at every judgment and decision boundary;
- cross-cutting constraints remain applicable without becoming a catch-all
  owner;
- alternatives can be evaluated without forcing premature convergence;
- superseded meaning remains historically attributable;
- residual Gaps and Risks remain visible until separately governed disposition;
  and
- architectural meaning remains independent of any one technical realization.

Sustainability is an architectural quality test, not a guarantee of operational
performance or a substitute for later validation.

## 18. Architectural Assumptions and Open Questions

### 18.1 Assumptions

- Canonical M8.1–M8.4 remain the controlling baseline throughout M8.5 review.
- Existing source authority conditions remain as recorded in M8.1 and assessed
  in M8.4.
- M8.2 unique semantic ownership and M8.3 authority-preserving handoffs remain
  durable constraints on every future option.
- Future governance decisions, product-planning choices, and technical
  realization will occur only under separate authority.

### 18.2 Open architectural questions

The following are review questions, not findings, corrections, or work:

- Is the evolution-subject model sufficient to expose every ownership and
  authority effect of a future capability proposition?
- Do the evolution modes distinguish new responsibility from refinement and
  composition without predetermining a target architecture?
- Are the dependency classes sufficient to prevent unauthorized future meaning
  from becoming a hidden prerequisite?
- Are the route model and sequencing constraints clearly architectural rather
  than product-planning or delivery constructs?
- Do the sustainability criteria preserve the full M8.1–M8.4 baseline while
  allowing future propositions to remain genuinely alternative?

Answers require independent review. They do not change M8.4 findings or
authorize M8.6 or later-stage substance.

## 20. Review Readiness

M8.5 is ready for independent Architecture Review when reviewers can verify
that:

- the evolution model is coherent, bounded, and implementation-independent;
- future capability emergence preserves current ownership and requires unique
  ownership before authority;
- increments, dependencies, prerequisites, routes, and sequencing constraints
  cannot be read as planning or delivery authorization;
- composition and future growth preserve M8.1 responsibility layers, M8.2
  capability ownership, and M8.3 semantic and handoff meaning;
- source ownership, source authority, human authority, Product Purpose, System
  Identity, the cross-cutting plane, and external Governance remain intact;
- M8.4 Gaps, Risks, Recommendations, and assessment-object boundaries remain
  unchanged and non-authorizing;
- the decision boundary correctly distinguishes architecture, governance,
  product planning, and implementation; and
- no M8.6, M8.7, implementation, delivery, or successor substance has been
  introduced.

The independently reviewed architectural meaning is Canonical / Closed. The
separate canonicalization transition changed authority and stage status only;
it did not select an evolution option, authorize implementation, or supply M8.6
or later-stage substance.
