# 1. Assessment Purpose and Evidence Boundary

M8.4 evaluates the integrated architecture established by canonical M8.1–M8.3
to identify material incompleteness, contradiction, ambiguity, evidence
insufficiency, maturity limitations, and architectural exposure.

The purpose is assessment, not correction. This document:

- records present architectural conditions as findings;
- relates those conditions to possible adverse consequences as risks;
- offers advisory future directions as recommendations;
- leaves every acceptance, correction, prioritization, authority transition,
  and work authorization to later legitimate decision authority.

The evidence boundary includes:

- canonical M8.1 identity, boundary, responsibility map, authority map, gaps,
  overlaps, unknowns, assumptions, invariants, and exclusions;
- canonical M8.2 capability identities, ownership partitions, routine
  capability inventory, overlap determinations, unresolved findings, and
  ownership invariants;
- canonical M8.3 information kinds, flow and handoff model, evidence and context
  boundaries, graph, invariants, and unresolved questions;
- canonical M7 governance constraints;
- `docs/DOCUMENTATION_MAP.md`, `docs/SYSTEMS.md`, and sources cited by the
  canonical M8.1–M8.3 evidence chains within their recorded authority
  conditions.

Absence of repository-visible evidence is recorded as absence. It is not filled
by inference, implementation knowledge, chat context, or desired conclusions.

**Product Purpose and System Identity remain architectural-object-level framing
concerns.** They are assessment constraints, not capabilities, issue owners, or
local responsibility assignments. Canonical M7 remains external to product
architecture and governs assessment review and authority transition only.

# 2. Assessment Method and Classification Discipline

## 2.1 Distinct assessment objects

| Object | Meaning | Authority boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finding | Evidence-backed current architectural condition. | Describes what the repository supports; does not predict or authorize. |
| Gap | A finding that required architectural meaning, coverage, evidence, maturity, validation, or traceability is absent, incomplete, ambiguous, or unsupported. | Documentation does not resolve it. |
| Risk | A possible adverse architectural consequence arising from a gap, dependency, uncertainty, or exposure. | Identification is not acceptance, treatment, or prediction. |
| Recommendation | Advisory future action that may reduce uncertainty or exposure. | Creates no decision, task, priority, owner, or authority. |
| Decision | An authorized choice to accept, resolve, defer, investigate, or act. | Not owned by M8.4. |

## 2.2 Issue classifications

Every issue uses one or more classifications without collapsing them:

| Classification | Assessment meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Architectural | Product identity, boundary, responsibility, capability, composition, or cross-capability structure. |
| Semantic | Meaning, terminology, epistemic kind, ownership partition, or interpretation. |
| Authority-related | Source, human, organizational, product, or governance authority and non-transfer boundaries. |
| Evidence-related | Attribution, sufficiency, provenance, traceability, correction, or support for a conclusion. |
| Maturity-related | Difference among approved direction, future architecture, prototype alignment, implementation qualification, and current readiness. |
| Procedural | Repository-visible review, decision, or authorized evolution concern under existing governance. |
| Tooling/workflow | Exposure arising from tools or operating practices; not itself product architecture. |
| Implementation | Technical realization or its absence; not automatically an architectural defect. |
| Product-validation | Evidence about product behavior, usability, outcomes, or readiness outside architectural coherence alone. |

An issue may span classes, but each class retains its own evidence and
consequence. An implementation or tooling condition cannot be relabeled as an
architectural conclusion, and an architectural gap cannot silently become an
implementation task.

## 2.3 Severity and plausibility

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Could invalidate the Management OS architectural identity, unique ownership, authority boundary, or legitimate governance transition. |
| High | Could materially prevent coherent integrated architecture or reliable later validation unless bounded explicitly. |
| Moderate | Material exposure is present but canonical controls contain it enough for bounded continuation. |
| Low | Limited architectural consequence under current controls; remains visible for traceability. |

Risk plausibility is assessed as `High`, `Moderate`, or `Low` from repository
evidence only. It is qualitative architectural judgment, not probability,
forecast, operational measurement, or risk acceptance.

# 3. Gap Taxonomy

| Gap class | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Missing architectural responsibility | A required product responsibility has no identifiable semantic owner or explicit external boundary. |
| Unresolved semantic ownership | Meaning is present but primary ownership remains disputed, partitioned without adequate boundary, or unsupported. |
| Incomplete capability coverage | The controlling capability inventory cannot be reconciled confidently to the authoritative corpus. |
| Incomplete information or handoff coverage | A cross-capability semantic relation lacks sufficient origin, receiver, kind, authority, acceptance, return, or interruption meaning. |
| Authority ambiguity | It is unclear which source, human, organizational, product, or governance authority applies. |
| Terminology inconsistency | The same term carries incompatible meanings or different terms obscure the same meaning. |
| Source-status ambiguity | Repository status language does not yield one unambiguous current authority condition without interpretation. |
| Evidence insufficiency | Available evidence cannot support the architectural conclusion or completeness claim under review. |
| Maturity gap | Approved architectural direction and present maturity differ materially or are insufficiently evidenced. |
| Realization-readiness gap | Architecture lacks enough bounded readiness evidence for later engineering architecture or implementation reliance. |
| Validation gap | Required architectural or product evidence has no complete allocation, criterion, or attributable result. |
| Traceability gap | A material conclusion, dependency, correction, or authority relation cannot be reconstructed through repository-visible evidence. |

# 4. Risk Taxonomy

| Risk class | Possible adverse consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Architectural coherence risk | Canonical parts may not compose into one durable product architecture. |
| Semantic duplication risk | Two sources or capabilities may be treated as owners of the same meaning. |
| Authority-transfer risk | Reference, handoff, composition, implementation, or automation may be mistaken for authority. |
| Human-judgment erosion risk | Recommendation, confidence, silence, or automation may substitute for accountable human judgment. |
| Evidence-integrity risk | Information may be promoted, corrected, summarized, or relied upon without preserving evidentiary boundaries. |
| Provenance-loss risk | Origin, derivation, correction, or version relationships may become unavailable or obscured. |
| Uncertainty-loss risk | Unknown, assumed, modeled, contested, stale, or incomplete meaning may be presented as settled. |
| Handoff failure risk | Responsibility may be unaccepted, misdirected, silently broadened, or returned without adequate meaning. |
| Source-of-truth conflict risk | Different authorities may be treated as interchangeable or current without scope and condition analysis. |
| Implementation-leakage risk | Technical realization may redefine enduring architectural meaning. |
| Automation-overreach risk | Automated preparation or action may infer approval, ownership, authority, or product truth. |
| Premature-completeness risk | Canonical architecture may be mistaken for validated, realized, or complete product capability. |
| Future-evolution risk | Later changes may silently invalidate ownership, handoff, evidence, or authority assumptions. |

# 10. Blocking-Condition Model

The model interprets existing stage purposes; it creates no governance gate,
lifecycle state, or authorization.

| Later activity | Blocking condition | Non-blocking residual condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M8.5 Evolution Architecture | A proposed evolution relies on an unowned responsibility, transfers authority, contradicts canonical M8.1–M8.3, or lacks evidence identifying the condition to evolve. | Known source depth, maturity, or validation gaps that are explicit and outside the proposed scope. |
| M8.6 Validation and Readiness | Validation responsibility, subject, source authority, success meaning, or evidence boundary is absent or ambiguous. | Unimplemented architecture whose readiness can be assessed explicitly as not demonstrated. |

| Later implementation work | The required governing architecture or engineering authority is absent, contradictory, or outside scope; human or source authority cannot be preserved. | Known implementation absence, provided separately authorized work and conformance evidence are established. |

A blocking condition stops only the affected claim or dependent scope. It does
not authorize correction, create a task, or expand M8.4 authority.

# 11. Residual-Risk Model

A risk may remain as an explicit residual exposure only when:

1. the supporting finding and source evidence are attributable;
2. affected scope and possible consequence are explicit;
3. current architectural controls are identified without overstating them;
4. uncertainty and missing evidence remain visible;
5. the residual exposure does not contradict system identity, unique semantic
   ownership, human authority, source authority, or canonical governance;
6. no later claim depends on pretending the risk is resolved or accepted;
7. any dependency or trigger for reconsideration is preserved;
8. acceptance, treatment, or work remains a separate authorized decision.

Residual risk is not resolution, acceptance, mitigation, waiver, or approval.
Critical or High residual exposure may still permit bounded architectural work
when its dependency is not invoked, but it prevents an unqualified completeness
or readiness claim in the affected scope.

# 12. Architectural Findings and Recommendations

1. Canonical M8.1–M8.3 form a coherent integrated product architecture; no
   contradiction invalidating their core identity, ownership, or handoff model
   is evidenced.
2. Four gaps are blocking for an eventual unqualified M8 completion claim:
   corpus coverage traceability, product-wide validation allocation, routine
   handoff completeness, and product-validation evidence breadth.
3. Eleven additional material gaps remain non-blocking because canonical
   boundaries contain them or they concern maturity, validation, or source depth
   rather than invalid core semantics.
4. Fifteen material risks remain; current canonical controls reduce but do not
   accept or eliminate their residual exposure.
5. The strongest existing controls are unique semantic ownership, explicit
   authority separation, epistemic-kind separation, non-transfer handoffs,
   source-condition preservation, and canonical M7 review discipline.
6. The largest evidence limitations concern corpus completeness, routine source
   detail, cross-source correction traceability, realization readiness, and
   product-wide validation.
