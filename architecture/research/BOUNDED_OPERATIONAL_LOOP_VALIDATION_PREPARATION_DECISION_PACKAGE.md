# Bounded Operational Loop Validation Preparation Decision Package

```text
Decision-support artifact for separate human authority consideration
```

## 1. Purpose

This package organizes the current research state, decision boundary, available
options, option consequences, remaining uncertainties, and authority
considerations for a possible human decision about bounded operational loop
validation preparation.

It does not recommend an option or make the decision. It does not authorize or
begin preparation, identify or select a real scenario, approve or execute
validation, create a prototype, define Engineering Architecture or
implementation, create M9, or create backlog work.

These materials provide research interpretation and decision support only. They

Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged. M8 remains Completed / Closed.

## 4. Current State

### 4.1 What the research has established

The completed research supports the following bounded observations.

#### Value proposition

The principal post-M8 uncertainty is whether the complete operational loop can
contribute to a real, observable, and verifiable improvement in management
capacity and operational condition for a bounded situation.

The hypothesized value is not “more analysis.” It concerns whether an
accountable human can preserve context, improve evidence-backed understanding,
make a timely and explainable Decision, carry intent into a source-owned Change,
verify material consequence, and reuse verified experience without authority or
semantic collapse.

#### Whole-loop subject

The research uses this analytical trace:

```text
Operational Reality
  → Matter / Context Continuity
  → Investigation
  → Evidence
  → Understanding
  → Human Decision
  → Source-Owned Change
  → Observed Outcome and Verification
  → Learning
```

This trace is not a lifecycle, workflow, state model, or protocol.

#### Candidate shape

Potentially informative candidates are recurrent, material but responsibly
bounded, non-safety-critical, evidence-accessible, dependent on genuine human
judgment, capable of one distinguishable and plausibly reversible source-owned
Change, observable within a justified horizon, and open to comparison and later
Learning.

No real candidate has been identified, assessed, ranked, recommended, or
selected.

#### Evidence posture

Evidence should be claim-specific, attributable, semantically valid, sufficiently
complete for the bounded claim, open to contrary and missing material,
traceable, comparative where applicable, uncertainty-preserving, and
independently inspectable where feasible.

Activity volume, AI interaction, Recommendation count, user confidence,
execution completion, modeled result, and stored Learning are insufficient by
themselves to demonstrate value.

#### Outcome posture

Outcome is the final consequence test. It requires attributable Observation or
source-owned Evidence and owned verification judgment relative to Decision
intent and the pre-stated operational condition. Outcome is not completion,
prediction, model confidence, satisfaction, or proof that prior reasoning was
fully correct.

#### Attribution posture

The strongest initial conclusion would normally be case-bounded association or
contribution, not unique causality. A defensible contribution inquiry would need
to trace changes in Evidence and Understanding through explicit human Decision,
actual source-owned Change, observed operational difference, comparison, and
alternative explanations.

#### Scenario assessment posture

Candidates could be examined qualitatively through:

- bounded ownership;
- Evidence availability;
- human Decision presence;
- Change reversibility and traceability;
- Outcome observability;
- comparison feasibility;
- attribution feasibility;
- Learning potential; and
- burden, harm, bias, and confounding.

No aggregate score or ranking is supported. Non-substitutable failures cannot be
averaged away.

#### Preparation posture

The research is sufficient to support human consideration of whether a bounded
candidate-identification and suitability-assessment activity should be
authorized. It is not sufficient to select a scenario or authorize validation
execution.

### 4.2 What remains assumed rather than demonstrated

The research currently assumes, without case evidence, that:

- at least one real recurrent and materially bounded candidate exists;
- an accountable human Decision owner can be identified;
- relevant source owners can permit responsible consideration of existing
  Evidence;
- pre-Decision and post-Change Evidence may be available and semantically valid;
- one prospective Change could be source-owned, distinguishable, responsibly
  reversible, and non-safety-critical;
- a material Outcome could be fixed and observed within a practical horizon;
- a credible comparison or baseline could exist;
- material confounders and external factors could be bounded in interpretation;
- participation, access, privacy, burden, and ethical conditions could be
  satisfied under existing authority;
- more than one candidate could be considered where reasonably possible; and
- preparation could occur without prototype, instrumentation, data model,
  workflow, or other implementation.

None of these assumptions has been validated for a real scenario.

### 4.3 Boundaries already defined

- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, hypothesis, Modeled / Scenario Output,
  Recommendation, Decision, Change record, Outcome, and Learning remain distinct.
- Decision remains human; Management OS and AI remain non-authoritative.
- Facts, records, and mutation remain source-owned.
- One Root Cause is not required.
- Operational Diagnosis remains research framing, not a canonical object.
- Positive, negative, null, interrupted, rejected, no-candidate, and
  indeterminate results remain legitimate.
- Preparation may examine suitability Evidence only, not perform a Change or
  observe a validation Outcome.
- Any later selection and validation decision must remain separate.
- Missing Evidence does not authorize implementation.

## 5. Decision Requested

### 5.1 The possible decision

The decision available for separate human authority consideration is:

> Whether to authorize, defer, request additional evidence before deciding, or
> decline a bounded validation-preparation activity limited to identifying and
> qualitatively assessing real candidate operational loop scenarios for a later
> human decision.

If authorization were chosen, the bounded preparation purpose would be to
produce decision support about candidate suitability. It would permit no
scenario selection or validation execution.

### 5.2 Decisions not requested

This package does not ask the authority to:

- select a candidate class or real scenario;
- rank candidates;
- approve validation;
- approve an operational Change;
- authorize access not already valid under applicable authority;
- make the operational Decision for a candidate;
- create a prototype or implementation;
- approve Engineering Architecture;
- claim product or implementation readiness;
- create M9, a milestone, stage, order, transition, or backlog task;
- amend governance or canonical architecture;
- resolve M8.4 Gaps, accept or treat Risks, adopt Recommendations, or select M8.5
  alternatives; or
- approve delivery, priority, staffing, schedule, or release commitments.

### 5.3 Maximum decision effect

The maximum effect of the possible decision is either:

- permission to begin a bounded preparation activity under explicitly recorded
  scope and existing authority;
- continuation of the current research-only state;
- a bounded request for additional decision evidence; or
- closure of the current proposal without preparation.

No option creates an automatic next step.

## 6. Available Options

The options are presented without recommendation, preference, score, or order of
merit.

### Option A — Authorize bounded preparation

Authorize only candidate identification and qualitative suitability assessment
within fixed purpose, evidence, authority, access, exclusion, and termination
boundaries.

### Option B — Defer the decision

Leave the proposal open for later consideration without authorizing preparation
or requesting a specific additional evidence action.

### Option C — Request additional decision evidence

Request bounded information necessary to decide whether preparation should be
authorized, without authorizing candidate assessment, selection, validation,
prototype, or implementation.

### Option D — Decline preparation

Decline the proposed transition from research to preparation. Research remains
available as non-authoritative evidence, and no candidate activity begins.

## 7. Option Consequences

### 7.1 Option A — Authorize bounded preparation

#### What would change

- A separately recorded bounded preparation activity could begin.
- Candidate operational conditions could be identified from existing
  attributable information under applicable access authority.
- Candidate suitability Evidence could be assembled and assessed qualitatively.
- Multiple candidates could be compared narratively without rank where feasible.
- A decision-support package could later return to human authority.

#### What would remain unchanged

- No scenario would be selected by authorization alone.
- No validation would be approved or executed.
- Human Operational Decision and source-owned Change authority would remain
  external to preparation.
- Canonical M7, M8.1–M8.7, and completed research would remain unchanged.
- No product-readiness, causal, Engineering Architecture, implementation, or
  delivery claim would follow.
- No M9 or successor would follow automatically.

#### Principal risks

- preparation expands into selection or validation planning;
- convenient or favorable candidates receive hidden preference;
- missing Evidence triggers prototype or implementation;
- assessment language becomes de facto authorization;
- AI summaries, scoring, or data abundance displace human judgment;
- access, privacy, burden, or source authority is assumed rather than established;
- preparation completion creates pressure to proceed; and
- research categories become treated as canonical objects or protocol.

#### Risk boundary needed

Fixed scope, qualitative non-ranked assessment, non-substitutable rejection
conditions, explicit stop conditions, existing source authority, no-candidate and
indeterminate outcomes, and a mandatory return to separate human consideration.

### 7.2 Option B — Defer the decision

#### What would change

- The decision would remain unresolved for later consideration.
- Time or external developments could alter Evidence availability, access,
  organizational conditions, or candidate visibility.

#### What would remain unchanged

- Research-only status would continue.
- No candidate identification, assessment, selection, or validation would begin.
- No authority, backlog, implementation, or milestone state would change.
- Canonical and control boundaries would remain as they are.

#### Principal risks

- relevant operational Evidence may become stale or unavailable;
- research context may be forgotten or misinterpreted later;
- informal candidate exploration may occur without recorded boundaries;
- deferral may be mistaken for rejection or passive approval; and
- unresolved assumptions remain unresolved.

#### Risk boundary needed

An explicit statement that deferral creates no authorization, no negative
finding, and no automatic reconsideration date or work commitment.

### 7.3 Option C — Request additional decision evidence

#### What would change

- A narrowly bounded evidence question could be stated for the authority
  decision itself.
- Existing repository or operational information could be examined only within
  separately valid access and scope.
- The authority could later reconsider with a clearer basis.

#### What would remain unchanged

- Preparation would not be authorized.
- Real candidates would not be assessed for suitability unless that assessment
  were separately authorized.
- No scenario would be selected and no validation would begin.
- Missing Evidence would not authorize collection tooling, prototype, or
  implementation.

#### Principal risks

- the evidence request becomes preparation in substance;
- broad information gathering creates hidden work or access expansion;
- requested evidence is selected to support a preferred option;
- activity or research volume substitutes for the decision question; and
- an open-ended request becomes an implicit backlog.

#### Risk boundary needed

One fixed decision question, identified existing sources, maximum permitted
inference, time and scope boundary if separately authorized, and permission to
return Indeterminate without expansion.

### 7.4 Option D — Decline preparation

#### What would change

- The current proposal would not progress to preparation.
- No candidate-identification or suitability-assessment activity would begin
  through this proposal.

#### What would remain unchanged

- Completed research would remain available and non-authoritative.
- Canonical M7, M8.1–M8.7, M8 closure, and control surfaces would remain
  unchanged.
- No negative conclusion about Management OS value would follow.
- No scenario class or candidate would be rejected on its merits without
  assessment.

#### Principal risks

- the central value-and-effect uncertainty remains unexamined;
- later implementation consideration may proceed elsewhere without bounded
  operational value evidence;
- relevant candidate opportunities or source evidence may be lost over time;
- decline may be misread as falsification of the value hypothesis; and
- informal, unbounded validation attempts may emerge outside the research
  boundaries.

#### Risk boundary needed

Record that decline concerns the proposed preparation transition only. It does
not falsify value, prohibit future research, authorize Engineering Architecture,
or create a successor direction.

## 9. Remaining Uncertainties

### 9.1 Real scenario availability

No real scenario has been identified. It remains unknown whether any recurrent,
material, bounded, non-safety-critical case satisfies the research conditions.

Unknowns include:

- recurrence and materiality;
- bounded operational scope;
- one accountable Decision owner;
- one source-owned and responsibly reversible prospective Change;
- manageable external factors; and
- credible later Learning opportunity.

### 9.2 Evidence availability

It remains unknown whether candidate contexts have:

- attributable pre-Decision and post-Change sources;
- sufficient provenance, time, scope, correction, and semantic clarity;
- visible contrary, negative, null, adverse, and missing Evidence;
- inspectability under applicable access conditions;
- Decision-basis and actual-Change traceability; and
- stable measure meaning.

Availability must not be inferred from expected system data or proposed
instrumentation.

### 9.3 Outcome observability

No case-specific Outcome has been stated. It remains unknown whether a material
intended operational condition can be fixed before observation, verified by an
accountable human, and observed within a justified horizon without relying on
activity, satisfaction, completion, or modeled effect.

### 9.4 Attribution boundary

No case-specific contribution path exists. It remains unknown whether:

- changes in Evidence and Understanding can be related to human Decision;
- Decision intent can be related to actual source-owned Change;
- the operational difference can be distinguished from natural variation,
  manager action outside Management OS, external events, and concurrent
  interventions; and
- a credible comparison can bound the inference.

### 9.5 Resource and access constraints

It remains unknown whether existing authority, people, time, attention, access,
privacy, confidentiality, correction, and operational burden permit even
preparation. This package does not create or estimate resources, schedules, or
staffing.

### 9.6 Ethical and operational constraints

It remains unknown whether candidate consideration can avoid coercion,
surveillance, blame, authority distortion, privacy harm, safety exposure,
employment consequence, or disruption. Root Cause pressure and post hoc blame
are particular risks in failure-related classes.

### 9.7 Candidate plurality

It remains unknown whether more than one credible candidate is visible.
Single-candidate consideration may heighten confirmation, convenience, and
selection bias.

### 9.8 Preparation without implementation

It remains unknown whether sufficient existing Evidence is available without
new instrumentation, interfaces, data structures, integrations, or prototypes.
If implementation is required merely to assess suitability, this proposal's
preparation boundary cannot be met.

## 10. Required Authority Considerations

### 10.1 Ownership

The human authority should consider:

- who would own the preparation conclusion;
- who owns operational Decisions in candidate contexts;
- who owns relevant facts, records, and prospective Change;
- whether affected participants and external authorities are visible; and
- how assessors, AI, and Management OS remain non-authoritative.

### 10.2 Access

The authority should consider whether existing applicable authority can support:

- bounded access to relevant source-owned information;
- preservation of provenance, correction, semantic meaning, and limitations;
- protection of privacy, confidentiality, and sensitive context;
- retention of contrary and adverse material;
- participant refusal, correction, or withdrawal where applicable; and
- a conclusion that access is insufficient.

This package does not grant access or define access mechanics.

### 10.3 Commitment

The authority should distinguish preparation commitment from future commitment:

- preparation may consume human attention and operational context;
- authorization should not imply candidate selection;
- completion should not imply validation;
- no-candidate and Indeterminate should remain acceptable;
- no resources, schedule, staffing, delivery, or implementation follow
  automatically; and
- sunk effort must not become pressure to continue.

### 10.4 Ethical constraints

The authority should consider:

- whether observation or candidate disclosure could create blame or coercion;
- whether vulnerable or affected participants bear disproportionate burden;
- whether failure, quality, productivity, or coordination evidence could be
  misused as personnel judgment;
- whether AI involvement creates perceived authority or surveillance;
- whether data minimization and purpose limitation can be preserved; and
- whether a candidate should be excluded because responsible preparation cannot
  be assured.

This package creates no ethical policy or governance rule.

### 10.5 Operational constraints

The authority should consider:

- whether preparation would disrupt the operation being considered;
- whether current reorganization, instability, or concurrent initiatives make
  evidence uninterpretable;
- whether owners have capacity for accurate context and correction;
- whether source records are sufficiently stable;
- whether the candidate environment is safety-critical or irreversible; and
- whether preparation can stop without operational consequence.

### 10.6 Evidence and uncertainty

The authority should consider:

- which assumptions are material to the decision now;
- whether additional evidence is decision-relevant or merely desirable;
- whether uncertainty can remain explicit;
- whether negative and contrary material can be retained;
- whether the authority is prepared to accept Defer, Decline, no-candidate, or
  Indeterminate; and
- whether any requested conclusion exceeds available Evidence.

### 10.7 Scope integrity

The authority should confirm whether it can keep separate:

- preparation from scenario selection;
- suitability from validation approval;
- validation approval from execution;
- operational Decision from assessment;
- Outcome from causal proof;
- value evidence from product readiness; and
- research from canonical or governance authority.

fix all of the following before preparation begins:

- bounded purpose;
- accountable human for preparation;
- candidate and operational discovery boundary;
- existing-source and access boundary;
- qualitative assessment dimensions;
- prohibition on scoring and ranking;
- supporting, contrary, missing, conflicting, and inaccessible Evidence posture;
- human and source-authority preservation;
- no-implementation boundary;
- no-candidate, rejection, and Indeterminate legitimacy;
- explicit exclusions;
- termination conditions;
- maximum permitted output; and
- mandatory return to separate human authority before selection or validation.

If these conditions cannot be fixed, Option A would not have a sufficiently
bounded meaning under this package.

## 12. Conditions Specific to Option C

If the accountable human considers requesting additional evidence, the request
would need to identify:

- the exact decision uncertainty to be reduced;
- the existing attributable sources that may answer it;
- the maximum permitted inference;
- the boundary preventing evidence gathering from becoming candidate
  assessment or preparation;
- the authority and access basis;
- conditions for Insufficient or Indeterminate evidence; and
- the return point for a later decision.

An open-ended request for more research, data, candidate discovery, tooling, or
implementation would not remain within Option C.
