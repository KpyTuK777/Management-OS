## 3. Shared Operational-Loop Frame

Each hypothesis concerns a bounded part or consequence of the synthesis model:

```text
Reality
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Understanding
  ↓
Human Decision
  ↓
Actual Change
  ↓
Observed Outcome
  ↓
Learning
```

This is not a lifecycle, workflow, governance process, validation protocol,
object model, or guaranteed progression. Each transition may be interrupted,
indeterminate, or affected by context. A candidate inquiry would still need one
bounded operational loop capable of preserving the complete consequence chain.

## 4. Shared Semantic and Authority Conditions

Every hypothesis remains subject to:

```text
Evidence ≠ Interpretation
Observation ≠ Interpretation
Modeled / Scenario Output ≠ Evidence or Fact
Recommendation ≠ Decision
Decision ≠ Change
Change ≠ Outcome
Outcome ≠ causal proof
Learning ≠ success declaration
```

Additional conditions are:

- Decision remains attributable human judgment;
- source owners retain authority over actual Change and source mutation;
- AI may support analysis but receives no Decision, validation, verification, or
  source-mutation authority;
- activity, completion, confidence, adoption, or user satisfaction does not
  establish operational value;
- Outcome is evaluated against a pre-stated intended operational condition;
- attribution remains a bounded contribution question, not automatic causality;
- favorable evidence must be considered with contrary, missing, and balancing
  evidence; and
- an indeterminate conclusion remains valid when evidence is insufficient.

## 5. Hypothesis Set

### H1 — Management OS reduces semantic loss across operational transitions

#### Hypothesis statement

> Within one bounded operational loop, use of Management OS is associated with
> less material semantic loss across Reality-to-Evidence,
> Evidence-to-Understanding, Understanding-to-Decision, Decision-to-Change,
> Change-to-Outcome, and Outcome-to-Learning transitions than a credible
> comparison condition.

This proposition is unconfirmed. “Associated with” deliberately avoids exclusive
causal language. “Semantic loss” remains research shorthand for material meaning,
provenance, uncertainty, authority, intent, or limitation that is omitted,
distorted, substituted, or detached during a transition; it is not a new object
or metric framework.

#### What would be tested

The inquiry would examine whether material meaning remains traceable across one
complete loop, including:

- whether source-owned observations retain provenance, time, context, and
  limitations;
- whether Evidence remains distinct from Interpretation and modeled output;
- whether contrary evidence and uncertainty remain visible in Understanding;
- whether Recommendation remains distinct from the human Decision;
- whether Decision intent and meaning survive into authorized, actual Change;
- whether actual Change remains distinct from Outcome; and
- whether Learning remains linked to Outcome evidence and attribution limits.

The inquiry would not test whether every wording difference is loss. Fidelity is
preservation of material meaning, not copied text or identical presentation.

#### Uncertainty potentially reduced

H1 could reduce uncertainty about whether canonical semantic boundaries can be
preserved in a real end-to-end operational loop and whether the product’s
compositional use improves that preservation relative to a credible comparison.

It would not establish overall product value, Decision correctness, causal
Outcome improvement, universal interoperability, or sufficiency across other
contexts.

#### Potential Evidence

Potential Evidence, subject to ownership and access, could include:

- a fixed description of the initial operational condition and source records;
- attributable observations with provenance, timing, limitations, and contrary
  material;
- investigation and understanding records showing Evidence, Interpretation,
  hypotheses, modeled output, Recommendations, and uncertainty separately;
- an attributable Decision record showing evidence considered, rationale,
  bounds, intended condition, and unresolved uncertainty;
- source-owned records of intended, authorized, actual, and observed Change;
- a trace of material meaning entering and leaving each transition;
- documented deviations, clarification requests, corrections, interruptions, or
  disputed interpretations;
- Outcome Observation and verification records; and
- Learning records retaining context, counterevidence, attribution limits, and
  correction history.

The number of records, transitions, or preserved fields would be activity
evidence only. It would not by itself establish reduced semantic loss.

#### Possible Outcome signals

Possible signals, only if fixed before observation and linked to a material
operational consequence, include:

- fewer material Decision corrections caused by previously available but lost
  Evidence or context;
- fewer consequential differences between attributable Decision intent and
  actual Change;
- reduced clarification or rework caused by meaning loss rather than by healthy
  challenge;
- improved ability of the accountable human and source owner to explain the same
  material bounds, uncertainty, and intended condition;
- fewer cases in which completion, modeled output, Recommendation, or Outcome is
  assigned the wrong semantic status; and
- a more complete trace for explaining an observed Outcome without inventing
  missing links.

These signals must not be optimized as a score. Some clarification and correction
may indicate healthy semantic challenge rather than failure.

#### Attribution limitations

- participants may communicate more carefully because they know they are being
  observed;
- training, facilitation, novelty, or management attention may explain change;
- a comparison case may differ in complexity, participants, authority, or data
  quality;
- later documentation can make fidelity appear stronger than it was at the time;
- visible records may omit informal meaning transfer;
- semantic judgments may depend partly on accountable human interpretation; and
- favorable Outcome can occur despite semantic loss, while high fidelity can
  coexist with an unfavorable Outcome.

H1 therefore concerns bounded contribution to transition fidelity, not proof that
Management OS caused the operational Outcome.

#### Falsification conditions

H1 would be weakened or falsified within its stated scope if sufficient evidence
shows that:

- material semantic loss is unchanged or greater than in the credible comparison;
- the apparent improvement results only from retrospective documentation;
- provenance, contrary evidence, uncertainty, or authority distinctions collapse
  despite completing the loop;
- Decision intent is not preserved into actual Change;
- preserving distinctions creates burden or delay without compensating fidelity
  or consequence value;
- the inquiry cannot distinguish genuine loss from harmless wording variation;
  or
- available evidence cannot support a determinate comparison.

The last condition may require an indeterminate conclusion rather than a claim
that H1 is false.

### H2 — Evidence and semantic separation improves Decision clarity

#### Hypothesis statement

> Within one bounded operational loop, explicit preservation of Evidence,
> Observation, Interpretation, hypothesis, modeled output, Recommendation, and
> human Decision as distinct propositions is associated with improved Decision
> clarity relative to a credible comparison condition.

This proposition is unconfirmed. “Decision clarity” is research shorthand for an
accountable human’s ability to state what was decided, why, on what evidence,
within which authority and bounds, and with which uncertainty. It is not a new
object, score, Decision quality guarantee, or success measure.

#### What would be tested

The inquiry would examine whether the accountable human can more clearly
distinguish:

- observed or source-owned material from its Interpretation;
- known, unknown, contradicted, and modeled propositions;
- Recommendation from the Decision actually owned;
- confidence from evidence sufficiency;
- Decision rationale from post-Outcome hindsight;
- Decision authority from Change authority; and
- intended condition from expected, modeled, and observed Outcome.

The inquiry would test clarity of accountable judgment, not agreement with an AI,
speed alone, confidence alone, or whether the Decision later produced a desired
Outcome.

#### Uncertainty potentially reduced

H2 could reduce uncertainty about whether the canonical semantic separation
meaningfully supports human Decision comprehension and corrigibility under real
operational conditions.

It would not establish that the Decision was correct, optimal, faster, authorized
for Change, or causally responsible for Outcome.

#### Potential Evidence

Potential Evidence could include:

- the Evidence set available before Decision, with source, provenance, time,
  limitations, and contradiction;
- separately represented Interpretations, hypotheses, modeled outputs, and
  Recommendations;
- accountable-human explanations recorded before Outcome is known;
- Decision identity, owner, authority basis, rationale, bounds, uncertainty,
  alternatives, and intended operational condition;
- questions, corrections, reversals, or delays attributable to semantic
  confusion;
- independent source-owner understanding of intended Change;
- a credible comparison using the same bounded operational proposition; and
- later audit showing whether the pre-Outcome rationale remained intelligible
  without being rewritten by Outcome knowledge.

User activity, AI interactions, produced Recommendations, confidence ratings, or
document completeness are insufficient alone.

#### Possible Outcome signals

Possible signals include:

- the accountable human can identify which propositions are Evidence and which
  are interpretations or modeled alternatives;
- the Decision’s identity, rationale, authority, bounds, uncertainty, and
  intended condition are materially unambiguous to relevant owners;
- known contradictory evidence is not omitted from the Decision basis;
- fewer corrections or reversals arise from conflating already available
  Evidence with Interpretation;
- required clarification decreases without suppressing challenge;
- Decision timing improves without deterioration in evidence use, authority
  clarity, correction, or downstream Outcome; or
- later Outcome does not cause the Decision record to be retrospectively
  rewritten as obviously correct or incorrect.

These are possible bounded signals, not an aggregate clarity score.

#### Attribution limitations

- clarity may improve because of facilitation, training, simplified context, or
  participant expertise rather than Management OS;
- self-reported clarity may reflect confidence, conformity, or satisfaction;
- a well-documented Decision can still be poorly reasoned;
- a fast or stable Decision can reflect premature closure;
- a favorable Outcome may create hindsight bias about prior clarity;
- comparison conditions may not have equivalent Evidence or authority; and
- explicit separation may add cognitive burden that offsets its benefit.

H2 cannot support the claim that clearer Decision records cause better Outcomes
without separate Outcome and attribution evidence.

#### Falsification conditions

H2 would be weakened or falsified within scope if sufficient evidence shows that:

- material Evidence and Interpretation remain conflated;
- the accountable human cannot explain the Decision basis and uncertainty more
  clearly than under the comparison;
- semantic separation changes document form but not human understanding;
- clarity improves only in self-report while corrections, ambiguity, or handoff
  drift remain unchanged or worsen;
- additional structure increases latency or cognitive burden without compensating
  Decision or Outcome value;
- Recommendations or modeled outputs acquire de facto Decision authority; or
- the evidence cannot distinguish Decision clarity from documentation quality.

An inability to form a credible comparison may produce an indeterminate result.

### H3 — An Outcome-oriented loop improves Learning after Change

#### Hypothesis statement

> Within one bounded operational loop, preserving actual Change, Outcome
> Observation, Outcome Interpretation, attribution limits, and correctable
> Learning is associated with more useful Learning after Change than a credible
> comparison condition.

This proposition is unconfirmed. “More useful Learning” means bounded experience
that remains traceable, correctable, contextual, and capable of informing later
human understanding or judgment. It is not a Learning object, success model,
performance score, standing rule, or authority.

#### What would be tested

The inquiry would examine whether:

- actual Change is evidenced independently from Decision intent and reported
  completion;
- Outcome is observed against a pre-stated intended operational condition;
- desired, undesired, mixed, null, unexpected, or indeterminate Outcome can be
  recorded without automatic success/failure classification;
- Outcome Interpretation remains distinct from Outcome Observation;
- attribution claims retain external factors, incomplete Change, comparison
  limits, and uncertainty;
- Learning remains linked to the Decision, actual Change, Outcome evidence,
  context, and counterevidence; and
- later comparable experience can correct or test the prior Learning.

The inquiry would not test whether favorable Outcome proves a correct Decision or
whether an unfavorable Outcome proves a bad Decision.

#### Uncertainty potentially reduced

H3 could reduce uncertainty about whether closing the loop through Outcome
verification produces Learning that is more usable and correctable than learning
based on activity completion, expectation, or narrative alone.

It would not establish generalized organizational learning, durable product
value, exclusive causality, or automatic improvement in later Outcomes.

#### Potential Evidence

Potential Evidence could include:

- the pre-Change operational condition, intended Outcome, observation horizon,
  and balancing conditions;
- the attributable human Decision and its uncertainty;
- source-owned Evidence of intended, authorized, actual, and observed Change;
- source-owned Outcome observations, including contrary or adverse effects;
- interpretation and attribution records separating fact, inference, and
  competing explanations;
- Learning statements with provenance, context, limits, counterevidence,
  correction behavior, and conditions of possible reuse;
- a credible comparison in which Learning is formed without equivalent Outcome
  discipline; and
- a later comparable situation, if available, showing whether prior Learning was
  examined, corrected, rejected, or responsibly used by a human.

The number of Learning records, lessons captured, knowledge accesses, or repeated
statements is activity evidence and not proof of useful Learning.

#### Possible Outcome signals

Possible signals include:

- Learning claims accurately distinguish observed Outcome from interpretation
  and attribution;
- Learning is revised when later evidence contradicts it;
- desired Outcome does not erase uncertainty or adverse balancing effects;
- undesired or indeterminate Outcome still produces bounded insight without
  declaring failure or blame;
- later human reasoning explicitly examines relevant prior experience without
  treating it as authority;
- repeated action is less likely to rely on a false completion-equals-success
  narrative; or
- a later comparable Decision, Change, or Outcome shows improvement plausibly
  connected to verified prior experience.

The final signal requires a longer observation horizon and stronger attribution
discipline than the earlier signals.

#### Attribution limitations

- later improvement may result from different people, context, resources, or
  external events;
- the availability of a comparable later situation is uncertain;
- knowledge of the prior Outcome may induce hindsight and confirmation bias;
- learning documentation can improve without changing later human behavior;
- repeated cases may be superficially similar but causally different;
- novelty, attention, facilitation, or organizational memory outside Management
  OS may explain the effect; and
- a desired Outcome can occur without useful Learning, while valuable Learning
  can follow an undesired or indeterminate Outcome.

H3 supports at most a bounded contribution question and cannot prove that
Management OS caused later Outcome improvement.

#### Falsification conditions

H3 would be weakened or falsified within scope if sufficient evidence shows that:

- Learning is formed without Outcome evidence or with Outcome and Interpretation
  collapsed;
- actual Change and observed Outcome cannot be distinguished;
- Learning becomes an uncorrectable success story, failure declaration, or
  authority for later action;
- accumulated Learning does not change later understanding or human judgment;
- later use repeats obsolete or context-inappropriate conclusions;
- Outcome-oriented recording adds burden without improving correctness,
  corrigibility, or later use;
- external explanations dominate any apparent Learning effect; or
- no credible later observation or comparison can determine usefulness.

The absence of a later comparable case may leave H3 indeterminate rather than
false.

## 6. Cross-Hypothesis Distinctions

| Hypothesis | Primary uncertainty | Nearest transition focus | What it must not claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Whether material meaning survives the whole loop | All transition boundaries | That fidelity alone proves value or favorable Outcome |
| H2 | Whether semantic separation improves accountable Decision clarity | Evidence → Understanding → Decision | That clarity proves Decision correctness or authority |
| H3 | Whether Outcome discipline improves correctable Learning after Change | Change → Outcome → Learning | That Learning proves success or causes later Outcome |

These differences enable qualitative comparison. They do not create an order,
score, or preferred hypothesis.

The hypotheses also interact. H2 may be relevant to H1, and H1 may affect the
interpretability of H3. A future authority would need to decide whether that
interaction is acceptable within a bounded primary hypothesis. The artifact does
not combine them into a composite hypothesis or assume that testing one validates
the others.

## 7. Selection Criteria

The criteria below support human judgment. They are non-compensatory where a
missing condition makes a hypothesis incapable of credible inquiry. They must not
be converted into points, weights, totals, tiers, rankings, or an AI choice.

### 7.1 Information value

Questions for consideration:

- Which unresolved post-M8 uncertainty would the hypothesis materially reduce?
- Would a positive, negative, mixed, or indeterminate result change future human
  understanding?
- Can evidence count against the hypothesis rather than merely illustrate it?
- Would the result remain meaningful without claiming total product success?

High intellectual interest alone is insufficient if the inquiry cannot affect a
bounded future judgment.

### 7.2 Boundedness

Questions for consideration:

- Can the proposition be limited to one operational loop, owner, context, Change,
  Outcome, and time horizon?
- Are affected sources, participants, dependencies, exclusions, and potential
  harms visible?
- Can the claim remain local rather than generalizing to Management OS as a
  whole?
- Can the inquiry stop or remain indeterminate without expanding scope?

### 7.3 Evidence availability

Questions for consideration:

- Is relevant pre-observation Evidence likely to exist with provenance and source
  authority?
- Can contrary, missing, and contextual Evidence remain visible?
- Can actual Change be evidenced independently of intent or completion?
- Is a credible comparison possible without inventing a baseline?
- Can Evidence be accessed ethically and with applicable permission?

Rich activity data cannot compensate for missing human Decision, actual Change,
or Outcome evidence.

### 7.4 Observability

Questions for consideration:

- Can the hypothesis-specific condition be observed without a new measurement
  system being smuggled into decision support?
- Can a primary Outcome and balancing conditions be stated before results?
- Is the observation horizon credible?
- Can observation remain distinct from interpretation and attribution?
- Can an indeterminate result be recognized?

### 7.5 Reversibility and responsible exposure

Questions for consideration:

- Could one future Change be bounded, interrupted, corrected, and responsibly
  reversed where appropriate?
- Is the exposure non-safety-critical and proportionate to the information value?
- Can adverse or displaced effects be observed?
- Would preparation or inquiry pressure participants into an operational Change?

A hypothesis requiring irreversible or disproportionate exposure is unsuitable
for a first bounded validation consideration.

### 7.6 Human ownership

Questions for consideration:

- Is there one accountable human owner of the operational judgment?
- Are Decision authority, Change authority, source ownership, Outcome
  verification ownership, and validation authority distinguishable?
- Can the human reject, revise, or leave the result indeterminate?
- Can AI remain non-authoritative throughout?

Missing accountable human ownership cannot be compensated by stronger Evidence
or a more observable Outcome.

A future human consideration could compare each hypothesis by documenting:

- evidence supporting suitability under every criterion;
- contrary and missing evidence;
- non-substitutable weaknesses;
- possible claim boundary;
- attribution and validity threats;
- participant and source-owner constraints;
- reasons for any indeterminate assessment; and
- questions requiring further evidence.

The output could state only a bounded qualitative judgment such as:

- sufficiently understood for authority consideration;
- conditionally understood with named missing evidence;
- currently unsuitable for the bounded purpose; or
- indeterminate.

These phrases are decision-support expressions, not canonical states, scores,
selection, recommendation, or validation approval.

## 9. Candidate Validation Shapes

The shapes below describe structural conditions common to a credible future
inquiry. They do not identify a real scenario or define execution.

### 9.1 One operational loop

The candidate shape contains one bounded path from operational Reality through
Evidence, Understanding, human Decision, actual Change, Outcome verification, and
possible Learning. It must not expand into organization-wide transformation or
claim to test the complete product.

### 9.2 One accountable manager

One real accountable manager owns the bounded operational Decision. This does not
imply that the manager owns all sources, Change execution, Outcome verification,
or validation authority. Those authorities must remain explicit and may be held
by different people.

### 9.3 One bounded Change

One prospective source-owned Change is distinguishable from concurrent activity,
proportionate, traceable to the human Decision, and responsibly reversible or
correctable. This support artifact neither defines nor authorizes that Change.

### 9.4 One observable Outcome

One material operational condition and relevant balancing effects could be
stated before observation. Activity, task completion, adoption, satisfaction, AI
usage, Recommendation count, or Change completion cannot substitute for Outcome.

### 9.5 Comparison possibility

A credible comparison might use an appropriate baseline, repeated instance,
temporal contrast, matched context, within-case counterfactual reasoning, or
other implementation-independent validation method identified by later
authority. The comparison must expose differences and limitations rather than
manufacture causal certainty.

No particular comparison is selected here.

## 10. Hypothesis-to-Shape Fit Questions

### H1 fit questions

- Are enough transitions visible to examine material meaning loss end to end?
- Can Decision intent and actual Change be compared from source-owned Evidence?
- Can fidelity be judged without treating identical wording as the target?
- Is there a credible comparison for transition quality?

### H2 fit questions

- Is genuine human judgment required rather than a routine or predetermined act?
- Can Evidence, Interpretation, modeled output, Recommendation, and Decision be
  observed separately before Outcome?
- Can clarity be examined without substituting confidence or documentation
  completeness?
- Can downstream consequence remain visible enough to prevent clarity from
  becoming a self-referential documentation measure?

### H3 fit questions

- Can actual Change and Outcome be observed independently?
- Is Outcome timing short enough for bounded inquiry but long enough to be
  meaningful?
- Can desired, undesired, mixed, and indeterminate conditions remain legitimate?
- Is a later comparable situation plausible for observing correction or reuse of
  Learning?

These questions disclose different evidence demands. They do not prefer one
hypothesis.

## 11. Conditions That Prevent Credible Selection Support

Selection support is insufficient when:

- the hypothesis is too broad to be falsified in one bounded loop;
- the only available signals are activity, confidence, adoption, or completion;
- no accountable human Decision is present;
- Evidence and Interpretation cannot be separated;
- actual Change cannot be distinguished from intended Change;
- Outcome cannot be pre-stated, observed, or owned;
- the comparison is constructed only after seeing results;
- attribution would rely only on Management OS use followed by Outcome;
- the necessary Change is safety-critical, disproportionate, or irreversible;
- source access, participant consent, or authority is assumed rather than
  evidenced;
- a hypothesis requires simultaneous testing of the whole product; or
- AI scoring, ranking, or recommendation would effectively make the selection.

Failure of these conditions does not prove the hypothesis false. It means the
available context cannot support its credible selection or validation.

## 13. Neutral Decision-Support Summary

H1, H2, and H3 expose different parts of the principal post-M8 uncertainty:

- H1 concerns preservation of material meaning across the complete loop;
- H2 concerns the contribution of Evidence and semantic separation to accountable
  Decision clarity; and
- H3 concerns whether Outcome discipline supports correctable Learning after
  actual Change.

Each hypothesis is conceptually grounded in the research arc and each remains
empirically unconfirmed. Each could yield positive, negative, mixed, or
indeterminate evidence. Their information value depends on a real bounded
context, sufficient Evidence, observable consequence, credible comparison,
responsible exposure, and accountable human ownership.

No hypothesis is ranked, scored, recommended, or selected. The evidence currently
supports only the structure for future human consideration.
