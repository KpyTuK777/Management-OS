# 1. Purpose and boundary

This paper conducts a bounded, qualitative assessment of the six fixed
synthetic operational cases as possible training and preparation environments
for future hypothesis-selection consideration. It asks what each case can help
people inspect conceptually, what boundaries it exercises, and where its
modeled construction makes it unsuitable for empirical inference.

Synthetic cases may support preparation, learning-design exploration, and
architecture inspection. They may expose whether a training representation
keeps context, Evidence, Interpretation, Decision, Change, Outcome, Learning,
uncertainty, and human authority distinct and related. They do not provide
empirical Evidence, and no behavior within them can validate H1–H5.

Suitability for training does not equal suitability for real validation. This
assessment is not empirical validation, hypothesis selection, case selection,
ranking, scoring, recommendation, scenario authorization, validation
authorization, or implementation planning.

The following inequalities govern every observation in this paper:

```text
Synthetic modeled scenario
≠
Operational Evidence

Modeled Outcome
≠
Observed Outcome

Training usefulness
≠
Product-value proof
```

A case can be conceptually relevant to a Product Value Hypothesis without being
a valid subject, baseline, observation setting, or source of Evidence for that
hypothesis. Designed richness can demonstrate only the properties of the
training material. It cannot show that Management OS preserves real context,
supports real managers, amplifies experience, creates recoverable organizational
memory, or develops managerial capability.

# 2. Assessment dimensions

The dimensions below are qualitative and non-compensatory. A favorable
observation on one dimension cannot offset a material weakness on another.
They are not criteria, scores, weights, totals, ranks, thresholds, or an
authorization mechanism.

| Dimension | Qualitative question | Boundary retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context richness | Does the modeled case provide enough temporal, functional, and situational context to exercise reconstruction? | Designed detail is not observed context. |
| Evidence diversity | Does it include differing source types, provenance, disagreement, gaps, and possible corrections? | Modeled records are not Evidence. |
| Semantic-boundary coverage | Can it exercise distinctions among Information, Evidence, Interpretation, Hypothesis, Decision, Change, Outcome, and Learning? | Exercise does not redefine canonical semantics. |
| Decision traceability | Can a learner revisit basis, uncertainty, alternatives, rationale, authority, and timing? | Traceability does not make a Decision correct. |
| Change visibility | Can intended, authorized, actual, partial, and deviating Change remain distinct? | Modeled Change is not actual Change. |
| Outcome complexity | Does the case resist a single-metric or uniformly positive interpretation? | Modeled Outcome is not observed Outcome. |
| Attribution difficulty | Are confounding, time lag, displacement, missingness, or concurrent Changes preserved? | Difficulty must not be resolved by invented certainty. |
| Human-authority relevance | Does accountable judgment remain with attributable humans across Decisions and boundary disputes? | AI or workflow cannot inherit Decision authority. |
| Multi-factor reasoning | Does the case require relationships among several plausible factors without forcing one cause? | Plausibility is not causal Evidence. |
| Learning and replay potential | Can the preserved modeled history support later reflection without rewriting the original branch? | Replay is modeled reflection, not repeated experience. |
| Suitability for H1–H5 consideration | Can the case help clarify what one or more hypotheses would require conceptually? | Conceptual relevance is not hypothesis validation or selection. |
| Risk of misleading empirical interpretation | Could plausibility, completeness, or a designed Outcome be mistaken for empirical support? | Material risk constrains use; it is not compensated by training richness. |

The dimensions describe different questions and must remain separate. In
particular, high modeled detail can increase both training usefulness and the
risk that users mistake the narrative for historical reality.

# 3. Case-by-case assessment

The six assessments use the same structure and authority boundary. Their order
follows the fixed source pool and expresses no priority.

## 3.1 Repeated Order Fulfillment Breakdown

### Strongest training uses

- Reconstructing one recurring operational matter across six modeled orders,
  multiple systems, roles, clocks, and handoffs.
- Keeping the visible fulfillment failure distinct from possible upstream
  conditions in sales, planning, production, and transport.
- Comparing intended exception handling with partial adoption, bypass, and
  displaced effects such as expediting or longer promises.
- Practicing bounded diagnosis without turning recurrence into a predetermined
  single root cause.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1 — Operational Context Preservation:** fragmented order, promise,
  production, picking, and carrier context can be modeled as a recoverable
  relationship set.
- **H2 — Better Decision Support:** competing explanations, assumptions,
  rejected alternatives, and exception authority can be made inspectable.
- **H3 — Experience Amplification:** the completed modeled sequence can be
  revisited for missed upstream signals and displacement effects.
- **H4 — Organizational Operational Memory:** the case can represent how the
  interpretation of a repeated breakdown changed over time.
- **H5 — Manager Capability Development:** repeated reconstruction can exercise
  pattern recognition while retaining differences among occurrences.

This conceptual breadth validates none of these hypotheses and creates no
preference among them.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case exercises the distinction among customer promise, readiness state,
source record, conflicting account, diagnosis, human Decision, intended Change,
actual adoption, and modeled Outcome. Commercial exception approval and
operations ownership remain attributable human authorities. A system may
surface relationships and uncertainty but may not declare the Cause, approve a
promise exception, or decide that the intervention succeeded.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Fewer missed promises coexist with expedited shipments, longer quoted lead
time, and a bypassed process. Different denominators and possible displacement
prevent the modeled reduction from standing alone as success. The case does not
isolate the exception review from promise freezing or shared-state visibility.

### Replay and reflection potential

The six occurrences permit replay at order, handoff, and recurring-matter
levels. Reflection can compare what was knowable at each promise revision with
what appears visible later, test alternative interpretations, and inspect
whether the intervention addressed recurrence or only shifted its expression.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test real context loss, user reconstruction burden, Decision quality,
actual fulfillment improvement, customer value, organizational retention, or
manager development. It cannot establish that the modeled information would be
available, trustworthy, lawful to use, or consulted in practice.

### Risks of overinterpretation

The narrative may imply that cross-system continuity necessarily reveals a
coherent recurring problem or that a daily exception review is the appropriate
response. Designed timestamps can make real data integration appear easier
than it is. The mixed modeled Outcome may be misread as balanced empirical
realism even though every effect was authored in advance.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Real use would require a legitimately authorized operational case, source-owned
Evidence access, a relevant fragmented baseline, actual users, observed
Decision and Change behavior, stable metric definitions, adequate Outcome
windows, customer and operational consequence boundaries, and an attribution
approach able to preserve concurrent factors and negative or null findings.

## 3.2 Procurement Approval Delay

### Strongest training uses

- Decomposing total elapsed time across requester, technical, finance,
  procurement, vendor, and clarification intervals.
- Separating formal authority, assumed emergency authority, policy
  interpretation, workflow state, and verbal recollection.
- Testing whether a readiness representation clarifies handoffs without
  converting disputed authority into an automatic answer.
- Inspecting the tradeoff between earlier preparation effort and later queue
  time.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1:** request revisions, policy version, quotation validity, technical
  dependencies, and clarification history can support modeled reconstruction.
- **H2:** urgency, equipment risk, cost, schedule risk, alternatives, and
  authority ambiguity can be displayed for accountable judgment.
- **H3:** replay can reveal where later knowledge changes the interpretation of
  apparent approval delay.
- **H4:** the evolution from “waiting for finance” to a multi-owner account can
  be represented as recoverable managerial history.
- **H5:** the case can exercise proportional reasoning about completeness,
  urgency, and escalation without offering a universal procurement pattern.

No conceptual association shows that Management OS produces the hypothesized
effect.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case separates a delay observation from responsibility and causal
hypothesis; policy text from its human interpretation; queue state from actual
work; and ordinary approval from exception authority. The modeled procurement,
finance, technical, maintenance, and exception roles retain their own authority.
Workflow routing cannot waive policy or infer approval.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Shorter approval-queue time occurs with longer intake preparation, unnecessary
exception routing, and unchanged vendor response. Total cycle time and burden
remain distinct from one queue metric. External response and request mix weaken
attribution to the pre-check.

### Replay and reflection potential

The case supports a time-segment replay: what each role had received, what was
missing, what authority was believed to exist, and what could have been decided
at each handoff. It can also test alternative routing branches without claiming
how the supplier or approvers would actually have behaved.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test actual policy compliance, procurement lead-time improvement,
equipment risk reduction, vendor behavior, real authority comprehension, or
the burden and adoption of a readiness view. It cannot establish whether any
real delay is primarily caused by missing information or approval design.

### Risks of overinterpretation

The structured interval breakdown may imply that all elapsed time is observable
and assignable. The scenario can encourage blame toward the role owning the
longest interval or present the pre-check as an obvious answer. Modeled policy
clarity may conceal real legal, financial, or delegation ambiguity.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Required conditions would include the applicable policy and delegation record,
authorized access to workflow and communication Evidence, comparable real
requests, direct observation of clarification work and user burden, vendor
dependencies, consequence measures, privacy boundaries, and human authority to
define and observe a bounded intervention.

## 3.3 Quality Decline After Capacity Increase

### Strongest training uses

- Reasoning across simultaneous capacity, speed, staffing, supplier, product
  mix, and inspection Changes.
- Preserving changes in measurement intensity and denominator semantics before
  interpreting quality trends.
- Tracing a compound Decision into multiple intended actions and actual
  deviations by line and shift.
- Holding action urgency and weak causal isolation together without inventing a
  single Cause.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1:** line settings, rosters, training, material lots, sampling plans,
  throughput, defects, and returns can be related across time.
- **H2:** competing hypotheses and the Evidence for and against them can support
  uncertainty-visible human reasoning.
- **H3:** later product-family and material-lot patterns can prompt structured
  re-engagement with the initial interpretation.
- **H4:** the case can preserve changes in measurement, intervention rationale,
  deviations, and later understanding.
- **H5:** repeated review can exercise confounder recognition, denominator
  discipline, and resistance to premature causal closure.

These are training affordances, not observations of product effect.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case distinguishes production Information, quality Evidence, metric
definition, Interpretation, causal Hypothesis, scoped human Decision, actual
configuration, and modeled Outcome. Production, quality, engineering, and
supplier-control responsibilities remain separate. Management OS may preserve
the reasoning basis but cannot establish Cause or exercise production authority.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Improved first-pass yield coexists with lower output, lagging returns,
product-family variation, lot concentration, and continued configuration
deviation. Several interventions happen together. Sampling changes and
time-lagged customer returns make before-and-after attribution especially weak.

### Replay and reflection potential

The case supports replay from the initial capacity Decision, first quality
signal, intervention Decision, and later Outcome windows. Alternative branches
can vary speed, training, or material treatment separately for reasoning
practice, while retaining that none represents a known counterfactual reality.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test real quality decline, causal effects of speed, staffing or
supplier lots, metric reliability, customer consequence, actual production
authority, or Management OS contribution. It cannot show that managers would
notice or use the preserved relationships under operational pressure.

### Risks of overinterpretation

Because the scenario explicitly supplies multiple plausible factors, learners
may treat the authored set as exhaustive. The later lot concentration may be
read as a hidden correct answer. A rich configuration record can create false
confidence that unmeasured factors and selection effects have been controlled.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Real validation would require governed production and quality Evidence,
consistent definitions and denominators, an observable pre-change baseline,
actual configuration and adoption records, sufficient lag for customer
Outcomes, product and supplier context, accountable operational Decisions, and
a design that can preserve rather than erase confounding and mixed findings.

## 3.4 Cross-functional Sales–Operations Coordination Failure

### Strongest training uses

- Separating opportunity, forecast, feasibility, capacity reservation, and
  customer commitment across sales, operations, and engineering.
- Reconstructing what each function could know from the capacity snapshot and
  language available at Decision time.
- Preserving divergent interpretations of an informal discussion without
  inventing agreement.
- Testing attributable joint coordination while retaining distinct commercial
  and operational authority.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1:** CRM transitions, capacity snapshots, communications, specifications,
  and dependencies can form a modeled cross-functional context.
- **H2:** uncertainty, stale information, alternatives, and separate authority
  can be made visible around a commitment Decision.
- **H3:** replay from each functional perspective can reveal missed semantic
  assumptions without rewriting the conversation.
- **H4:** the evolution from provisional feasibility to disputed commitment can
  be preserved beyond disconnected records.
- **H5:** reflection can exercise perspective-taking, semantic precision, and
  recognition of authority limits.

Multiple conceptual relationships do not imply selection or empirical breadth.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case directly exercises state meaning and handoff fidelity. A forecast is
not a reservation; feasibility is not approval; an internal reservation is not
a customer commitment; a recorded status is not proof of shared understanding.
Commercial, operations, and engineering authority remain attributable and
cannot be collapsed into generic consensus or automated workflow completion.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Fewer ambiguous commitments coexist with slower commercial response and a
formally completed feasibility record based on stale capacity. Traceability,
speed, semantic correctness, and delivery performance are separate possible
Outcomes. The scenario shows no customer or financial consequence that can be
attributed to the state design.

### Replay and reflection potential

The case supports role-based replay at the moment of informal discussion,
status transition, engineering dependency discovery, and customer confirmation.
It can reveal hindsight effects by hiding later information during early branch
review and then restoring the preserved sequence.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test actual cross-functional behavior, adoption of explicit states,
revenue or delivery effects, customer trust, capacity accuracy, or whether
recorded traceability materially supports real Decisions. It does not validate
any coordination workflow or authority arrangement.

### Risks of overinterpretation

Explicit states may appear to solve what in reality depends on relationships,
incentives, timeliness, and negotiation. The case may imply that the informal
conversation is the failure point or that semantic precision always outweighs
response speed. Authored role perspectives may be mistaken for complete human
accounts.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Missing conditions include an authorized live operational boundary, actual
source records and participant accounts, known state and authority definitions,
observable baseline coordination behavior, current capacity data, real user
interaction with the proposed relationships, customer and operational Outcomes,
and safeguards against retrospective blame and selective documentation.

## 3.5 Shift Schedule Change With Unexpected Consequences

### Strongest training uses

- Distinguishing schedule policy, published roster, swaps, absence, and actual
  worked practice.
- Holding workforce, customer-service, skill-coverage, fatigue, and supervisory
  context together without reducing them to one metric.
- Comparing intended handoff reduction with unexpected correction, sick-leave,
  preference, and coverage effects.
- Exercising proportional response under qualitative and quantitative
  uncertainty.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1:** rationale, rosters, attendance, workload, handoffs, employee accounts,
  service measures, and concurrent changes can be related over time.
- **H2:** service and workforce tradeoffs, uncertainty, and human concerns can
  remain visible to the accountable owner.
- **H3:** later consequences can support reflection on assumptions available at
  the original schedule Decision.
- **H4:** policy evolution, actual practice, dissent, and mixed Outcomes can be
  represented as recoverable operational history.
- **H5:** review can exercise context-sensitive judgment, unintended-consequence
  recognition, and resistance to single-metric optimization.

The synthetic workforce narrative cannot provide Evidence for any hypothesis.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case distinguishes policy from actual Change, employee statement from
causal Evidence, service Outcome from workforce consequence, and observation
from attribution. Scheduling, employment, privacy, and operational authorities
remain with legitimate humans. AI reasoning cannot decide acceptable labor
tradeoffs, infer fatigue as fact, or replace protected employee voice.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Peak wait time and late-shift corrections improve while sick leave remains
elevated, handoffs rise, and employee accounts remain divided. Demand change
and a supervisor vacancy confound the comparison. Short observation time
prevents the modeled pattern from representing a stable effect.

### Replay and reflection potential

The original rationale, intended roster, actual worked roster, and later
consequences support a layered replay. Reflection can compare what was expected
with what emerged and explore alternative overlap or assignment branches while
retaining worker impact and authority constraints.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test fatigue, employee sentiment, labor compliance, schedule
preference, service improvement, absence causality, transfer to another
workforce, or Management OS value. It cannot show that modeled qualitative
accounts are representative or that any schedule Change is appropriate.

### Risks of overinterpretation

The case may encourage causal attribution from schedule length to sick leave or
corrections. A seemingly balanced set of employee comments can conceal the
absence of real participation, power dynamics, and privacy risk. The modeled
remedy may appear ethically or operationally endorsed merely because it is
included.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Real work would require lawful authority, workforce participation, privacy and
access controls, actual schedule and attendance Evidence, stable service and
workforce definitions, demand and skill context, a sufficient observation
window, protected dissent, implementation-fidelity observation, and authority
to address rather than merely record adverse consequences.

## 3.6 Apparent Incident Reduction That Was Actually Reporting Suppression

### Strongest training uses

- Detecting that a measurement and reporting mechanism changed before treating
  a dashboard decline as operational improvement.
- Distinguishing incident occurrence, submission, review, classification,
  dashboard inclusion, proxy signals, and absence of reports.
- Preserving original submissions, corrections, missingness, privacy, dissent,
  and protected escalation.
- Practicing reasoning where the unobserved population cannot be reconstructed
  and apparent positive Outcome may be misleading.

### Product Value Hypotheses it can help examine conceptually

- **H1:** reporting rules, original and classified reports, proxy signals,
  accounts, definitions, and missingness can be represented with provenance.
- **H2:** uncertainty and measurement change can be surfaced before accountable
  safety judgment, without AI declaring an incident or Cause.
- **H3:** the case can be replayed to inspect how a plausible early
  interpretation changed when additional modeled signals appeared.
- **H4:** original submissions and correction history can represent evolution
  of understanding without converting historical classification into truth.
- **H5:** reflection can exercise missing-data discipline, metric skepticism,
  authority awareness, and resistance to false positive narratives.

The case's apparent richness does not make it safety Evidence or product proof.

### Semantic and authority boundaries it exercises

The case separates absence of reports from evidence of absence, proxy signal
from incident Evidence, original submission from later classification, and
dashboard inclusion from underlying Outcome. Safety classification, response,
privacy, access, and personnel Decisions remain with authorized humans. A
system must not suppress, invent, adjudicate, or expose protected reports.

### Modeled Outcome complexity

Reported incidents rise after direct reporting returns, near-miss detail
increases, and the underlying incident rate remains unknown. The apparent
earlier reduction may reflect suppression, but its magnitude and any real
safety trend cannot be established. Reporting Outcome and safety Outcome move
on different semantic planes.

### Replay and reflection potential

The case supports staged replay of dashboard interpretation, anonymous and
proxy signals, discovery of the reporting-rule change, and later restoration of
direct reporting. It is suitable for testing whether later information prompts
revision without rewriting what was knowable or treating the alternative
branch as actual history.

### What it cannot test

It cannot test real incident rates, suppression, misconduct, worker trust,
reporting behavior, safety improvement, privacy protection, causal effects, or
Management OS value. It cannot justify a safety, personnel, or reporting-policy
Decision.

### Risks of overinterpretation

The title and narrative reveal a causal interpretation and can become a hidden
preferred answer. Learners may overstate “suppression” despite incomplete
modeled Evidence or treat proxies as confirmed incidents. The case's moral and
safety salience may amplify AI-generated certainty, hindsight bias, blame, or
inappropriate disclosure.

### Missing conditions for real-world validation

Missing conditions include protected and lawful reporting channels, independent
human safety authority, privacy and non-retaliation safeguards, access to
source-owned records, reliable metric definitions, observation of actual user
behavior, a defensible basis for reasoning about missing reports, sufficient
Outcome time, and procedures for responding to safety Evidence without making
validation goals primary.

# 4. H1–H5 coverage analysis

This section records conceptual preparation coverage, not case or hypothesis
selection. A case may cover several hypotheses because the hypotheses concern
related but distinct parts of the operational loop. Conceptual coverage creates
no causal Evidence, does not establish suitability for empirical validation,
and does not prioritize a hypothesis.

## H1 — Operational Context Preservation

All six cases may support preparation for H1 consideration, through different
forms of modeled reconstruction:

- **Repeated Order Fulfillment Breakdown:** continuity across occurrences,
  functions, systems, promise revisions, and handoff timestamps.
- **Procurement Approval Delay:** request versions, policy context, owned time
  intervals, vendor dependencies, and authority assumptions.
- **Quality Decline After Capacity Increase:** configuration, staffing, material,
  product mix, inspection changes, denominators, and lagged returns.
- **Sales–Operations Coordination Failure:** cross-functional state meaning,
  communications, capacity snapshots, and engineering dependencies.
- **Shift Schedule Change:** Decision rationale, planned and actual rosters,
  workforce accounts, demand, and service consequences.
- **Apparent Incident Reduction:** reporting definitions, original and revised
  classifications, proxy signals, privacy boundaries, and missingness.

The modeled availability of context cannot show that equivalent real context
would be available, preserved better than a baseline, usable, or worth its
burden.

## H2 — Better Decision Support

All six can help inspect what evidence-grounded, uncertainty-visible,
human-accountable reasoning might require:

- fulfillment supplies competing upstream explanations and displacement risk;
- procurement supplies urgency, policy, risk, elapsed time, and disputed
  exception authority;
- quality supplies confounding, metric discontinuity, and compound Changes;
- sales–operations supplies semantic disagreement, stale context, and separate
  functional authority;
- shift scheduling supplies workforce-service tradeoffs and qualitative human
  input; and
- incident reporting supplies missing-not-at-random information, correction
  history, and protected safety judgment.

Training can inspect the support structure. It cannot demonstrate a better
Decision, better-supported real reasoning, adoption, or product contribution.

## H3 — Experience Amplification

Each fixed case has a completed modeled history that may support deliberate
re-engagement:

- fulfillment can reveal recurrence and displaced effects;
- procurement can revise a simplistic “finance delay” account;
- quality can expose later patterns, confounders, and denominator changes;
- sales–operations can replay different functional perspectives;
- shift scheduling can compare initial assumptions with unexpected human and
  service consequences; and
- incident reporting can revisit an apparent positive trend after the
  observation mechanism becomes visible.

This is designed reflection on synthetic material. It is not amplification of
a lived operational experience and cannot show that later reflection is richer
than ordinary recollection in real use.

## H4 — Organizational Operational Memory

All six can model recoverability of evolving understanding rather than a pile
of final records. The relevant histories differ: repeated-order pattern
formation; procurement responsibility revision; quality and measurement
evolution; cross-functional commitment meaning; schedule policy versus worked
practice; and original incident submission versus later classification.

Synthetic preservation cannot create organizational memory. There is no real
organization, personnel transition, reliance, access practice, retention
burden, correction dispute, or historical authority to observe.

## H5 — Manager Capability Development

The cases may support preparation for different forms of judgment practice:

- distinguishing recurrence from superficial similarity;
- decomposing delay without defaulting to blame;
- reasoning with interacting factors and weak attribution;
- maintaining cross-functional semantic and authority boundaries;
- recognizing unintended consequences and human impact; and
- challenging apparent improvement when the reporting mechanism changes.

Practice performance on authored cases cannot show development, transfer,
acceleration, durable judgment change, or causal contribution by Management OS.
Repeated exposure could instead teach the authored story or interface. H5 would
remain empirically Unknown.

# 6. Principle of sufficient investigative depth

The following owner product insight is preserved as a research input, not
adopted as canonical method:

> Investigation should seek sufficient Evidence-backed depth for a
> proportionate Change, not maximum theoretical depth.

The synthetic cases can help explore what this principle might demand without
creating a checklist, lifecycle, threshold, or universal stopping rule.

## Recurrence

Repeated Order Fulfillment Breakdown can distinguish several occurrences from
one asserted pattern. Incident reporting can show that a trend in reports is
not automatically a trend in underlying events. Quality and scheduling can ask
whether effects recur across shifts, products, or time rather than relying on
an aggregate. Synthetic recurrence remains authored, so it cannot establish
how much repetition would be sufficient in reality.

## Spread

Sales–operations and fulfillment can explore spread across functions and
handoffs. Quality can explore spread across line, shift, product family, and
material lot. Scheduling can explore spread across employee groups, time
periods, and service Outcomes. Preparation should retain that broader spread
may justify broader inquiry but does not automatically justify a broader
Change.

## Shared explanation

Each case can test whether several observations have a defensible shared
explanation or merely temporal proximity. Fulfillment and quality make
premature unification especially visible; procurement and sales–operations
show how semantic or authority ambiguity can look like a single delay or
coordination cause; incident reporting shows how a measurement change can
explain a reported pattern without explaining the underlying operation.

## Previous correction history

Fulfillment includes repeated handling and bypass, procurement includes request
revisions, quality includes configuration and inspection changes, scheduling
includes schedule adaptation, and incident reporting includes reclassification
and correction history. These can exercise the question of whether earlier
corrections changed the matter, displaced it, or failed to persist. Authored
history cannot show actual organizational response or recurrence.

## Proportionality between problem scale and Change scale

The cases permit reflection on whether the proposed Change is commensurate with
the understood problem: an exception queue for recurring mixed orders, a
readiness view for procurement ambiguity, compound containment for quality
risk, explicit states for cross-functional commitment, bounded overlap changes
for schedule consequences, and reporting-path separation for possible
suppression. None of these modeled Changes is endorsed. A learner should be
able to question whether each is too narrow, too broad, too early, or unsupported.

Sufficient depth remains contextual human judgment constrained by Evidence,
risk, authority, urgency, reversibility, and consequence. The cases may reveal
questions about proportionality; they cannot establish a canonical method for
answering them.

# 7. Synthetic replay boundary

All six cases contain enough designed sequence to support some future modeled
replay, provided their information is staged rather than revealed as an
omniscient final narrative. Richness for replay means only that the case has
modeled context, Decision, Change, Outcome, uncertainty, and later information
that can be revisited.

The replay boundary is fixed:

```text
Historical reality
≠
Replay branch

Alternative Decision
≠
Historical Decision

Modeled Change
≠
Actual Change

Modeled Outcome
≠
Observed Outcome
```

- **Repeated Order Fulfillment Breakdown** supports occurrence-by-occurrence
  and recurring-matter replay, including alternative exception handling.
- **Procurement Approval Delay** supports handoff-timeline replay with different
  information and authority states.
- **Quality Decline After Capacity Increase** supports multi-factor replay, but
  its several simultaneous modeled Changes make counterfactual stories
  especially unsafe.
- **Sales–Operations Coordination Failure** supports perspective and
  decision-time context replay across functions.
- **Shift Schedule Change** supports policy-to-practice replay with unexpected
  workforce and service consequences.
- **Apparent Incident Reduction** supports interpretation revision as reporting
  rules, proxy signals, and missingness become visible, but must not turn its
  titled explanation into certainty.

Replay may support reflection, alternative-hypothesis generation, and
inspection of what was knowable at a modeled moment. It cannot reconstruct what
would actually have happened. An alternative branch is a reasoning artifact,
not an unobserved historical fact, equivalent experience, causal estimate, or
empirical Outcome.

# 8. Unsuitability and rejection conditions

A synthetic case should not be used even for preparation when any condition
below is present and cannot be corrected without changing the case's identity.
Rejection here means rejection from a bounded training use, not selection of an
alternative case.

- **Semantic simplicity that teaches little:** the case collapses Evidence,
  Interpretation, Decision, Change, and Outcome into one obvious narrative.
- **Hidden preferred answer:** wording, title, facilitator material, or supplied
  facts make one diagnosis or Decision the expected answer while presenting the
  task as open reasoning.
- **Unrealistic Evidence completeness:** every material fact is conveniently
  available, consistent, timely, attributable, and comparable, concealing real
  gaps and access constraints.
- **Outcome designed to reward one Decision:** a positive modeled result is
  authored to confirm the intervention, or adverse and null consequences are
  excluded.
- **AI-generated certainty:** AI output converts modeled plausibility into fact,
  supplies unsupported Cause, resolves disagreement, or presents a branch as
  predicted reality.
- **Collapsed authority:** the case lets a workflow, record, AI, facilitator, or
  generic team exercise authority belonging to accountable humans or distinct
  roles.
- **Causal story known in advance:** the exercise asks learners to discover a
  predetermined Cause rather than assess competing explanations and limits.
- **Insufficient uncertainty:** missing information, measurement limits,
  implementation variance, confounding, dissent, and alternative explanations
  are absent or merely decorative.
- **Risk of being mistaken for real Evidence:** provenance, labeling, storage,
  presentation, or reuse could allow synthetic records or Outcomes to enter
  organizational memory, reporting, validation, or Decisions as historical
  material.

Additional grounds for non-use include privacy-like fictional detail that could
be mistaken for a real person, unsafe domain framing that invites operational
action, internal contradictions that prevent the intended semantic exercise,
or a facilitator process that reveals later information before a decision-time
replay. Training richness cannot compensate for a material authority,
provenance, or empirical-confusion risk.
