## 3. Finding

The Reader is structurally correct but blocked for first-user comprehension.

Wave 4 correctly made Ukrainian the primary visible language and moved source
metadata behind progressive disclosure. It did not establish the required
distinction between an accessible explanation and a close translation of source
or regulatory language. Long sentence structure, institutional phrasing,
methodological labels, and claim identifiers still shape the first reading layer.

This is not a request for simpler decoration. It is a missing content-projection
contract inside the already fixed product behavior:

```text
fixed historical claim
→ source-faithful plain-language explanation
→ evidence and attribution summary
→ exact original inspection
```

The Product Realization Foundation already permits AI or the system to summarize
within fixed claims and requires plain-language explanations of repository terms.
It also requires claim/source depth on deliberate inspection. Therefore the
correction does not require new canonical, Product, Interaction Architecture, or
Engineering Architecture meaning if it remains within the boundaries defined
below.

## 5. Required three-level content model

### Level 1 — Plain-language explanation

Primary reading layer. It answers: **what can the user understand from the fixed
record at this point?**

Required properties:

- concise, natural Ukrainian;
- source-faithful and no stronger than its support;
- one claim by default; more than one claim only with clause-level traceability;
- explicit attribution when a statement comes from a later SEC finding, Knight
  statement, filing, or other attributable source;
- explicit uncertainty whenever the fixed claim contains or depends on it;
- no Claim ID, class code, commit, blob, or locator in the normal first scan;
- no implied actor knowledge, motive, causal mechanism, Root Cause, or
  counterfactual result.

Level 1 is a presentation derived from governed material. It is not a historical
claim, source, accepted Evidence, analyst conclusion, or canonical record.

### Level 2 — Evidence and attribution summary

Secondary comprehension layer. It answers: **why may this explanation be shown,
who established it, and what limits how we read it?**

Required fields:

- attribution in human language;
- source publication date;
- event time when the fixed record provides it;
- explicit distinction between event time and later publication/finding time;
- claim class translated into human meaning rather than only A–E notation;
- hindsight status;
- confidence or uncertainty boundary in accessible Ukrainian;
- source scope or contradiction warning where material.

Level 2 must be reachable from the Level 1 passage without opening repository or
Git detail.

### Level 3 — Original inspection

Verification layer. It answers: **what exactly is fixed, where is it supported,
and how was it classified?**

Required fields:

- exact fixed claim text, clearly labelled;
- a bounded verbatim source excerpt resolved through the fixed locator; if the
  source cannot be accessed or the excerpt cannot be verified, retain the source
  identity and show that limitation instead of synthesizing an excerpt;
- literal Ukrainian translation, clearly distinguished from Level 1;
- exact Claim ID;
- claim class code and complete methodological label;
- source identity;
- exact citation locator;
- attribution and source date;
- commit and blob identities where part of fixed source resolution;
- complete historical-knowledge, hindsight, confidence, contradiction,
  Evidence-gap, Replay-eligibility, and reviewer metadata;
- direct link to the fixed phase and source where available.

Level 3 remains collapsed until deliberate inspection. Its availability is
mandatory; its visual prominence is not.

## 9. Plain-language transformation contract

Every transformed passage must carry an inspectable transformation record with:

| Obligation | Required proof |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed source support | Exact source claim ID or bounded supporting claim set |
| No added fact | Every factual clause maps to fixed text or is removed |
| Preserved uncertainty | Every material unknown, scope limit, contradiction, or later-source boundary remains visible at Level 1 or immediately adjacent Level 2 |
| No invented causation | Temporal sequence, co-occurrence, or regulatory finding cannot be rewritten as causal proof |
| No invented actor knowledge | No person or group is said to know, notice, interpret, decide, or intend without fixed support |
| Unchanged attribution | `SEC later found`, `Knight stated`, filing-reported, and fixed-record absence remain distinguishable |
| Unchanged historical/Replay boundary | Explanation cannot enable Replay, counterfactual execution, or event-time hindsight |
| Direct traceability | Level 1 resolves to Level 2 and Level 3 without identity loss |
| Original availability | Exact original and literal translation remain inspectable |

Additional transformation rules:

1. Prefer one Level 1 passage per fixed claim.
2. Split long source claims into short clauses only when all clauses retain the
   same attribution, time basis, and confidence.
3. Do not combine facts from differently timed sources without identifying each
   source and later-source status.
4. Do not convert absence of public Evidence into proof that an event did not
   happen.
5. Do not replace qualified quantities (`approximately`, `more than`) with exact
   quantities.
6. Preserve distinctions among event time, observation time, statement time,
   filing period, and later regulatory publication time.
7. If faithful simplification cannot be produced, show the source-bound wording
   and mark the passage as requiring human editorial review.
