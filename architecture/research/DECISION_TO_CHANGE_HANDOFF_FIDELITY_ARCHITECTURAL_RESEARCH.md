# Decision-to-Change Handoff Fidelity — Architectural Research

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No Handoff object, Change object, workflow, lifecycle, governance mechanism, validation, milestone, backlog, or work created
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper investigates the quality of the transition from accountable Human
Decision to Actual Change in Management OS. It asks what Decision meaning must
survive, how the source owner may interpret the intended Change, which risks can
distort or exceed the Decision, what Evidence can establish fidelity, and
whether existing canonical capabilities appear conceptually sufficient.

The term **handoff fidelity** is research shorthand for the evidence-backed
relationship among Decision intent, communicated meaning, source-owned Change
interpretation, and Actual Change. It does not create a canonical object,
semantic kind, metric, score, state, gate, protocol, or capability.

## 3. Preserved Semantic Boundaries

```text
Recommendation ≠ Decision
Decision ≠ Change
Decision authority ≠ Change authority
Intended Change ≠ Authorized Change
Authorized Change ≠ Actual Change
Actual Change ≠ Observed Change
Change ≠ Outcome
Outcome ≠ Causal proof
Learning ≠ Success claim
```

Additional boundaries:

- Modeled / Scenario Output is not Evidence, Fact, Recommendation authority,
  Decision, Actual Change, or Outcome.
- Confidence, presentation, reuse, similarity, automation, and completion cannot
  promote one semantic kind into another.
- AI may assist interpretation but cannot own Decision, source mutation, or
  fidelity verification.
- A source-owned execution record can establish activity within its scope but
  not observed operational Outcome.
- Favorable Outcome cannot retroactively prove perfect handoff fidelity.
- Unfavorable Outcome cannot by itself prove handoff failure.

## 4. Research Meaning of Handoff Fidelity

Handoff fidelity concerns whether enough of the accountable human Decision
survives the transition into source-owned action for the Actual Change to remain
recognizably bounded by that Decision.

It is not literal copying. The Change owner may need to interpret the Decision
within source-specific constraints, operational context, professional judgment,
and current Evidence. Fidelity therefore means **preserved accountable meaning
with visible source-owned interpretation**, not mechanical identity between a
Decision statement and execution record.

A research-form fidelity question is:

> Does attributable Evidence show that the Actual Change preserved the Decision
> owner’s intended operational condition, bounded effect, authority limits, and
> material constraints, while making any source-owned interpretation, deviation,
> partial execution, interruption, or substitution explicit?

This question does not create a pass/fail state or validation result.

## 5. Decision-to-Change Transition

### 5.1 Four distinct meanings

The transition involves at least four distinguishable meanings.

#### Decision intent

What the accountable human seeks to accomplish or avoid in operational reality.
Intent may include:

- intended operational condition;
- reason the condition matters;
- accepted tradeoffs;
- prohibited consequences;
- expected horizon;
- uncertainty the human accepts; and
- conditions for reconsideration.

Intent answers **why and toward what consequence** the Decision points.

#### Decision meaning

The complete bounded judgment made by the human, including:

- decision subject and identity;
- selected disposition;
- scope and affected context;
- rationale and Evidence basis;
- assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations;
- intended Change boundary;
- authority and source constraints;
- expected condition;
- timing, prerequisites, and dependencies;
- interruption, correction, revocation, or no-action conditions; and
- explicit non-effects.

Decision meaning includes intent but is broader. It answers **what was actually
decided and within which bounds**.

#### Change interpretation

The source owner’s or authorized executor’s understanding of how the Decision
relates to the source-owned operational reality. It may include:

- source-specific action available;
- operational prerequisites;
- translation of broad intent into a bounded intervention;
- affected records, participants, or responsibilities;
- source constraints and professional judgment;
- feasibility and timing;
- ambiguity or conflict detected;
- need for clarification; and
- proposed deviation, substitution, or interruption.

Change interpretation is not the human Decision and cannot silently amend it.
It is source-owned understanding necessary to act responsibly.

#### Actual Change

What operational alteration actually occurred, as supported by source-owned
Evidence. It may be:

- faithful to the intended Change;
- narrower or broader;
- partial or staged;
- delayed;
- substituted;
- interrupted;
- corrected or reversed;
- combined with another Change;
- refused or not executed; or
- unknown because source Observation is insufficient.

Actual Change answers **what happened**, not what was intended, authorized,
reported, or expected.

### 5.2 Relationship among the four meanings

```text
Decision intent
  ⊂ Decision meaning
        ↓ authority-preserving handoff
Change interpretation
        ↓ source-owned authorization and action
Actual Change
```

The diagram is explanatory, not a workflow. Each arrow may contain uncertainty,
clarification, refusal, interruption, correction, or return to human Decision.

### 5.3 Why the meanings cannot collapse

- Intent without complete Decision meaning may omit bounds and authority.
- Decision meaning without source interpretation may be infeasible or ambiguous
  in operational reality.
- Change interpretation without attributable Decision may become an unowned
  Recommendation or unauthorized Decision substitute.
- Actual Change without Decision and interpretation trace may be unrelated,
  routine, externally caused, or unauthorized.
- A complete execution record does not prove the source understood or preserved
  Decision intent.

## 6. Meaning That Should Survive the Handoff

No universal field set is created here. Conceptually, fidelity may require
preservation of the following meaning when material to the bounded Decision.

### 6.1 Decision identity and accountability

- which Decision is being acted upon;
- who made it;
- when it was made;
- under what authority; and
- whether it remains current, revoked, superseded, or conditional.

### 6.2 Intended operational condition

- current condition relevant to the Decision;
- intended condition;
- material difference sought;
- consequence to avoid; and
- expected observation horizon.

### 6.3 Scope and non-scope

- affected operational subject;
- included and excluded sources, participants, locations, or contexts;
- bounded blast radius; and
- effects explicitly not authorized.

### 6.4 Evidence basis and uncertainty

- material Evidence supporting the judgment;
- contrary, missing, stale, or conflicting Evidence;
- assumptions and hypotheses;
- confidence limitations; and
- conditions under which new Evidence should trigger reconsideration.

The source owner need not receive every analytical detail, but omission of
material Evidence or uncertainty may distort Change interpretation.

### 6.5 Authority and ownership

- Decision authority;
- Change authorization authority;
- source owner;
- executor or responsible actor;
- affected external authority; and
- conditions requiring further human judgment.

### 6.6 Constraints and dependencies

- legal, governance, operational, ethical, professional, source, and capacity
  constraints;
- prerequisites and dependencies;
- timing and duration;
- reversibility or correction considerations; and
- interaction with concurrent Changes.

### 6.7 Interruption and exception meaning

- conditions for stopping or delaying;
- conditions for clarification;
- permitted deviation;
- prohibited substitution;
- revocation or reversal condition; and
- route back to accountable human Decision when source interpretation cannot
  remain within bounds.

This does not define an escalation protocol or lifecycle.

### 6.8 Expected observation and verification boundary

- what source evidence could show Actual Change;
- what operational condition would later matter to Outcome;
- who owns each observation; and
- distinction between execution completion and consequence verification.

## 7. Handoff Fidelity Risks

### 7.1 Semantic loss

**Phenomenon:** material Decision meaning does not reach the Change owner.

Possible losses include:

- intended condition;
- excluded effects;
- uncertainty;
- contrary Evidence;
- authority limits;
- reversibility;
- prerequisites;
- timing; or
- verification expectations.

**Possible consequence:** the Change may be locally coherent but no longer
answer the human Decision.

**Evidence question:** Which material parts of Decision meaning were available
to the Change owner at interpretation and execution time?

### 7.2 Interpretation drift

**Phenomenon:** source-owned interpretation gradually or immediately changes the
Decision meaning without explicit human reconsideration.

Possible mechanisms:

- ambiguous language;
- local convention;
- optimization for source convenience;
- changed context;
- repeated retelling;
- assumption treated as instruction;
- Recommendation treated as requirement;
- modeled output treated as expected truth; or
- executor preference.

**Possible consequence:** Actual Change appears related but reflects a different
Decision.

**Evidence question:** Where did source interpretation add, remove, narrow,
broaden, or substitute material meaning, and was that difference visible to the
Decision owner?

### 7.3 Partial execution

**Phenomenon:** only part of the authorized and intended Change occurs.

Possible forms:

- some actions completed and others absent;
- only one affected source changed;
- timing or duration shortened;
- prerequisite failed;
- Change interrupted or rolled back;
- responsible actors applied it inconsistently; or
- execution records overstate completion.

**Possible consequence:** Outcome analysis assumes an intervention that did not
occur fully.

**Evidence question:** What did source-owned Observation show about actual scope,
timing, duration, interruption, and deviation?

### 7.4 Unauthorized modification

**Phenomenon:** Actual Change exceeds, differs from, or bypasses Decision or
source authority.

Possible forms:

- scope expanded;
- protected or excluded source altered;
- additional action added;
- human judgment replaced by automation;
- condition changed after authorization without reconsideration;
- a Recommendation executed as if approved; or
- executor possessed capability but not authority.

**Possible consequence:** operational effect cannot be attributed to the bounded
Decision, and source ownership is violated.

**Evidence question:** Which authority covered each actual alteration, and where
did execution exceed that authority?

### 7.5 Context mismatch

**Phenomenon:** Decision meaning was valid for one context, but Change occurred
under materially different conditions.

Possible changes include:

- operational demand;
- staffing or capacity;
- external policy;
- source state;
- participant needs;
- evidence currency;
- dependencies;
- concurrent interventions; or
- timing.

**Possible consequence:** faithful literal execution may become unfaithful to
the Decision’s underlying intent.

**Evidence question:** Which context assumptions remained true at execution, and
which differences required human reconsideration?

### 7.6 Ownership ambiguity

**Phenomenon:** Decision owner, Change authorizer, source owner, executor, or
verification owner is missing, conflicting, or assumed.

**Possible consequence:** no one can own interpretation, authorization,
deviation, or correction; activity may proceed without accountable effect.

**Evidence question:** Which person or source owned each distinct judgment and
effect, and where was ownership inferred rather than attributable?

### 7.7 Authorization loss

**Phenomenon:** an attributable Decision exists, but valid Change authorization
does not survive or never existed.

**Possible consequence:** Decision without Change, blocked execution, rejected
mutation, or unauthorized action.

**Evidence question:** Was authorization current, sufficient, and applicable to
the Actual Change rather than only the intended action?

### 7.8 Fidelity theater

**Phenomenon:** records appear complete and aligned while material operational
meaning or Actual Change differs.

Possible mechanisms:

- copied Decision text without source understanding;
- checklist completion;
- status synchronization;
- AI-generated summaries;
- self-attested completion;
- activity metrics; or
- retrospective narrative adjusted after Outcome.

**Possible consequence:** documentation alignment is mistaken for operational
fidelity.

**Evidence question:** What source-owned Evidence connects Decision meaning to
Actual Change independently of presentation and assertion?

### 7.9 Correction invisibility

**Phenomenon:** clarification, deviation, interruption, reversal, or correction
occurs but is not preserved.

**Possible consequence:** later Outcome and Learning rely on an inaccurate
Change history.

**Evidence question:** Are revisions, disagreements, exceptions, and actual
corrections attributable and time-ordered without rewriting the earlier record?

## 8. Handoff Fidelity Is Not Outcome Fidelity

A faithful handoff can produce a null, adverse, unexpected, or indeterminate
Outcome because the Decision interpretation, Evidence, context, intervention
theory, or external conditions were inadequate.

An unfaithful handoff can coincidentally produce a favorable Outcome because of
external factors, natural variation, or an unauthorized but effective Change.

Therefore:

```text
Handoff fidelity
  → concerns preservation from Decision meaning to Actual Change

Outcome evaluation
  → concerns observed consequence relative to Decision intent

Attribution
  → concerns the bounded relationship among Decision, Actual Change, context,
    comparison, and Outcome
```

None substitutes for another.

## 9. Evidence Relationship

### 9.1 Evidence that Decision exists

Potential Evidence includes:

- accountable human identity;
- existing authority basis;
- bounded Decision question;
- explicit judgment or disposition;
- date and attestation;
- rationale and material Evidence considered;
- uncertainty and limitations;
- intended operational condition;
- intended Change boundary;
- constraints and non-effects; and
- revocation, reconsideration, or interruption conditions.

This Evidence establishes Decision meaning within its scope. It does not
establish Change authorization or execution.

### 9.2 Evidence of intended Change

Potential Evidence includes:

- the intervention the Decision owner contemplated;
- intended source and affected scope;
- expected timing and duration;
- prerequisites and dependencies;
- permitted and prohibited effects;
- desired relation to the intended condition;
- reversibility and correction considerations; and
- ambiguity explicitly left to source-owned interpretation.

Intended Change may be part of Decision meaning, but it is not Actual Change.

### 9.3 Evidence of Change interpretation

Potential Evidence includes:

- source owner’s understanding of Decision intent and bounds;
- source-specific feasibility and constraints;
- clarification questions and answers;
- identified context differences;
- proposed action, substitution, narrowing, staging, or refusal;
- authority confirmation;
- assumptions introduced by the Change owner; and
- conditions returned for human reconsideration.

This Evidence is Interpretation. It does not prove execution.

### 9.4 Evidence of Change authorization

Potential Evidence includes:

- source and mutation owner;
- applicable human authorization;
- scope, time, constraints, conditions, and exceptions;
- affected sources and participants;
- executor authority; and
- interruption, revocation, or correction limits.

Authorization cannot be inferred from Decision intent alone.

### 9.5 Evidence of Actual Change

Potential Evidence includes source-owned records of:

- before/after state of the intervention surface;
- actual action, actor, source, time, scope, and duration;
- partial, failed, substituted, delayed, interrupted, reversed, or additional
  action;
- deviations from authorization and Decision intent;
- concurrent Changes; and
- uncertainty or missing Observation.

This Evidence should describe what happened, not merely what was planned,
authorized, reported, or marked complete.

### 9.6 Evidence of handoff fidelity

Fidelity evidence requires traceable comparison across the chain:

```text
Decision identity and meaning
  ↔ intended Change
  ↔ source-owned Change interpretation
  ↔ Change authorization
  ↔ Actual Change Evidence
```

Relevant questions include:

- Did the same Decision identity remain attributable?
- Did intended condition and non-effects survive?
- Were authority and source ownership preserved?
- Were material uncertainties and context assumptions visible?
- Did interpretation add or remove meaning?
- Were deviations returned to human judgment where necessary?
- Did Actual Change remain within authorized scope?
- Are partial execution, interruption, and correction visible?

Fidelity is a human evidence judgment. No aggregate score or automated
confidence can establish it.

### 9.7 Evidence of observed Change

Observed Change requires attributable source Evidence about the intervention
surface. A completion assertion, human intention, plan, Recommendation,
authorization, or AI summary is insufficient.

Observed Change may establish:

- what source state changed;
- when and for how long;
- who or what executed it;
- actual scope;
- deviations; and
- whether it remained or was reversed.

It does not establish the intended operational consequence.

### 9.8 Evidence of observed Outcome

Outcome Evidence concerns the operational condition that the Decision intended
to affect, possibly in a different source from the Change record. It requires:

- pre-stated intended condition;
- source-owned Observation after Change;
- justified horizon;
- baseline, trajectory, comparison, or intended range;
- adverse, delayed, displaced, null, and missing effects;
- accountable verification judgment; and
- uncertainty and limitations.

### 9.9 Evidence of attributed Outcome

Attribution requires more than faithful handoff and observed Outcome:

```text
Decision intent
  → faithful or known-deviation Actual Change
  → observed operational difference
  → plausible mechanism
  → comparison
  → external factors and concurrent Changes
  → bounded human contribution judgment
```

The conclusion may remain association, plausible contribution, material
contribution, insufficient attribution, or indeterminate. Outcome does not
become causal proof.

## 10. Human Authority Roles

The term **role** here describes existing responsibility distinctions, not new
organizational roles.

### 10.1 Decision owner

The Decision owner is the accountable human who:

- owns the bounded judgment;
- establishes intent and intended condition;
- considers material Evidence, alternatives, uncertainty, and consequences;
- defines or accepts scope and constraints;
- identifies non-effects;
- owns reconsideration when material meaning changes; and
- remains accountable for the Decision, not necessarily for source execution.

The Decision owner cannot make source-owned facts true by deciding them.

### 10.2 Change owner

The Change owner is the applicable source or accountable human responsible for
the operational alteration within source authority. The Change owner may:

- interpret Decision meaning in source context;
- confirm or reject feasibility and authority;
- expose ambiguity, changed context, and dependencies;
- execute, assign, interrupt, correct, or refuse Change within authority;
- preserve Actual Change Evidence; and
- return material deviations for human reconsideration.

The Change owner cannot silently replace the Decision or broaden authority.

### 10.3 Decision owner and Change owner may differ

The same person may own both responsibilities in a simple bounded case. In other
contexts they may be different. Fidelity requires the difference to remain
visible rather than assume hierarchical seniority or technical capability
creates authority.

### 10.4 Verification owner may also differ

The person or source that verifies Outcome may differ from both Decision and
Change owners. Outcome ownership depends on the operational condition and
applicable source Evidence, not execution self-certification.

### 10.5 Authority gap positions

An authority-related gap may appear when:

- no Decision owner exists;
- Decision owner lacks authority for the judgment;
- Change owner or mutation authority is absent;
- Decision and Change authority conflict;
- material deviation has no accountable return point;
- verification owner is absent; or
- Learning is treated as authority to change future practice.

These remain evidence-relative conditions, not an Authority Gap object.

## 11. Why AI Cannot Confirm Handoff Fidelity Alone

### 11.1 AI lacks source authority

AI can compare text and identify apparent differences. It does not own the
source record, operational fact, human Decision, authorization, or Actual Change.

### 11.2 Text alignment is not operational alignment

A Decision document and execution summary may use matching language while
Actual Change differs. Conversely, source-specific language may differ while
preserving intent faithfully.

### 11.3 AI cannot observe missing operational reality by inference

If source-owned Evidence is absent, AI cannot infer Actual Change from plan,
authorization, likelihood, or usual behavior.

### 11.4 AI cannot resolve authority ambiguity

AI may retrieve authority sources or expose conflicts. It cannot decide which
human owns the effect, grant permission, or validate an unauthorized Change.

### 11.5 AI cannot self-verify modeled expectations

An AI-generated expected Change or modeled Outcome cannot become the baseline
for claiming fidelity merely because execution resembles it.

### 11.6 Appropriate assistive use

Within existing boundaries, AI may help humans:

- compare Decision, interpretation, authorization, and execution records;
- identify missing identities, sources, constraints, or Evidence;
- highlight textual inconsistencies and possible scope drift;
- surface unrecorded assumptions or uncertainty;
- distinguish assertions from source-owned observations; and
- draft questions for accountable human review.

The human fidelity judgment must cite source-owned Evidence and remain
challengeable.

## 12. Failure Understanding Without New Objects

### 12.1 Decision exists, no Change

Existing Evidence can preserve Decision, absent authorization, blocked or
missing execution, Interpretation of causes, and later human reconsideration.

### 12.2 Change differs from Decision

Decision Evidence, Change interpretation, authorization, Actual Change record,
and deviation Observation can preserve the mismatch without a Handoff Failure
object.

### 12.3 Change unauthorized

Existing authority sources, Decision record, source-owned Actual Change
Evidence, and human Findings can represent the condition without inventing a new
semantic kind.

### 12.4 Change faithful, Outcome absent

Fidelity Evidence can coexist with null, adverse, delayed, or indeterminate
Outcome. Existing Outcome and Learning meanings preserve that distinction.

### 12.5 Change unfaithful, Outcome favorable

Actual Change and Outcome may be observed, but attribution to the original
Decision is weakened. Favorable consequence does not authorize the deviation or
prove fidelity.

### 12.6 Learning after fidelity loss

Learning may preserve which meaning was lost, where interpretation drifted, how
authority or context changed, and what later questions matter. It does not
declare success or automatically create a handoff rule.

## 13. Product Implications

### 13.1 Distinctions the product may need to preserve

Without proposing objects or UI, the research suggests future product evidence
should be able to keep visible:

- Decision identity and owner;
- intended condition and Change boundary;
- source-owned interpretation;
- Change authority and owner;
- Actual Change and deviations;
- clarification, refusal, interruption, correction, and reversal;
- context at Decision and execution time;
- Change Observation versus Outcome Observation;
- attribution uncertainty; and
- Learning without success or authority promotion.

### 13.2 Questions the product may help humans ask

- Which Decision is this Change related to?
- What did the accountable human intend?
- Which material bounds and non-effects apply?
- What did the source owner understand?
- What context changed before execution?
- Was the interpreted Change authorized?
- What actually occurred in the source?
- Where did Actual Change differ from intent or authorization?
- Was the deviation returned to human judgment?
- Which Evidence establishes execution rather than assertion?
- Which separate Evidence establishes operational Outcome?
- What contribution can be claimed without causal overreach?

These are research questions, not workflow or interface requirements.

## 14. Existing Capability Sufficiency

### 14.1 Relevant existing responsibilities

The canonical architecture already includes conceptual responsibilities for:

- Operational Matter continuity;
- bounded Investigation;
- Evidence provenance, uncertainty, contradiction, and correction;
- Interpretation, hypotheses, scoped Cause, and modeled alternatives;
- advisory Recommendation;
- accountable human Decision;
- source ownership and mutation authority;
- semantic and authority-preserving handoffs;
- source-owned action and Operational Transition;
- interruption, correction, and traceability;
- Outcome verification; and
- bounded Learning, Memory, and Knowledge.

### 14.2 Conceptual sufficiency finding

Existing capabilities appear conceptually sufficient to represent:

```text
Decision intent and meaning
  → source-owned Change interpretation
  → Change authorization
  → Actual Change Evidence
  → Change Observation
  → Outcome Observation and verification
  → bounded attribution and Learning
```

No evidence in the current research requires a new Handoff object, Change
object, fidelity capability, failure object, lifecycle, workflow, or governance
mechanism.

### 14.3 Why no new object is justified

Handoff fidelity appears to be a relationship among existing attributable
artifacts and semantic kinds. It does not yet show independent identity,
ownership, persistence, correction, handoff, or authority requirements that
existing Matter, Evidence, Decision, source-owned Change records, Outcome, and
Learning cannot compose.

### 14.4 What remains unproven

Conceptual sufficiency does not demonstrate that current product realization can:

- retrieve authoritative Decision and source records;
- preserve complete material meaning across real handoffs;
- detect semantic loss or drift reliably;
- observe partial, substituted, or unauthorized Actual Change;
- distinguish stale intent from changed context;
- identify Decision and Change authority correctly;
- support interruption and correction without defining workflow;
- prevent AI-generated fidelity overclaim;
- link Change Evidence to separate Outcome Evidence; or
- support credible bounded attribution in practice.

These are product, evidence, and validation questions rather than proof of a new
canonical capability.

## 17. Research Findings

### Finding 1 — Fidelity concerns preserved meaning, not copied text

Source-owned interpretation is necessary, but material additions, losses, and
deviations must remain visible and accountable.

### Finding 2 — Intent, meaning, interpretation, and Actual Change are distinct

Each has different ownership and Evidence. Collapsing them hides drift and
authority loss.

### Finding 3 — Decision and Change authority may differ

The Decision owner owns human judgment; the Change owner owns source-specific
authorization and execution within applicable authority.

### Finding 4 — Actual Change requires source-owned Evidence

Plans, authorization, completion assertions, AI summaries, and textual alignment
cannot establish what occurred.

### Finding 5 — Fidelity and Outcome are independent questions

Faithful Change can produce poor Outcome, and unfaithful Change can coincide with
favorable Outcome. Neither result collapses the semantic boundary.

### Finding 6 — Attribution requires both fidelity and consequence Evidence

Observed Change and Outcome co-occurrence does not establish contribution.
Decision intent, actual intervention, comparison, context, and alternatives
remain material.

### Finding 7 — AI cannot verify fidelity alone

AI lacks human Decision authority, source authority, and direct ownership of
operational facts. Its comparison remains advisory.

### Finding 8 — Existing capabilities appear conceptually sufficient

Current Matter, Investigation, Evidence, Decision, handoff, source-owned action,
Outcome, and Learning responsibilities can compose the relationship without a
new object or capability.

### Finding 9 — Real product adequacy remains an evidence question

Retrieval, semantic preservation, Change observability, authority clarity,
correction, Outcome separation, and attribution remain unvalidated in real use.

## 18. Research Conclusion

Decision-to-Change handoff fidelity is best treated as a bounded, human-reviewed
relationship among existing semantic meanings and source-owned Evidence:

```text
Decision intent
  → Decision meaning
  → source-owned Change interpretation
  → Change authorization
  → Actual Change
  → source-owned Change Observation
```

Fidelity exists only as far as Evidence supports preservation of material
meaning and authority across that chain. It cannot be established from document
completion, textual similarity, AI confidence, or favorable Outcome.

Existing Management OS capabilities appear conceptually sufficient. The open
questions concern real source Evidence, human interpretation, authority,
context, correction, observability, Outcome separation, and bounded attribution,
not a missing canonical object.
