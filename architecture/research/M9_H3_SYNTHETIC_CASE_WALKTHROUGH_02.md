# Context

Sales accepted a large customized order after an informal discussion with an
operations manager. The CRM recorded the deal as committed. The production plan
treated the same order as provisional because tooling and engineering review
were incomplete.

Sales interpreted the informal conversation as confirmation of capacity.
Operations interpreted it as permission to assess feasibility. The shared
conversation therefore carried different operational meanings across the two
functions.

The customer later requested delivery confirmation. Neither Sales nor
Operations could reconstruct an agreed commitment. The CRM commitment, the
provisional production plan, the incomplete engineering review, and the
participants' later recollections did not converge on one authoritative account
of what had been promised.

Forecast, opportunity, feasibility, capacity reservation, and customer
commitment were related states but not equivalent ones. Commercial authority
and operational authority were distributed. Capacity information changed over
time and could be treated as more certain or current than it had been at the
moment of discussion.

The later failure affected retrospective accounts. The context therefore
included both contemporaneous records and later interpretations with different
provenance and evidentiary limits.

# Operational Diagnosis

## Matter under review

A customized order appeared committed commercially while remaining provisional
operationally, and the organization could not reconstruct a shared authorized
commitment when the customer requested confirmation.

## Formation of understanding

1. The CRM and production plan represented the same order differently.
2. The difference was not merely a status mismatch: each state carried a
   different commitment meaning.
3. Sales and Operations assigned different meanings to the same informal
   conversation.
4. Tooling and engineering review remained incomplete when the order was treated
   as committed in CRM.
5. The capacity snapshot available during the discussion did not provide
   permanent or complete feasibility assurance.
6. Commercial, operational, and engineering authorities were distributed.
7. Meeting notes, chat excerpts, and later recollections did not supply a single
   authoritative agreement.
8. Later failure influenced retrospective interpretation.

## Working diagnostic statement

The coordination failure involved loss of semantic and authority fidelity
across commercial commitment, operational feasibility, capacity reservation,
and customer promise. The preserved information showed incompatible states and
interpretations while leaving the relative contribution of ambiguous language,
state design, stale context, incomplete dependencies, and human handoff
unresolved.

## Unresolved uncertainty

- What each participant reasonably understood at the exact time of discussion.
- Whether the CRM transition relied directly on the discussion or on another
  assumption.
- Whether a current capacity reservation existed at any relevant moment.
- How tooling and engineering dependencies would have affected delivery.
- Whether clearer semantics alone would have changed the customer commitment.
- How much later recollection was altered by hindsight.
- Which factors most materially produced the coordination failure.

No single Cause was accepted. The diagnosis preserved enough distinction to
support accountable human judgment without inventing agreement.

# Decision

The modeled commercial and operations authorities jointly decided that custom
orders could not move to committed status until:

- a named operations owner recorded feasibility; and
- the commercial owner recorded acceptance of delivery risk.

The Decision preserved separate commercial and operational authorities rather
than treating coordination as automatic consensus. Management OS preserved the
Evidence basis, uncertainty, dependencies, state meanings, and authority
relationships; it did not exercise either authority.

# Change

The actual modeled Change:

- added an explicit feasibility state;
- added an explicit capacity-reservation state;
- added an explicit customer-commitment state;
- made transitions attributable;
- allowed a short-lived reservation to expire; and
- kept unresolved engineering dependencies visible rather than converting them
  into implied approval.

The states were used in the following review window. Actual use included one
feasibility record completed with stale capacity information. The Change
therefore made transitions explicit without ensuring that the context attached
to every completed state was current.

# Outcome

In the following modeled review window:

- fewer ambiguous commitments were observed;
- one sales response was slower;
- one order's feasibility record was completed using stale capacity
  information; and
- the explicit states improved traceability within the scenario.

The observed traceability and reduction in ambiguity did not prove better
commercial performance, operational performance, delivery performance, or
customer Outcome. The slower response and stale feasibility context remained
part of the same mixed Outcome.

No reliable basis established that the state Change alone caused the observed
differences. Order mix, human behavior, context freshness, dependencies, and the
short review window remained attribution limits.

# Learning

The completed historical branch produced the following bounded Learning:

- A shared conversation did not guarantee shared semantic meaning.
- Forecast, opportunity, feasibility, reservation, and customer commitment
  required distinct interpretation.
- Commercial acceptance could not substitute for operational feasibility or
  engineering completion.
- Attributable state transitions improved reconstruction but did not ensure that
  the underlying context was current.
- Separate commercial and operational authority could participate in one
  Decision without becoming generic consensus.
- Later recollections could not replace the Decision-time capacity snapshot,
  notes, messages, and unresolved dependencies.
- Fewer ambiguous commitments coexisted with slower response and stale context;
  one visible improvement did not define the whole Outcome.
- Traceability did not prove improved commercial or operational performance.

This Learning remained attached to the synthetic completed case. It did not
become organizational Learning or validate H3.

## Replayed Decision reasoning

### Decision question restated

What bounded human Decision could make the transition from commercial interest
to customer commitment inspectable without treating provisional capacity,
informal discussion, or incomplete engineering work as authorized feasibility?

### Evidence basis revisited

- The CRM treated the customized order as committed.
- The production plan treated it as provisional.
- Sales and Operations interpreted the same conversation differently.
- Tooling and engineering review were incomplete.
- Capacity information was time-bound and changed over time.
- No authoritative record established a shared commitment.
- Commercial, operational, and engineering authorities remained separate.

### Assumptions made explicit

- A named operations owner could record feasibility without inheriting
  commercial authority.
- A commercial owner could accept delivery risk without declaring operational
  feasibility.
- An explicit reservation state could preserve a time-bounded capacity
  relationship.
- Reservation expiry could prevent old capacity context from appearing current,
  but only if actual feasibility recording used current information.
- Unresolved engineering dependencies could remain visible through commitment
  consideration.

### Alternatives visible in the reasoning branch

- Continue using the CRM committed state with a clarifying note.
- Require engineering completion before any commercial acceptance.
- Use only an operations feasibility confirmation without separate commercial
  risk acceptance.
- Preserve separate feasibility, reservation, and commitment states with
  attributable owners.

No alternative was enacted in Replay. No modeled alternative Outcome was
generated.

### Authority check

Commercial and operations authorities retained their separate Decisions.
Engineering responsibility for unresolved dependencies remained distinct. AI
surfaced semantic mismatches, context freshness, and tradeoffs but did not
confirm feasibility, accept risk, or create consensus.

### Replayed rationale attached to the historical Decision

The historical Decision was reconsidered as a semantic-and-authority handoff
intervention. Its immediate purpose was not recorded as improving sales or
delivery performance. It required attributable operational feasibility and
commercial risk acceptance before commitment while preserving that capacity
could expire, engineering dependencies could remain unresolved, and response
speed could be affected.

## Historical branch preserved

Replay did not change the historical Decision. The same modeled commercial and
operations authorities still required a named operations owner to record
feasibility and the commercial owner to record acceptance of delivery risk
before a custom order could move to committed status.

Replay did not change the actual Change or Outcome. Explicit feasibility,
capacity-reservation, and customer-commitment states still existed; reservations
could still expire; fewer ambiguous commitments were still observed; one sales
response was still slower; and one feasibility record still used stale capacity
information.

# Additional Learning

The Replay branch made the following additional understanding visible without
rewriting Historical Learning:

- The historical Decision could be understood as preserving a handoff between
  two separate authorities rather than creating a single approval step.
- Feasibility, capacity reservation, and customer commitment were not merely
  workflow stages; each carried a distinct time, authority, and reliance
  meaning.
- Commercial acceptance of delivery risk did not repair stale operational
  context or incomplete engineering dependencies.
- A formally complete feasibility state could still be semantically unreliable
  when its capacity context was stale.
- Reservation expiry preserved time sensitivity, but freshness also depended on
  which context the feasibility owner actually used.
- The slower sales response exposed a tradeoff in the historical Outcome without
  establishing that faster or slower response was preferable.
- An alternative reasoning branch could examine different authority allocations
  without inventing agreement, execution, or customer Outcomes.
- The same historical Outcome supported reflection on semantic fidelity and
  context freshness even though it could not establish Decision correctness or
  causal effectiveness.

The additional Learning did not establish that the historical Decision was
right or that any alternative Decision would have been better.

# H3 Reflection

## Result

**Partially**

## Reflection record

Replay produced additional bounded Learning beyond the first completed pass. It
made the Decision's semantic-and-authority handoff scope more explicit,
distinguished state completion from context freshness, exposed reservation
expiry as insufficient by itself, and allowed alternative authority reasoning
to be examined without creating alternative Outcomes.

The result is only partial because the first pass already preserved the central
Learning about divergent meaning, distributed authority, stale context, and
mixed Outcome. The Replay used an authored synthetic history rather than a
completed real operational experience. The walkthrough cannot show that a real
manager obtained substantially more Learning than from the original experience,
that the additional understanding would persist, or that Management OS caused
it.

This `Partially` result describes the internal training walkthrough only. It
neither confirms nor falsifies H3.

# Observed Architectural Friction

- The preserved material did not establish a precise sequence between the
  informal discussion, CRM commitment transition, production-plan treatment,
  dependency review, and customer confirmation request.
- The same informal conversation carried incompatible meanings, but the record
  could preserve language and interpretations without establishing shared
  semantic acceptance.
- Forecast, opportunity, feasibility, reservation, and customer commitment were
  related closely enough that generic status language repeatedly risked
  collapsing their distinct meanings.
- Capacity context had both identity and freshness; a link to a capacity
  snapshot did not by itself express whether that snapshot remained fit for a
  later feasibility record.
- Joint Decision traceability was difficult to express without implying merged
  commercial and operational authority or automatic consensus.
- Engineering dependencies influenced feasibility while remaining outside both
  commercial risk acceptance and operations capacity ownership.
- Later recollections required persistent separation from contemporaneous notes,
  messages, and capacity context because the later failure affected them.
- The actual Change required a visible relationship between the completed
  feasibility state and the stale capacity information used for it; otherwise
  state completion appeared more reliable than observed.
- Replay naturally required explicit links from each reasoning statement to the
  information available at the Replay point.
- The Replay branch needed persistent separation from the historical branch at
  Interpretation, rationale, and alternative levels while sharing the same
  Observation, Evidence, Decision, Change, and Outcome.
- It was difficult to express additional Learning without making it appear to
  replace or improve the Historical Learning.
- The Outcome contained traceability, ambiguity, response-speed, and context-
  freshness effects, creating semantic tension whenever a single Outcome label
  was used.
- Authority over commercial commitment, operational feasibility, engineering
  dependencies, capacity reservation, and customer communication remained
  distributed and could not be represented as one generic owner.

These are observations from the walkthrough. They are not recommendations,
architecture changes, new objects, or new capabilities.
