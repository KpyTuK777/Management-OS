## 1. Purpose and Evidence Basis

The prior hypothesis-selection support defined three unconfirmed alternatives,

> What must be sufficiently understood—and what must remain explicitly
> unresolved—before a real human authority could responsibly select one
> hypothesis for a distinct next consideration?

The evidence basis is:

- `POST_M8_OPERATIONAL_LOOP_DECISION_SUPPORT_SUMMARY.md`; and

## 2. Scope of Refinement

The refinement concerns hypothesis-selection readiness, not scenario readiness
or validation readiness. The distinction is:

```text
Hypothesis understood for selection consideration
        ≠
Hypothesis selected by human authority
        ≠
Candidate scenario assessed
        ≠
Scenario selected
        ≠
Validation approved
        ≠
Validation executed
```

No expression in this paper is a canonical readiness class, governance state,
lifecycle state, review disposition, approval, or automatic transition.

## 3. Fixed Hypothesis Context

The refinement applies equally to the three previously defined hypotheses:

- **H1:** Management OS reduces semantic loss across operational transitions.
- **H2:** Evidence and semantic separation improves Decision clarity.
- **H3:** An Outcome-oriented loop improves Learning after Change.

All three remain validation hypotheses. None is a current claim about Management

attempt to test; it does not assert that the proposition is true.

## 4. Core Decision Boundaries

### 4.1 Selection ≠ authorization

Hypothesis selection would identify one primary uncertainty for further bounded
preparation or consideration. It would not authorize a real-world validation,
operational exposure, participant involvement, source access, data use, Change,
or implementation.

Selection determines “which proposition should be developed further, if any.”
Authorization determines “which action may occur, under whose authority, within
which bounds and constraints.” Those are different Decisions.

### 4.2 Suitability ≠ validation approval

A hypothesis may appear suitable because it is understandable, bounded,
falsifiable, observable in principle, and informative. That judgment does not
establish that a real scenario, Evidence, comparison, access, ethical conditions,
resources, or validation authority exists.

Suitability supports consideration. It creates no permitted operational effect.

### 4.3 Hypothesis ≠ claim

A hypothesis is a proposition designed to permit evidence for and against it. A
claim asserts a conclusion at a stated evidence strength. Until validation is
separately authorized, executed, and evaluated, H1–H3 remain unconfirmed
propositions.

The hypothesis does not become a claim because it is:

- precisely worded;
- architecturally coherent;
- selected for further work;
- considered likely;
- supported by research insight;
- repeated in repository artifacts; or
- preferred by a human authority.

### 4.4 Selection readiness ≠ selection

Readiness means that the authority could make a bounded and attributable choice
without relying on hidden assumptions. It does not mean that the choice should
be made, that one option is preferred, or that deferral is no longer legitimate.

### 4.5 Selection support ≠ recommendation

Decision support may organize alternatives, evidence, uncertainties, and
consequences. It must not rank hypotheses, assign weights, aggregate scores, or
generate a winner. The accountable human may select none.

## 5. Selection-Readiness Conditions

The conditions below are cumulative and claim-specific. Strength in one condition
cannot compensate for absence of a non-substitutable condition such as human
ownership, falsifiability, observable Outcome, or credible attribution boundary.

### 5.1 Hypothesis identity is stable

A hypothesis is sufficiently identified when the material proposition can be
stated without changing meaning across the decision-support materials.

The statement should expose:

- the bounded subject;
- the proposed relationship or contribution;
- the relevant operational-loop transition or consequence;
- the unit of possible observation;
- the intended comparison posture;
- the scope to which a later conclusion would be limited; and
- the semantic kinds that must remain distinct.

Selection readiness is absent when different readers would be choosing materially
different propositions under the same H1, H2, or H3 label.

### 5.2 Tested and excluded propositions are explicit

The authority should be able to distinguish what the hypothesis would attempt to
test from what it cannot establish.

For example:

- H1 may concern material transition fidelity; it cannot establish overall
  product success or favorable Outcome.
- H2 may concern accountable Decision clarity; it cannot establish Decision
  correctness, Change authority, or causal Outcome improvement.
- H3 may concern correctable Learning grounded in Outcome evidence; it cannot
  establish generalized organizational learning, success, or later causal
  effect.

If exclusions are not explicit, selection may silently authorize a much broader
claim than the hypothesis can support.

### 5.3 Falsification remains possible

The hypothesis is understood only when material evidence could count against it.
At least one credible negative, mixed, null, or indeterminate path must be
describable without being reclassified as success.

The authority should understand:

- which observation would weaken the hypothesis;
- which observation would be irrelevant activity;
- which missing evidence would force an indeterminate conclusion;
- which burden or adverse effect could outweigh apparent benefit; and
- why a favorable Outcome would still not prove exclusive causality.

A proposition that can explain every possible result is not sufficiently bounded
for selection consideration.

### 5.4 Information value is decision-relevant

The potential result should reduce a material post-M8 uncertainty. The authority
should be able to explain how a positive, negative, mixed, or indeterminate result
could inform later human consideration without presupposing implementation.

Information value is insufficient when:

- the result would only confirm an architectural distinction already known;
- the only effect is producing more records or activity;
- no plausible result could change future understanding;
- the proposition is too broad for a bounded conclusion; or
- the value depends on treating validation as total product proof.

### 5.5 Boundedness is intelligible

At hypothesis-selection time, a real scenario need not be selected. However, the
hypothesis should be capable in principle of being bounded to:

- one operational loop;
- one primary accountable human Decision owner;
- one bounded operational context;
- one distinguishable actual Change;
- one primary observable Outcome with balancing conditions;
- one credible comparison posture;
- one stated observation horizon; and
- one local claim boundary.

If the hypothesis can only be examined through organization-wide transformation,
multiple uncontrolled authority domains, or a universal product claim, it is not
sufficiently bounded for first-hypothesis selection.

### 5.6 Evidence categories are identifiable

Selection does not require the future validation Evidence to have been collected.
It does require a credible account of which evidence categories would be needed
and whether their existence could be investigated without inventing new facts or
authority.

Relevant categories may include:

- pre-observation operational Reality and baseline Evidence;
- source-owned observations with provenance, timing, context, and limitations;
- separated Interpretation, hypothesis, modeled output, and Recommendation;
- attributable human Decision and rationale;
- source-owned evidence of intended, authorized, actual, and observed Change;
- pre-stated Outcome and balancing dimensions;
- comparison and confounder Evidence;
- Outcome Observation, Interpretation, and bounded attribution; and
- correctable Learning linked to experience.

The categories differ by H1, H2, and H3. A generic statement that “data is
available” is not enough.

### 5.7 Evidence sufficiency for selection consideration

Evidence is sufficient for selection consideration only when it supports the
bounded judgment that a hypothesis is understandable and plausibly investigable.
It does not need to support the hypothesis itself.

At minimum, selection-support Evidence should allow the human to judge:

- the hypothesis identity and exclusions;
- its post-M8 information value;
- its falsifiability;
- plausible evidence sources and material access constraints;
- whether a bounded candidate shape might exist;
- whether an Outcome could be meaningful and observable in principle;
- the principal attribution and validity threats;
- potential burden, harm, and reversibility concerns; and
- which unknowns must remain unresolved until scenario assessment or later
  preparation.

This is claim-specific sufficiency:

```text
Evidence sufficient to consider selecting a hypothesis
        ≠
Evidence sufficient to select a scenario
        ≠
Evidence sufficient to approve validation
        ≠
Evidence sufficient to support a validation conclusion
```

### 5.8 Outcome observability is plausible in principle

The hypothesis should relate to a material observable condition rather than only
activity, documentation, confidence, usage, or completion.

Selection-support Evidence should make it plausible that a later candidate could
define before observation:

- a primary Outcome dimension;
- relevant balancing or adverse conditions;
- an accountable verification owner;
- an appropriate observation horizon; and
- the distinction between actual Change, observed condition, Outcome
  Interpretation, and attribution.

The actual Outcome and threshold remain future scenario-specific questions.
Plausibility does not fix them.

### 5.9 Attribution limits are bounded enough to investigate

Attribution uncertainty need not be eliminated. It must be visible and bounded
enough that a later inquiry could avoid a false causal claim.

The selection consideration should identify likely threats such as:

- context change;
- concurrent interventions;
- incomplete or divergent Change;
- selection and confirmation bias;
- novelty and observer effects;
- measurement distortion;
- unequal comparison conditions;
- external events; and
- hindsight or outcome bias.

The hypothesis is not selection-ready when any favorable result would remain
indistinguishable from Management OS usage followed by Outcome co-occurrence.

### 5.10 Responsible reversibility is plausible

Although no Change is selected or authorized, the hypothesis should be capable of
future examination through a bounded, proportionate, interruptible, correctable,
and responsibly reversible Change where appropriate.

Selection readiness is absent if the only plausible inquiry would require:

- safety-critical exposure;
- irreversible or disproportionate Change;
- hidden participant coercion;
- unbounded operational disruption;
- unauthorized source mutation; or
- implementation whose effects cannot be separated from validation.

### 5.11 Human ownership can be distinguished

Selection-support Evidence should make the relevant ownership categories
intelligible even though specific future actors may not yet be assigned:

- hypothesis-selection authority;
- operational Decision owner;
- source owner;
- Change authority and execution owner;
- Outcome verification owner;
- participant and affected-party interests; and
- any later validation authority.

If the inquiry depends on AI, research authorship, or an assessment artifact
acting as Decision owner, it is not selection-ready.

### 5.12 Uncertainty is acceptable for selection consideration

“Acceptable” is a human authority judgment, not an AI calculation. Uncertainty may
remain when it is:

- explicitly identified;
- bounded to a known proposition or evidence gap;
- non-destructive to hypothesis identity and falsifiability;
- compatible with a later negative, mixed, or indeterminate result;
- separable from missing authority or unacceptable exposure;
- not concealed by a score or confidence label; and
- unlikely to force scope expansion before a meaningful inquiry can be formed.

Uncertainty is not acceptable merely because it is common, difficult, or
expensive to resolve. The accountable human must state why remaining uncertainty
does not make selection misleading or irresponsible.

## 6. Evidence Sufficiency Boundaries

### 6.1 What may support selection readiness

Potential support includes:

- stable hypothesis definitions and exclusions;
- repository-visible research trace;
- evidence-source mapping;
- known constraints and missing information;
- qualitative analysis of boundedness, observability, reversibility, ownership,
  information value, and attribution;
- evidence that a relevant candidate class may contain real cases;
- contrary evidence and reasons a candidate shape may fail; and
- documented inability to answer questions that properly belong to a later
  scenario assessment.

### 6.2 What is insufficient

The following cannot establish selection readiness alone:

- architectural coherence;
- research volume or number of supporting documents;
- AI confidence or narrative completeness;
- stakeholder interest or intuitive appeal;
- expected positive Outcome;
- availability of activity data;
- a plausible modeled scenario;
- number of candidate classes;
- a score, rank, weighted comparison, or majority preference;
- one favorable anecdote;
- Recommendation from a research artifact; or
- pressure to begin validation or implementation.

### 6.3 No evidence substitution

Evidence strong for one dimension cannot substitute for a missing
non-compensatory condition:

- high information value cannot compensate for impossible Outcome observation;
- rich data cannot compensate for absent accountable human ownership;
- reversible Change cannot compensate for an unfalsifiable hypothesis;
- clear hypothesis wording cannot compensate for unacceptable attribution;
- strong scenario appeal cannot compensate for missing source authority; and
- expected Learning cannot compensate for absence of actual Change or Outcome
  evidence.

## 7. Non-Selection Conditions

The conditions below require the human consideration to stop short of selecting
the affected hypothesis on the available basis. They do not prove the hypothesis
false or permanently reject it.

### 7.1 Insufficient Evidence

Non-selection is required when:

- material hypothesis meaning or exclusions remain ambiguous;
- evidence sources are only assumed or modeled;
- source provenance and accessibility cannot be investigated;
- relevant contrary evidence is missing or suppressed;
- only activity, usage, confidence, or completion signals are available;
- actual Change could not be distinguished from Decision intent;
- comparison would be created after seeing results;
- falsification conditions cannot be evidenced; or
- evidence is insufficient to distinguish suitability from narrative plausibility.

The appropriate decision-support conclusion is “insufficient evidence” or
“indeterminate,” not an invented score or inferred winner.

### 7.2 Unclear ownership

Non-selection is required when:

- no accountable human could own the relevant operational Decision;
- hypothesis-selection authority is inferred rather than attributable;
- Decision authority and Change authority are collapsed;
- source ownership or mutation authority is ambiguous;
- Outcome verification would be owned by the system or AI;
- affected participants and external authority domains cannot be bounded; or
- an assessment artifact would effectively make the choice.

Ownership ambiguity is not compensated by high Evidence volume.

### 7.3 Impossible Outcome observation

Non-selection is required when:

- the hypothesis has no material operational consequence beyond activity;
- Outcome can only be chosen after results;
- the observation horizon is unavailable, disproportionate, or meaningless;
- no accountable verification owner can be identified in principle;
- actual Change and observed condition cannot be separated;
- balancing or displaced effects cannot be observed; or
- modeled output, satisfaction, adoption, completion, or Recommendation count
  would have to substitute for Outcome.

An observable intermediate signal does not rescue a hypothesis whose claimed
consequence remains unobservable.

### 7.4 Unacceptable attribution limits

Non-selection is required when:

- the maximum possible conclusion would still rely only on sequence or
  co-occurrence;
- actual Change cannot be traced;
- concurrent interventions dominate the bounded condition;
- comparison contexts are materially incomparable;
- context or external events cannot be observed sufficiently;
- observer, novelty, or selection effects cannot be bounded;
- the only permitted narrative would be “Management OS caused the Outcome”; or
- uncertainty would make any result incapable of reducing the stated post-M8
  question.

Attribution need not prove causality. It must at least permit a disciplined
bounded-contribution inquiry.

### 7.5 Unacceptable exposure or irreversibility

Non-selection is required when plausible future examination depends on
safety-critical, high-harm, coercive, irreversible, legally uncontrolled, or
otherwise disproportionate exposure.

The desire for information cannot authorize operational risk.

### 7.6 Hypothesis bundling

Non-selection is required when the proposed hypothesis actually combines H1,
H2, H3, total product value, implementation feasibility, and organization-wide
effect into one proposition. A first bounded validation question must permit a
local conclusion and must not use one result to validate the entire operational
loop automatically.

### 7.7 Hidden implementation dependency

Non-selection is required on the current basis when the hypothesis cannot be made
observable without first deciding APIs, schemas, services, runtime, UI,
infrastructure, delivery, or another implementation design. That condition may
indicate a future evidence or architecture question, but this refinement cannot
resolve or authorize it.

## 8. Acceptable and Unacceptable Uncertainty

### 8.1 Potentially acceptable uncertainty

Subject to human judgment, selection consideration may proceed while uncertainty
remains about:

- the exact future scenario, because selection is not scenario selection;
- exact evidence values, because validation has not occurred;
- whether the hypothesis will be supported, because it must remain falsifiable;
- which legitimate positive, negative, mixed, or indeterminate result will arise;
- the precise size of a contribution, provided a bounded contribution question
  is plausible; and
- implementation possibility, provided no implementation is required to make the
  selection Decision itself.

### 8.2 Unacceptable uncertainty

Selection consideration must not proceed to selection when uncertainty concerns:

- what proposition is actually being selected;
- whether the hypothesis is falsifiable;
- whether any material Outcome could exist;
- whether human authority and source ownership can remain preserved;
- whether future exposure could be responsibly bounded;
- whether evidence could distinguish Change from Outcome;
- whether attribution is inherently impossible; or
- whether selection would be misrepresented as validation approval.

### 8.3 Uncertainty ownership

AI may expose and organize uncertainty. Only an accountable human authority can
judge whether remaining uncertainty is acceptable for the limited selection
Decision. That judgment must be attributable and reasoned; a confidence threshold
or model score cannot replace it.

## 9. Human Judgment Requirements

### 9.1 Questions AI cannot decide

AI cannot decide:

- which post-M8 uncertainty is most material to Management OS’s accountable
  stakeholders;
- whether the potential information value justifies human and operational burden;
- whether remaining uncertainty is acceptable;
- whether participant, ethical, privacy, safety, and operational exposure is
  proportionate;
- whether a person or body has applicable selection authority;
- whether source owners consent to a future inquiry;
- whether attribution limits are acceptable for the intended management use;
- whether one hypothesis should be selected, deferred, or left unselected;
- whether a Recommendation should be adopted; or
- whether any later preparation or validation should be authorized.

AI output can identify missing inputs or inconsistencies. It cannot turn that
analysis into human judgment.

### 9.2 Inputs requiring authority

A future attributable hypothesis-selection Decision would require:

- accountable authority identity;
- applicable authority basis for hypothesis selection;
- the exact hypothesis identity and bounded selection effect;
- evidence considered, including contrary and missing evidence;
- an explicit judgment on information value;
- an explicit judgment on acceptable uncertainty;
- confirmation that selection creates no scenario or validation authorization;
- explicit disposition, including the legitimate possibility of selecting none;
- human-owned rationale;
- Decision date; and
- human attestation.

No field can be inferred from research authorship, repository access, job title,
current date, AI output, or analytical readiness.

### 9.3 Human questions before selection

The accountable authority would need to answer:

1. Which single uncertainty would the selected hypothesis isolate?
2. What result could count against it?
3. What would remain unproven even after a favorable result?
4. Why is the potential information decision-relevant?
5. Can the proposition plausibly fit one bounded operational loop?
6. Which evidence categories would be necessary, and what is currently known
   about their existence and access?
7. What material Outcome could exist beyond activity or documentation?
8. What attribution claim is the strongest that could responsibly be examined?
9. Which attribution claim is prohibited?
10. Could future Change and exposure remain proportionate and reversible?
11. Who would own Decision, Change, sources, Outcome verification, and later
    validation authority?
12. Which uncertainty remains, and why is it acceptable for selection only?
13. Why would another hypothesis, deferral, or no selection remain legitimate?
14. What exact non-effects must the selection preserve?

The questions support judgment; they do not predetermine an answer.

## 10. Qualitative Readiness Expression

A future evidence assessment may describe a hypothesis using one of the following
plain-language expressions:

- **Sufficiently understood for selection consideration:** the hypothesis,
  exclusions, information value, falsification, boundedness, evidence categories,
  Outcome plausibility, attribution boundary, responsible exposure, and ownership
  are clear enough for human judgment.
- **Conditionally understood:** named evidence or authority questions remain, and
  the human must decide whether they can legitimately remain unresolved at
  selection time.
- **Insufficiently understood:** one or more non-substitutable conditions are not
  supported.
- **Indeterminate:** available evidence is missing, conflicting, inaccessible, or
  semantically invalid for the readiness judgment.

These are research expressions only. They are not canonical classifications,
review dispositions, lifecycle states, rankings, Recommendations, selections, or
authority effects.

No expression is assigned to H1, H2, or H3 by this artifact.

## 11. Refinement of the Selection Decision Boundary

If a future human selection Decision occurs, its maximum effect should be:

> One named validation hypothesis is selected as the primary proposition for a
> separately bounded next consideration, subject to recorded uncertainty and
> explicit non-effects.

That maximum effect does not:

- state that the hypothesis is true;
- select a candidate class or real scenario;
- establish evidence availability in a real context;
- fix a baseline, comparison, Outcome, threshold, or validation method;
- approve validation preparation or execution;
- authorize participant involvement, source access, data use, or Change;
- create product-readiness, causal, success, or implementation claims;
- create Engineering Architecture, implementation, M9, or backlog; or
- change canonical M7, M8, or governance.

The selection record would need to preserve these non-effects explicitly.
