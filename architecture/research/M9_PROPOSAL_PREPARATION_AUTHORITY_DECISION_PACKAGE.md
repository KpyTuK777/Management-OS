## 5. Why a Proposal Might Be Useful

The following considerations may support authorizing preparation without
constituting a recommendation:

- A bounded proposal could turn the broad possible next Decision domain into a
  concrete object that an accountable human can inspect, challenge, revise,
  defer, or reject.
- It could make the separation between proposal preparation and M9 opening
  explicit before any successor-milestone consideration occurs.
- It could state possible scope, boundaries, Decision identities, and
  non-effects in one place, reducing ambiguity about what a later M9
  consideration would and would not contain.
- It could test whether any coherent milestone description can be formed from
  the completed research without selecting H1–H5, a scenario, validation
  design, implementation, roadmap, metrics, or experiments.
- It could expose missing authority inputs or unresolved scope contradictions
  before any request to create or open a milestone is considered.
- It could provide a bounded basis for a later human decision while preserving
  the freedom to decline the proposal or take no further action.

These possible uses concern decision clarity only. They do not demonstrate that
a proposal is needed, timely, viable, or likely to be approved.

## 6. Why a Proposal Might Be Premature

The following considerations may support deferral, an evidence request, or
declining preparation without constituting a recommendation:

- The remaining dominant uncertainty is empirical, while the permitted
  proposal content excludes hypothesis selection, scenario selection,
  validation design, metrics, and experiments. The result may therefore be too
  abstract to improve the eventual milestone Decision materially.
- No H1–H5 selection or priority exists, so a possible milestone scope may lack
  a sufficiently bounded product-value subject.
- No scenario has been selected, so Evidence accessibility, Outcome
  observability, attribution limits, and practical feasibility remain unknown.
- Additional document production may repeat already established authority and
  non-effect boundaries, increasing artifact volume without reducing the
  decisive uncertainty.
- A proposal-shaped artifact may create informal momentum, anchoring, or an
  appearance of inevitability even when it has no authority effect.
- Preparation effort could be disproportionate if the accountable human is not
  presently willing to consider a successor milestone or if a different
  Decision domain is more relevant.

These considerations do not establish that preparation should be deferred or
declined. They identify limits the human authority may weigh.

## 7. Neutral Options

The options below are unranked. Their order expresses no preference.

### A. Authorize preparation

Authorize preparation of one bounded M9 proposal containing only a possible
milestone description, possible scope, possible boundaries, possible Decision
identities, and possible non-effects. This option would authorize drafting only;
it would not create or open M9 or authorize anything downstream.

### B. Defer

Make no current Decision to authorize preparation and preserve the question for
later reconsideration. Deferral would create no proposal work, no evidence
request, and no presumption about a later disposition.

### C. Request additional evidence

Do not authorize proposal preparation at present; instead, identify the exact
additional evidence required for this authority question and its acceptable
source and boundary. This option would not itself authorize validation,
experiments, metrics, implementation, or collection of evidence outside a
separately authorized scope.

### D. Decline

Decline authorization to prepare an M9 proposal under the current Decision
identity and evidence basis. Declining would not erase the completed research
or prohibit a future, materially distinct consideration supported by a new
identity and evidence basis.

No option is selected, preferred, recommended, or ranked by this package.
