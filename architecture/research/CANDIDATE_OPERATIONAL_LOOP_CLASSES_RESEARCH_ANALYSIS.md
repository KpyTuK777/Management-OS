# Candidate Operational Loop Classes — Research Analysis

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No scenario selection, ranking, approval, validation authorization, milestone, backlog, or work authorization
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper prepares structured material for future human consideration of
possible classes of bounded operational loops. It compares the evidential
properties, observable consequences, attribution risks, limitations, and
assessment dimensions of several classes without selecting a real scenario or
recommending a winning class.

It builds on five non-authoritative research papers:

1. **Bounded Operational Value and Evidence Proposition — Architectural
   Research**;
2. **Bounded Operational Loop Selection and Validation Criteria — Architectural
   Research**;
3. **Operational Loop Validation Protocol — Architectural Research**;
4. **Bounded Operational Loop Validation Scenario — Architectural Research**;
   and
5. **Bounded Operational Loop Scenario Assessment Framework — Architectural
   Research**.

Those papers establish research boundaries for value, whole-loop observation,
Evidence, Outcome, falsification, scenario suitability, attribution, and human
authority. They do not authorize this paper to select a class or case.

## 2. Authority and Semantic Boundary

Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged. This analysis cannot create
canonical meaning, resolve M8.4 objects, select M8.5 alternatives, assign an
M8.6 readiness class, or authorize validation, Engineering Architecture,
implementation, delivery, or a successor milestone.

The analysis preserves:

- Outcome as the final consequence test for a claimed operational effect;
- Learning as downstream of attributable experience and verified Outcome;
- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, hypothesis, Modeled / Scenario Output,
  Recommendation, Decision, Change record, Outcome, and Learning as distinct
  semantic kinds;
- accountable human Decision authority;
- source-owned facts, records, and mutation;
- bounded contribution rather than automatic causal attribution;
- no mandatory single Root Cause; and
- Operational Diagnosis only as research framing for a possible composition of
  existing responsibilities, not a canonical object.

The candidate classes are analytical groupings. They are not canonical classes,
objects, capabilities, taxonomy, backlog categories, or roadmap options.

## 3. Comparison Posture

### 3.1 What is being compared

Each class is examined for whether real cases within it might support a bounded
whole-loop inquiry:

```text
Operational Reality
  → Matter / Context Continuity
  → Investigation
  → Evidence
  → Understanding
  → Human Decision
  → Source-Owned Change
  → Observed Outcome and Verification
  → Learning
```

### 3.2 What is not being inferred

Class-level plausibility does not show that:

- a real candidate exists;
- Evidence is available in a particular context;
- a Change is responsible or authorized;
- Outcome can be attributed;
- Management OS creates value for the class;
- one class is better than another; or
- validation should proceed.

Suitability is case-specific. A strong class can contain unsuitable cases, and a
case from a less obvious class may be credible when its evidence and boundaries
are unusually strong.

### 3.3 No ranking or score

The comparison uses narrative analysis and dimension-specific questions. It
does not assign points, weights, totals, tiers, or rank order. Numerical scoring
would hide non-substitutable boundaries and embed an unreviewed selection
policy. No class is a default winner.

## 4. Shared Assessment Dimensions

Every class is considered through the same seven dimensions:

| Dimension | Human consideration question |
| --- | --- |
| Bounded ownership | Can one accountable human Decision owner, bounded operational scope, source ownership, and prospective Change ownership be identified? |
| Evidence availability | Can attributable pre-Decision and post-Change Evidence, including contrary and missing material, exist? |
| Human Decision presence | Does the situation require contestable accountable judgment rather than a predetermined or automated response? |
| Change reversibility | Could one source-owned intervention be bounded, distinguished, interrupted, corrected, or responsibly reversed? |
| Outcome observability | Can a material intended operational condition and observation horizon be fixed before results and verified from source-owned Observation or Evidence? |
| Comparison feasibility | Is there a credible baseline, trajectory, prior instance, contemporaneous comparison, or pre-stated intended range? |
| Learning potential | Could later comparable experience test whether verified learning improves judgment without becoming authority? |

Failure of human Decision, source ownership, valid Evidence, observable Outcome,
responsible exposure, or plausible attribution cannot be compensated by strength
in other dimensions.

## 5. Recurrent Process Deviation

### 5.1 Class shape

A recurring process or operating condition departs from an intended range,
standard, or expectation within a bounded responsibility. The deviation has a
material operational consequence, but the appropriate response is not fully
predetermined.

### 5.2 Why it may be suitable

- Repetition may provide a baseline, variation history, and later comparable
  cases.
- Current and intended conditions may already be represented in source-owned
  operational records.
- The deviation can require Investigation of several contributors rather than
  one Root Cause.
- One bounded procedural, coordination, capacity, or control Change may be
  distinguishable and reversible.
- Stabilization, recurrence, burden, or downstream consequence may be observable.

### 5.3 Potential Evidence

- source records of normal and deviating process conditions;
- timestamps, volumes, exceptions, rework, delays, or condition assessments;
- Matter history and contextual changes;
- Investigation questions, hypotheses, contrary Evidence, and missing Evidence;
- accountable human Decision intent and alternatives;
- source-owned record of the actual Change; and
- post-Change condition, recurrence, side effects, and verification judgment.

### 5.4 Potential Outcomes

- sustained return to an acceptable operating condition;
- reduced recurrence or severity under comparable exposure;
- faster stabilization without degraded quality or increased burden;
- reduced rework, interruption, or downstream consequence; or
- improved correction when the deviation reappears.

Restored procedural conformance is not automatically the Outcome. The
consequence must matter operationally.

### 5.5 Attribution risks

- regression toward normal conditions may look like Change effect;
- volume, staffing, demand, or upstream inputs may change concurrently;
- the standard itself may be incomplete or inappropriate;
- increased monitoring may temporarily improve behavior;
- multiple local corrections may be inseparable; and
- recurrence may be labeled consistently while underlying contexts differ.

### 5.6 Limitations

This class becomes too simple when the response is dictated by an existing rule.
It becomes too broad when deviation spans several independent processes or
owners. Process metrics may invite proxy optimization and hide displaced harm.

### 5.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Often bounded by process owner, but cross-process sources may fragment accountability |
| Evidence | Operational records may be rich, though measure meaning and missing context require inspection |
| Decision | Present when several explanations or responses are plausible; absent when response is automatic compliance |
| Reversibility | Often feasible for a limited operating adjustment; not guaranteed for policy or structural change |
| Outcome | Potentially visible through condition, stabilization, recurrence, and balancing effects |
| Comparison | Prior deviations and normal range may help, provided exposure and measurement are comparable |
| Learning | Recurrence can test later judgment, but surface similarity must not become authority |

## 6. Coordination Breakdown

### 6.1 Class shape

A bounded handoff or collaboration repeatedly loses context, ownership, Decision
intent, timeliness, or accountable follow-through, producing a material
operational consequence.

### 6.2 Why it may be suitable

- It directly exercises Matter continuity, semantic handoffs, human authority,
  and source ownership.
- Several participants may hold different Evidence or interpretations, creating
  genuine Investigation and judgment.
- A bounded Change in responsibility clarity, information movement, or
  coordination practice may be reversible.
- Delay, rework, interruption, missed condition, or stabilization may be
  observable.
- Repeated handoffs may provide comparison and Learning opportunities.

### 6.3 Potential Evidence

- source records before and after the handoff;
- timestamps and evidence of delay, interruption, duplication, or rework;
- missing or contradictory context at the receiving boundary;
- ownership and Decision records;
- participant observations with role and bias disclosed;
- the actual coordination Change in its owning source; and
- downstream condition, recovery, burden, and adverse effects.

### 6.4 Potential Outcomes

- reduced material context or intent loss;
- faster responsible Decision or recovery without reduced quality;
- fewer ownership gaps, avoidable interruptions, or rework cycles;
- improved fidelity between Decision and source-owned Change; or
- reduced operational consequence from repeated handoff failures.

More messages, faster acknowledgement, or completed handoffs are activity
signals, not sufficient Outcomes.

### 6.5 Attribution risks

- observation may change participant attention and behavior;
- novelty or leadership emphasis may temporarily improve coordination;
- staffing, relationship quality, or workload may explain the result;
- responsibilities may be genuinely shared rather than merely unclear;
- the bottleneck may shift elsewhere; and
- self-report may overstate context quality.

### 6.6 Limitations

Social dynamics, privacy, power, and authority effects may be difficult to bound.
The class is weak when no single Decision owner exists or when the material
Outcome is distant from the coordination event.

### 6.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Requires one accountable Decision owner despite distributed participation and source ownership |
| Evidence | Handoff and timing records may exist, but semantic loss and participant context can be difficult to evidence |
| Decision | Often strong where incomplete or conflicting context requires judgment |
| Reversibility | A bounded coordination adjustment may be reversible, while organizational redesign is not |
| Outcome | Strong only when downstream operational consequence is visible beyond interaction activity |
| Comparison | Repeated handoffs may help, but people, workload, and case complexity must be comparable |
| Learning | Later handoffs can test reuse, provided prior practice does not become mandatory authority |

## 7. Quality Variation

### 7.1 Class shape

A product, service, decision, or operational output varies materially relative
to an intended condition, and the variation cannot be explained or addressed by
a predetermined response alone.

### 7.2 Why it may be suitable

- Variation may be repeatedly observable from source-owned records.
- Several process, context, Evidence, or judgment contributors may be plausible.
- The human may need to balance quality, time, cost, burden, and risk.
- A bounded Change may produce visible improvement or adverse displacement.
- Later outputs may test recurrence and Learning.

### 7.3 Potential Evidence

- source-owned quality observations and definition history;
- distribution, variation, defects, rework, acceptance, or correction records;
- context, workload, input, staffing, and process conditions;
- Evidence supporting and contradicting possible explanations;
- human Decision rationale and tradeoffs;
- actual Change and deviations from intent; and
- post-Change quality, burden, delay, downstream effect, and adverse consequence.

### 7.4 Potential Outcomes

- reduced material variation under comparable conditions;
- sustained improvement in the intended quality condition;
- fewer consequential defects or correction cycles;
- earlier detection and correction without increased false intervention;
- maintained quality with reduced burden; or
- better balancing of quality against time, cost, and operational stability.

### 7.5 Attribution risks

- measurement criteria may change;
- inspection intensity may create apparent deterioration or improvement;
- easy-to-measure proxies may replace actual quality;
- input mix or demand complexity may change;
- delayed quality effects may fall outside observation; and
- local improvement may displace cost or harm downstream.

### 7.6 Limitations

Quality is often multidimensional and contested. A single measure may not
represent the intended condition. Attribution weakens when several upstream
contributors or long feedback delays cannot be separated.

### 7.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Output and Decision ownership may be clear, but quality meaning may be shared across affected sources |
| Evidence | Often measurable, with substantial risk of proxy, definition, and detection distortion |
| Decision | Strong when tradeoffs and uncertainty are genuine; weak when response is fixed inspection or correction |
| Reversibility | Local process adjustment may be reversible; structural quality change may not be |
| Outcome | Observable when source-owned quality and balancing conditions are timely and meaningful |
| Comparison | Historical distributions may help if input mix, detection, and definitions remain comparable |
| Learning | Repeated output permits learning, but generalized rules can ignore changing context |

## 8. Stabilization Problem

### 8.1 Class shape

A bounded operation repeatedly becomes unstable or requires excessive time,
attention, or intervention to return to an acceptable operating condition.

### 8.2 Why it may be suitable

- Initial, unstable, intended, and stabilized conditions may be observable.
- Investigation may need to distinguish several interacting contributors.
- Human judgment may select among containment, correction, no-action, or staged
  responses.
- One bounded reversible Change may be possible.
- Time-to-stability, durability, burden, and recurrence can provide consequence
  evidence.

### 8.3 Potential Evidence

- source-owned condition history and variability;
- prior instability events and recovery trajectories;
- intervention, workload, capacity, and external-factor records;
- Evidence and competing interpretations before Decision;
- Decision intent, expected condition, and bounds;
- actual source-owned Change and concurrent responses; and
- stabilization timing, durability, side effects, recurrence, and verification.

### 8.4 Potential Outcomes

- faster and sustained return to an acceptable condition;
- lower operational burden or intervention intensity;
- reduced severity or recurrence under comparable exposure;
- earlier detection and correction of worsening conditions; or
- improved later response based on verified prior experience.

### 8.5 Attribution risks

- the operation may recover naturally;
- regression toward typical conditions may be strong;
- several containment actions may occur together;
- external demand or capacity may shift;
- “stable” may conceal degraded performance; and
- observation may stop before recurrence or displaced effect appears.

### 8.6 Limitations

Stability requires a credible source-owned meaning and time horizon. This class
is unsuitable when instability is safety-critical, structural, or dominated by
uncontrolled external variability.

### 8.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Often identifiable within one operating responsibility, unless recovery spans many domains |
| Evidence | Condition and recovery history may be rich but natural variability must be visible |
| Decision | Strong where response requires tradeoffs under uncertainty |
| Reversibility | Contained intervention may be reversible; emergency or structural action may not be |
| Outcome | Time, durability, burden, recurrence, and balancing effects may be observable |
| Comparison | Prior recovery trajectories may help but external exposure must be comparable |
| Learning | Repeated instability can test later judgment, with risk of applying the last response mechanically |

## 9. Operational Failure Recovery

### 9.1 Class shape

A bounded operation fails to achieve an intended result, and a responsible human
must understand the condition, decide how to recover, execute a source-owned
response, and verify restored or changed operation.

### 9.2 Why it may be suitable

- Failure creates a material difference between current and intended condition.
- Recovery can exercise the whole loop under genuine uncertainty.
- Source evidence may include conditions before, during, and after failure.
- Human Decision and Change intent may be explicit.
- Recovery time, durability, recurrence, and burden may be observable.

### 9.3 Potential Evidence

- source records of intended result and actual failure condition;
- timelines, interruptions, prior warnings, context, and affected scope;
- Investigation Evidence, contradictory material, and competing explanations;
- accountable Decision, expected recovery condition, and response bounds;
- source-owned recovery Change and concurrent action; and
- observed restoration, residual degradation, recurrence, adverse effects, and
  later response.

### 9.4 Potential Outcomes

- restoration of the intended operating condition;
- shorter responsible recovery without hidden residual harm;
- reduced severity or recurrence of comparable failure;
- improved interruption, correction, or escalation judgment; or
- improved later recovery supported by verified Learning.

Closing the failure record or completing recovery tasks is not sufficient
Outcome evidence.

### 9.5 Attribution risks

- urgency may produce many simultaneous actions;
- natural recovery or external support may dominate;
- post-failure narratives may overstate causal certainty;
- blame pressure may force a single Root Cause;
- only successful recoveries may be retained; and
- immediate restoration may hide later instability.

### 9.6 Limitations

Safety-critical, legally material, irreversible, or high-harm failures are
unsuitable for a first bounded validation. Attribution is weak when recovery
requires many independent owners or interventions.

### 9.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Often clear during bounded recovery, but escalation may distribute authority |
| Evidence | Failure timelines may be rich; retrospective reconstruction and missing pre-failure Evidence are risks |
| Decision | Genuine under uncertainty, though urgency can narrow alternatives |
| Reversibility | Recovery actions may be reversible, but emergency action and failure consequence may not be |
| Outcome | Restoration, durability, burden, and recurrence may be observable |
| Comparison | Prior failures or expected recovery conditions may help; exact comparability is often limited |
| Learning | Later failures can test learning, with high risk of overgeneralizing one explanation |

## 10. Decision-Latency Condition

### 10.1 Class shape

A recurrent operational matter remains undecided longer than the condition
reasonably permits because context, Evidence, ownership, alternatives, or
authority are fragmented or unclear.

### 10.2 Why it may be suitable

- Matter continuity and handoff evidence may be directly relevant.
- Genuine human Decision remains central.
- Delay sources and changes in understanding may be observable.
- A bounded coordination or evidence-availability Change may be reversible.
- Time-to-Decision and downstream consequence may be compared across instances.

### 10.3 Potential Evidence

- timestamps from signal, Matter, Investigation, Evidence, and Decision;
- missing context, interruptions, waits, and ownership changes;
- Decision alternatives and uncertainty;
- source-owned Change intended to improve continuity or availability; and
- downstream Outcome, correction, reversal, quality, burden, and adverse effect.

### 10.4 Potential Outcomes

- faster sufficiently supported Decision;
- reduced avoidable delay without increased error or hidden risk;
- improved downstream operational condition due to timely Action; or
- better interruption or deferral when Evidence remains insufficient.

Decision speed alone is not the final consequence test.

### 10.5 Attribution risks

- urgency or leadership attention may create a novelty effect;
- faster Decisions may be lower quality;
- case complexity may differ;
- external approval delays may dominate; and
- the downstream operational Outcome may be too distant.

### 10.6 Limitations

This class tests whole-loop value only when a material downstream Outcome can be
observed. Otherwise it primarily tests management capacity or process activity.

### 10.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Decision owner may be clear, while delays may arise in external authority domains |
| Evidence | Timing data may be strong; Evidence quality and missing context require semantic analysis |
| Decision | Central and genuine when alternatives remain contestable |
| Reversibility | Information or coordination Change may be reversible |
| Outcome | Weak if limited to speed; stronger when downstream condition and quality are observed |
| Comparison | Prior matters may help, with substantial complexity and urgency differences |
| Learning | Repeated Decisions can test improvement, but speed must not become an automatic rule |

## 11. Recurring Exception or Rework Loop

### 11.1 Class shape

The same or comparable operational output repeatedly requires exception handling,
correction, rework, or clarification before it can achieve its intended use.

### 11.2 Why it may be suitable

- Rework and exceptions may have attributable source records.
- Context and Evidence loss across handoffs may be visible.
- Human judgment may be needed to distinguish legitimate variation from
  preventable failure.
- One bounded reversible Change may address a contributor without claiming a
  universal Cause.
- recurrence, burden, delay, and downstream quality may be observable.

### 11.3 Potential Evidence

- exception and correction histories;
- original and revised outputs;
- source context, handoff records, and missing Evidence;
- operational burden, delay, and affected consequence;
- Decision and actual Change records; and
- post-Change exception frequency, severity, burden, quality, and side effects.

### 11.4 Potential Outcomes

- reduced avoidable rework under comparable conditions;
- faster correction without degraded quality;
- reduced context loss or repeated clarification;
- lower operational burden; or
- better distinction between legitimate exception and preventable deviation.

### 11.5 Attribution risks

- reporting intensity may change;
- input complexity or volume may shift;
- rework may move to another team or become hidden;
- fewer exceptions may reflect weakened detection; and
- local standardization may reduce flexibility or quality.

### 11.6 Limitations

This class is too simple when correction is predetermined and no genuine human
Decision exists. Rework count alone is an activity proxy unless connected to a
material operating condition.

### 11.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Often bounded, though rework may cross creator, reviewer, and consumer sources |
| Evidence | Exception records may be available, with risks of hidden work and changing detection |
| Decision | Present when legitimate variation and response require judgment; absent in routine correction |
| Reversibility | A bounded handoff or practice Change is often reversible |
| Outcome | Burden, delay, quality, and recurrence may be visible beyond exception count |
| Comparison | Repeated outputs may help if complexity and exposure remain comparable |
| Learning | Later exceptions can test learning, with risk of rigid rule formation |

## 12. Resource-Contention Condition

### 12.1 Class shape

A bounded operation repeatedly experiences conflict over capacity, attention,
shared resources, or sequencing, requiring an accountable human tradeoff.

### 12.2 Why it may be suitable

- Human Decision and tradeoffs are explicit.
- Evidence about demand, capacity, consequence, and alternatives may exist.
- One reversible allocation or operating-policy Change may be traceable.
- throughput, delay, burden, or intended operational condition may be observable.
- repeated contention may support comparison and Learning.

### 12.3 Potential Evidence

- source-owned demand, capacity, wait, workload, and consequence records;
- affected obligations and constraints;
- competing interpretations and Recommendation rationale;
- accountable allocation Decision;
- actual source-owned Change; and
- downstream condition, displaced effects, fairness concerns, and later
  contention.

### 12.4 Potential Outcomes

- improved intended operational condition under bounded capacity;
- reduced material delay or overload;
- improved recovery or correction;
- reduced displaced harm; or
- better future tradeoff judgment with verified experience.

### 12.5 Attribution risks

- demand varies independently;
- local improvement shifts burden elsewhere;
- prioritization may be confused with validation authority;
- affected stakeholders may be outside the boundary;
- capacity changes may occur concurrently; and
- short-term throughput may obscure quality or sustainability.

### 12.6 Limitations

This class can drift into product priority, delivery planning, or organizational
authority. It is suitable only when the operational scope, Decision authority,
and displaced effects remain bounded.

### 12.7 Dimension application

| Dimension | Class-specific consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Requires explicit allocation Decision owner and visibility of affected source owners |
| Evidence | Capacity and demand records may exist, but meaning and hidden burden require inspection |
| Decision | Strong due to genuine tradeoffs under constraint |
| Reversibility | Bounded allocation Change may be reversible, subject to downstream commitments |
| Outcome | Delay, overload, throughput, quality, and displaced harm may be observed |
| Comparison | Repeated contention helps only when demand and capacity exposure are comparable |
| Learning | Later tradeoffs can test learning without turning one allocation into standing authority |

## 13. Cross-Class Observations

### 13.1 Evidence-rich classes are not automatically preferable

Process, quality, and exception classes may produce abundant records. Those
records can remain activity or proxy evidence if the intended operational
condition, Decision, Change, and Outcome are not traceable.

### 13.2 Human-rich classes may be attribution-poor

Coordination, failure recovery, and resource contention often contain genuine
judgment but also social, authority, and external factors that complicate
attribution.

### 13.3 Observable classes may still lack value

Stabilization, recovery, and process deviation may show clear operational
movement. Natural recovery, regression, changed exposure, or concurrent action
may explain the Outcome.

### 13.4 Recurrent classes improve Learning potential but not causal certainty

Recurrence can support comparison and later learning. It does not establish one
Cause, comparable context, or transferability.

### 13.5 No class removes the need for case evidence

Every class requires real evidence of ownership, Decision, source authority,
Change boundedness, Outcome, comparison, confounders, and responsible exposure.
Class membership is not a suitability disposition.

## 14. Human Questions Before Any Selection

Before selecting any real scenario, the accountable human authority should be
able to answer the following questions from evidence.

### Operational reality and value

1. What real operational condition exists independently of Management OS?
2. Why is the consequence material, and to whom?
3. What current and intended condition could bound the value claim?
4. Is the scenario being considered because it is evidential, or because it is
   convenient, visible, measurable, or expected to succeed?

### Scope and ownership

5. Who owns the human Decision?
6. Which sources own facts, records, and any possible Change?
7. Which participants, constraints, or affected Outcomes sit outside the stated
   boundary?
8. Can the scenario remain bounded without hiding a material authority domain?

### Evidence and understanding

9. Which Evidence exists before Decision, and which material Evidence is
   missing or inaccessible?
10. Can contrary, null, negative, and adverse evidence remain visible?
11. Are Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, modeled output, Recommendation,
    and Decision distinguishable in the available sources?
12. Does the situation require genuine human judgment without assuming one Root
    Cause or creating a Diagnosis object?

### Change and responsible exposure

13. Could one Change be source-owned, distinguishable, bounded, and responsibly
    reversible?
14. What concurrent interventions might prevent interpretation?
15. Could participation, observation, or Change create disproportionate harm,
    coercion, privacy loss, or authority distortion?

### Outcome and comparison

16. What material Outcome could be fixed before observation?
17. Who owns Outcome verification?
18. What observation horizon is justified?
19. What baseline, prior instance, contemporaneous comparison, or intended range
    is credible?
20. What would count as negative, null, adverse, or indeterminate?

### Attribution and Learning

21. How could a change in Understanding and human Decision be traced into the
    actual source-owned Change?
22. Which alternative explanations and external factors must be considered?
23. What maximum bounded contribution claim could the evidence support?
24. Is a later comparable situation plausible for examining Learning?
25. How will prior experience remain contextual and challengeable rather than
    become authority through similarity?

### Decision boundary

26. What decision is actually being requested: further evidence, suitability
    assessment, selection consideration, preparation, or something else?
27. Which conclusions and actions remain explicitly outside that decision?
28. Can the authority legitimately reject every class and candidate?

## 15. Data Needed for Informed Human Consideration

The human should not decide from class descriptions alone. At minimum, each real
candidate under consideration would need an attributable evidence set covering:

### Candidate identity and materiality

- bounded operational-condition description;
- source, timing, scope, affected operation, and consequence;
- recurrence history or credible recurrence basis;
- reason the condition matters to an accountable owner; and
- reason the candidate entered consideration.

### Ownership and authority

- accountable Decision owner;
- owners of relevant facts and records;
- owner of any prospective Change;
- affected participants and external authority constraints; and
- evidence that Management OS, AI, and assessors remain non-authoritative.

### Evidence feasibility

- available pre-Decision sources and limitations;
- possible contrary and missing Evidence;
- post-Change Observation sources;
- provenance, correction, time, and semantic meaning;
- access and inspectability limits; and
- known measurement changes or proxy risks.

### Decision and Change feasibility

- plausible alternatives and material uncertainty;
- evidence that Decision remains genuinely open;
- prospective Change surface and source ownership;
- reversibility, interruption, correction, and blast-radius considerations; and
- known concurrent interventions.

### Outcome and comparison feasibility

- intended operational-condition candidate;
- possible primary Outcome and balancing dimensions;
- source-owned observation and verification ownership;
- justified horizon;
- baseline, trajectory, comparable cases, or intended range;
- exposure and context differences; and
- potential adverse, delayed, displaced, null, and indeterminate evidence.

### Attribution and validity

- pre-existing trajectory;
- manager knowledge and action outside Management OS;
- external events and confounders;
- selection, confirmation, novelty, and measurement-distortion risks;
- plausible contribution mechanism; and
- maximum permitted inference.

### Learning feasibility

- credible later comparable events;
- how context and provenance could remain attached;
- how later use, correction, rejection, or non-use could be observed; and
- why similarity would not become automatic Evidence or authority.

These are research data needs, not a canonical schema, data model, collection
plan, or implementation requirement.

## 16. Human Consideration Boundary

Human consideration may compare evidence, uncertainty, burden, and
non-substitutable boundaries across classes and candidates. It should not be
replaced by:

- a score or weighted total;
- an AI-generated ranking;
- data availability alone;
- user enthusiasm;
- expected ease of implementation;
- predicted positive Outcome;
- scenario-class prestige or apparent representativeness; or
- pressure to ensure validation begins.

The human may conclude:

- more candidate evidence is needed;
- a candidate should receive a separate suitability assessment;
- a candidate or class is unsuitable for the bounded purpose;
- available evidence is indeterminate; or
- no current candidate warrants further consideration.

This paper does not make any of those judgments for a real case.

## 17. Non-Recommendation

No candidate class is recommended as a winner. The analysis shows different
evidential opportunities and different limitations:

- process deviation and exception classes may offer strong operational records
  but risk proxy and compliance substitution;
- coordination classes may stress handoff and authority boundaries but face
  social and novelty confounding;
- quality classes may offer repeated observations but risk measurement
  distortion and displaced effects;
- stabilization and failure-recovery classes may expose consequence clearly but
  face natural recovery and concurrent intervention;
- decision-latency classes center human judgment but require a downstream
  operational Outcome; and
- resource-contention classes expose tradeoffs but may drift into priority and
  delivery authority.

Only case-specific evidence can support later suitability assessment. Assessment
would remain distinct from selection; selection distinct from approval; approval
distinct from preparation and execution.

## 18. Non-Decision and Non-Authorization

This paper creates no canonical candidate class, taxonomy, scenario, assessment,
object, capability, lifecycle, protocol, governance authority, review gate,
score, ranking, selection, implementation requirement, prototype, Engineering
Architecture, validation preparation or execution, milestone, M9, stage, order,
backlog task, product roadmap, or delivery commitment.

Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged. M8 remains Completed / Closed.
Outcome remains the final consequence test; semantic kinds remain distinct;
human Decision and source ownership remain controlling; bounded contribution
remains distinct from causality; Root Cause remains non-mandatory; and
Operational Diagnosis remains research framing only.
