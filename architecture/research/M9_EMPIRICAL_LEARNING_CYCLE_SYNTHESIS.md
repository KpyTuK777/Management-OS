# 1. Purpose and interpretive boundary

This synthesis answers one descriptive question:

> What responsibilities and transitions appear necessary for one bounded
> empirical-learning inquiry to remain inspectable and semantically honest?

The answer is a conceptual composition of responsibilities already visible in
the source architecture and research. It is not a claim that every inquiry must
instantiate every responsibility, use one order, or adopt a product workflow.
It neither opens empirical work nor defines how an organization must conduct it.

The following distinctions govern the whole paper:

```text
Synthesis ≠ lifecycle
Composition ≠ mandatory sequence
Conceptual transition ≠ repository state transition
Learning cycle ≠ product workflow
Iteration ≠ automatic repetition
Diagram arrows ≠ causal claims or authority
```

Any arrows below mean only that information or responsibility may become
relevant to another responsibility. They do not mean causation, completion,
permission, required order, automatic state movement, or transfer of human
authority.

This paper creates no canonical lifecycle, mandatory workflow, state machine,
governance process, stage-model selection, validation protocol, implementation
method, automatic transition rule, metric, KPI, or experiment.

# 2. Starting point: bounded uncertainty

A coherent inquiry appears to begin not with activity or a presumed product
benefit, but with bounded uncertainty. The starting condition contains:

- an explicit Product Value Hypothesis or other bounded empirical question;
- a statement of what is unknown;
- a defined claim boundary identifying what a possible finding could and could
  not support; and
- no presumed positive result.

The question must make room for strengthening, weakening, unsupported,
negative, null, contrary, mixed, and indeterminate findings. It must not embed a
preferred Outcome, treat architecture coherence as product-value Evidence, or
assume that the necessary Evidence, case, authority, implementation, or Outcome
observability exists.

An unselected hypothesis may be considered analytically, but empirical work
cannot proceed merely because a hypothesis is documented. A hypothesis must be
separately selected by attributable human authority before empirical work.
Selection of a hypothesis would still not select a case, approve Evidence
access, authorize validation, authorize an operational Change, or authorize
implementation.

The bounded starting point makes the inquiry falsifiable in spirit without
pretending that every result will resolve the question. It also provides the
reference against which Evidence sufficiency, Outcome relevance, attribution,
and stopping can later be judged.

# 3. Candidate learning-cycle composition

The following is one descriptive composition. Numbering makes the
responsibilities addressable; it does not establish mandatory order. Depending
on the inquiry, responsibilities may overlap, recur through fresh human
consideration, be unnecessary, or end the inquiry.

```text
Bounded question and hypothesis consideration
  → case, Evidence, Outcome, and attribution consideration
  → bounded design and separate authorization
  → actual observation, accountable Decision, and source-owned Change
  → Outcome verification, interpretation, and Learning
  → critical challenge and possible feedback consideration
  → human stopping, iteration, or transition consideration
```

The diagram is not a lifecycle or causal model.

## 3.1 Question identity

**Purpose:** Give the empirical uncertainty a stable, inspectable identity,
including the exact subject, expected product contribution, comparison or
reference condition where relevant, context, and exclusions.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Ambiguity about what the inquiry is asking and what a
possible result could legitimately mean.

**Required semantic boundaries:** A question is not Evidence, a hypothesis, a
Decision, validation, or a product-value claim. Scope and expected effect must
remain distinct from observed fact.

**Applicable human authority:** An accountable human frames or accepts the
question within the authority granted for preparation. The system may help make
ambiguity visible.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** The question is compound,
unfalsifiable, circular, implicitly positive, or too broad for one bounded
inquiry.

**Must not be confused with:** Selecting a hypothesis, authorizing a case,
defining a metric, or opening validation.

## 3.2 Hypothesis consideration and attributable selection

**Purpose:** Compare the bounded question with one or more unvalidated Product
Value Hypotheses and, only under separate authority, record which hypothesis is
selected for empirical consideration.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Which product-value proposition the inquiry would
actually bear on and who accepted that focus.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Consideration is not selection; selection is
not validation; relevance to several hypotheses is not Evidence for any of
them. H1–H5 retain their distinct identities and Unknown validation status.

**Applicable human authority:** Hypothesis selection remains a separate,
attributable human Decision. AI may compare wording, scope, dependencies, and
risks but cannot select.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** No hypothesis fits the question,
the expected effect cannot be bounded, or the available authority declines to
select.

**Must not be confused with:** Ranking H1–H5, selecting a stage model, promising
a positive Outcome, or authorizing empirical work.

## 3.3 Candidate case or scenario consideration

**Purpose:** Determine whether a candidate operational context could make the
selected question inspectable without treating convenience as suitability.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether a bounded episode or setting could expose the
relevant context, behavior, Change, Outcome, and product contribution.

**Required semantic boundaries:** A synthetic case is training material; a
historical case is preserved reality with retrospective limits; a live case is
actual operational reality. Case consideration is not case selection.

**Applicable human authority:** An authorized human selects or rejects a case
or scenario and preserves operational ownership, privacy, risk, and access
constraints.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** No suitable case exists, the case
is biased toward a result, the relevant Outcome lies outside it, or participation
and consequence boundaries are unacceptable.

**Must not be confused with:** Hypothesis selection, training suitability,
representativeness of Management OS generally, or validation authorization.

## 3.4 Evidence and access readiness

**Purpose:** Inspect whether legitimate source-owned Evidence can be accessed,
related, contested, corrected, and protected sufficiently for the exact claim.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether the inquiry can observe its subject rather than
infer it from missing, modeled, inaccessible, or semantically unsuitable
information.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Recorded Information is not automatically
Evidence; access is not ownership; availability is not sufficiency; AI output
and synthetic content are not empirical Evidence.

**Applicable human authority:** Source owners and legitimate access authorities
approve use. Evidence interpretation and correction retain their applicable
human and source authority.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Material Evidence is absent,
inaccessible, unreliable, non-contestable, unsafe to use, or insufficient for
the bounded claim.

**Must not be confused with:** Data completeness, permission to validate,
technical integration readiness, or proof of the hypothesis.

## 3.5 Outcome observability and attribution boundary

**Purpose:** Establish whether a consequence relevant to the question could be
observed and what product, operational, external, temporal, and measurement
factors would constrain attribution.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether activity can be distinguished from consequence
and whether any observed association could bear on product contribution.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Activity and completion are not Outcome;
Outcome observation is not causal attribution; an operational Outcome is not
automatically a product-value Outcome.

**Applicable human authority:** Accountable domain humans accept the Outcome
meaning and observation boundary. Attribution conclusions remain human-owned
and contestable.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** The Outcome is unobservable,
lagged beyond the inquiry, defined after the result, dominated by external
factors, or inseparable from concurrent Changes.

**Must not be confused with:** Metric selection by this synthesis, a success
threshold, validation readiness, or a guaranteed causal design.

## 3.6 Bounded empirical-design consideration

**Purpose:** Consider a proportionate arrangement of question, case, Evidence,
observation, comparison, attribution limits, human participation, risk, and
stopping conditions.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether the proposed inquiry shape could answer the
bounded question honestly and safely.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Design consideration is not a protocol,
experiment, implementation requirement, validation authorization, or actual
observation.

**Applicable human authority:** Appropriately accountable humans consider and,
where authorized, approve an empirical design. Specialist ethical, legal,
operational, privacy, and source authorities remain intact.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** The design cannot separate
modeled from observed content, imposes disproportionate burden, lacks a credible
reference condition, or cannot preserve adverse and indeterminate results.

**Must not be confused with:** The non-canonical validation-protocol research,
product workflow, Engineering Architecture, or permission to execute.

## 3.7 Separate authorization

**Purpose:** Make explicit whether the precise bounded empirical work may occur,
with named scope, authorities, exclusions, and conditions.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether preparation has legitimate permission to move
into empirical activity.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Readiness assessment is not authorization;
one authorization does not imply Evidence access, operational Decision, Change,
implementation, or future iteration authority.

**Applicable human authority:** Only the accountable authority defined by
governance may authorize validation. Other approvals remain separately owned.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Authority is unavailable,
conditions are unmet, scope is ambiguous, or authorization is withheld.

**Must not be confused with:** Automatic transition, repository state inferred
from a diagram, completion of preparation, or approval of a positive claim.

## 3.8 Observation of actual operational reality

**Purpose:** Observe relevant real conditions, actions, interactions, and
changes in a live or legitimate historical context under the authorized scope.

**Uncertainty reduced:** What actually occurs or occurred, as distinct from what
the architecture, plan, participant, or model says should occur.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Observation is not Interpretation; absence of
a record is not evidence of absence; historical, contemporaneous, and modeled
material must remain distinguishable.

**Applicable human authority:** Source owners and accountable participants
retain authority over their records and actions. Observers do not inherit
operational authority through observation.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Behavior changes through novelty
or observation, records are incomplete, relevant events do not occur, or the
authorized window misses the consequence.

**Must not be confused with:** Synthetic rehearsal, AI simulation, validation
success, or a complete representation of reality.

## 3.9 Human Decision and source-owned Change where applicable

**Purpose:** Preserve the accountable Decision and the actual, source-owned
Change when the inquiry includes operational intervention, including variance
between intended, authorized, and enacted Change.

**Uncertainty reduced:** What humans decided, what was actually changed, by
whom, under what authority, and with what divergence.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Recommendation is not Decision; Decision is
not Change; intended Change is not actual Change; recorded completion is not
implementation fidelity.

**Applicable human authority:** The accountable operational Decision maker and
source capability owner authorize and enact their respective Decision and
Change. AI cannot act as either.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** No Decision occurs, Change is not
executed, adoption is partial, the handoff loses rationale or constraints, or
concurrent Changes obscure the intervention.

**Must not be confused with:** A requirement that every inquiry intervene,
implementation authorization, product-driven control, or causal proof.

## 3.10 Outcome observation and verification

**Purpose:** Observe and verify consequences against the bounded Outcome meaning
while preserving adverse, mixed, null, contrary, and indeterminate results.

**Uncertainty reduced:** What followed in the relevant context and whether the
observation is sufficiently grounded to support a bounded interpretation.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Completion is not Outcome; observed Outcome
is not expected Outcome; Outcome verification is not attribution or Learning.

**Applicable human authority:** Source owners provide observations; accountable
humans accept verification appropriate to the domain and claim boundary.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Observation time is insufficient,
definitions shift, verification is unavailable, the actual Change is unclear,
or consequences conflict across affected groups.

**Must not be confused with:** A positive product claim, universal success,
validation of Management OS, or automatic closure.

## 3.11 Interpretation and attribution assessment

**Purpose:** Relate the Evidence, actual Change, observed Outcome, alternative
explanations, and product interaction without overstating causal contribution.

**Uncertainty reduced:** What the observations may support, weaken, leave
unsupported, or fail to distinguish.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Association is not causation; interpretation
is not Evidence; product use is not product contribution; confidence is not
authority.

**Applicable human authority:** Accountable humans accept or contest
interpretation within their scope. AI may compare hypotheses and expose
inconsistencies but cannot declare causal truth.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Attribution is impossible,
novelty or external factors dominate, comparison is unsuitable, or competing
explanations remain equally plausible.

**Must not be confused with:** Outcome observation, blame allocation, product-
value proof, or exclusion of an indeterminate result.

## 3.12 Learning synthesis

**Purpose:** Preserve what the inquiry changes in bounded understanding,
including limitations, Evidence quality, failed assumptions, operational
conditions, and remaining uncertainty.

**Uncertainty reduced:** What is now better understood and what remains unknown
after the inquiry.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Outcome is not Learning; interpretation is not
automatically adopted Learning; Learning does not rewrite Evidence, history, or
the authority of prior Decisions.

**Applicable human authority:** Humans accept, reject, qualify, or adopt
Learning. Organizational reuse remains subject to Memory, Knowledge, access,
and current-context authority.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** The synthesis becomes a success
narrative, erases negative or unresolved findings, overgeneralizes from one
case, or cannot distinguish product learning from operational learning.

**Must not be confused with:** Organizational memory automatically created by a
record, canonical truth, validation of all H1–H5, or a new mandate.

## 3.13 Critical challenge or review

**Purpose:** Test the inquiry's claim boundary, Evidence use, semantic fidelity,
authority preservation, attribution, contrary findings, and overreach.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether the emerging Learning survives independent or
adversarial inspection within the available Evidence.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Review is not source authority, re-performance
of the inquiry, automatic correction, approval, or governance transition.

**Applicable human authority:** An appropriately independent human reviewer or
review body challenges the work; original authorities retain their defined
roles unless governance says otherwise.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** Review lacks access or
independence, discovers semantic handoff failure, exposes unsupported claims, or
cannot resolve a material disagreement.

**Must not be confused with:** A mandatory gate defined here, validation
authorization, guaranteed objectivity, or permission for downstream work.

## 3.14 Architecture or product feedback consideration

**Purpose:** Consider whether bounded Learning raises a product-understanding,
hypothesis, capability, architecture-gap, or implementation-support question.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether the finding has a legitimate implication worth
separate accountable consideration beyond the inquiry itself.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Feedback is not an architecture Decision, a
Gap, a new capability, a requirement, backlog priority, or implementation
authority.

**Applicable human authority:** Product, architecture, governance, and source
owners separately disposition feedback within their respective authority.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** The finding is too local,
attribution is weak, the feedback exceeds the claim, or current architecture
already owns the relevant responsibility.

**Must not be confused with:** Automatic canonical amendment, roadmap change,
defect declaration, or proof that implementation is needed.

## 3.15 Stopping, iteration, or transition consideration

**Purpose:** Decide whether to stop, preserve an indeterminate result, refine a
question, consider another bounded inquiry, or seek separate authority for a
different kind of work.

**Uncertainty reduced:** Whether further work is likely to reduce material
uncertainty proportionately and legitimately.

**Required semantic boundaries:** Iteration is not automatic repetition;
stopping is not success or failure; transition consideration is not a
repository state transition.

**Applicable human authority:** An accountable human makes each stopping,
iteration, or transition Decision. Any new hypothesis, case, access, validation,
Change, or implementation authority remains separate.

**Likely failure or indeterminate condition:** No authorized next step exists,
additional work is unlikely to improve confidence, harm or burden is
disproportionate, or the remaining uncertainty cannot be resolved.

**Must not be confused with:** A loop cadence, automatic re-entry, lifecycle
completion, or authority created by the prior inquiry.

# 4. Parallelism and conditionality

The responsibilities compose around an inquiry; they do not form a rigid
waterfall. Several relationships may be concurrent or conditional:

- Evidence readiness and Outcome observability may be assessed together because
  the Evidence needed depends on the Outcome claim, while Outcome observability
  depends on available and legitimate sources.
- Case consideration, Evidence access constraints, human participation, and
  attribution limits may inform one another before any selection or
  authorization.
- Claim-boundary refinement and hypothesis consideration may alternate while
  remaining preparation only.
- Critical challenge may occur during design, observation, interpretation, or
  Learning rather than only after a completed account.
- Learning synthesis may begin with limitation and provenance notes during the
  inquiry, provided later synthesis does not convert provisional Interpretation
  into established Learning.
- Outcome observation may overlap with ongoing Change when consequences unfold
  at different times, but observation windows and Change versions must remain
  distinguishable.

Some responsibilities may be unnecessary for some inquiry shapes. A
retrospective historical inquiry may include no new operational Decision or
Change. An observation-only inquiry may inspect context preservation during
active work without intervening. Structured re-engagement for H3 or H5 may
require a completed case but no new implementation. Engineering Architecture
and implementation may be unnecessary; their absence is not a gap unless the
bounded inquiry actually requires separately authorized product support.

The composition may legitimately terminate before empirical work when no
hypothesis is selected, no suitable case exists, Evidence is inaccessible or
insufficient, Outcome is unobservable, attribution cannot bear the proposed
claim, authority is unavailable, risk is disproportionate, or the empirical
design cannot remain semantically honest.

New Evidence may require human reconsideration of the question, case, Outcome
boundary, or design. A return is a fresh act of judgment, not an automatic
transition. Some uncertainty may remain permanently unresolved. An
indeterminate inquiry may still create bounded Learning about Evidence quality,
case suitability, observability, attribution limits, or inquiry design.

# 5. Relationship to H1–H5

No Product Value Hypothesis is selected, ranked, preferred, or validated by
this synthesis. Different hypotheses may require different inquiry shapes, and
conceptual fit does not establish validation readiness.

## H1 — Operational Context Preservation

H1 may be observable during active work or through legitimate historical
reconstruction. Relevant responsibilities include question identity, Evidence
and provenance, case boundaries, contemporaneous observation, and later ability
to reconstruct relationships and uncertainty. An inquiry would need an
appropriate fragmented reference condition and real user interaction; a
well-structured synthetic record cannot show comparative preservation.

## H2 — Better Decision Support

H2 may be examined around an active accountable Decision episode. The
composition preserves the Evidence basis, uncertainty, alternatives, rationale,
authority, intended Change, actual Change, and later Outcome. The claim concerns
support and inspectability, not whether the Decision was correct. Product use,
a complete-looking Decision record, and a positive Outcome do not by themselves
show better Decision support.

## H3 — Experience Amplification

H3 may require structured re-engagement with a completed real case and its
preserved history. Replay and critical challenge can help inspect missed
signals, alternative interpretations, and bounded lessons without altering the
historical branch. A synthetic case can prepare the reflection method but cannot
amplify a lived experience or supply empirical support for H3.

## H4 — Organizational Operational Memory

H4 may require continuity across time, actors, personnel changes, access
contexts, corrections, and changes in understanding. Learning synthesis alone
is insufficient: authorized actors would need to recover provenance, rationale,
uncertainty, actual Change, Outcome, and revision history later. One preserved
case does not show durable organizational recoverability.

## H5 — Manager Capability Development

H5 may require repeated, structured re-engagement across time and cases, plus
observation of judgment development rather than interface familiarity or
narrative memorization. It may draw on replay, critical challenge, hypothesis
formation, uncertainty preservation, and transfer limits. No automatic cadence
or repetition is implied, and one bounded inquiry cannot establish accelerated
capability development.

Evidence for one hypothesis would not validate another. H1 and H2 may be
observable during active work; H3 and H5 may require structured re-engagement;
H4 may require continuity across time or actors. These are descriptive
differences, not a sequence or priority.

# 6. Synthetic, historical, and replay materials

The following labels are descriptive distinctions for this synthesis. They do
not create new canonical semantic kinds, objects, capabilities, or lifecycle
states.

| Material context | Descriptive role | Evidence position | Primary limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Synthetic case | Modeled training material for architecture, UX, information-model, workflow, AI-reasoning, or replay preparation. | No empirical Evidence. | Authored facts and Outcomes cannot show real behavior or product contribution. |
| Historical case | A real preserved operational episode for which historical Evidence may exist. | May contain legitimate historical Evidence with source authority and provenance. | Retrospective completeness, recall, missingness, selection, and present-day interpretation remain uncertain. |
| Replay branch | Modeled alternative reasoning or Decision applied to preserved history without altering it. | The historical inputs retain their status; the alternative branch and its effects are modeled. | Cannot establish the alternative Outcome that would really have occurred. |
| Live bounded case | Actual operational context observed prospectively or contemporaneously within a defined scope. | May support empirical learning if separately authorized and sufficiently evidenced. | Observation effects, incomplete Evidence, operational risk, lag, attribution, access, and authority remain material. |

A historical case is not complete merely because records exist. A replay branch
must never overwrite the historical Decision, actual Change, observed Outcome,
or later corrections. A live bounded case does not automatically become valid
Evidence for product contribution. Synthetic usefulness remains preparation
only.

# 7. Evidence model across the composition

The composition depends on Evidence responsibilities rather than on record
volume:

- **Source identity and provenance:** preserve who or what produced an
  observation, under which source authority, at what time, and with what later
  corrections.
- **Evidence availability:** make visible what can and cannot legitimately be
  accessed within privacy, consent, ownership, security, and operational
  boundaries.
- **Evidence sufficiency relative to claim:** judge sufficiency only against the
  exact bounded claim, plausible alternatives, and required observation window.
- **Contestability:** retain disagreement, challenge, correction, provenance,
  and the ability to inspect how an Interpretation used the Evidence.
- **Missing Evidence:** preserve known gaps, inaccessible sources, non-reporting,
  changed definitions, and uncertainty rather than silently completing them.
- **Source-owned observations:** keep observations within their source meaning
  and authority while relating them across the inquiry.
- **Historical, contemporaneous, and modeled distinction:** label and preserve
  when material was observed, later reconstructed, or authored for analysis.

The governing inequalities are:

```text
More Evidence ≠ sufficient Evidence
Recorded information ≠ Evidence automatically
AI output ≠ Evidence
Synthetic material ≠ empirical Evidence
```

Sufficiency is claim-relative and cannot be declared merely because a record is
detailed, internally consistent, or machine-readable. AI can help locate,
compare, structure, and challenge material, but its summary, prediction,
classification, inference, or generated branch is not source Evidence.

# 8. Human authority across the composition

Authority remains distributed and separately human-owned. Completion of one
Decision cannot imply another.

| Decision or disposition | Human authority boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis selection | An attributable human authority selects the bounded hypothesis; documentation and AI comparison do not select it. |
| Case or scenario selection | An authorized human accepts the operational relevance, risk, and scope; training suitability does not select a real case. |
| Access approval | Legitimate source, privacy, security, legal, and organizational authorities approve access within scope. |
| Empirical-design approval | Accountable humans accept the bounded design and its ethical, operational, evidentiary, and attribution limits. |
| Validation authorization | The designated governance authority separately authorizes the precise empirical work; readiness or design cannot self-authorize. |
| Accountable operational Decision | The legitimate operational owner decides whether and how to act; validation goals and AI do not displace this authority. |
| Change authorization | The source capability or operational authority approves actual Change; a Decision record is not execution permission. |
| Interpretation acceptance | Where acceptance has authority effect, accountable humans accept, qualify, contest, or reject the Interpretation. |
| Learning adoption | Humans decide what bounded Learning is accepted and how it may be reused without converting history into current truth. |
| Architecture-feedback disposition | Authorized architecture and product owners decide whether feedback warrants inquiry, rejection, deferral, or a separate Decision. |
| Iteration or stopping Decision | Accountable humans decide whether further work is proportionate and separately authorized. |

AI may assist analysis, preparation, comparison, traceability, uncertainty
surfacing, replay, and critical challenge. It cannot inherit, merge, imply, or
exercise any of these authorities.

# 9. Outcome, attribution, and Learning

Outcome integrity depends on keeping activity, consequence, product
contribution, attribution, and Learning distinct:

```text
Activity ≠ Outcome
Completion ≠ Outcome
Observed association ≠ causal attribution
Positive Outcome ≠ product-value proof
Negative Outcome ≠ automatic product failure
```

An indeterminate Outcome is valid. An operational effect may be positive while
the product contribution is absent or unknown. A negative Outcome may result
from the operational Decision, incomplete Change, external conditions,
measurement change, novelty, or an unsupported hypothesis; it does not
automatically establish general product failure. Conversely, a positive Outcome
may have occurred without Management OS contribution.

Learning may concern limitations, Evidence quality, missingness, semantic
handoff failure, Outcome observability, attribution boundaries, case
suitability, user burden, inquiry design, or the product hypothesis itself. It
does not need a positive operational Outcome to be legitimate.

Learning does not rewrite history. Later understanding may qualify an earlier
Interpretation while the original Evidence, Decision rationale, actual Change,
and observed Outcome remain recoverable. One case does not validate Management
OS generally, and one finding cannot be generalized beyond its Evidence and
claim boundary without separate support.

# 10. Failure, falsification, and indeterminate paths

The following are legitimate possible dispositions or findings:

- the hypothesis is weakened;
- the hypothesis remains unsupported;
- the case is unsuitable;
- Evidence is insufficient;
- the Outcome is unobservable;
- the Change is not executed;
- a semantic handoff fails;
- necessary authority is unavailable;
- attribution is impossible;
- no product contribution is observed;
- the result is dominated by novelty or external factors; or
- the result remains indeterminate.

These conditions are not automatically process failures. Case unsuitability,
Evidence insufficiency, unavailable authority, or unobservable Outcome may be a
responsible reason to terminate before empirical work. Non-execution of Change
may be a valid operational outcome of human authority. Weakening or lack of
support may be valuable empirical findings. An indeterminate result may expose
the limits of the case, Evidence, design, attribution, or product question.

Integrity fails when a condition is hidden, reclassified as success, repaired
with modeled data, blamed on a participant without Evidence, or generalized
beyond the bounded claim. Falsification remains hypothesis-specific and
Evidence-bounded; failure to support one hypothesis does not automatically
falsify another or Management OS as a whole.

# 11. Product and architecture feedback boundary

Empirical Learning may become an input to separate consideration of:

- product understanding;
- Product Value Hypothesis refinement;
- future capability questions;
- architecture-gap consideration; or
- implementation-support questions.

The feedback relationship is non-automatic:

```text
Empirical Learning
  → possible accountable feedback consideration
  → possible rejection, preservation, refinement, or separate inquiry
```

The arrows do not amend or authorize anything. Empirical Learning does not
automatically:

- amend Product Vision;
- amend canonical M8;
- create a new object or capability;
- authorize implementation;
- reprioritize backlog; or
- prove product value.

A local usability problem is not automatically an architecture Gap. A missing
implementation support is not automatically a new product capability. A
bounded finding must retain its context, Evidence, attribution, and authority
limits when presented to product or architecture owners. Feedback requires
separate accountable consideration and any resulting change requires its own
authority.

# 12. Iteration and stopping

After one inquiry, accountable humans may consider several bounded
dispositions:

- stop because the named uncertainty was sufficiently reduced for its bounded
  purpose;
- stop because the inquiry is infeasible, unsafe, disproportionate, or lacks
  authority;
- preserve an indeterminate result without forcing closure;
- refine the hypothesis through separate consideration;
- consider another case without implying its selection;
- repeat a bounded inquiry with improved Evidence and fresh authorization;
- consider architecture or product feedback; or
- decline further work.

No disposition follows automatically from another. “Sufficiently reduced” does
not mean eliminated, validated generally, or proven positive. Another case is
not automatically required after an indeterminate result. Repetition does not
create causal confidence merely through volume, and improved Evidence does not
carry forward expired consent, access, case, Change, or validation authority.

This synthesis defines no loop, cadence, transition rule, completion rule, or
repository mutation. Every iteration or stopping Decision remains bounded,
human, attributable, and separately authorized.

acceptance criteria, mandatory checklist, or transition conditions:

- Was the question explicit?
- Was the claim bounded?
- Was the hypothesis attributable?
- Was the case suitable?
- Was Evidence sufficient for the exact claim?
- Were modeled and observed materials separated?
- Was authority preserved?
- Was actual Change distinguished from intended Change?
- Was Outcome observed rather than inferred from activity?
- Were attribution limits visible?
- Did Learning preserve history and uncertainty?
- Is another iteration likely to reduce uncertainty materially?

Different inquiry shapes may need different reflective questions. An
unfavorable or unknown answer may prompt human reconsideration or stopping, but
this list itself neither blocks nor authorizes a transition.
