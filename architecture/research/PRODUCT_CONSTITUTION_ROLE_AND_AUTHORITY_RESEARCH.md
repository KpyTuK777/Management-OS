## 1. Role Question

### 1.1 Potential problem

Management OS product understanding is distributed across Product Vision, Systems, Owner Journey, source-owned canonical architectures, canonical M8, and a growing research corpus. That distribution protects semantic ownership, but it also makes ordinary product-direction questions expensive to orient: a reader may need to reconstruct the relationship among operational context, Evidence, understanding, human Decision, source-owned Change, observed Outcome, and Learning before evaluating a proposed direction.

The Product Constitution candidate potentially addresses this orientation problem. It provides a short, implementation-independent product compass; condenses product beliefs and invariant boundaries; and gives readers product-philosophy questions without requiring the entire canonical corpus to be reread for every preliminary discussion.

Potential usefulness does not establish authority. A clear synthesis may help a reader think while remaining unable to decide conformance, resolve conflict, authorize work, or speak for a canonical source.

### 1.2 Parallel-authority risk

The same compression that may make the candidate useful creates a “super-source” risk. A short document is easier to quote than its dependencies. Repeated use could make it the practical Product Vision, architecture summary, or feature filter even while its header says non-canonical. De facto reliance can emerge from habit, AI retrieval priority, linking, or omission of source citations; repository status labels alone cannot prevent it.

The role question is therefore not only whether the candidate is useful. It is whether a possible use can remain visibly subordinate to the sources that own the relevant meaning.

## 2. Relationship to Product Vision

### 2.1 Product Vision ownership

`docs/PRODUCT_VISION.md` defines product purpose, canonical mission, core principles, long-term direction, operational reasoning posture, human approval boundaries, and the intended hierarchy from understanding through verified operational effect to reusable Learning. `docs/DOCUMENTATION_MAP.md` identifies Product Vision as canonical for product purpose and principles. Product Vision therefore retains product-purpose authority.

The candidate cannot interpret its own consistency as authority over Product Vision. If wording overlaps, Vision remains the source. If wording conflicts, Vision prevails until an independently authorized change to Vision occurs.

### 2.2 What the candidate does

The candidate repeats and synthesizes Vision themes: the operational manager as owner, progressively stronger understanding, explicit human Decision, verified action, Outcome, reusable Learning, AI assistance without control, Evidence/interpretation separation, and improvement of future operational capability.

It also gives compact owner-derived language not expressed in exactly the same form in Product Vision, especially:

- “the operational manager's ecosystem” as a two-sentence identity;
- an appropriate governed place for materially relevant contributions, including governed external references;
- Decision as explicitly non-terminal through Change, Outcome, and Learning;
- a consolidated list of protections, prohibitions, limits, and product-evaluation questions; and
- explicit rejection of mandatory single Root Cause analysis in this product-philosophy context.

These differences are currently permissible as non-canonical synthesis because the candidate labels their status and does not contradict the canonical purpose. Their presence may also be evidence for a future question about whether Product Vision expresses the desired product understanding completely. It is not evidence that Vision is already amended or that the candidate may fill an authoritative gap.

### 2.3 Preserved equations

```text
Product Constitution ≠ Product Vision
Synthesis ≠ amendment
Clarity ≠ authority
```

A future reader may use synthesis to locate a question. Only the authority applicable to Product Vision can decide whether that question changes canonical product purpose or principles.

## 3. Relationship to Systems and Canonical M8

### 3.1 Systems ownership

`docs/SYSTEMS.md` and the Documentation Map preserve product-system responsibilities, current-state descriptions, and boundaries. The candidate names no system, transfers no responsibility, and creates no write ownership. Its ecosystem language expressly permits source-owned records and rejects absorbed authority.

The candidate can explain integrated product meaning only at product-philosophy resolution. It cannot answer which system owns a record, which capability may act, which source prevails, or which boundary applies in a concrete architecture question.

### 3.2 M8 ownership

Canonical M8 retains authority for the reviewed architectural baseline, capability and ownership coherence, information/Evidence/handoff architecture, Gap and Risk assessment, evolution alternatives, validation and readiness analysis, and integrated architectural review. The candidate neither re-disposes M8 findings nor selects its alternatives.

The operational management loop in the candidate is explicitly a product-understanding model, not a lifecycle, protocol, workflow, governance process, state machine, or authorization path. Research-supported composability therefore does not become canonical architecture merely because a short product document repeats it.

### 3.3 Use boundary

```text
Product philosophy ≠ architecture
Product question ≠ architecture gate
Constitution language ≠ source authority
```

A product-philosophy statement may prompt examination of a design. It cannot establish architectural conformance, override a system owner, create a capability expectation, or substitute for the source-owned detail needed to resolve the design.

## 5. Possible Role Models

The following models are symmetric alternatives. Their order is descriptive, not a ranking.

### A. Reviewed research compass

The candidate remains a reviewed research artifact that readers may consult for orientation. It receives no separately recognized product role.

### B. Non-canonical product-philosophy reference

The candidate could be separately recognized as an auxiliary reference for product thinking while remaining unable to decide authority, governance, conformance, approval, or architecture.

### C. Input to a future bounded Product Vision evaluation

The candidate could remain evidence input rather than a directly used reference. A separately authorized future evaluation could consider whether any synthesized language exposes a Product Vision question.

### D. No further role

The candidate could remain a historical learning artifact documenting product understanding at the reviewed time, with no continuing operational use.

No model is selected, preferred, recommended, or authorized by this research.

## 6. Authority Model by Option

### 6.1 Model A — Reviewed research compass

**Authority effect:** None. Review status increases confidence in boundary fidelity, not authority.

**Permissible use:** Read for orientation, vocabulary, product-philosophy discussion, or discovery of canonical questions.

**Prohibited use:** Treat as a conformance standard, feature gate, approval criterion, canonical interpretation, architecture source, or work authorization.

**Citation boundary:** Cite as reviewed research and preserve candidate status; cite Product Vision, Systems, M8, or another source owner for authoritative claims.

**Canonical relationship:** Subordinate and non-corrective. Conflict is resolved by the applicable canonical source, not by synthesis.

**Correction and supersession implication:** Ordinary research attribution can identify later correction or a newer research artifact, but such history does not amend canonical sources or define a lifecycle.

**Parallel-authority risk:** Repeated convenient citation could make reviewed research function like a product source despite having no formal role.

### 6.2 Model B — Non-canonical product-philosophy reference

**Authority effect:** No inherent authority is conceptually required, but separate recognition could easily be misread as status elevation. The intended non-effects would need to remain explicit.

**Permissible use:** Potentially frame preliminary product questions, help explain distributed product meaning, and support discussion before authoritative source analysis.

**Prohibited use:** Decide a product direction, block or approve work, establish acceptance criteria, redefine purpose, allocate system responsibility, judge architecture, or authorize governance or transition.

**Citation boundary:** Every consequential use would still need the owning canonical source. “The Constitution says” could not conclude an authoritative question.

**Canonical relationship:** Auxiliary only. Product Vision, Systems, source-owned architecture, M8, and M7 prevail within their domains.

**Correction and supersession implication:** A recognized reference creates a stronger stale-content risk. Its correction or withdrawal would require attributable handling, but this research does not define who acts or how.

**Parallel-authority risk:** Acknowledged operational usefulness may become de facto governance even without formal authority.

**Operational-use question:** Operational use is conceptually possible if “use” means non-binding orientation or inquiry support. If it consistently determines action, rejection, priority, or approval, it has crossed from reference use into an undeclared governance rule or authority source.

### 6.3 Model C — Input to a future bounded Product Vision evaluation

**Authority effect:** None. Evidence input cannot amend Vision or authorize evaluation by itself.

**Permissible use:** Preserve synthesized propositions and review evidence for a separately authorized human consideration of Product Vision completeness or clarity.

**Prohibited use:** Pre-judge the evaluation, assume that difference is defect, import research wording automatically, or treat evaluation readiness as adoption.

**Citation boundary:** Cite as evidence about a possible Vision question, not as the answer or replacement language.

**Canonical relationship:** Product Vision remains controlling before, during, and after any consideration unless a legitimate separate authority changes it.

**Correction and supersession implication:** The fixed candidate remains historical input. Later evidence may supplement or contradict it without rewriting the reviewed identity.

**Parallel-authority risk:** The candidate could become a presumed amendment package if its evidentiary role is forgotten.

### 6.4 Model D — No further role

**Authority effect:** None.

**Permissible use:** Historical inspection, provenance reconstruction, and learning about the research episode.

**Prohibited use:** Current product-direction reference, conformance source, gate, or substitute for current canonical documents.

**Citation boundary:** Cite only as historical research with its reviewed commit/blob and temporal context.

**Canonical relationship:** None beyond documenting a past synthesis of then-current sources.

**Correction and supersession implication:** Historical integrity favors preserving the fixed artifact; later research may correct the record without making this artifact current.

**Parallel-authority risk:** Stale quotations may still create apparent current authority if historical status is omitted.

## 7. Evolution Question

If product vision changes, the candidate may become partially stale even when every sentence was faithful at review time. Conceptually possible responses include leaving the fixed candidate as historical evidence, preparing later attributed research, reconsidering a recognized reference, or asking whether canonical Vision itself should be evaluated. These possibilities are not a process, cadence, or lifecycle.

The right to initiate examination cannot be inferred from authorship, repository access, usefulness, review success, or AI capability. A person may surface a discrepancy as information; that does not mean the person can commission review, amend the candidate, change Vision, or establish a new status. Applicable authority would have to come from existing canonical governance and the authority owning the affected domain.

The central evolution risk is asymmetric ease: a concise non-canonical document may be easier to edit and distribute than canonical sources, yet more influential in practice. If it changes quickly while product work follows it, it can govern without declared authority. Conversely, freezing it can make it misleading as the product evolves.

Ordinary research attribution is sufficient to preserve who said what, when, and against which sources. It is not by itself sufficient to grant an evolving reference operational legitimacy, resolve conflicts, or withdraw reliance. Whether more is required remains an open authority question; this paper creates no maintenance role, cadence, transition, or review protocol.

## 9. Questions Before Any Future Adoption Decision

Readiness for human consideration is not adoption. Before any future decision, an accountable authority would need enough evidence to answer at least:

1. **Intended role:** What exact problem would the role solve, and what uses remain outside it?
2. **Accountable authority:** Who is legitimately able to decide that role, and under which existing governance function?
3. **Authority basis:** What canonical authority permits recognition without inventing a new document class or governance power?
4. **Precise non-effects:** What decisions, gates, conformance judgments, source responsibilities, and transitions can the reference never create?
5. **Indexing and classification:** How would readers and AI discover its status without mistaking discoverability for precedence?
6. **Conflict resolution:** How would a user identify and defer to Product Vision, Systems, M8, M7, or source-owned architecture when wording diverges?
7. **Correction and withdrawal:** How could misleading or stale use be corrected or withdrawn while preserving historical identity?
8. **Attributable human rationale:** Why is the chosen role justified, what evidence supports it, what alternatives were considered, and what uncertainty remains?
9. **Use evidence:** Is there evidence that the role improves product thinking without creating parallel authority?
10. **AI behavior:** Can AI preserve classification, provenance, and precedence under realistic retrieval and summarization conditions?

This list is a research question set, not a gate, protocol, checklist mandate, or authorization to begin an adoption matter.

## 10. Research Conclusion

Four roles are conceptually possible:

- a reviewed research compass with orientation value but no formal product role;
- a non-canonical product-philosophy reference with bounded, non-decisional use and substantial de facto-authority risk;
- evidence input to a future separately authorized Product Vision evaluation; or
- a historical learning artifact with no further operational role.

Each preserves formal non-authority in concept, but their practical risks differ in kind. Models A and B risk convenient synthesis displacing source authority; Model B additionally risks operational use becoming an undeclared gate. Model C risks evidence being mistaken for a presumed amendment. Model D risks useful learning becoming inaccessible and old language being quoted without temporal context. These risk descriptions do not rank the models.

Open questions remain about intended use, legitimate authority, classification, source precedence in practice, correction and withdrawal, AI retrieval behavior, empirical usefulness, and whether non-binding operational use can remain non-governing.

The candidate, its successful bounded critical review, and the canonical comparison provide sufficient evidence for a future accountable human to consider the role question if separately authorized. They do not provide sufficient evidence to choose a role, recommend adoption, establish product value, or authorize a transition.
