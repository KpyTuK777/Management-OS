# Authority Gap as Operational Reality — Architectural Research

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No Authority Gap object, semantic kind, governance model, lifecycle, workflow, approval, milestone, or work created
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper investigates whether an apparent authority gap can be understood as a
possible condition of operational reality using existing Management OS
capabilities and semantic kinds.

The question arises from the authority-consideration attempt described in
`AUTHORITY_BOUNDARY_AND_DECISION_READINESS_LEARNING_ANALYSIS.md`. The analytical
materials were ready, but accountable authority identity, disposition,
human-owned rationale, date, and attestation were unavailable. The system
correctly preserved:

```text
Disposition: NONE
Authority effect: NONE
```

This paper studies what that condition may mean. It does not diagnose a real
organization, classify a governance failure, create an Authority Gap object,
assign ownership, or authorize correction.

## 2. Research Boundary

Canonical M7 remains the external governance authority. Canonical M8.1–M8.7
remain the architectural constraint baseline. M8 remains Completed / Closed.

This research preserves:

- Operational Reality as independent of its representation in Management OS;
- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, Recommendation, Decision, Outcome, and
  Learning as distinct semantic kinds;
- Decision as accountable human judgment;
- source-owned facts, records, and mutation;
- Outcome as observed and owned verification of consequence rather than causal
  proof;
- AI and Management OS as non-authoritative assistance;
- no mandatory single Root Cause; and
- no promotion of a useful research label into canonical identity or authority.

The term **authority gap** is lower-case research shorthand unless quoted in a
title. It names a question about a possible condition, not a new semantic kind,
record, capability, state, defect class, lifecycle position, or governance
concept.

## 3. Research Question

The primary question is:

> When an operational matter is analytically ready for a Decision but the
> attributable human authority needed to make or own that Decision is absent,
> unclear, insufficient, unavailable, or unable to act, can the condition be
> understood through existing Operational Matter, Investigation, Evidence,
> Interpretation, Decision, Outcome, and Learning responsibilities?

Related questions are:

- When is the condition a meaningful operational signal?
- When is it merely a normal missing input?
- What Evidence would support either interpretation?
- What must not be inferred from the absence of Decision?
- Does existing architecture compose sufficiently, or is there evidence of a
  distinct architectural responsibility gap?

## 4. Authority Gap Phenomenon

### 4.1 Working research description

An authority gap may be suspected when a bounded operational Decision appears
necessary or expected, but the conditions for an attributable and effective
human Decision are not present.

Possible manifestations include:

- no accountable person has been identified;
- a person is named but their authority basis is absent or narrower than the
  requested effect;
- several people appear to own the Decision and none has final accountability;
- the accountable person is unavailable within the material operational horizon;
- relevant source or participation authority is missing;
- the Decision request combines effects owned by different authorities;
- nobody owns the rationale or acceptance of uncertainty and consequences;
- an advisory body or system is being asked to substitute for accountable human
  authority;
- the authority exists but required Evidence is unavailable; or
- the authority intentionally withholds judgment pending conditions not yet met.

These manifestations are not equivalent. Some concern authority identity or
scope; some concern evidence, availability, timing, request design, or an
intentional human posture. Grouping them under one label may aid research but can
also hide materially different conditions.

### 4.2 Absence of Decision versus wrong Decision

**Absence of Decision** means no attributable human judgment has selected or
owned a disposition for the bounded question. Its observable signs may include
empty authority fields, no attestation, no disposition, or no authority effect.

**Wrong Decision** is an evaluation of a Decision that actually occurred. It
requires evidence of:

- an attributable human judgment;
- the question and intent decided;
- the Evidence and uncertainty available at the time;
- applicable authority and constraints;
- the actual Change or no-action consequence; and
- a criterion against which the Decision is considered wrong.

The two conditions differ fundamentally:

| Condition | What exists | What may be investigated | What cannot be inferred automatically |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No Decision | No attributable human disposition for the bounded question | Why the Decision is absent; whether a Decision was required; ownership, authority, Evidence, timing, and request conditions | That a bad Decision occurred, Governance failed, delay is harmful, or action should proceed anyway |
| Decision made and later questioned | Attributable human judgment exists | Decision basis, authority, reasoning, Change, Outcome, uncertainty, and later Evidence | That an unfavorable Outcome proves the Decision was irrational or unauthorized |

No Decision cannot be evaluated as correct or incorrect in the same sense as an
actual Decision. It can be evaluated only relative to whether a Decision was
materially required and whether its absence had observable consequence.

### 4.3 Non-decision versus explicit no-action Decision

An explicit **no-action Decision** is still a Decision. A human authority has
considered the question and chosen not to act, possibly with rationale,
conditions, and a future observation posture.

An **unmade Decision** contains no attributable judgment. Silence, an empty
field, elapsed time, or lack of Change must not be promoted into a no-action
Decision.

This distinction matters because an explicit no-action Decision can be traced to
intent and later Outcome. An unmade Decision has no owned intent to verify.

### 4.4 Authority gap versus input gap

An absent Decision may result from a normal input gap rather than an authority
gap.

Examples of input gaps include:

- the Decision question is not yet fixed;
- relevant Evidence is missing or contradictory;
- the intended effect is outside current scope;
- the accountable person has not yet been asked;
- the decision date has not arrived;
- required source confirmation is legitimately pending; or
- the matter is not sufficiently material to require a Decision.

An authority gap becomes a stronger interpretation when evidence shows that:

- a materially time-bound Decision is required;
- the question and necessary Evidence are sufficiently ready;
- the missing condition concerns who may decide, whether their authority covers
  the effect, or whether accountable ownership can be exercised;
- the absence persists beyond the justified Decision horizon; and
- material operational consequence follows or is credibly exposed.

Even then, “authority gap” remains an Interpretation supported by Evidence, not
a Fact automatically emitted from missing fields.

## 5. When the Condition Is an Operational Signal

An absent or blocked Decision may constitute an operational signal when there is
attributable evidence of a difference between the current authority condition
and the authority condition needed for a material operational purpose.

### 5.1 Possible signal conditions

- a material operational condition requires a Decision within a bounded horizon;
- Evidence and analysis are sufficiently ready for human consideration;
- no accountable owner can be identified;
- named ownership conflicts with actual authority scope;
- the Decision repeatedly moves among people or bodies without accountable
  resolution;
- action proceeds without an attributable Decision because authority is unclear;
- source owners reject or cannot recognize the presumed Decision authority;
- the absence causes observable delay, repeated Investigation, duplicated work,
  uncontrolled exposure, or loss of intent;
- similar matters repeatedly stop at the same authority boundary; or
- the organization relies on recommendations, AI, consensus, or procedure to
  simulate a Decision owner.

### 5.2 Evidence needed to treat it as a signal

Potentially relevant Evidence includes:

- the bounded Decision question and material operational purpose;
- evidence that the question is ready enough for consideration;
- expected or required Decision horizon and its basis;
- existing authority sources and their recorded scope;
- communications or records showing ownership ambiguity, conflict, refusal, or
  unavailable authority;
- source-owner responses to the requested effect;
- repeated transfers, delays, or abandoned consideration;
- operational consequences attributable to the absence or ambiguity;
- explicit statements by relevant humans; and
- contrary evidence showing that no Decision was yet required or that authority
  was available.

No single empty field or delayed response is sufficient by itself.

### 5.3 Signal does not equal diagnosis or defect

An operational signal indicates a condition worth bounded attention. It does not
establish:

- that Governance is defective;
- that a particular person failed;
- that a new authority role is needed;
- that a Decision should be made immediately;
- that the requested Decision is valid;
- that the system should escalate automatically;
- that work is blocked globally; or
- that an Authority Gap object should be created.

Signal interpretation remains evidence-relative and human-reviewable.

## 6. When It Is Merely an Absent Input

The condition should remain a missing input rather than an operational authority
interpretation when:

- no material Decision need has been established;
- the subject or scope is still ambiguous;
- the Evidence is insufficient for consideration;
- the expected authority has not yet been asked or given reasonable opportunity;
- timing remains within normal or justified bounds;
- the matter is awaiting a legitimate upstream source-owned condition;
- no operational consequence or repeated pattern is visible;
- the Decision request exceeds or misstates existing authority; or
- the absence is a normal field state in a document that is not yet under human
  consideration.

The correct representation may simply be:

```text
Required input unavailable
Decision not made
Authority effect none
```

No broader interpretation is required unless Evidence supports it.

## 7. Relationship to Existing Semantic Kinds

### 7.1 Observation

Observation may record that:

- a Decision field is empty;
- no accountable person is named;
- a request was sent and no response exists;
- several owners are named inconsistently;
- an authority source does not cover the requested effect;
- time has elapsed; or
- no authority effect was recorded.

Observation does not establish why the condition exists or whether it matters.

### 7.2 Evidence

An Observation becomes Evidence only relative to a declared question, such as:

> Is the preparation Decision absent because accountable authority is
> unavailable after the materials became ready?

Evidence should preserve source, time, scope, authority, relevance, uncertainty,
and contrary material. An empty field is Evidence of an empty field, not proof
of governance failure or organizational incapacity.

### 7.3 Interpretation

“An authority gap exists” is an Interpretation that relates Evidence about:

- Decision need;
- readiness;
- authority identity and scope;
- timing;
- requests and responses;
- source ownership; and
- operational consequence.

Alternative interpretations may include missing Evidence, premature request,
intentional deferral, invalid scope, unavailable participant, or no material need
for Decision.

Interpretation must remain challengeable and cannot become Fact through
repetition, confidence, presentation, or AI generation.

### 7.4 Recommendation

A Recommendation may advise a human to:

- clarify the Decision question;
- identify the applicable authority source;
- request an accountable owner;
- separate combined decisions;
- obtain missing Evidence;
- defer explicitly;
- record no-action; or
- stop unauthorized activity.

The Recommendation cannot assign authority, make the Decision, authorize action,
or prove that an authority gap exists.

### 7.5 Decision

Several different Decisions may later arise:

- whether the authority-gap Interpretation is supported;
- who has existing authority for the original question;
- whether the original Decision should be made, deferred, reframed, or declined;
- whether authority clarification requires a separately governed act; or
- whether no action is appropriate.

Each remains accountable human judgment under existing authority. Management OS
does not decide who ought to have authority.

### 7.6 Outcome

Outcome depends on the bounded Decision and intended condition. Possible later
observations may include:

- an accountable authority was identified and made a Decision;
- the Decision request was correctly reframed or separated;
- the matter was explicitly deferred or declined;
- unauthorized action was prevented;
- delay or duplicated work decreased;
- authority ambiguity persisted; or
- no material operational consequence changed.

The existence of a Decision is not itself proof of a good Outcome. Likewise, the
absence of Decision can be an observed result of one consideration attempt but
must not be called an operational success without an intended condition and
owned verification.

Outcome does not prove that the authority-gap Interpretation or any prior
Recommendation was uniquely correct.

### 7.7 Learning

Learning may arise when attributable experience shows:

- which authority questions recur;
- which signals distinguish missing Evidence from missing authority;
- where Decision requests routinely exceed authority scope;
- which forms of decision support reduce ambiguity;
- when explicit stopping preserves integrity;
- which interpretations were later corrected; or
- how authority clarification affected later operational consequence.

Learning remains contextual, correctable, and non-authoritative. A repeated
pattern does not create a new role, rule, escalation path, or canonical object.

## 8. Boundary Conditions

### 8.1 Authority gap is not automatically Governance failure

Governance failure would require evidence that applicable governance authority,
structure, or operation failed a relevant criterion. An absent Decision may
instead reflect:

- a valid boundary;
- an invalid or premature request;
- missing Evidence;
- ordinary unavailability;
- intentional deferral;
- scope outside existing authority;
- a local ownership ambiguity; or
- a situation not requiring Decision.

The research label cannot classify Governance.

### 8.2 No Decision is not a bad Decision

No Decision lacks attributable judgment and intent. It may have harmful,
protective, neutral, or unknown consequence, but it is not a Decision to evaluate
as good or bad.

An explicit no-action Decision is different and must remain attributable.

### 8.3 Missing owner is not automatically system error

A missing owner may indicate:

- the matter is not yet bounded;
- the requested effect spans several sources;
- the relevant authority is external;
- the organization has not assigned accountability;
- the field is premature; or
- the current product representation does not know the source identity.

Only the last possibility concerns system representation directly, and even it
requires Evidence before classification as an error.

### 8.4 Delay is not automatically authority gap

Some Decisions require time for Evidence, consultation, external permission, or
reflection. Delay becomes material only relative to an intended or required
horizon and observable consequence.

### 8.5 Authority scope is not organizational hierarchy alone

Formal seniority does not automatically establish Decision authority for a
source-owned fact, mutation, professional judgment, or governed transition. The
applicable authority basis and effect must be explicit.

### 8.6 Visibility is not permission to intervene

Detecting possible authority ambiguity does not authorize Management OS, AI, an
analyst, or another participant to assign an owner, escalate, decide, or mutate
governance.

## 9. Relationship to the Operational Loop

An authority-related condition can affect several positions without becoming a
new loop object.

```text
Operational Reality
  → signal of missing, conflicting, or insufficient authority
Matter / Context Continuity
  → preserves the bounded question, owners, authority sources, timing, and impact
Investigation
  → tests whether the condition is authority, Evidence, scope, timing, or request related
Evidence
  → preserves attributable support, contradiction, and authority-source limits
Understanding
  → compares interpretations without promoting one to Fact
Recommendation
  → may advise clarification or stopping, but cannot assign authority
Human Decision
  → only an applicable human may decide the original or clarification question
Source-Owned Change
  → any change remains with the applicable source or governance authority
Outcome
  → observes whether the intended operational condition changed
Learning
  → preserves bounded experience without creating authority
```

This trace is explanatory and not a workflow, lifecycle, or escalation protocol.

### 9.1 Before Decision

Authority ambiguity may prevent the loop from progressing from Understanding or
Recommendation into accountable Decision. The appropriate system behavior may
be to expose the missing condition and stop rather than simulate progress.

### 9.2 Between Decision and Change

A human may make a Decision but lack authority over the source-owned Change. The
gap then concerns execution authority or source ownership, not absence of
Decision. The Decision remains real but may have no authorized effect.

### 9.3 During Outcome verification

If authority or Decision intent is unclear, later activity cannot be compared
reliably with an owned intended condition. Completion may occur, but Outcome
verification becomes ambiguous.

### 9.4 During Learning

Learning should preserve whether the prior limitation concerned missing
authority, missing Evidence, poor request design, unavailable owner, or another
condition. Collapsing all cases into “authority gap” would reduce future value.

## 10. Management Implications

### 10.1 Authority readiness may be part of operational understanding

Managers may need visibility into whether a matter has:

- a clear Decision question;
- an accountable human owner;
- authority sufficient for the requested effect;
- source-owner participation;
- Evidence sufficient for consideration;
- explicit rationale and uncertainty ownership; and
- a justified Decision horizon.

This visibility supports judgment. It does not create a mandatory checklist or
new state model.

### 10.2 Stopping can preserve operational integrity

When authority is absent, stopping may prevent:

- unauthorized Change;
- Recommendation-to-Decision promotion;
- fabricated human intent;
- ambiguous accountability;
- invalid repository transition;
- source-authority violation; and
- false Outcome claims.

Stopping is not universally correct. Its value depends on the bounded condition
and consequence.

### 10.3 Authority ambiguity can create operational cost

Possible consequences include:

- Decision delay;
- repeated or duplicated Investigation;
- lost context during owner search;
- repeated Recommendation without accountable judgment;
- work proceeding without authority;
- rejected source mutation;
- inability to verify Outcome against owned intent;
- participant frustration or disengagement; and
- escalation pressure or blame.

These consequences require Evidence. They must not be assumed from the label.

### 10.4 Authority clarity can also constrain invalid work

A visible boundary may reveal that the requested Decision should not be made in
the proposed scope. Clarification can lead to legitimate deferral, decline,
separation of questions, or return for Evidence rather than faster approval.

### 10.5 Management response remains contextual

Possible human responses may include clarification, reframing, evidence request,
consultation, explicit deferral, no-action, decline, or separately governed
authority change. This paper neither recommends nor authorizes any response.

## 11. Evidence for Bounded Investigation

If a real authority-related condition were investigated later, relevant Evidence
might include:

### Decision subject

- exact question and requested effect;
- material operational purpose;
- current and intended condition;
- time horizon; and
- Evidence that a human Decision is actually needed.

### Authority sources

- canonical governance or organizational authority sources;
- source-ownership records;
- delegated authority and limits;
- exception or transition authority; and
- conflicts or ambiguity among applicable sources.

### Human context

- named accountable people and their own understanding of authority;
- requests, responses, refusals, deferrals, and availability;
- accepted or rejected responsibility;
- conflicts or constraints; and
- evidence of rationale ownership.

### Operational consequence

- elapsed time relative to a justified horizon;
- repeated handoff or reassignment;
- duplicated analysis or work;
- unauthorized or rejected Change;
- impact on the operational condition; and
- adverse, null, delayed, or protective effects.

### Contrary evidence

- Evidence was not ready;
- no Decision was required;
- authority was available but not consulted;
- the request exceeded valid scope;
- intentional deferral was appropriate;
- delay was within normal conditions; or
- the apparent gap resulted from incomplete representation rather than
  operational authority.

No new data model or collection mechanism is implied.

## 12. Learning Opportunities

Research may later examine:

- recurring patterns in where analytical readiness meets unavailable authority;
- which conditions predict valid stopping versus harmful delay;
- how often Recommendations are mistaken for authorization;
- where selection and approval are collapsed;
- whether source authority is visible at the Decision-to-Change boundary;
- how unmade, deferred, declined, no-action, and unauthorized conditions differ;
- which evidence helps accountable humans clarify authority without changing
  Governance; and
- how later Outcome changes or fails to change after authority clarification.

These are research opportunities, not backlog items or authorized studies.

## 13. Product Capability Analysis

### 13.1 Existing capabilities that appear relevant

The current architecture already provides responsibilities that can compose
around a possible authority-related signal:

- **Operational Matter and continuity** can preserve the bounded situation,
  context, participants, timing, and unresolved Decision need.
- **Investigation** can ask whether the limitation concerns Evidence, authority,
  scope, timing, ownership, or request design.
- **Evidence architecture** can preserve authority-source provenance,
  contradiction, uncertainty, and limitations.
- **Operational reasoning** can compare alternative explanations without
  promoting them to Fact.
- **Recommendation** can remain advisory.
- **Decision architecture** preserves explicit accountable human judgment and
  distinguishes no-action from no Decision.
- **source-owned handoffs and Change** preserve mutation authority.
- **Outcome verification** can test consequences relative to an owned Decision
  intent when one exists.
- **Learning, Memory, and Knowledge responsibilities** can preserve bounded,
  correctable experience without creating authority.
- **Governance remains external** and retains authority for any actual change to
  decision rights or rules.

### 13.2 Current compositional finding

The existing architecture appears conceptually sufficient to represent:

- the observed absence of Decision or authority input;
- Evidence about authority sources and operational consequence;
- competing Interpretations;
- a Recommendation for human consideration;
- explicit stopping without simulated authority;
- a later human Decision if one occurs; and
- Outcome and Learning after attributable action.

This support is compositional. No evidence in the studied event requires a new
canonical Authority Gap object, semantic kind, capability, lifecycle, or
governance layer.

### 13.3 What is not established

The research does not establish that current product realization can:

- reliably detect authority-related conditions;
- retrieve or interpret applicable authority sources;
- distinguish missing Evidence from missing authority in real contexts;
- preserve all relevant ownership and timing evidence;
- support human clarification without implying escalation;
- prevent user or AI overinterpretation; or
- demonstrate value through observed operational Outcomes.

Those are evidence and realization questions, not proof of a new architectural
object.

### 13.4 Research conclusion on product need

At present, the strongest supportable conclusion is:

> Existing canonical responsibilities appear sufficient for conceptual
> composition around a possible authority-related operational signal. Whether
> this composition is adequate in real use remains a research question requiring
> evidence. No distinct Authority Gap object or capability is justified by the
> current material.

## 14. Conditions That Could Motivate Further Architectural Research

Further architecture research might become justified if attributable evidence
shows that existing composition cannot preserve materially required meaning,
for example:

- authority-related conditions require stable identity independent of Matter;
- they must be referenced, corrected, or relied upon across matters in a way
  existing Evidence and Learning cannot support;
- a distinct semantic handoff is necessary and cannot be expressed as Evidence,
  Interpretation, Recommendation, or Decision limitation;
- absence, conflict, insufficiency, and unavailability of authority require
  materially different ownership behavior not represented today;
- Outcome verification repeatedly fails because intended authority condition
  cannot be traced; or
- source and human authority are systematically lost in existing composition.

Even such evidence would justify research, not automatic creation of an object
or governance change.

## 15. Research Findings

### Finding 1 — Authority-related absence can be operationally meaningful

When a material Decision is ready enough for consideration but accountable
authority cannot be identified or exercised, the condition may affect delay,
action, accountability, source ownership, and Outcome verification.

### Finding 2 — Absence is not yet an authority-gap Fact

The label remains an Interpretation requiring Evidence about Decision need,
readiness, authority, timing, and consequence. A missing input may be normal.

### Finding 3 — No Decision and bad Decision are non-equivalent

No Decision contains no accountable judgment to evaluate. A bad-Decision claim
requires an actual Decision, criterion, Evidence, and Outcome context.

### Finding 4 — Governance failure requires separate evidence

Authority ambiguity, missing owner, or delayed Decision does not by itself show
that canonical Governance failed.

### Finding 5 — Existing semantic kinds can preserve the inquiry

Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, Recommendation, Decision, Outcome, and
Learning provide distinct roles without a new semantic kind.

### Finding 6 — Existing capabilities appear conceptually composable

Matter, Investigation, Evidence, Reasoning, Recommendation, human Decision,
source-owned Change, Outcome, Learning, and external Governance can represent the
condition and its consequences at the architectural level.

### Finding 7 — Product adequacy remains unproven

Conceptual composition does not demonstrate that current realization detects,
explains, or supports real authority-related situations effectively.

### Finding 8 — No new canonical object is justified

The studied evidence does not show identity, ownership, persistence, handoff, or
failure-of-composition requirements sufficient to create an Authority Gap object.

## 16. Possible Future Research Directions

Without creating work or authority, future research could consider:

- comparative cases of missing Evidence versus missing authority;
- explicit no-action Decision versus unmade Decision;
- authority conflict versus authority unavailability;
- Decision authority versus source-mutation authority;
- operational consequences of unresolved ownership;
- how users interpret system warnings or missing-authority observations;
- whether AI language creates false authority expectations;
- how Outcome can verify consequence after authority clarification; and
- whether repeated cases reveal failure of current composition.

No direction is selected or authorized here.

## 17. Research Conclusion

An authority gap can be a useful research interpretation of operational reality
when a material Decision is needed, analytical conditions are sufficiently
ready, accountable authority is absent or unusable, and operational consequence
is visible. The same surface condition may also be only a missing input,
premature request, valid delay, intentional deferral, or scope problem.

Existing semantic kinds and capabilities appear sufficient to compose the
investigation without a new object:

```text
Observation
  → Evidence for a bounded authority question
  → competing Interpretation
  → optional Recommendation
  → accountable human Decision, if authority exists
  → source-owned Change, if authorized
  → observed Outcome
  → bounded Learning
```

The composition preserves the central boundary: the system may expose and
explain a possible authority-related condition, but it cannot assign authority
or decide in place of the human.
