## 2. Repository Model Before M9

M8.1 defines six mutually related **product responsibility views**, explicitly not
a stack, tier, workflow, containment model, or execution order:

1. P1 Owner Experience and Contribution;
2. P2 Operational Continuity and Context;
3. P3 Investigation, Evidence, and Reasoning;
4. P4 Decision, Change, Execution Handoff, and Outcome;
5. P5 Learning, Knowledge, and Operational Memory; and
6. P6 Assistance, Recommendation, and Adaptation.

P1 already owns the coherent means by which the owner perceives, contributes,
inspects reasoning, exercises judgment, approves, rejects, and maintains
continuity. M8.1 also maps situation presentation and guided interaction to
canonical experience, interaction, and perceptual architecture sources.

The Documentation Map already classifies `GUIDED_INVESTIGATION_INTERACTION.md`
and `NATURAL_CAPTURE_INTERACTION_FOUNDATION.md` as **Interaction architecture**.
Other sources are classified as product-experience or experience architecture.
Therefore Interaction Architecture is not a newly discovered repository concern.
It is already an architectural artifact category whose product responsibility is
primarily governed through P1 and whose semantics depend on P2–P6 and the
cross-cutting trust plane.

## 3. What Belongs to Product Realization

Product Realization fixes the bounded product proposition before technical form:

- primary user, user job, case basis, purpose, entry, and exit;
- product capabilities and explicit exclusions;
- semantic interaction sequence and success boundary;
- which canonical concepts must be composed without changing their meaning;
- human/AI responsibility and authority boundaries;
- what the product must make possible and what remains unknown; and
- the evidence needed to justify later architectural consideration.

M9's Product Realization Foundation legitimately added product-level interaction
contracts, but it expressly avoided screen topology, components, schemas, APIs,
runtime, and implementation allocation.

## 4. What Belongs to Interaction Architecture

As a distinct architectural concern and artifact discipline, Interaction
Architecture owns the coherent translation of product semantics into
technology-independent interaction structure, including:

- attention hierarchy and progressive disclosure;
- interaction states, transitions, interruption, return, and bounded exit;
- user control, correction, deferral, confirmation, and attributable response;
- inspectability of Evidence, source, uncertainty, hindsight, and authority;
- Working Set interaction semantics without Evidence promotion;
- cross-capability interaction continuity and degraded behavior;
- perceptual and accessibility obligations at interaction-contract level; and
- traceability from user job and canonical meaning to each interaction contract.

It must not own domain truth, source authority, human judgment, canonical concept
definition, screen styling, component design, runtime architecture, persistence,
API design, or implementation allocation.

## 5. What Belongs to Engineering Architecture

Engineering Architecture owns the technical structure required to realize an
approved product and interaction contract:

- runtime and platform boundaries;
- technical responsibility allocation;
- state ownership, persistence, synchronization, and identity mechanisms;
- data representation and integrity constraints;
- integration, security, privacy enforcement, observability, and failure paths;
- technical interfaces and dependency direction; and
- architecture-level verification and implementation constraints.

It does not choose the user job, rewrite product semantics, determine Evidence
meaning, change human authority, or substitute technical convenience for an
interaction requirement. It also does not itself authorize implementation.

## 6. Separability Assessment

The three concerns are **analytically separable but not three demonstrated
canonical layers**.

```text
Product Realization
fixes why, for whom, what product capability, and bounded product meaning

Interaction Architecture
fixes technology-independent interaction contracts that preserve that meaning

Engineering Architecture
fixes technical responsibilities and constraints that can realize those contracts
```

Their outputs, questions, review criteria, and failure modes differ. M9 exposed
this separation when product interaction meaning became substantially more
specific while technical questions remained deliberately deferred. Existing
canonical interaction artifacts independently corroborate the middle concern.

However, separability of work and artifacts does not establish a seventh product
responsibility layer. The canonical layers describe enduring semantic ownership,
not stages between Product Realization and engineering work. Interaction cuts
across P1–P6, with P1 as its primary owner, rather than sitting vertically between
the product and engineering layers.

## 7. Does M9 Support a New Layer?

**No, not on current evidence.** M9 supports stronger recognition of Interaction
Architecture as an explicit architectural discipline and governed handoff, but
does not support introducing it as a new canonical product layer.

Reasons:

1. canonical P1 already owns owner interaction and contribution;
2. canonical interaction-architecture artifacts already exist;
3. the six-layer model explicitly rejects workflow-stack interpretation;
4. Interaction Architecture consumes meanings across all six responsibility views;
5. M9 exercised one historical reader slice, not multiple product contexts;
6. no evidence shows a stable responsibility orphan that P1 and existing sources
   cannot own; and
7. adding a layer now would risk duplicating P1, experience architecture,
   perceptual architecture, and cross-cutting quality responsibilities.

M9 provides one strong instance of a missing explicit handoff, not sufficient
empirical evidence for a canonical layer-model amendment.

## 8. Why Engineering Architecture Should Not Simply Absorb It

The rejection of a new layer does **not** mean Engineering Architecture should
absorb interaction responsibility. Existing repository evidence assigns
interaction meaning upstream of technical form. If engineering absorbs attention
hierarchy, user control, progressive disclosure, uncertainty visibility, or
reflection boundaries, implementation choices would begin deciding product and
human-authority meaning.

The supported repository model is therefore:

- keep Interaction Architecture as an explicit, technology-independent
  architecture discipline primarily owned through P1;
- require traceable interaction contracts before technical allocation;
- let Engineering Architecture consume and preserve those contracts; and
- retain separate review and authority boundaries without calling the discipline
  a new canonical product layer.

## 9. Minimum Later Repository Changes

No repository change is authorized by this assessment. If the accountable human
later chooses to formalize the supported discipline without creating a new layer,
the minimum candidate changes would be:

1. an attributable Decision defining Interaction Architecture as an explicit
   architecture discipline/handoff under the existing six-layer model;
2. a bounded amendment to architectural documentation that maps primary ownership
   to P1 and dependencies to P2–P6 and the trust plane;
3. a review criterion requiring Product Realization → Interaction Architecture →
   Engineering Architecture traceability where user interaction is material;
4. Documentation Map clarification of artifact responsibility and precedence; and
5. only then, a separately authorized transition for affected control surfaces.

These are candidates, not recommendations or authorized changes. Workbench Laws,
Product Constitution, M7/M8, ROADMAP, and BACKLOG remain unchanged.
