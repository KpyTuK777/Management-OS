# 1. Purpose and Boundary

This artifact performs a bounded qualitative assessment of exactly the eight
candidates in the fixed Published Operational Case Candidate Pool. It examines
documentation strengths, gaps, and possible preparation needs for a future H3
Published Historical Walkthrough. It does not perform a walkthrough or Replay.

The governing distinctions are:

```text
Published case ≠ complete historical record
Public documentation ≠ source-owned operational record
Retrospective account ≠ contemporaneous Evidence
Known Outcome ≠ known Decision rationale
Case suitability ≠ case selection
Published material ≠ permission to claim full historical truth
```

This assessment may identify documentation strengths and gaps only. It cannot
establish Evidence sufficiency, historical truth, Product Value, validation
readiness, or authority to proceed.

## 1.2 Public-source claim discipline

The following labels govern material claims in this assessment:

- **[A] Confirmed in authoritative public source:** stated in a regulator,
  inquiry, investigation, court, government, or other source acting within an
  identifiable public authority.
- **[O] Official organizational source:** stated by the organization involved;
  attributable, but not independent merely because it is public.
- **[S] Reported by secondary source:** attributable reporting or analysis that
  may supplement but cannot silently replace missing primary Evidence.
- **[I] Interpretive reconstruction:** an assessment inference about what the
  source landscape might permit; not historical Evidence.
- **[U] Unknown or unavailable:** not established in the reviewed public record.

Regulatory findings retain their legal and procedural framing. Court opinions
retain their procedural posture. Organizational statements retain their
self-reporting limits. Source existence does not guarantee adequate content,
and link access does not transfer source ownership or reuse rights.

# 3. Qualitative Assessment Dimensions

The assessment uses these non-compensatory dimensions:

- public-source authority;
- source diversity;
- contemporaneous Evidence availability;
- chronological reconstructability;
- Context richness;
- Decision visibility;
- Decision-rationale visibility;
- Change visibility;
- Change-fidelity visibility;
- Outcome observability;
- attribution difficulty;
- authority-boundary visibility;
- uncertainty preservation;
- replay plausibility;
- potential for Additional Learning;
- hindsight-bias exposure;
- source-access durability;
- copyright and reuse constraints;
- risk of misleading simplification; and
- manageable walkthrough scope.

Descriptions are qualitative and non-compensatory. A well-supported dimension
cannot offset a material gap in another. No numeric score, weight, total, star
rating, ordering, or overall rating is produced.

The permitted descriptive vocabulary includes `well-supported in public
sources`, `partially supported`, `fragmented`, `disputed`, `materially
incomplete`, `unknown`, and `requires tighter episode boundary`. These terms
describe a dimension; they do not create a verdict.

# 6. H3-Specific Suitability Observations

A published H3 case would need more than a coherent failure story. Relevant
characteristics include:

- a completed historical episode with a declared endpoint;
- contemporaneous information that can be separated from later findings;
- a visible difference between what was known then and what became known later;
- accountable human judgment without invented cognition;
- meaningful alternative reasoning grounded only in Decision-time information;
- non-obvious turning points rather than a pre-authored answer;
- uncertainty that survived the original investigation or response;
- a visible intended Change, Actual Change, and Outcome distinction; and
- enough attributable material to support learning beyond a standard summary.

Across the pool, short episodes can support staged disclosure but may lack
rationale. Large investigations can support source diversity but overwhelm one
walkthrough. Famous cases can offer extensive records while also encouraging
participants to recall a cultural script rather than reason from staged
Evidence. Formal recommendations make intended Change visible but do not show
Change fidelity or later effect.

The following protections remain controlling:

```text
Rich story ≠ suitable H3 case
Famous case ≠ Evidence-rich case
Many sources ≠ coherent Evidence
Clear failure ≠ meaningful replay
New retrospective interpretation ≠ historical truth
```

Potential for Additional Learning is present only as an [I] assessment. It does
not show that a manager would obtain additional Learning, that a Replay would be
effective, or that H3 is supported.

# 7. Replay Integrity Risks

A future published walkthrough could lose integrity by:

- introducing investigation findings, later technical tests, or later records at
  a historical Decision moment;
- treating hindsight as foresight because the Outcome is known;
- inventing motivation, confidence, authority, or rationale where records are
  silent;
- flattening engineers, managers, boards, contractors, operators, and regulators
  into one Decision owner;
- treating a recommendation, directive, announced remediation, or completed task
  as the Actual Change;
- confusing a regulator's findings with complete causal truth outside the
  regulator's mandate;
- turning structured re-engagement into blame or moral judgment;
- choosing an alternative Decision because the historical Outcome is known;
- implying an alternative Decision would have produced a favorable Outcome;
- using a persuasive but unfalsifiable counterfactual;
- replacing disputed source accounts with one polished narrative;
- confusing original organizational Learning with later interpretive Learning;
  or
- allowing the modeled Replay branch to overwrite the historical Decision,
  Change, Outcome, or Learning.

Controls would need to include an information-release ledger, source and time
labels, explicit unknowns, separate historical and modeled branches, authority
mapping, and a prohibition on modeled alternative Outcomes. These are
preparation observations, not a protocol or validation design.

# 8. Unsuitability or Pause Conditions

A candidate should not proceed to a separate selection consideration when:

- the bounded episode cannot be defined;
- chronology cannot be reconstructed credibly;
- the relevant Decision identity is missing;
- available sources reveal Outcome but not the decision process;
- the case is too broad for one walkthrough;
- critical sources are inaccessible;
- major factual claims depend on low-authority summaries;
- historical and Replay branches cannot be kept separate;
- the case invites predetermined moral or technical answers; or
- source constraints prevent attributable reconstruction.

These are preparation conditions, not permanent exclusions. A later source,
narrower boundary, or different Decision identity could change the preparation
picture through separate attributable consideration.
