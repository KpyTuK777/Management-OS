# Bounded Operational Loop Validation Scenario — Architectural Research

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No scenario selection, validation preparation or execution, milestone, backlog, or work authorization
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper investigates how a first concrete bounded operational scenario could
later be selected for validation of the Management OS operational value
proposition. It evaluates scenario properties, rejection conditions,
pre-existing evidence needs, attribution limits, and candidate scenario classes.

It does not select a real scenario, identify an organization or participant,
define a validation exercise, create a prototype, or authorize preparation or
execution.

The research builds on three non-authoritative papers:

1. **Bounded Operational Value and Evidence Proposition — Architectural
   Research**, which frames the hypothesized value as improved management
   capacity connected to observed operational consequence;
2. **Bounded Operational Loop Selection and Validation Criteria — Architectural
   Research**, which recommends a recurrent, material, bounded, evidence-rich,
   reversible, and observable loop type; and
3. **Operational Loop Validation Protocol — Architectural Research**, which
   examines claim boundaries, observation, Evidence, Outcome evaluation,
   attribution, bias, human authority, and pre-validation conditions.

These sources remain research evidence. Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain
unchanged and controlling within their existing scopes.

## 2. Preserved Architectural Boundaries

This paper preserves:

- Outcome as the final consequence test for claimed operational effect;
- Learning as downstream of attributable experience and verified Outcome;
- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, hypothesis, Modeled / Scenario Output,
  Recommendation, Decision, Change record, Outcome, and Learning as distinct
  semantic kinds;
- Decision as accountable human judgment;
- source-owned facts, records, and mutation authority;
- Management OS and AI as non-authoritative assistance;
- multiple contributing factors and uncertainty without a mandatory single Root
  Cause; and
- Operational Diagnosis only as research framing for a possible composition of
  existing responsibilities, never as a canonical object.

The term **validation scenario** is a research label for a bounded real-world
situation that might be considered later. It does not create a scenario object,
state, lifecycle, protocol, schema, capability, or authority.

## 3. Scenario Selection Problem

### 3.1 Why the first scenario matters

The first scenario shapes what can be learned about the Management OS value
hypothesis. A poorly selected scenario may generate a favorable demonstration
while failing to test management value, or may generate an uninterpretable
result because the operational environment is too broad and confounded.

The first scenario is critical for five reasons:

1. **Claim calibration:** it determines whether the research can support a
   bounded value claim rather than activity, adoption, or technical claims.
2. **Loop coverage:** it determines whether the full path from Operational
   Reality through verified Outcome and Learning can be observed.
3. **Attribution quality:** it determines whether a Management OS contribution
   can be distinguished from prior practice, external change, and concurrent
   intervention.
4. **Boundary stress:** it reveals whether human Decision, source ownership,
   semantic separation, and uncertainty survive a real operational situation.
5. **Learning quality:** it determines whether positive, negative, null, and
   indeterminate evidence can inform later judgment without overgeneralization.

A first scenario should not be selected to maximize the probability of success.
It should maximize the probability of obtaining a credible, bounded, and
falsifiable result.

### 3.2 The central selection tension

A scenario must be sufficiently complex to require real management judgment and
exercise the complete operational loop. It must also be sufficiently bounded to
make the initial condition, Decision, Change, Outcome, and material confounders
inspectable.

```text
Too simple
  → clean activity evidence
  → weak test of management value

Appropriately bounded
  → real uncertainty and human judgment
  → one traceable Change
  → observable consequence
  → falsifiable bounded contribution claim

Too complex
  → high materiality but many owners and interventions
  → weak comparison and attribution
  → indeterminate result
```

The appropriate scenario is not necessarily representative of every future use.
Its purpose is to test whether the value mechanism can be observed coherently in
one responsible scope.

### 3.3 What makes a scenario evidential

A scenario becomes evidential when it can produce evidence about all material
parts of a fixed value claim, including evidence that may contradict the claim.
It should have:

- an operational condition independent of its representation in Management OS;
- a meaningful difference between current and intended condition;
- a bounded accountable owner and source authority;
- genuine uncertainty and competing plausible responses;
- attributable Evidence before Decision;
- one distinguishable source-owned Change;
- an Outcome that can be observed against a pre-stated condition or comparison;
- visible external factors and alternative explanations;
- legitimate negative, null, interrupted, and indeterminate results; and
- a later opportunity to test whether verified experience supports Learning.

Availability, convenience, user enthusiasm, or abundant activity data does not
make a scenario evidential.

## 4. Necessary Scenario Characteristics

### 4.1 Recurrent operational deviation

The scenario should concern a deviation that has occurred more than once or has
a credible possibility of meaningful recurrence. Recurrence may provide:

- evidence of the normal range and unwanted condition;
- prior instances for comparison;
- visibility into variation and external factors;
- an opportunity to observe stabilization or reduced recurrence; and
- later comparable situations in which Learning may or may not improve judgment.

Recurrence is not proof of one Cause. Repeated surface similarity may conceal
different contexts and contributors. Comparable exposure, scope, and meaning
must be examined.

### 4.2 Material but proportionate consequence

The deviation should matter enough that improved judgment and Outcome would be
valuable. It should not be so consequential that a first validation creates
disproportionate safety, legal, financial, employment, privacy, or irreversible
exposure.

Triviality cannot test management value; excessive stakes make initial inquiry
irresponsible and may distort Decision behavior.

### 4.3 Bounded ownership

The scenario should have:

- one identifiable accountable human Decision owner;
- a bounded team, process, or operational responsibility;
- identifiable sources that own relevant facts and records;
- one source or clearly bounded set of sources that own the Change; and
- visible affected participants and external authority constraints.

Distributed consultation is compatible with bounded ownership. Ambiguous
Decision accountability or mutation authority is not.

### 4.4 Genuine human Decision

More than one response should be plausibly defensible under uncertainty. The
human should need to interpret Evidence, weigh consequences, accept uncertainty,
and choose Action, no-action, delay, revision, or interruption.

A fixed-rule task or pre-approved routine does not adequately test the
Management OS human-judgment proposition. A Recommendation accepted without
independent judgment is an authority-boundary failure, not validation success.

### 4.5 Available and contestable Evidence

The scenario should have attributable pre-Decision evidence and a credible path
to post-Change Observation. Relevant contrary, negative, and missing Evidence
must be preservable.

The necessary Evidence should be accessible without inventing an implementation
requirement or making Management OS the source of truth for external reality.

### 4.6 Reversible and distinguishable Change

The scenario should permit one bounded intervention that:

- follows an explicit human Decision;
- remains owned by the applicable operational source;
- is distinguishable from other concurrent Changes;
- can be interrupted, corrected, or reversed where the context permits;
- has an expected relation to the intended condition; and
- does not require irreversible commitment merely to obtain evidence.

Reversibility improves responsible exposure and interpretation, but does not
eliminate harm or guarantee attribution.

### 4.7 Observable Outcome

The intended operational condition should be observable within a justified near-
or medium-term horizon. The Outcome should be meaningful to the responsible
human and distinct from:

- completing a Matter or Investigation;
- accepting a Recommendation;
- recording a Decision;
- completing the Change;
- user activity or satisfaction;
- AI output quality; and
- modeled or predicted effect.

The scenario should also permit observation of adverse, delayed, displaced, and
null effects.

### 4.8 Credible comparison

At least one comparison basis should exist before selection:

- attributable initial condition and trajectory;
- meaningfully comparable prior instances;
- repeated observations of normal and deviating conditions;
- a credible contemporaneous comparison where appropriate; or
- a pre-stated intended condition or threshold with visible assumptions.

The comparison must preserve material differences in exposure, participants,
timing, sources, and context. Similar labels do not make cases comparable.

### 4.9 Manageable external factors

Material external influences should be identifiable and bounded in
interpretation. A scenario need not be isolated, but the Outcome should not be
dominated by inseparable reorganization, staffing shocks, policy changes,
market events, or several simultaneous interventions.

### 4.10 Learning opportunity

There should be a credible later occasion to examine whether verified
experience affected judgment or Change in a meaningfully comparable situation.
Learning cannot be proven merely by recording a lesson after the first Outcome.

## 5. Scenario Selection Sufficiency

The characteristics above are not additive scores. Some boundaries are
non-substitutable.

A scenario cannot become suitable because it is highly material if Outcome is
not observable. Rich Evidence cannot compensate for absent human Decision.
Strong recurrence cannot compensate for ambiguous source ownership. A reversible
Change cannot compensate for an unfalsifiable value claim.

### Research sufficiency screen

| Dimension | Minimum sufficient condition | Evidence of insufficiency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operational reality | Existing source-observed condition independent of the research | Synthetic, hypothetical, or modeled situation only |
| Materiality | Consequence matters to accountable operational ownership | Convenience or documentation completion is the only benefit |
| Recurrence | Prior or credible future comparable instances exist | Unique event with no comparison or Learning opportunity |
| Scope | Subject, team/process, time, and affected sources can be bounded | Organization-wide or continuously changing boundary |
| Human judgment | Contestable choice exists under uncertainty | Predetermined rule or automatic response |
| Evidence | Pre-Decision and post-Change sources are attributable and contestable | Retrospective belief or inaccessible data is the main basis |
| Change | One source-owned intervention is traceable and responsibly reversible | No intervention or several inseparable interventions |
| Outcome | Intended condition and horizon can be fixed before observation | Only activity, completion, or modeled effect is visible |
| Comparison | A credible baseline or comparable condition exists | No reference point beyond narrative expectation |
| Confounders | Major external factors can be identified and discussed | External change dominates and cannot be separated |
| Authority | Decision and mutation ownership remain explicit | AI, researcher, or non-owning system would become de facto authority |
| Learning | Later comparable judgment is plausible | Stored reflection is the only possible Learning signal |

Failure of a non-substitutable dimension should reject the scenario for
complete-loop validation. It may still be useful for a different bounded
research question, but no such question is created here.

## 6. Scenario Rejection Criteria

### 6.1 Too simple

Reject a scenario when:

- the answer is predetermined by an existing rule;
- no meaningful Investigation or competing interpretation exists;
- no human tradeoff or uncertainty exists;
- the Change is administrative or purely representational;
- success means only completion or user activity; or
- the same evidence would demonstrate only capture, workflow, or usability.

Such a scenario may produce a smooth demonstration while failing to test the
value proposition.

### 6.2 Too strategic or structurally broad

Reject an initial scenario when it concerns:

- organization-wide transformation;
- long-horizon strategic direction;
- many independent Decision owners;
- several authority domains and source systems whose Changes cannot be bounded;
- effects dominated by structural, market, regulatory, or staffing shifts; or
- an Outcome that cannot be interpreted within a practical horizon.

High importance does not make a scenario suitable for first validation.

### 6.3 No Outcome visibility

Reject a scenario when:

- only Recommendation, Decision, or execution completion can be observed;
- the intended operational condition cannot be stated before the Change;
- no owner can verify the consequence;
- Outcome depends only on self-reported confidence or satisfaction;
- the necessary horizon is unknown or impractical; or
- modeled output or forecast would be used as observed consequence.

### 6.4 Excessive external factors

Reject a scenario when simultaneous Changes or external events make the
intervention relationship uninterpretable, including major reorganization,
policy replacement, acute staffing disruption, market shock, or multiple
inseparable improvement initiatives.

External factors need not be absent. They must be visible enough to prevent a
false contribution claim.

### 6.5 Safety-critical or disproportionate exposure

Reject an initial scenario when validation participation or a reversible-change
assumption could expose people or operations to material safety, legal, ethical,
employment, security, privacy, financial, or irreversible harm.

This paper does not define risk controls or governance. It observes that a first
value inquiry should not obtain evidence by creating disproportionate exposure.

### 6.6 Evidence and authority defects

Reject a scenario when:

- source ownership is ambiguous;
- Evidence cannot be accessed or its meaning cannot be established;
- the researcher, Management OS, or AI would need to make the Decision;
- Change would require unauthorized mutation;
- participants cannot preserve contrary evidence or no-action;
- the scenario was chosen after a favorable Outcome was visible; or
- only completed or successful cases would be retained.

## 7. Evidence Required Before Scenario Selection

Before a scenario could be selected, evidence should already support the
selection claim. This is different from Outcome evidence collected later.

### 7.1 Operational-condition evidence

- attributable description of the current or recurring deviation;
- source identity, timing, scope, participants, and operational consequence;
- evidence that the condition is material to an accountable owner;
- prior instances or trajectory where available;
- current uncertainty and plausible alternative explanations; and
- evidence that the scenario is not created solely for validation.

### 7.2 Ownership and authority evidence

- accountable human Decision ownership;
- source ownership of relevant facts and records;
- source ownership of any prospective Change;
- affected people and material external authority boundaries; and
- evidence that AI, Management OS, or the validation observer need not receive
  Decision or mutation authority.

### 7.3 Evidence-availability assessment

- relevant pre-Decision sources and their limitations;
- possibility of contrary and negative Evidence;
- post-Change observation sources;
- correction and provenance availability;
- likely missing evidence; and
- whether the evidence boundary can remain proportionate and inspectable.

### 7.4 Comparison evidence

- initial condition or stable range;
- prior comparable cases and differences;
- exposure, volume, timing, and context needed to interpret recurrence;
- potential contemporaneous comparison if one naturally exists; and
- known limits of the comparison.

### 7.5 Change feasibility as an architectural observation

Without defining implementation, the selection evidence should show that one
bounded, source-owned, attributable, and responsibly reversible Change is
plausible. The actual Change should not be predetermined merely to make the
scenario selectable; the accountable human Decision remains open.

### 7.6 Outcome observability assessment

- intended operational condition candidate;
- potential primary Outcome dimension;
- plausible observation horizon;
- source-owned observations that could support verification;
- possible adverse, delayed, displaced, and null effects; and
- an accountable verification owner.

These items do not select the Outcome or define metrics. They test whether a
falsifiable Outcome claim could later be fixed.

## 8. Evidence and Attribution Boundary

### 8.1 Selection evidence is not value evidence

Evidence that a scenario is recurrent, bounded, observable, and evidence-rich
supports suitability for inquiry. It does not show that Management OS creates
value in that scenario.

Likewise:

- a rich Matter history does not prove continuity improved;
- available Evidence does not prove Investigation will improve judgment;
- a reversible Change does not prove a good Outcome;
- an observable condition does not prove attribution; and
- a comparable prior case does not prove generalization.

### 8.2 Avoiding attribution error

The selection should preserve the possibility of examining:

- pre-existing operational trajectory;
- manager knowledge and actions outside Management OS;
- concurrent interventions and external events;
- differences between Decision intent and actual Change;
- null, negative, delayed, and adverse observations;
- selection and novelty effects;
- natural variation and regression toward typical conditions; and
- alternative mechanisms that could explain the Outcome.

A scenario that cannot expose these factors is likely to generate a story of
causality rather than evidence of contribution.

### 8.3 Bounded contribution

A later validation could consider a bounded contribution claim only if evidence
can trace:

```text
Initial condition
  → material Evidence and change in Understanding
  → explicit human Decision
  → distinguishable source-owned Change
  → observed operational difference
  → comparison and alternative explanations
  → owned contribution judgment with uncertainty
```

The strength of the claim could range from association to plausible or material
contribution. “Management OS caused the Outcome” is not warranted by usage,
temporal order, AI participation, acceptance of a Recommendation, or favorable
Outcome alone.

Outcome may confirm that the intended condition changed without proving that
the prior Interpretation, scoped Cause, Recommendation, Decision, or system was
uniquely correct.

## 9. Candidate Scenario Classes

The following comparison evaluates scenario classes, not real cases.

### 9.1 Process deviation

**Shape:** a recurring departure from an intended operating condition or
standard within one bounded process.

**Strengths:**

- source-owned observations may already exist;
- current and intended conditions may be distinguishable;
- recurrence and stabilization may be observable;
- one bounded Change may be possible; and
- comparable prior or later instances may support Learning.

**Risks:**

- the standard may be treated as automatically correct;
- conformance may replace Outcome;
- variation may have multiple legitimate causes; and
- measurement may encourage local optimization at the expense of broader
  consequence.

**Research suitability:** high when the deviation is material, the response is
not predetermined, and Outcome is broader than restored procedural compliance.

### 9.2 Recurring operational failure

**Shape:** repeated failure to achieve an intended operational result, with
visible consequence and several possible contributing factors.

**Strengths:**

- strong materiality and visible need for judgment;
- prior instances can support comparison;
- hypotheses and contrary Evidence may be meaningful;
- recurrence and severity can be observed; and
- Learning can be tested in subsequent instances.

**Risks:**

- pressure to find one Root Cause or assign blame;
- each recurrence may differ materially;
- several interventions may already be underway;
- severity may create disproportionate exposure; and
- regression to typical performance may be mistaken for impact.

**Research suitability:** high when the failure is non-safety-critical,
instances are meaningfully comparable, and one intervention can be bounded.

### 9.3 Coordination breakdown

**Shape:** repeated loss of context, ownership, Decision intent, or timely action
across a bounded handoff or collaboration boundary.

**Strengths:**

- directly tests Matter continuity and M8 handoff boundaries;
- human judgment and distributed Evidence are often material;
- delay, rework, interruption, or recovery may be observable;
- source ownership can remain explicit; and
- one bounded coordination Change may be reversible.

**Risks:**

- participant behavior and attention may change because of observation;
- responsibility may be genuinely distributed;
- activity metrics can masquerade as improved coordination;
- social and authority effects may dominate; and
- the observed bottleneck may move rather than disappear.

**Research suitability:** high when there is one accountable Decision owner, a
clear operational consequence, and source records beyond self-report.

### 9.4 Quality variation

**Shape:** recurring variation in a product, service, decision, or operational
output relative to a meaningful intended condition.

**Strengths:**

- variation and Outcome may be observable;
- repeated cases can support comparison;
- several interpretations and responses may exist;
- correction and Learning may be visible; and
- burden and adverse effects can be balanced against improvement.

**Risks:**

- the quality measure may be an incomplete proxy;
- detection may occur long after the relevant Decision;
- several upstream contributors may be inseparable;
- measurement changes may create apparent improvement; and
- local quality gains may displace time, cost, or other Outcomes.

**Research suitability:** medium-high when the quality condition is source-owned,
timely, decision-relevant, and balanced against material side effects.

### 9.5 Decision-latency condition

**Shape:** recurring delay in reaching an accountable Decision for a bounded
operational matter.

**Strengths:**

- timing and handoff evidence may exist;
- the effect of missing context or Evidence may be visible;
- a bounded Change may improve information or responsibility continuity; and
- time-to-Decision can be compared.

**Risks:**

- faster Decision may reduce quality;
- speed is an activity or capacity measure, not final Outcome;
- delay may be justified by uncertainty or external authority;
- Outcome may occur too late for practical observation.

**Research suitability:** medium unless a material downstream operational
Outcome can be observed alongside timing and quality.

### 9.6 Recurring stabilization problem

**Shape:** a bounded operation repeatedly becomes unstable or takes excessive
time and effort to return to an acceptable condition.

**Strengths:**

- the intended condition and stabilization horizon may be observable;
- recurrence, duration, and burden may provide comparison;
- genuine Investigation and human Decision are likely;
- one reversible intervention may be possible; and
- adverse or displaced effects can be observed.

**Risks:**

- external variability may dominate;
- “stable” may hide degraded performance;
- several concurrent responses may be difficult to separate; and
- natural recovery may be credited to the Change.

**Research suitability:** high when the operating condition has a credible
source-owned baseline and concurrent interventions can be bounded.

### 9.7 Resource-contention condition

**Shape:** repeated operational conflict over bounded capacity, attention, or
shared resources requiring explicit prioritization and tradeoff.

**Strengths:**

- genuine human Decision and tradeoffs are present;
- Evidence, alternatives, and intended consequence can be explicit;
- a reversible allocation Change may be traceable;
- burden and throughput effects may be observable.

**Risks:**

- may drift into product priority or delivery planning;
- local gains may create displaced harm;
- affected parties and authority may be broad;
- Outcome may depend on demand variability.

**Research suitability:** medium when the authority and affected scope are truly
bounded and no roadmap or delivery decision is implied.

## 11. Preferred Scenario Shape

The research supports a preferred shape rather than a named scenario:

> A recurring process deviation, coordination breakdown, operational failure,
> or stabilization problem within one bounded operational responsibility, where
> one accountable manager must decide among plausible responses using
> attributable and contestable Evidence; one non-safety-critical and reversible
> source-owned Change can be distinguished; an intended operational condition
> can be observed within a near- or medium-term horizon; prior or later instances
> provide credible comparison; and material confounders remain visible.

This shape is preferred because it combines:

- enough complexity to test context, Investigation, Evidence, Interpretation,
  human Decision, Change, Outcome, and Learning;
- enough recurrence to support comparison and falsification;
- enough boundedness to preserve one Decision owner and distinguish one Change;
- enough observability to make Outcome rather than activity the consequence
  test; and
- responsible exposure suitable for a first inquiry.

The preferred shape does not imply that all four classes are equally suitable
or that the first available case should be chosen.

## 12. Conditions Before Selecting a Real Scenario

Before a separate authority could responsibly select the first real scenario,
the following should be possible.

### 12.1 Fixed selection proposition

- The candidate's operational condition, accountable owner, scope, sources, and
  material consequence can be described without relying on a proposed solution.
- The reason for selection is explicit and not based on an already favorable
  Outcome.
- Inclusion, exclusion, and rejection considerations can be recorded before
  validation evidence is interpreted.

### 12.2 Whole-loop plausibility

- A real Matter can preserve context.
- Investigation and contestable Evidence could affect Understanding.
- A genuine human Decision remains open.
- One source-owned Change is possible but not predetermined.
- The intended operational condition and observation horizon can be pre-stated.
- Later comparable experience can potentially test Learning.

### 12.3 Evidence feasibility

- Pre-Decision and post-Change source evidence exists or can legitimately be
  observed without implementation invention.
- Contrary, null, negative, adverse, and missing evidence can remain visible.
- A credible comparison can be justified.
- Major external factors and concurrent interventions can be identified.
- Evidence can remain proportionate, semantically valid, and independently
  inspectable where applicable.

### 12.4 Authority and exposure integrity

- Decision remains with the accountable human.
- Management OS, AI, and observers remain non-authoritative.
- Facts, records, and Change remain source-owned.
- No mandatory Root Cause or canonical Diagnosis object is required.
- The prospective Change is bounded, non-safety-critical, and responsibly
  reversible.
- No participant must accept disproportionate harm, coercion, or authority
  distortion for the validation to be complete.

### 12.5 Claim and attribution integrity

- A bounded value claim, counterclaim, falsification conditions, Outcome,
  comparison, and indeterminate conditions can be fixed in advance.
- Selection evidence can remain distinct from later value evidence.
- Association, contribution, and causality can remain distinct.
- Positive, negative, null, interrupted, and indeterminate results are all
  legitimate.
- The maximum conclusion remains case-bounded and non-authorizing.

If these conditions cannot be met, the real scenario should not be selected for
whole-loop validation. The missing condition may justify further bounded
research, but it does not automatically create an architectural Gap or work.

## 13. Is the Research Sufficient for Validation Preparation?

### 13.1 What the research now supplies

Across the four completed papers, the research now provides:

- a bounded operational value hypothesis;
- a complete-loop value mechanism;
- selection and rejection principles;
- required loop characteristics;
- evidence sufficiency and insufficient-evidence distinctions;
- Outcome dimensions and comparison conditions;
- falsification, failure, null, and indeterminate conditions;
- attribution and bounded-contribution limits;
- bias and validity threats;
- human and source-authority boundaries;
- pre-validation conditions; and
- a preferred scenario shape with candidate class comparison.

This is sufficient research basis to **consider a separately authorized
validation-preparation activity** whose sole purpose would be to identify and
assess candidate real scenarios against fixed boundaries.

### 13.2 What the research does not yet supply

The research does not supply:

- a selected real scenario;
- consent, access, participation, or source authority;
- an actual baseline or evidence inventory;
- a fixed case-specific value claim or Outcome;
- an observation horizon or comparison decision;
- a case-specific confounder assessment;
- a validation plan, method, governance decision, or execution authority;
- a prototype or implementation mechanism; or
- evidence that any candidate satisfies the research conditions.

Therefore the research is not sufficient to begin validation execution. Moving
from research to preparation would require separate authority and a bounded
purpose that does not silently become execution, prototype definition, or
implementation planning.

## 14. Recommendation

The recommended next consideration is a separately authorized,
implementation-independent **candidate scenario identification and suitability
assessment**, not validation execution.

Such preparation, if authorized later, should:

1. identify more than one real candidate within the preferred scenario shape;
2. record pre-existing evidence for materiality, recurrence, ownership,
   Evidence availability, Change boundedness, Outcome observability, comparison,
   confounders, and Learning opportunity;
3. apply explicit rejection conditions before favoring a candidate;
4. retain the option to conclude that no candidate is currently suitable;
5. avoid selecting by convenience, enthusiasm, data abundance, or expected
   success; and
6. return only a bounded suitability conclusion, not a validation result or
   authority to proceed.

This recommendation is not an authorization, lifecycle step, governance
process, backlog item, or scenario selection. A later decision would have to
determine whether preparation itself should be opened and under what authority.
