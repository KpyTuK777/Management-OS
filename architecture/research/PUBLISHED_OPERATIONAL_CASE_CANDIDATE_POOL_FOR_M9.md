## Purpose

This artifact identifies a bounded set of real operational episodes for which a
substantial body of public material exists. A candidate may later be considered,
through a separate attributable process, for a possible H3 Published Historical
Walkthrough.

Presence in this pool means only that open material appears capable of supporting
inquiry into the following reconstruction path:

```text
Context
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Change
  ↓
Outcome
  ↓
Learning
  ↓
Replay
```

The descriptions below are source-map observations, not completed
reconstructions. They do not decide whether any source is accurate, complete,
independent, mutually consistent, ethically usable, or sufficient for H3. Terms
such as Evidence, Decision, Change, Outcome, Learning, and Replay identify
potential reconstruction roles only.

## Pool Construction Discipline

- Only publicly accessible, well-documented operational episodes are included.
- Official investigation, regulatory, court, corporate, or institutional records
  form the core public basis for every candidate.
- Public availability is not treated as permission for unrestricted reuse or as
  proof of source completeness.
- Evidence richness and visibility are described narratively; they are not scored.
- No ordering below communicates preference, comparative quality, readiness, or
  priority.
- Multiple operational categories may apply to the same episode. Category labels
  are descriptive and non-exclusive.

# Candidate Pool

## Knight Capital software deployment incident (2012)

**Working title:** Knight Capital: dormant code activation and uncontrolled market
orders

**Domain:** electronic securities trading

**Operational category:** Operational execution; Engineering; Incident Response;
Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission published an
enforcement release and detailed administrative order. Public company filings also
record the trading loss and subsequent control investment. Core sources are the
[SEC enforcement release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222)
and the [KCG 2013 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1569391/000144530514000824/kcg2013123110-k.htm).

**Evidence richness:** The SEC record supplies a bounded timeline, code-deployment
conditions, the dormant function, erroneous order flow, automated emails, control
gaps, incident-response conditions, quantities, financial consequences, and prior
control-review context. Company filings provide a separate corporate account of
loss and later investment.

**Decision visibility:** The record exposes the pre-incident deployment approach,
the failure to respond to generated messages, actions during the incident, the
settlement, and mandated independent control review. Individual reasoning and the
full internal escalation record are not wholly public.

**Outcome visibility:** Order volume, unwanted positions, market disruption,
reported loss, regulatory penalty, and corporate consequences are publicly stated.
Longer-term operational effectiveness of the control changes is less directly
observable.

**Replay plausibility:** Timestamps, deployment facts, system behavior, warning
messages, response sequence, and regulatory findings could support replay of what
signals existed and when. Replay would need to distinguish SEC findings, company
statements, and any later interpretation.

**Major limitations:** The SEC order is an enforcement settlement consented to
without admission or denial of its findings. Proprietary code, full logs, interviews,
internal communications, and implementation evidence for all remediations are not
public. The record is stronger for incident formation and immediate consequence
than for long-horizon learning.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The episode contains a compact,
time-sensitive path from deployment Evidence through human response, operational
Change, cascading Outcome, and control-oriented follow-up while retaining clear
limits on internal visibility.

## NASA NOAA-N Prime spacecraft handling mishap (2003)

**Working title:** NOAA-N Prime: spacecraft damage during an integration and test
handling operation

**Domain:** spacecraft manufacturing, integration, and test

**Operational category:** Manufacturing; Engineering; Safety; Operational
execution; Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** NASA publishes an official reviewed lesson with the
driving event, findings, lessons, and recommendations in the public Lessons Learned
Information System: [LLIS lesson 1580](https://llis.nasa.gov/lesson/1580). NASA
also explains the status and lifecycle of the
[public Lessons Learned system](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-professionals/appel-lessons-learned/).

**Evidence richness:** The published lesson identifies the handling operation,
improperly configured cart, procedural and oversight conditions, long-duration
contract context, configuration-control issues, and ten linked lessons. The source
also connects the episode to training, procedure discipline, planning, safety, and
government-contractor oversight.

**Decision visibility:** The lesson describes departures from procedure and
oversight expectations and publishes recommended corrective directions. It does
not expose every contemporaneous conversation, approval, or individual rationale.

**Outcome visibility:** Damage to the fully assembled spacecraft and the resulting
investigation are visible. The public lesson is explicit about intended prevention,
but downstream adoption and measured recurrence outcomes are not comprehensively
reported in the lesson itself.

**Replay plausibility:** The bounded physical operation, identifiable interfaces,
procedural configuration, contractor/government roles, and enumerated lessons could
support a structured replay. Any reconstruction would need the underlying mishap
report and source attachments, where accessible, rather than relying only on the
curated lesson.

**Major limitations:** LLIS is a reviewed institutional summary, not the full
investigation record. Its recommendations may compress disagreement and missing
Evidence. NASA's broader LLIS corpus is not itself one case; this pool therefore
bounds the candidate to NOAA-N Prime lesson 1580. Public proof of implementation
and later effect is incomplete.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The episode joins a concrete manufacturing
operation to procedure execution, configuration control, safety oversight, formal
lessons, and institutional replay within an explicitly public learning system.

## Toyota unintended-acceleration response (2007–2014)

**Working title:** Toyota: floor-mat entrapment, sticky pedals, recalls, and safety
communication

**Domain:** automotive manufacturing and product safety

**Operational category:** Manufacturing; Engineering; Safety; Incident Response;
Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** NHTSA publishes recall-query records, technical
assessments, and the NASA Engineering and Safety Center assessment. The U.S.
Department of Justice publishes the Deferred Prosecution Agreement and admitted
Statement of Facts. Core entry points include the
[NHTSA/NASA technical assessment](https://www.nhtsa.gov/document/technical-support-national-highway-traffic-safety-administration-nhtsa-reported-toyota),
the [NHTSA recall-query closing record](https://www.nhtsa.gov/file-downloads/download?p=nhtsa%2Fdownloads%2FTQ10-001%2FTQ10-001+Resumes%2FTQ10001+Closing+Resume.pdf),
and the [DOJ case record](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/programs/victim-witness-services/united-states-v-toyota-corporation).

**Evidence richness:** The public corpus contains complaint and recall chronologies,
technical testing, competing causal questions, physical pedal-interference
findings, examination of proposed electronic causes, company communications,
regulatory inquiries, admitted facts, penalties, and monitor obligations.

**Decision visibility:** Recall scope and timing, communications decisions,
regulatory investigation decisions, technical-assessment choices, and the later
agreement are visible across attributable sources. Internal corporate deliberation
is visible only where disclosed or incorporated into official records.

**Outcome visibility:** Recall populations, technical conclusions, civil and
criminal resolutions, penalties, and monitoring requirements are public. The
corpus does not provide a single uncontested measure of safety effect after all
changes.

**Replay plausibility:** Dated complaints, information requests, recall notices,
technical tests, public claims, and later admitted facts allow reconstruction of
what different actors reported or knew at different times. Replay must keep
separate the floor-mat, sticky-pedal, electronic-cause, driver-action, and
communication questions.

**Major limitations:** “Unintended acceleration” covers multiple phenomena and a
long time window; careless bounding would collapse distinct causes and claims.
Technical reports, enforcement facts, consumer allegations, litigation positions,
and media accounts have different authority. Injury and causation claims require
particular care and cannot be inferred from recall or complaint counts.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The material can expose how technical
Evidence, safety decisions, public communication, corrective action, regulatory
review, and residual causal uncertainty interact across organizational boundaries.

## Target Canada supply-chain and market exit (2011–2015)

**Working title:** Target Canada: rapid market launch, inventory-system breakdown,
and exit

**Domain:** retail supply chain and enterprise systems

**Operational category:** Supply Chain; Operational execution; Engineering;
Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** Public court records describe the Canadian expansion,
inventory forecasting and replenishment problems, store rollout, public statements,
and later litigation. Canadian insolvency records preserve the court-supervised
exit. Core entry points are the
[Eighth Circuit opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/20/04/181831P.pdf),
the [federal district court record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-mnd-0_16-cv-01315/pdf/USCOURTS-mnd-0_16-cv-01315-0.pdf),
and the [Canadian CCAA record index](https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/office-superintendent-bankruptcy/en/ccaa-records-list/ccaa-records-target-canada-co-et-al).

**Evidence richness:** The public corpus describes the simultaneous store rollout,
new distribution and IT infrastructure, inventory data and forecasting problems,
manual workarounds, operational symptoms, financial disclosures, the decision to
discontinue Canadian operations, and the supervised wind-down.

**Decision visibility:** Expansion pace, infrastructure choices, public reporting,
remedial efforts, and exit are visible at an organizational level. The exact
decision records for system selection, go-live gates, data acceptance, and local
workarounds are only partially exposed.

**Outcome visibility:** Store openings, inventory availability problems, reported
financial effects, exit, and insolvency proceedings are public. Isolating the
effect of supply-chain and data failures from assortment, pricing, competitive,
real-estate, and expansion-timing factors is not supported by those outcomes alone.

**Replay plausibility:** Dated corporate disclosures, allegations tested in court,
judicial descriptions, and CCAA records could support a staged reconstruction of
launch, stabilization efforts, and exit. Each proposition would need to retain its
source and procedural status.

**Major limitations:** Judicial opinions summarize records assembled for securities
litigation and do not constitute a complete operational investigation; some widely
repeated operational details originate in journalism quoted by litigants. Internal
system data, governance records, and post-change performance series are not fully
public. The company-wide outcome is heavily confounded.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The episode offers a cross-functional case
in which data quality, enterprise-system adoption, supply-chain execution, rollout
speed, local workarounds, and strategic exit remain related but should not be
collapsed into a single asserted cause.

## TSB Bank IT migration (2018)

**Working title:** TSB: core-banking migration decision, service disruption, and
operational remediation

**Domain:** retail banking technology and operational resilience

**Operational category:** Engineering; Operational execution; Incident Response;
Safety; Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** The FCA and PRA publish detailed final notices; TSB
published an independent review and its response; annual reports document impacts
and remediation. Core sources are the
[FCA Final Notice](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/final-notices/tsb-bank-plc-2022.pdf),
the [PRA Final Notice](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/regulatory-action/final-notice-from-pra-to-tsb-bank.pdf),
the [Slaughter and May review](https://www.tsb.co.uk/content/dam/tsb-public/documents/media-centre/Slaughter-and-May-final-report.pdf),
and [TSB's published response](https://www.tsb.co.uk/news-releases/slaughter-and-may.html).

**Evidence richness:** Public records cover programme history, implementation model,
governance, outsourcing, plans and re-plans, testing, go-live readiness, deferred
functionality, migration, technical disruption, customer effects, remediation,
redress, management and operating-model changes, and regulatory findings.

**Decision visibility:** The predominant single-event migration model, schedule
commitments, re-planning, test treatment, go-live decision, assurances presented to
the board, and later governance changes are described with unusual specificity.
The published sources also preserve disagreement between the independent review,
TSB, and regulators.

**Outcome visibility:** Service disruption across channels, affected customer base,
time to business-as-usual, redress, penalties, and certain post-migration operational
claims are public. Some later performance claims are company-authored and require
independent contextualization.

**Replay plausibility:** A dated programme record, decision gates, test status,
assurances, technical events, channel consequences, and follow-up actions could
support a multi-perspective replay while preserving what the board was told at the
time.

**Major limitations:** Confidential technical records, full board materials, vendor
records, and customer-level Evidence are not public. Sources differ in scope and
interpretation. Later regulatory standards must not be retroactively substituted for
the requirements applicable in 2018.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The episode provides a visible bridge from
programme Evidence and human go-live judgment through actual migration, operational
harm, remediation, governance change, regulatory findings, and contested learning.

## Space Shuttle Challenger launch decision and accident (1986)

**Working title:** Challenger STS-51-L: launch decision, O-ring failure, and return
to flight

**Domain:** crewed spaceflight operations and engineering governance

**Operational category:** Engineering; Safety; Operational execution; Incident
Response; Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** NASA provides the Rogers Commission report, implementation
of recommendations, mission material, historical analysis, and additional public
records. Entry points include NASA's
[Challenger accident resource page](https://www.nasa.gov/challenger-sts-51l-accident/),
the [NASA safety learning summary](https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/challenger),
and the [congressional investigation record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=19870002391).

**Evidence richness:** The corpus includes engineering history, temperature and
O-ring Evidence, launch-eve communications, testimony, management and contractor
roles, the launch decision, accident analysis, Commission findings, recommendations,
redesign, governance response, and return-to-flight history.

**Decision visibility:** Public testimony and Commission records expose competing
recommendations, escalation paths, the launch decision process, information that did
not reach senior decision-makers, and later corrective decisions.

**Outcome visibility:** The loss of vehicle and crew, programme stand-down,
investigation, hardware redesign, organizational changes, and return to flight are
extensively documented. Longer-term recurrence prevention and institutional learning
remain interpretive questions, especially when later Shuttle history is introduced.

**Replay plausibility:** Contemporaneous engineering data, communication sequences,
organizational roles, testimony, findings, and implementation records can support a
time-bounded replay of what was known, communicated, omitted, decided, and changed.

**Major limitations:** The corpus is enormous and shaped by multiple retrospective
narratives. Hindsight bias and simplified “technical versus management” accounts are
material risks. A walkthrough would require a narrow temporal and decision boundary;
it could not responsibly reconstruct personal cognition beyond attributable records.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The case makes the relationship between
technical Evidence, dissent, authority, schedule pressure, decision handoffs,
catastrophic Outcome, formal corrective action, and organizational learning unusually
visible.

## Deepwater Horizon / Macondo blowout (2010)

**Working title:** Macondo: well-control decisions, barrier failure, and process
safety response

**Domain:** offshore drilling and process safety

**Operational category:** Safety; Operational execution; Engineering; Incident
Response; Organizational Learning; Supply Chain

**Public availability:** The U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board
publishes a multi-volume final investigation and executive summary; other public
official investigations provide additional perspectives. The CSB case hub is the
[Macondo Blowout and Explosion investigation](https://www.csb.gov/macondo-blowout-and-explosion/),
with public [Volume 1](https://www.csb.gov/file.aspx?DocumentId=5930) and
[Volume 2](https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/7/vol_2_final_version.pdf).

**Evidence richness:** The record covers drilling and temporary-abandonment context,
tests and interpretations, contractor and operator interfaces, barrier design,
well-control actions, blowout-preventer behavior, emergency response, fatalities and
spill consequences, regulatory conditions, organizational factors, and formal
recommendations.

**Decision visibility:** Operational choices before the blowout, interpretation of
negative-pressure tests, emergency actions, safety-management choices, regulatory
decisions, and later corrective recommendations are publicly analyzed. Some source
Evidence was disputed across investigations and litigation.

**Outcome visibility:** Explosion, deaths, injuries, rig loss, duration of the
uncontrolled flow, environmental disaster, investigations, enforcement, and multiple
industry or regulatory responses are public. The scale and multiplicity of changes
make later causal attribution difficult.

**Replay plausibility:** Detailed technical chronology, barrier model, testimony,
test data, organizational interfaces, and several official perspectives could
support replay of bounded decision points and emergency response.

**Major limitations:** The full episode spans many organizations, legal proceedings,
technical systems, and years of aftermath. Official reports differ in mandate and
emphasis. A useful walkthrough would need a sharply declared unit of analysis and
could not treat recommendations or rule changes as measured safety outcomes.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The episode can expose how distributed
Evidence, contractor interfaces, human interpretation, barrier management,
time-critical action, severe Outcome, and system-level learning remain connected
without implying one simple root cause.

## Boeing 737 MAX design, certification, accidents, and return to service

**Working title:** Boeing 737 MAX: design assumptions, certification handoffs, two
accidents, and corrective return

**Domain:** commercial aircraft engineering, certification, and operations

**Operational category:** Engineering; Manufacturing; Safety; Operational
execution; Incident Response; Organizational Learning

**Public availability:** The U.S. House Committee on Transportation and
Infrastructure publishes its final report, hearing material, transcribed interviews,
and document sets. Accident authorities and the FAA publish additional investigation,
recommendation, certification, and return-to-service records. Core entry points are
the Committee's [investigation archive](https://democrats-transportation.house.gov/committee-activity/boeing-737-max-investigation)
and [final report](https://democrats-transportation.house.gov/imo/media/doc/2020.09.15%20FINAL%20737%20MAX%20Report%20for%20Public%20Release.pdf).

**Evidence richness:** The public record covers programme objectives and pressure,
technical assumptions, MCAS evolution, safety assessment, delegated certification,
pilot information, internal communications, two accident sequences, grounding,
investigations, design and training changes, certification review, and return to
service.

**Decision visibility:** Engineering, programme, disclosure, delegation,
certification, grounding, corrective-design, and return-to-service decisions are
visible across multiple attributable institutions. The corpus contains both
contemporaneous records and retrospective findings.

**Outcome visibility:** Two fatal accidents, worldwide grounding, investigations,
regulatory and legislative actions, design changes, training changes, certification,
and return to service are public. Later operational performance cannot by itself
validate every identified change or learning claim.

**Replay plausibility:** Document sets, interviews, design history, certification
records, accident reports, and dated decisions could support replay at several
bounded levels. The unit would have to be declared in advance because programme,
certification, accident, and return-to-service replays are not interchangeable.

**Major limitations:** The corpus is exceptionally large, multinational, contested,
and still subject to later developments. Accident investigation findings,
congressional findings, regulatory actions, corporate statements, and litigation
records have different mandates. Combining both accidents may erase flight-specific
conditions; isolating one accident may erase programme-level context.

**Why potentially interesting for H3:** The case offers extensive attributable
material for examining how engineering assumptions, economic and schedule
constraints, organizational interfaces, regulatory authority, operator Evidence,
corrective Change, and safety learning propagate across a complex system.

## Desired Candidate Not Included in This Pool

British Airways' May 2017 IT outage was considered only against the public-material
boundary used to construct this pool. Public corporate reporting and contemporary
reporting make the initiating outage, service disruption, customer impact, and
financial effect visible. However, no sufficiently detailed public independent
forensic report was located that exposes the technical sequence, decision record,
implemented corrective changes, and later operational observations as one
attributable chain. It is therefore not counted as a candidate here. This boundary
statement is not a ranking, recommendation, permanent exclusion, or finding that a
future source search could not change the available record.

# Candidate Pool Boundaries

```text
Pool ≠ Selection

Pool ≠ Recommendation

Pool ≠ Validation

Pool ≠ Evidence sufficiency

Pool ≠ Published truth
```

More explicitly:

- Inclusion does not select a case for H3 or any other use.
- Inclusion does not recommend, prioritize, rank, score, shortlist, or compare a
  candidate favorably against another.
- Inclusion does not validate a case, source, reconstruction method, hypothesis,
  Management OS, or Product Value.

  scope and method.
- Publication does not make a statement true, complete, neutral, current, mutually
  consistent, or authoritative outside the source's own role.

  investigation conclusion, and reported observation retain different provenance
  and authority.
- This artifact creates no walkthrough, validation protocol, experiment, synthetic

  or milestone transition.
