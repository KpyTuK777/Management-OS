# Bounded Operational Loop Scenario Assessment Framework — Architectural Research

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No scenario selection, validation preparation or execution, prototype, milestone, or work authorization
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper investigates how concrete candidate scenarios could be assessed
before a separate authority decides whether any validation-preparation step
should be considered. It defines research dimensions, evidence questions,
qualitative assessment logic, rejection conditions, and decision boundaries.

It does not assess or select a real scenario. It does not create a canonical
assessment process, score, gate, object, lifecycle, governance procedure, or
approval mechanism.

The research builds on four non-authoritative papers:

1. **Bounded Operational Value and Evidence Proposition — Architectural
   Research**;
2. **Bounded Operational Loop Selection and Validation Criteria — Architectural
   Research**;
3. **Operational Loop Validation Protocol — Architectural Research**; and
4. **Bounded Operational Loop Validation Scenario — Architectural Research**.

Together they establish a research basis for examining value, loop completeness,
Evidence, Outcome, falsification, scenario types, and pre-validation conditions.
They remain research evidence only. Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged.

## 2. Preserved Boundaries

The framework preserves:

- Outcome as the final consequence test for a claimed operational effect;
- Learning as downstream of attributable experience and verified Outcome;
- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, hypothesis, Modeled / Scenario Output,
  Recommendation, Decision, Change record, Outcome, and Learning as distinct
  semantic kinds;
- Decision as accountable human judgment;
- source-owned facts, records, and mutation authority;
- AI and Management OS as non-authoritative assistance;
- a bounded-contribution model rather than an automatic causal claim;
- no mandatory single Root Cause; and
- Operational Diagnosis only as research framing for a possible composition of
  existing responsibilities, not a canonical object.

The term **framework** means a research structure for disciplined comparison.
It does not create an operational tool, form, workflow, protocol, role,
repository record type, or implementation requirement.

## 3. Assessment Question

The assessment question is:

> Does repository-visible and source-attributable evidence show that this
> candidate scenario is sufficiently bounded, observable, contestable, and
> authority-preserving to be presented to a separate authority for possible
> validation-preparation consideration?

The assessment does not ask whether:

- the scenario will produce a positive Outcome;
- Management OS creates value in the scenario;
- a validation should be approved;
- the scenario should be selected over another candidate;
- a prototype or implementation should be built;
- an M8.5 alternative should be chosen; or
- Engineering Architecture is ready or authorized.

Suitability is about whether the value proposition could be investigated
credibly. It is not evidence that the proposition is true.

## 4. Unit and Scope of Assessment

The unit is one described real-world candidate scenario, bounded by:

- operational condition and material consequence;
- accountable human Decision owner;
- affected team, process, or operational responsibility;
- relevant source owners;
- possible whole-loop scope;
- plausible source-owned Change boundary;
- intended operational condition and observation horizon;
- comparison opportunity;
- material external factors; and
- maximum conclusion that a future inquiry might support.

This description is a research input, not a new canonical scenario artifact.
If the boundary changes materially, prior assessment conclusions should not be
silently reused for the changed scenario.

## 5. Assessment Principles

### 5.1 Evidence before preference

Assessment should begin from attributable evidence of the candidate condition,
not from a favored solution, participant enthusiasm, available tooling, or an
expected positive result.

### 5.2 Claim-specific sufficiency

Evidence is sufficient only for a stated dimension and bounded claim. Strong
Evidence for recurrence does not establish Outcome observability. Strong
ownership evidence does not establish Change reversibility. No dimension is
validated by document or data volume alone.

### 5.3 Non-substitution

Some dimensions are non-substitutable. A scenario with no genuine human
Decision cannot compensate through excellent Outcome data. A scenario with no
observable Outcome cannot compensate through rich Evidence. A scenario with
ambiguous mutation authority cannot compensate through strong recurrence.

### 5.4 Contrary and missing evidence

Assessment must retain evidence that weakens suitability, including missing
sources, ambiguous ownership, non-comparable instances, irreversible exposure,
confounders, and observation limits. Uncertainty is not resolved by confidence.

### 5.5 Human judgment without hidden authority

Interpretation is unavoidable. The assessor should explain why evidence supports
or fails to support each dimension, preserve alternatives, and state limits.
Judgment must not be hidden behind an aggregate score, model output, checklist
completion, or apparent mathematical precision.

### 5.6 Case-bounded conclusion

Any assessment statement applies only to the fixed candidate scenario and
available evidence. It does not generalize to its scenario class, Management OS,
or another operational context.

## 6. Research Assessment Expressions

For clarity, a dimension may be described using one of four research expressions:

| Expression | Research meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Attributable evidence is sufficient to support the dimension for the fixed candidate scope, with limitations visible |
| Conditionally supported | Evidence supports the dimension only if stated non-authorizing conditions remain true; the conditions do not become tasks |
| Unsupported | Sufficient evidence shows that the candidate does not meet the dimension or violates a non-substitutable boundary |
| Indeterminate | Available evidence is missing, conflicting, semantically invalid, inaccessible, or insufficient for judgment |

These expressions are research vocabulary only. They are not canonical statuses,
readiness classes, review dispositions, lifecycle states, or authority gates.

No expression should be converted into a number. “Conditionally supported” does
not mean partial approval, and “Indeterminate” must not be treated as weak
support.

## 7. Assessment Dimensions

### 7.1 Operational reality and materiality

Although not one of the seven primary comparison dimensions, every candidate
first needs a real and material subject.

**Research question:** Does an operational condition exist independently of the
assessment, and does its consequence matter to an accountable owner?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- source-owned observations of the current or recurring condition;
- timing, scope, affected operation, and consequence;
- prior instances or operational trajectory;
- owner evidence of materiality; and
- evidence that the scenario was not created solely for validation.

**Weakness indicators:** synthetic or modeled scenario only, documentation
convenience as the sole benefit, no material current/intended-condition
difference, or a scenario chosen after favorable results appeared.

If no real material condition exists, the candidate cannot test operational
value.

### 7.2 Bounded ownership

**Research question:** Are Decision accountability, operational scope, source
ownership, affected parties, and possible mutation authority explicit enough to
preserve human and source authority throughout the loop?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- one identifiable accountable human Decision owner;
- a bounded team, process, or operational responsibility;
- identified sources that own relevant facts and records;
- an identified source owner for any prospective Change;
- visible consultation, affected participants, and external authority; and
- evidence that observers, Management OS, and AI remain non-authoritative.

**Contrary or limiting evidence may include:**

- distributed accountability with no final human Decision owner;
- competing or hidden sources of truth;
- ambiguous authority to make or reverse Change;
- a boundary spanning several independent authority domains;
- reliance on AI or the assessment itself to decide; or
- affected participants or constraints excluded from the scope.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** a candidate is unsuitable when Decision or
mutation ownership cannot be established without inventing new authority.

### 7.3 Evidence availability

**Research question:** Can sufficient, attributable, contestable, and
semantically valid Evidence exist before Decision and after Change for the fixed
value claim?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- pre-Decision source observations and records;
- provenance, time, scope, correction, and limitations;
- contrary, negative, and missing Evidence;
- a credible path to post-Change operational Observation;
- evidence about Decision basis and actual Change; and
- source material available for independent inspection where applicable.

**Insufficient evidence includes:**

- participant confidence or retrospective story alone;
- activity volume;
- AI output or modeled result;
- inaccessible sources whose meaning cannot be checked;
- data selected only after Outcome;
- Recommendation acceptance;
- execution completion; or
- a favorable condition with no trace to Decision and Change.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** a candidate is unsuitable when material claims
would depend on assertion, semantic promotion, inaccessible Evidence, or removal
of contrary material.

### 7.4 Human Decision presence

**Research question:** Does the scenario require an accountable human to choose
among plausible responses under uncertainty, including no-action, delay,
revision, interruption, or reversal?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- more than one defensible response;
- material uncertainty and tradeoffs;
- Evidence that could alter judgment;
- an accountable owner whose intent and bounds can be observed; and
- separation of Recommendation, modeled alternatives, and Decision.

**Weakness indicators:** predetermined rule, automatic response, administrative
completion, no meaningful alternative, or de facto Decision by AI, interface,
group consensus, or assessor.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** without genuine human judgment, the scenario may
test automation or execution but not the bounded Management OS value hypothesis.

### 7.5 Change reversibility and traceability

**Research question:** Is one prospective source-owned Change plausibly bounded,
distinguishable, interruptible, correctable, and responsibly reversible?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- one identifiable intervention surface;
- clear source ownership;
- plausible trace from Decision intent to actual Change;
- ability to observe deviations and concurrent Changes;
- limited blast radius and bounded consequence; and
- possibility of interruption, correction, or reversal where appropriate.

**Weakness indicators:** irreversible or safety-critical action, several
inseparable interventions, organization-wide change, hidden mutation, or Change
predetermined merely to create a validation scenario.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** a first candidate is unsuitable when responsible
exposure or source ownership cannot be preserved.

This dimension does not approve or define the Change. It assesses only whether a
future human Decision could lead to an observable bounded intervention.

### 7.6 Outcome observability

**Research question:** Can a material intended operational condition and
justified observation horizon be stated before results, and can attributable
Observation or source-owned Evidence support owned verification?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- current and intended condition candidates;
- a primary Outcome dimension relevant to the accountable human;
- source-owned post-Change observations;
- a justified near- or medium-term horizon;
- an accountable verification owner;
- possible adverse, delayed, displaced, and null effects; and
- separation of Outcome from activity, completion, satisfaction, and model
  output.

**Weakness indicators:** Outcome chosen after observation, subjective confidence
as the only measure, distant or unstable horizon, no verification owner, or only
execution completion being visible.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** without observable and owned consequence, the
candidate cannot test value even if every upstream activity is recorded.

### 7.7 Comparison feasibility

**Research question:** Is there a credible basis for judging whether the
operational condition differed, while preserving context and uncertainty?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- attributable initial condition and trajectory;
- repeated pre-Change observations;
- meaningfully comparable prior instances;
- a naturally available contemporaneous comparison;
- a pre-stated intended range or threshold; or
- a structured counterfactual with explicit assumptions.

**Weakness indicators:** no reference beyond expectation, incomparable prior
cases, changed measurement meaning, unknown exposure, comparison selected after
Outcome, or historical data dominated by other changes.

Comparison does not establish causality. It bounds the difference and tests
whether the observed condition exceeds expected variation.

### 7.8 Attribution feasibility and bounded contribution

**Research question:** Could evidence distinguish a plausible Management OS
contribution from mere usage, temporal sequence, external change, and concurrent
intervention?

**Supporting evidence may include a trace from:**

```text
Initial condition
  → Evidence changed or confirmed Understanding
  → explicit human Decision
  → distinguishable source-owned Change
  → observed operational difference
  → comparison and alternative explanations
  → owned contribution judgment with uncertainty
```

**Material alternative explanations may include:** manager knowledge outside
Management OS, prior work, natural recovery, regression toward typical
conditions, participant attention, novelty, staffing or policy change, market
conditions, and other interventions.

**Non-substitutable boundary:** attribution is impossible when actual Change is
unknown, material confounders dominate, no comparison exists, or only usage and
Outcome co-occurrence connect Management OS to the condition.

The assessment must not infer “Management OS caused the Outcome.” At most, it
asks whether a future bounded contribution judgment could be investigated.

### 7.9 Learning potential

**Research question:** Is there a credible later opportunity to examine whether
verified experience improves judgment or Change in a meaningfully comparable
situation?

**Supporting evidence may include:**

- recurrent or comparable future situations;
- preserved context, provenance, and correction history;
- an accountable later human Decision;
- ability to observe reuse, rejection, or revision of prior learning; and
- ability to compare later judgment or consequence without treating similarity
  as authority.

**Weakness indicators:** unique event, no later Decision opportunity, a stored
lesson as the only signal, or reuse that would erase differences in context and
Evidence.

Learning potential may be conditionally supported when a later comparable event
is plausible but not guaranteed. It cannot establish current value or replace
Outcome verification.

### 7.10 Burden, harm, and confounding

This cross-cutting dimension tests whether assessment and possible future
validation remain interpretable and proportionate.

**Research question:** Can material burden, adverse effect, external change,
selection effect, novelty, and measurement distortion remain visible enough to
bound the claim?

**Weakness indicators include:** safety-critical exposure, coercion, privacy or
employment harm, excessive observation burden, organization-wide disruption,
several concurrent initiatives, measure gaming, or a novelty effect likely to
dominate the result.

The framework does not define risk controls. It rejects the idea that value
evidence should be obtained through disproportionate or uninterpretable
exposure.

## 8. Assessment Method

The following is a research reasoning method, not a lifecycle or governance
process.

### 8.1 Fix the candidate description

Describe the operational condition, material consequence, owner, scope,
sources, recurrence, possible Decision, possible Change boundary, intended
condition, horizon, comparison, and known external factors without prescribing
a solution.

### 8.2 State the suitability claim

State what the assessment is evaluating:

> Available evidence is or is not sufficient to present this fixed candidate to
> a separate authority for possible validation-preparation consideration.

The claim must not become “the candidate should be validated.”

### 8.3 Gather dimension-specific evidence

For each dimension, identify:

- supporting Evidence;
- contrary Evidence;
- missing or inaccessible Evidence;
- semantic and authority limits;
- material assumptions;
- affected scope; and
- alternative interpretations.

The same source may support one dimension and be insufficient for another.

### 8.4 Apply non-substitutable boundaries first

Before broader interpretation, identify whether any candidate lacks:

- real material Operational Reality;
- bounded human Decision authority;
- source-owned Change authority;
- semantically valid Evidence;
- observable and owned Outcome;
- responsible exposure; or
- any plausible attribution path.

A demonstrated failure of one of these boundaries should be recorded directly.
It must not be averaged away.

### 8.5 Form qualitative dimension judgments

Use Supported, Conditionally supported, Unsupported, or Indeterminate only with
an evidence explanation. Record why the evidence is sufficient or insufficient,
which limitations remain, and what conclusion is prohibited.

### 8.6 Examine cross-dimension coherence

Assess whether the supported dimensions compose. Examples of incoherence
include:

- good Outcome data with no traceable Decision or Change;
- clear ownership but inaccessible Evidence;
- reversible Change but no material consequence;
- recurrence without comparability;
- human Decision whose intent cannot reach the source-owned Change; or
- Learning opportunity based only on surface similarity.

Integrated suitability cannot be inferred by counting supported dimensions.

### 8.7 Preserve alternative conclusions

At minimum, preserve these possible research conclusions:

- evidence supports presenting the candidate for separate authority
  consideration;
- evidence supports presentation only with explicit unresolved conditions;
- evidence demonstrates that the candidate is unsuitable; or
- evidence is indeterminate and cannot support suitability judgment.

These conclusions are not review dispositions or authority outcomes.

### 8.8 Record maximum permitted inference

Every assessment should state what it does not show: value, causality,
validation approval, implementation readiness, or generalization.

## 9. Why Automatic Scoring Is Inappropriate

### 9.1 Scores conceal non-substitution

A high total could hide absence of human Decision, source authority, Outcome, or
attribution. These boundaries cannot be compensated by recurrence, data volume,
or convenience.

### 9.2 Weights create hidden policy

Assigning numerical weights decides which values matter most. That would embed
an unreviewed priority or governance judgment inside an apparently neutral
calculation.

### 9.3 Precision exceeds evidence

Scenario evidence is contextual, incomplete, and partly interpretive. A score
such as 78/100 would imply comparability and precision not supported by the
evidence.

### 9.4 Ranking can become selection

Ordering candidates by score may silently select a scenario before an
authorized human considers material differences, adverse effects, and
uncertainty.

### 9.5 Model-assisted scoring can acquire authority

AI or an analytical model may summarize Evidence or expose inconsistencies. It
cannot decide weights, resolve ambiguity, rank suitability, or promote its
confidence into Decision authority.

### 9.6 Appropriate comparison form

Candidate comparison should use a qualitative evidence matrix with narrative
judgments, non-substitutable failures, uncertainties, and cross-dimension
relationships visible. The assessor may compare candidates but should not
declare a winner.

## 10. Human Judgment Boundary

Human judgment is required to interpret materiality, Evidence sufficiency,
comparability, burden, confounding, and the significance of uncertainty.

The assessor's judgment must remain:

- explicit rather than hidden in a score;
- traceable to Evidence;
- open to contrary interpretation;
- bounded to the fixed candidate;
- distinct from Recommendation and selection;
- non-authoritative regarding validation; and
- unable to transfer Decision authority from the separate governing authority.

Management OS or AI may organize sources, compare stated dimensions, locate
missing information, or draft a bounded summary. It may not decide suitability,
rank candidates, select a scenario, approve validation, or infer authority from
confidence or consistency.

## 11. Rejection Conditions

### 11.1 Candidate is unsuitable when

- no real material operational condition exists;
- the scenario is too simple to require Investigation and human judgment;
- the scope is strategic, organization-wide, or dominated by multiple owners;
- human Decision accountability is absent or ambiguous;
- relevant facts or Change authority cannot remain source-owned;
- the possible Change is safety-critical, disproportionate, irreversible, or
  inseparable from other interventions;
- Outcome cannot be pre-stated, observed, or owned for verification;
- only activity, satisfaction, execution completion, or model output can be
  measured;
- no credible comparison is possible;
- external factors dominate the likely result;
- attribution could rely only on Management OS usage followed by Outcome;
- contrary or adverse Evidence cannot remain visible; or
- validation would require new canonical meaning, authority, lifecycle, or
  implementation merely to make the case testable.

### 11.2 Evidence is insufficient when

- source identity, time, scope, provenance, or semantic meaning is unclear;
- material Evidence is inaccessible or retrospective only;
- the candidate description depends mainly on assertion or participant belief;
- recurrence is claimed without comparable exposure or context;
- only favorable or completed cases are visible;
- the intended Outcome or threshold would be chosen after observation;
- actual Change cannot be distinguished from Decision intent;
- missing, conflicting, null, negative, or adverse evidence is hidden;
- data quantity substitutes for relevance and completeness; or
- evidence supports one dimension but is generalized to integrated suitability.

Insufficient Evidence produces an Indeterminate dimension judgment, unless
sufficient evidence affirmatively demonstrates unsuitability.

### 11.3 Outcome attribution is impossible when

- no initial condition or trajectory exists;
- no attributable human Decision can be identified;
- actual source-owned Change is unknown;
- several material Changes are inseparable;
- observation sources or measurement meanings change incompatibly;
- the observation horizon cannot be justified;
- natural variation or external change overwhelms the claimed difference;
- no alternative explanations can be examined; or
- the only connection is sequence, correlation, usage, confidence, or narrative
  coherence.

When attribution is impossible, Outcome may still be observed. The candidate
cannot support the bounded Management OS contribution question.

## 12. Cross-Dimension Failure Patterns

| Pattern | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence-rich but decision-poor | Tests analysis or information availability, not accountable human judgment |
| Decision-rich but outcome-blind | Tests reasoning and choice, not operational consequence |
| Outcome-visible but change-opaque | Cannot connect Decision or intervention to observed condition |
| Recurrent but incomparable | Surface repetition does not support a baseline or Learning claim |
| Reversible but trivial | Responsible exposure exists, but management value is not materially tested |
| Material but strategic | Consequence matters, but scope and confounders prevent bounded attribution |
| Measurable but proxy-dominated | Activity or convenient metrics replace the intended operational condition |
| AI-assisted but authority-ambiguous | A favorable result would not preserve accountable human Decision |
| Complete narrative but contrary-evidence poor | Coherence becomes confirmation rather than falsifiable assessment |
| Strong immediate Outcome but no durable observation | Novelty, temporary stabilization, or displaced harm may be mistaken for value |

## 13. Assessment Output Boundary

A research assessment may record:

- the fixed candidate description;
- the evidence boundary;
- dimension-level qualitative judgments and rationale;
- non-substitutable failures;
- cross-dimension coherence or contradiction;
- limitations, assumptions, contrary Evidence, and unknowns;
- possible bias and attribution threats;
- maximum permitted inference; and
- a non-authoritative recommendation about whether the candidate is sufficiently
  evidenced to be presented for separate authority consideration.

It must not record:

- scenario selection;
- validation approval;
- validation plan or execution instructions;
- prototype or implementation requirements;
- Engineering Architecture readiness;
- product priority or delivery commitment;
- canonical status or new object identity;
- a new governance disposition; or
- a conclusion that Management OS creates value.

## 14. Decision Boundary

### Assessment is not selection

Assessment describes evidence and suitability limits for one fixed candidate.
Selection chooses among candidates or chooses to proceed. This framework does
not perform selection.

### Comparison is not ranking

Comparison exposes material differences. It does not aggregate them into an
order of preference or make tradeoffs on behalf of an accountable authority.

### Recommendation is not authorization

A recommendation may state that evidence supports presenting a candidate for
consideration. It cannot authorize preparation, validation, access, participation,
Change, prototype, or implementation.

### Suitability is not validation approval

Suitability means the scenario appears capable of supporting a credible bounded
inquiry. Approval would require a distinct authority decision under applicable
governance. Suitability does not show that value exists or that validation will
succeed.

### Validation approval would not be execution

Even if a later authority approved preparation or validation, that decision
would remain distinct from actual execution, operational Decision, source-owned
Change, Outcome verification, and any subsequent transition.

### Positive Outcome is not causal authority

A favorable Outcome supports only the bounded claim justified by the Evidence.
It does not prove unique causality, validate all of Management OS, or authorize
architecture and implementation.

## 15. Conditions for Separate Authority Consideration

Before an authority could responsibly decide the next step, the following
conditions should exist for at least one fixed candidate.

### 15.1 Complete assessment evidence

- candidate scope and identity are stable enough for judgment;
- each primary dimension has supporting, contrary, and missing Evidence;
- non-substitutable boundaries are explicitly evaluated;
- cross-dimension coherence is examined;
- confounders, bias, burden, and harm remain visible; and
- the result is traceable without relying on an aggregate score.

### 15.2 Bounded suitability conclusion

- the conclusion is Supported, Conditionally supported, Unsupported, or
  Indeterminate in research terms;
- rationale and limitations are explicit;
- any conditions preserve non-authority and do not become hidden tasks;
- maximum permitted inference is stated; and
- no value or causal result is claimed.

### 15.3 Preserved authority

- accountable human Decision ownership is identified;
- source ownership of facts, records, and possible Change is clear;
- Management OS, AI, and assessor remain non-authoritative;
- participation and exposure can remain responsibly bounded under existing
  authority; and
- the authority may reject every candidate or request further evidence without
  creating work automatically.

### 15.4 Falsifiable future proposition

- intended operational condition and possible Outcome dimension can be stated;
- a credible comparison is feasible;
- attribution alternatives can be examined;
- negative, null, interrupted, and indeterminate results are legitimate;
- the full loop can reach owned Outcome verification and possible later
  Learning; and
- no mandatory Root Cause or canonical Diagnosis object is required.

### 15.5 Separate transition boundary

The evidence package should make clear which decision is being requested:

- whether candidate identification should continue;
- whether a candidate is eligible for validation-preparation consideration;
- whether further research is needed; or
- whether no candidate is currently suitable.

It should not combine scenario selection, validation approval, execution,
prototype, Engineering Architecture, implementation, or delivery into one
decision.

## 16. Recommendation

The completed research is sufficient to define how candidate scenarios could be
assessed qualitatively before an authority decision. The next justified
consideration is not to run validation or select a scenario automatically. It is
to determine, through separate authority, whether candidate-scenario assessment
should be authorized as a bounded evidence activity.

If such assessment is later authorized, it should:

1. examine more than one candidate where possible;
2. use the same disclosed dimensions without numerical ranking;
3. preserve non-substitutable rejection conditions;
4. record supporting, contrary, missing, and conflicting Evidence;
5. retain Unsupported and Indeterminate conclusions;
6. identify whether attribution can be investigated rather than assumed;
7. return a recommendation for authority consideration only; and
8. stop before selection, preparation, or validation execution unless a later
   distinct decision explicitly authorizes the next bounded action.

For a separate authority to decide the next step responsibly, at least one
candidate assessment should demonstrate bounded ownership, genuine human
Decision, sufficient Evidence availability, a responsibly reversible and
source-owned possible Change, observable Outcome, credible comparison, bounded
attribution feasibility, and plausible Learning potential. It should also show
that burden, harm, and confounding do not make the inquiry irresponsible or
uninterpretable.

This recommendation is research interpretation only. It creates no request,
order, active task, approval, or entitlement to proceed.
