# Candidate Operational Loop Consideration Brief

## Document Status

```text
Research-based decision support only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No candidate selection, ranking, approval, validation authorization, or work authorization
```

## 1. Purpose

This brief organizes research evidence for future accountable-human
consideration of possible bounded operational loop candidates. It summarizes
eight candidate classes, the evidence and Outcome visibility each may offer,
their attribution challenges, and the facts and questions required before any
real candidate could be considered for selection.

The brief does not select a class or scenario, recommend a winner, approve
validation preparation or execution, or create a milestone, M9, backlog item,
prototype, Engineering Architecture, implementation requirement, or delivery
commitment.

This supporting document synthesizes six non-authoritative research papers:

Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged. Outcome remains the final
consequence test. Learning remains downstream of attributable experience and
verified Outcome. Human Decision and source ownership remain controlling.

## 3. Semantic and Authority Guardrails

- Observation records a perceived or measured condition within a source and
  scope; it is not automatically Evidence for every claim.
- Evidence supports or contradicts a declared question with provenance and
  limitations; it is not Interpretation.
- Interpretation relates Evidence into possible meaning; it is not Fact.
- Modeled / Scenario Output explores possible conditions; it is not Evidence,
  Observation, Fact, Prediction, Recommendation, Decision, or Outcome.
- Recommendation advises; it is not Decision or authorization.
- Decision remains accountable human judgment. Management OS and AI do not
  decide.
- Change remains owned by the applicable operational source.
- Outcome requires attributable Observation or source-owned Evidence and owned
  verification judgment; execution completion does not establish consequence.
- Learning does not become truth or authority through storage, reuse, confidence,
  or similarity.
- Management OS does not require one Root Cause.
- Operational Diagnosis remains research framing for a possible composition of
  existing responsibilities, not a canonical object.

## 4. Decision Boundaries

```text
Assessment ≠ Selection
Comparison ≠ Ranking
Recommendation ≠ Authorization
Suitability ≠ Validation Approval
```

In addition:

- class-level potential is not case-level suitability;
- Evidence availability is not Evidence of value;
- scenario selection would not approve validation;
- validation approval would not execute validation;
- positive Outcome would not prove unique causality; and
- a bounded contribution result would not authorize architecture or
  implementation.

## 5. Shared Whole-Loop Subject

Every candidate must be capable of exercising a real, bounded consequence chain:

```text
Operational Reality
  → Matter / Context Continuity
  → Investigation
  → Evidence
  → Understanding
  → Human Decision
  → Source-Owned Change
  → Observed Outcome and Verification
  → Learning
```

This is an analytical trace, not a lifecycle or product workflow. A candidate
that ends at analysis, Recommendation, Decision, or execution completion cannot
test the complete operational value proposition.

## 7. Class Consideration Cards

### 7.1 Recurrent process deviation

**Potential value visibility:** whether preserving context and Evidence improves
human judgment about a recurring departure and produces sustained operational
improvement rather than procedural completion.

**Expected Evidence:** source-owned normal and deviating conditions, recurrence
and exposure, context changes, competing explanations, Decision intent, actual
Change, post-Change condition, adverse effects, and verification.

**Possible Outcome:** material stabilization, reduced comparable recurrence or
severity, reduced rework or burden, or improved correction.

**Attribution challenges:** natural return to normal, changed inputs or volume,
simultaneous corrections, monitoring effects, and an inappropriate standard.

**Suitability questions:**

- Is the response genuinely contestable rather than predetermined compliance?
- Is the intended condition operationally meaningful beyond process conformance?
- Are recurrence and exposure meaningfully comparable?
- Can one Change be separated from other corrections?

### 7.2 Coordination breakdown

**Potential value visibility:** whether Matter continuity, semantic handoffs,
and explicit ownership improve a material downstream consequence.

**Expected Evidence:** records before and after the handoff, timing, missing or
contradictory context, ownership, participant observations, Decision, Change,
and downstream condition.

**Possible Outcome:** less material context loss, avoidable delay, rework,
interruption, ownership failure, or downstream degradation.

**Attribution challenges:** novelty, leadership attention, changed workload,
staffing, relationship dynamics, self-report, and movement of the bottleneck.

**Suitability questions:**

- Can one accountable Decision owner be identified despite distributed work?
- Does source evidence exist beyond participant belief?
- Is the downstream consequence close enough to observe?
- Can a bounded coordination Change remain reversible and source-owned?

### 7.3 Quality variation

**Potential value visibility:** whether evidence-backed understanding and human
tradeoffs reduce consequential variation without shifting harm elsewhere.

**Expected Evidence:** source-owned quality observations, definition history,
input and context variation, corrections, hypotheses, Decision tradeoffs, actual
Change, quality distribution, burden, delay, and adverse consequences.

**Possible Outcome:** sustained improvement in the intended quality condition,
fewer consequential defects, reduced correction cycles, or maintained quality
with lower burden.

**Attribution challenges:** changed measurement, detection intensity, proxy
optimization, input mix, delayed feedback, and displaced cost or risk.

**Suitability questions:**

- Is quality meaning source-owned and stable enough for comparison?
- Are balancing conditions observable?
- Does the case require genuine judgment rather than automatic inspection?
- Can delayed or displaced consequences remain visible?

### 7.4 Stabilization problem

**Potential value visibility:** whether the loop contributes to faster, more
durable stabilization with proportionate management effort.

**Expected Evidence:** operational trajectory, instability and recovery history,
workload, capacity, external factors, Investigation, Decision, actual Change,
stabilization duration, recurrence, and side effects.

**Possible Outcome:** sustained acceptable condition, shorter stabilization,
lower intervention burden, reduced severity or recurrence, or improved later
correction.

**Attribution challenges:** natural recovery, regression, concurrent containment,
demand change, hidden degraded state, and premature observation.

**Suitability questions:**

- Is “stable” a meaningful source-owned condition rather than a convenient
  metric?
- Can natural variability and recovery be estimated?
- Can one intervention be distinguished?
- Is the case non-safety-critical and responsibly bounded?

### 7.5 Operational failure recovery

**Potential value visibility:** whether the complete loop supports accountable
recovery and verified restoration without collapsing into blame or one Cause.

**Expected Evidence:** intended result, failure condition, timeline, prior
signals, Evidence and hypotheses, Decision, source-owned recovery Change,
restoration, residual degradation, recurrence, and later learning.

**Possible Outcome:** restored operational condition, shorter responsible
recovery, reduced residual harm or recurrence, and improved later response.

**Attribution challenges:** urgency, multiple simultaneous actions, external
support, retrospective reconstruction, Root Cause pressure, and selection of only
successful recoveries.

**Suitability questions:**

- Is consequence moderate enough for a first bounded inquiry?
- Can recovery actions and external support be separated?
- Is Outcome more than task closure or immediate restoration?
- Can null, failed, interrupted, and adverse recovery evidence be retained?

### 7.6 Decision-latency condition

**Potential value visibility:** whether preserved context and Evidence improve
timeliness of adequate human judgment and a material downstream condition.

**Expected Evidence:** signal-to-Decision timing, waits, interruptions, context
loss, Evidence gaps, alternatives, Decision intent, Change, downstream Outcome,
correction, quality, and burden.

**Possible Outcome:** faster sufficiently supported Decision without degraded
quality, hidden risk, or downstream harm, followed by improved operational
consequence.

**Attribution challenges:** case complexity, urgency, leadership attention,
external approval, speed-quality tradeoff, and distant Outcome.

**Suitability questions:**

- Is latency avoidable and operationally consequential?
- Can adequate Decision quality be observed alongside speed?
- Is a downstream Outcome available within the horizon?
- Are cases comparable in complexity and urgency?

### 7.7 Recurring exception/rework loop

**Potential value visibility:** whether the loop reduces avoidable repeated work
and context loss while preserving legitimate variation and quality.

**Expected Evidence:** exception history, original and revised outputs, context
and handoff records, operational burden, Decision, Change, post-Change exception
pattern, quality, delay, and side effects.

**Possible Outcome:** less avoidable rework, faster adequate correction, reduced
burden, improved downstream quality, or better distinction between legitimate
exception and deviation.

**Attribution challenges:** reporting changes, hidden rework, changed input
complexity, weaker detection, moved burden, and excessive standardization.

**Suitability questions:**

- Does the case require human judgment rather than routine correction?
- Is rework connected to a material operational condition?
- Can hidden and displaced work be observed?
- Are output complexity and exposure comparable?

### 7.8 Resource-contention condition

**Potential value visibility:** whether accountable evidence-backed tradeoffs
improve a bounded operating condition under constraint without hiding displaced
harm.

**Expected Evidence:** source-owned demand, capacity, waits, workload,
constraints, consequences, alternatives, allocation Decision, actual Change,
downstream and displaced Outcomes.

**Possible Outcome:** lower material delay or overload, improved intended
condition, better correction, or reduced displaced harm.

**Attribution challenges:** demand variability, shifted burden, broad affected
parties, concurrent capacity change, and confusion with priority or delivery
authority.

**Suitability questions:**

- Are authority and affected scope truly bounded?
- Can displaced burden and balancing effects be observed?
- Is the Change reversible despite downstream commitments?
- Can the inquiry avoid becoming product prioritization or delivery planning?

## 8. Facts Required Before Any Selection

Class descriptions are insufficient. Every real candidate requires attributable
facts in the following areas.

### 8.1 Operational condition

- real source-observed condition independent of the consideration activity;
- current and intended condition;
- material consequence and affected scope;
- recurrence history, exposure, and variability; and
- reason the candidate entered consideration.

### 8.2 Ownership and authority

- accountable human Decision owner;
- owners of material facts and records;
- owner of any possible Change;
- affected participants and external authority; and
- evidence that AI, Management OS, and observers remain non-authoritative.

### 8.3 Evidence availability

- available pre-Decision sources and limitations;
- contrary, negative, null, adverse, and missing Evidence;
- post-Change Observation sources;
- provenance, time, correction, scope, and semantic meaning; and
- access, inspectability, privacy, and measurement limitations under existing
  applicable authority.

### 8.4 Human Decision

- plausible alternatives and material tradeoffs;
- uncertainty capable of affecting judgment;
- no-action, delay, revision, interruption, or reversal options; and
- separation of modeled output, Recommendation, and Decision.

### 8.5 Change feasibility

- prospective source-owned Change surface;
- boundedness, reversibility, correction, and blast radius;
- concurrent interventions;
- potential harm or displaced effect; and
- evidence that the Change is not predetermined to make the case appear valid.

### 8.6 Outcome and comparison

- possible primary operational Outcome and balancing conditions;
- verification owner and source-owned observations;
- justified observation horizon;
- baseline, trajectory, comparable instances, contemporaneous comparison, or
  pre-stated intended range;
- differences in exposure and context; and
- conditions for positive, negative, null, adverse, interrupted, and
  indeterminate results.

### 8.7 Attribution and Learning

- plausible trace from Evidence and Understanding through human Decision and
  actual Change;
- external factors, prior manager knowledge, and concurrent action;
- selection, confirmation, novelty, measurement, and regression risks;
- maximum bounded contribution claim; and
- credible later comparable situation for observing use, correction, rejection,
  or non-use of Learning.

These are decision-support facts, not a canonical schema or data requirement.

## 9. Questions for the Accountable Human

Before any selection, the accountable human should answer:

### Value and boundary

1. Which real operational condition is being considered, and why does it matter?
2. What bounded management-capacity change is hypothesized?
3. What material Outcome would test consequence rather than activity?
4. What is explicitly outside the candidate scope?

### Ownership and judgment

5. Who owns the Decision?
6. Which sources own facts, records, and possible Change?
7. Does the situation require genuine human judgment among plausible responses?
8. Can Management OS, AI, and the assessor remain non-authoritative?

### Evidence

9. Which pre-existing Evidence supports the candidate's materiality and
   recurrence?
10. Which Evidence is contrary, missing, inaccessible, or semantically limited?
11. Can Evidence remain distinct from Interpretation, model output,
    Recommendation, Decision, and Outcome?
12. Can negative and indeterminate evidence be retained without pressure for
    success?

### Change and exposure

13. Can one prospective Change remain source-owned, distinguishable, bounded,
    and responsibly reversible?
14. Which simultaneous Changes or external events threaten interpretation?
15. Could participation, observation, or Change create disproportionate harm,
    coercion, privacy loss, or authority distortion?

### Outcome and attribution

16. Can Outcome and horizon be fixed before observation?
17. What comparison is credible, and where does comparability fail?
18. Which alternative explanations could account for the Outcome?
19. What evidence could support only association, plausible contribution,
    material contribution, or no attribution?
20. What result would falsify or leave the value claim indeterminate?

### Learning and decision request

21. Is a later comparable situation available to test Learning?
22. How will prior experience remain contextual and challengeable?
23. Is the requested action assessment, selection, preparation, approval, or
    execution—and are those decisions kept separate?
24. Can the responsible authority conclude that no candidate should proceed?

## 10. Candidate Unsuitability Conditions

A candidate should not be considered suitable for whole-loop validation when:

- no real material operational condition exists;
- the case is too simple to require Investigation and human Decision;
- the case is strategic, organization-wide, or dominated by many authority
  domains;
- Decision or mutation ownership is ambiguous;
- material Evidence is inaccessible, retrospective-only, or semantically invalid;
- contrary, adverse, or missing Evidence cannot remain visible;
- the possible Change is safety-critical, disproportionate, irreversible, or
  inseparable from other interventions;
- Outcome cannot be pre-stated, observed, and owned for verification;
- only activity, satisfaction, completion, or modeled effect can be observed;
- no credible comparison exists;
- external factors dominate the likely result;
- attribution would rely only on Management OS use followed by Outcome;
- Learning could be represented only by storing a lesson;
- the candidate was chosen because favorable results were already known; or
- making the candidate testable would require new canonical meaning, governance,
  lifecycle, authority, prototype, or implementation.

Insufficient Evidence should produce uncertainty, not favorable inference. A
candidate may remain unsuitable or indeterminate even when its class appears
promising.

## 11. Human Consideration View

A future human comparison should place real candidates side by side using
narrative Evidence for:

- bounded ownership;
- Evidence availability;
- human Decision presence;
- Change reversibility;
- Outcome observability;
- comparison feasibility;
- attribution feasibility;
- Learning potential;
- burden, harm, and confounding; and
- maximum permitted inference.

The view should show supporting, contrary, and missing Evidence and any
non-substitutable failure. It should not calculate a total, rank candidates, or
declare a winner.

Human judgment should remain explicit, evidence-backed, challengeable, and
separate from authority. AI may help organize or summarize stated evidence but
may not weight dimensions, rank classes, select a scenario, or approve a next
step.

## 12. Use Boundary

This brief may support a future human conversation by making classes, evidence
needs, questions, and exclusions visible. It does not itself establish that:

- candidate identification is authorized;
- any real candidate is suitable;
- one class should be preferred;
- validation preparation or execution should begin;
- a prototype is necessary;
- Engineering Architecture or implementation is ready; or
- a successor milestone should exist.

Any later assessment, selection, recommendation, approval, preparation, and
execution would remain distinct acts under separate applicable authority.
