# Operational Loop Validation Protocol — Architectural Research

## Research Status

```text
Research evidence only
Implementation-independent, non-authoritative, and non-normative
No validation execution, governance process, milestone, backlog, prototype, or work authorization
```

## 1. Purpose

This paper investigates principles that could make a future bounded operational
loop validation observable, falsifiable, semantically coherent, and resistant
to false value claims. It does not start, design in implementation detail, or
authorize such a validation.

The word **protocol** in the title means a research organization of analytical
principles. It does not establish a canonical lifecycle, mandatory sequence,
governance procedure, review gate, role, status model, handoff protocol, or
execution method.

This research follows:

1. **Bounded Operational Value and Evidence Proposition — Architectural
   Research**, which identifies value-and-effect validity as the principal
   question and recommends considering one bounded whole-loop validation; and
2. **Bounded Operational Loop Selection and Validation Criteria — Architectural
   Research**, which identifies a recurrent, material, non-safety-critical,
   evidence-rich, reversible, and observable operational deviation as the most
   suitable first loop type.

Neither earlier paper is canonical or authorizing. Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7
remain unchanged and controlling within their existing scopes.

## 2. Preserved Premises

This research preserves the following boundaries:

- Management OS is not a system whose required purpose is discovery of one Root
  Cause.
- Operational Diagnosis remains research framing for a possible composition of
  existing responsibilities; it is not a canonical object.
- Observation, Evidence, Interpretation, hypothesis, Modeled / Scenario Output,
  Recommendation, Decision, Change record, Outcome, and Learning remain
  semantically non-equivalent.
- Decision remains explicit accountable human judgment. Management OS and AI do
  not decide.
- Mutation remains with the applicable source owner.
- Outcome verification is the final consequence test for claimed operational
  effect. Execution completion and modeled effect do not establish Outcome.
- Learning follows attributable experience and Outcome verification. It cannot
  replace either or retroactively rewrite prior Evidence and Decision
  provenance.
- A positive research result would not grant Engineering Architecture,
  implementation, delivery, canonical, governance, or successor authority.

## 3. Research Meaning of Validation

Within this paper, bounded validation means possible future collection and
independent examination of evidence about one fixed operational value claim.
Its subject would be one material operational situation and the continuity of
its consequence chain:

```text
Operational Reality
        ↓
Matter / Context Continuity
        ↓
Investigation
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Understanding
        ↓
Human Decision
        ↓
Source-Owned Change
        ↓
Observed Outcome and Verification
        ↓
Learning
```

The trace is analytical, not a product workflow. A future inquiry could observe
responsibilities distributed across people, Management OS capabilities, and
external source systems. The validation question is whether their composition
supports a bounded management Outcome while preserving meaning and authority.

## 4. Validation Boundary

### 4.1 Proposed validation subject

A useful bounded validation would examine whether, for one pre-defined class of
operational situation and accountable human owner, the complete loop:

1. preserves material operational context;
2. improves evidence-backed understanding or uncertainty calibration;
3. contributes to a timely, explainable, accountable human Decision;
4. preserves Decision intent into one bounded source-owned Change;
5. contributes to an attributable improvement in a pre-stated operational
   condition;
6. exposes null, adverse, delayed, displaced, and contradictory effects; and
7. supports correctable Learning for a meaningfully comparable later situation.

The subject should be fixed by operational situation, beneficiary, intended
condition, loop scope, Evidence boundary, Change boundary, observation horizon,
comparison basis, material confounders, and prohibited conclusions before the
result is interpreted.

### 4.2 Unit of claim

The strongest initially supportable unit is normally the **bounded case**, not
the whole product, organization, capability portfolio, or user population.

A possible research-form claim is:

> In this bounded operational situation, evidence supports that use of the
> complete loop materially contributed to a specified change in management
> capacity and to an observed improvement in the pre-stated operational
> condition, within the recorded scope, comparison, uncertainty, and
> alternative explanations.

Every part of this claim must remain independently challengeable. “Contributed”
must not be silently promoted to “caused.”

### 4.3 What one bounded validation cannot prove

One validation cannot by itself prove:

- universal or product-wide value;
- that Management OS uniquely caused the Outcome;
- that one Root Cause was found or eliminated;
- that every capability in the canonical architecture is necessary or
  sufficient;
- that Operational Diagnosis requires a distinct canonical object;
- organizational scalability or transfer across materially different contexts;
- implementation conformance, technical feasibility, performance, reliability,
  security, usability, or operational supportability;
- Engineering Architecture or implementation readiness;
- that M8.4 Gaps are resolved or Risks accepted or treated;
- that an M8.5 alternative should be selected;
- that an M8.6 readiness class applies;
- that favorable Outcome proves the prior Interpretation, scoped Cause,
  Recommendation, or Decision was completely correct; or
- that Learning from one case is authoritative in later cases.

### 4.4 Prohibited claims

A future validation should not make claims of the following form from the
bounded evidence:

- “Management OS creates value” without scope and limitation;
- “Management OS caused the Outcome” from sequence, usage, belief, or
  correlation alone;
- “AI improved the Outcome” because AI output was used or accepted;
- “the Root Cause was found” when evidence supports only contributing factors,
  a scoped Cause, or a useful working explanation;
- “Operational Diagnosis is validated” when no canonical object exists;
- “the Decision was correct” solely because the Outcome was favorable;
- “the Change succeeded” because execution completed;
- “Learning occurred” because a Learning, Memory, or Knowledge record exists;
- “the product is validated” from one case;
- “architecture is implementation-ready” from operational value evidence; or
- “implementation is authorized” from any research conclusion.

## 5. Fixed Claim and Counterclaim Posture

Before observation, a future inquiry would need to state:

- the bounded value claim;
- plausible alternative explanations;
- conditions that would count against the claim;
- conditions that would make the evidence indeterminate;
- the minimum material Outcome difference relevant to the responsible human;
- known adverse or displacement effects to observe; and
- the maximum conclusion permitted by the selected case.

This protects the inquiry from redefining value after seeing activity or
Outcome data. The counterclaim is not necessarily “Management OS has no value.”
It may be narrower: the complete loop did not materially contribute to the
specified Outcome in this context, or the evidence cannot distinguish its
contribution.

## 6. Observation Model

### 6.1 Observation principle

Each loop position should be observable enough to show what occurred, which
meaning it carried, who owned it, and how it related to adjacent positions. The
observation model must not force all positions into one artifact or system.

Observation does not mean unrestricted recording. Evidence must remain
proportionate, source-authorized, privacy-aware, and limited to the bounded
claim. This paper does not define collection tooling or policy.

### 6.2 Observable elements by loop position

| Loop position | What should be observable | What observation must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operational Reality | The initial operational condition, relevant scope, timing, actors, consequences, and source-owned observations | The representation is complete reality or every observation is Evidence |
| Matter / Context Continuity | Stable subject identity, material context, changes over time, source references, ownership, uncertainty, and interruptions | Matter itself proves priority, Cause, or value |
| Investigation | Declared question, scope, hypotheses, sought and missing Evidence, contrary material, revisions, and stopping rationale | Completion means sufficient understanding or success |
| Evidence | Source, provenance, time, relevance, semantic kind, limitations, support and contradiction, and admission purpose | Volume, confidence, or source prestige makes Evidence universally sufficient |
| Understanding | Interpretations, competing explanations, uncertainty, optional scoped Cause, modeled alternatives, and changes caused by Evidence | Synthesis is Fact, one Root Cause, Decision, or canonical Diagnosis |
| Human Decision | Accountable human, alternatives considered, intent, justification, uncertainty, expected condition, bounds, and no-action or revision where applicable | Recommendation, model output, consensus, or interface action made the Decision |
| Source-Owned Change | Owning source, actual intervention, timing, scope, deviations from Decision intent, interruption, correction, and completion | Execution completion establishes Outcome |
| Observed Outcome | Post-Change operational condition, time horizon, source-owned Observation or Evidence, adverse and delayed effects, comparison, and verification judgment | Favorable change proves unique causality or prior reasoning correctness |
| Learning | What verified experience may inform later, provenance, applicable context, uncertainty, correction, and later use or non-use | A stored lesson is truth, authority, or evidence for every similar case |

### 6.3 Transition observability

It is insufficient to observe only the positions. The future inquiry would also
need enough evidence of the transitions:

- why Operational Reality became a Matter;
- how Investigation changed the Evidence boundary;
- how Evidence changed or failed to change Understanding;
- how Understanding influenced but did not replace human Decision;
- how Decision intent reached the source-owned Change;
- how the actual Change relates to the observed condition;
- how Outcome was verified against intent and comparison; and
- how verified experience later influenced, failed to influence, or misled
  Learning and judgment.

Transition evidence helps distinguish mere co-occurrence from a plausible
mechanism of contribution.

## 7. Evidence Requirements

### 7.1 Evidence classes potentially required

A future bounded inquiry would likely require an evidence body containing:

1. **Initial-condition evidence** — attributable operational observations before
   the relevant Decision and Change.
2. **Context-continuity evidence** — what was preserved, updated, lost, or
   corrected as the Matter developed.
3. **Investigation evidence** — questions, hypotheses, search boundary, contrary
   evidence, missing evidence, and revisions.
4. **Decision-basis evidence** — what Evidence and Interpretation were available
   to the human, which alternatives and uncertainty mattered, and what remained
   unknown.
5. **Decision evidence** — accountable judgment, intent, expected condition,
   bounds, and timing.
6. **Change evidence** — source-owned record of the intervention actually made,
   including deviations, interruption, correction, and concurrent Changes.
7. **Outcome evidence** — post-Change operational observations over the
   justified horizon, including null, adverse, delayed, and displaced effects.
8. **Comparison evidence** — baseline, comparable prior instances,
   contemporaneous comparison, or pre-stated expected condition with explicit
   limitations.
9. **Attribution evidence** — trace from changes in understanding and judgment
   through Decision and actual Change, plus alternative explanations and
   confounders.
10. **Learning evidence** — later use, correction, or rejection of the verified
    experience in a meaningfully comparable situation.
11. **Burden evidence** — time, attention, coordination, verification load,
    displacement, and adverse human or operational consequences relevant to net
    value.

These are research evidence categories, not a new canonical taxonomy or data
requirement.

### 7.2 Evidence sufficiency qualities

Evidence is sufficient only relative to the fixed claim. The body should be:

- attributable to an identified source and relevant time;
- semantically valid for the claim made from it;
- relevant to every material claim component;
- complete enough to include contrary, missing, null, and adverse material;
- traceable across Decision intent, actual Change, and observed Outcome;
- comparable enough to support the claimed difference;
- explicit about uncertainty, confounders, context, and transfer limits;
- independently inspectable where applicable; and
- proportionate to the strength of the conclusion.

More evidence is not automatically better. Redundant activity records cannot
repair the absence of a valid Outcome observation or Decision-to-Change trace.

### 7.3 Insufficient evidence

The following are insufficient by themselves:

- number of Matters, Investigations, Evidence items, hypotheses,
  Recommendations, Decisions, Changes, or Learning records;
- AI interactions, generated content, response speed, confidence, or acceptance;
- user sessions, engagement, retention, satisfaction, agreement, or perceived
  usefulness;
- output fluency, completeness, consistency, or presentation quality;
- completion of a workflow, Investigation, Decision record, task, or Change;
- prototype behavior or implementation conformance;
- model evaluation, scenario comparison, simulation, forecast, or predicted
  Outcome;
- post hoc testimony without source or comparison evidence;
- a favorable operational change with no traceable intervention relationship;
- a repeated pattern without comparable exposure and context; or
- a stored lesson without evidence of later improved judgment or consequence.

These may provide supporting context. None becomes Outcome evidence or causal
authority through volume, confidence, reuse, similarity, or automation.

### 7.4 Semantic separation in the evidence body

| Semantic kind | Permitted role | Prohibited promotion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation | Records a perceived or measured condition in a source and scope | Universal Fact or Evidence for an unstated question |
| Evidence | Supports or contradicts a declared claim with provenance and limits | Interpretation, Recommendation, Decision, or Outcome |
| Interpretation | Relates Evidence into meaning, explanation, or hypothesis | Fact, proof, or authority merely because persuasive |
| Modeled / Scenario Output | Explores assumptions, alternatives, and possible consequences | Evidence, Observation, Fact, Prediction, Recommendation, Decision, or Outcome |
| Recommendation | Advises an accountable human with rationale and uncertainty | Decision or authorization |
| Decision | Records accountable human judgment and intent | Evidence that the expected Outcome occurred |
| Change record | Shows what source-owned intervention occurred | Verified operational success |
| Outcome | Records attributable observed consequence plus owned verification judgment | Proof of unique cause or complete correctness of prior reasoning |
| Learning | Preserves a bounded, correctable relation to verified experience | Canonical truth, authority, or current-case Evidence by similarity alone |

## 8. Outcome Evaluation

### 8.1 Outcome principle

Outcome is the final consequence test for the bounded value claim. It compares
the observed operational condition with Decision intent and the pre-stated
intended condition. It must remain distinct from:

- the system operating as designed;
- the manager completing an Investigation or Decision;
- the source executing a Change;
- the user reporting satisfaction or confidence;
- a model predicting success; and
- a reviewer finding the narrative plausible.

Learning may follow Outcome, but it cannot establish the Outcome retroactively.

### 8.2 Candidate Outcome dimensions

One bounded proposition may select relevant dimensions such as:

- **condition improvement** — movement toward and sustained presence within the
  intended operating condition;
- **stabilization** — time and durability of recovery from an unstable or
  adverse condition;
- **recurrence** — frequency or severity of meaningfully comparable unwanted
  conditions under comparable exposure;
- **Decision adequacy** — timeliness, correction, explainability, or avoidance
  of known unsupported assumptions relative to the stated purpose;
- **continuity** — preservation of material context, Evidence, ownership, and
  Decision intent across handoffs;
- **Change fidelity** — correspondence between human Decision intent and actual
  source-owned intervention;
- **correction capacity** — detection, interruption, revision, or reversal of an
  ineffective or harmful course;
- **management burden** — time, attention, coordination, and verification cost
  required for the achieved consequence;
- **adverse and displaced effects** — harm or degradation outside the favored
  measure; and
- **later judgment** — improvement in a meaningfully comparable later situation
  with traceable, bounded reliance on verified experience.

The dimensions are research possibilities, not canonical metrics. A future
inquiry should pre-state the primary Outcome dimension and material balancing
conditions rather than select whichever improves.

### 8.3 Baseline and comparison conditions

A credible Outcome judgment requires a reference point appropriate to the claim.
Possible forms include:

- the attributable initial condition before the Change;
- repeated pre-Change observations showing trajectory or variability;
- meaningfully comparable prior instances;
- a contemporaneous comparison under sufficiently similar conditions;
- a pre-stated expected condition, threshold, or range; or
- a structured counterfactual judgment with visible assumptions and
  uncertainty.

The comparison should preserve differences in exposure, timing, participants,
external change, and operational context. A prior case is not comparable merely
because it has the same label. A threshold chosen after observation cannot
credibly test the original claim.

No comparison form guarantees causality. It bounds the difference and enables
alternative explanations to be examined.

### 8.4 Positive, negative, and null results

- A **positive bounded result** requires an observed material improvement and a
  credible contribution relationship after costs, adverse effects, uncertainty,
  and alternative explanations are considered.
- A **negative bounded result** occurs when sufficient evidence shows no material
  improvement, degradation, disproportionate burden, authority erosion, or
  failure of the hypothesized mechanism.
- A **null result** records no material difference relative to the selected
  comparison within the justified horizon.

None of these automatically generalizes beyond the fixed case. Negative and
null results are valid evidence, not execution failure to be hidden.

### 8.5 Indeterminate result

The result should remain indeterminate when:

- the initial condition or intended Outcome was not fixed clearly enough;
- relevant Evidence is missing, inaccessible, stale, contradictory, or
  semantically invalid;
- the actual Change is unknown or differs materially from Decision intent;
- the observation occurs too early or too late for a justified inference;
- material confounders or concurrent interventions cannot be distinguished;
- the comparison is not meaningfully comparable;
- the Outcome is observable but contribution is not;
- adverse or contrary evidence is incomplete;
- the loop is incomplete; or
- evidence supports incompatible interpretations without a justified bounded
  conclusion.

Indeterminate is not failure, success, or permission to broaden the claim. It
protects the evidence boundary when the available material cannot support a
verdict.

## 9. Attribution Model

### 9.1 Prohibited causal shortcut

```text
Management OS was used
        ↓
The Outcome occurred
        ↓
Management OS caused the Outcome
```

This inference is invalid without evidence of mechanism, Change, comparison,
alternative explanations, and confounders. Temporal sequence, user belief, AI
involvement, narrative coherence, and correlation are insufficient.

### 9.2 Bounded contribution chain

A more defensible contribution judgment would trace:

```text
Fixed initial condition and value claim
        ↓
Attributable Evidence changed or confirmed Understanding
        ↓
Understanding influenced explicit human Decision
        ↓
Decision produced a traceable source-owned Change
        ↓
Relevant operational condition changed
        ↓
Comparison and alternative explanations were examined
        ↓
Owned contribution judgment with scope and uncertainty
```

The contribution claim should distinguish:

- use of Management OS;
- change in management capacity or behavior;
- actual intervention;
- occurrence of Outcome;
- plausible mechanism connecting them; and
- strength and limits of attribution.

The conclusion may range from association, through plausible or material
contribution, to insufficient attribution. Stronger causal wording requires
stronger evidence and is never implied by completion of the loop.

## 10. Bias and Validity Threats

### 10.1 Attribution bias

**Threat:** favorable Outcome is credited to Management OS because it was
visible in the sequence, while manager expertise, source systems, external
events, prior work, or other interventions are discounted.

**Research controls:** record pre-existing trajectory, concurrent Changes,
external factors, prior manager knowledge, deviations from Decision intent, and
credible alternative explanations; match conclusion strength to evidence.

### 10.2 Selection bias

**Threat:** the case is chosen because it is likely to succeed, has an unusually
engaged owner, has unusually rich data, or already shows improvement. The
opposite bias may arise from selecting only exceptional failures.

**Research controls:** state candidate inclusion and exclusion reasoning before
results; disclose why the case is available; avoid generalization; retain cases
that produce null or adverse evidence.

### 10.3 Confirmation bias

**Threat:** Investigation seeks supporting Evidence, ambiguous material is
interpreted favorably, contrary hypotheses receive weaker tests, or Outcome
criteria shift after observation.

**Research controls:** pre-state claim, counterclaims, falsification conditions,
primary Outcome, and material adverse effects; preserve contrary and missing
Evidence; require interpretations to remain challengeable.

### 10.4 Novelty effect

**Threat:** attention, enthusiasm, extra support, or changed behavior caused by a
new process produces a temporary improvement unrelated to durable Management OS
value.

**Research controls:** observe an appropriate duration; distinguish initial
engagement from sustained condition; record extraordinary support; seek later
comparable use before making Learning or durability claims.

### 10.5 Measurement distortion

**Threat:** convenient proxies replace the operational condition; participants
optimize the measure; increased recording changes behavior; or measurement
burden itself affects the Outcome.

**Research controls:** connect each measure to the intended condition; observe
balancing and adverse effects; preserve source meaning; disclose missing
dimensions; do not infer value from activity metrics.

### 10.6 Additional validity threats

| Threat | Possible distortion | Research response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regression to the mean | An extreme condition improves naturally and is credited to the Change | Preserve trajectory and comparable prior variation |
| Maturation and timing | Independent learning or elapsed time produces improvement | Record relevant parallel changes and observation timing |
| Instrument change | The source or meaning of a measure changes during observation | Preserve source identity, definition, and discontinuities |
| Recall bias | Participants reconstruct prior understanding after knowing the Outcome | Prefer contemporaneous attributable records and retain corrections |
| Authority bias | People agree with AI, leadership, or reviewers because outputs appear authoritative | Preserve explicit human judgment, dissent, uncertainty, and non-authority |
| Survivorship bias | Only completed loops are examined | Retain interrupted, abandoned, no-action, failed, and indeterminate cases |
| Contamination | Learning or intervention crosses into a comparison context | Record cross-context influence and weaken comparison claims accordingly |
| Semantic collapse | Evidence, model, Recommendation, Decision, completion, and Outcome become one success narrative | Maintain kind-specific provenance and prohibit promotion |

Bias cannot always be eliminated. It must be visible enough to bound the claim
and may make the result indeterminate.

## 11. Human Authority Boundary

### 11.1 Human Decision

The accountable human owns Decision. A future validation may observe how
Evidence, Interpretation, hypotheses, modeled alternatives, and Recommendations
influenced judgment. It must not transfer accountability to Management OS, AI,
the research design, a confidence score, or group consensus.

The Decision observation should make visible:

- who was accountable;
- what question was decided;
- which alternatives and uncertainties mattered;
- what Evidence was available and missing;
- how Recommendations or modeled outputs were treated;
- what intent and bounds governed the Change; and
- whether no-action, delay, revision, interruption, or reversal remained
  available.

This is an observation requirement for the research claim, not a new canonical
Decision schema.

### 11.2 Management OS non-authority

Management OS may preserve context, organize Evidence, expose contradictions,
support Interpretation, compare alternatives, prepare Recommendations, and
maintain traceability within canonical boundaries. It does not approve,
prioritize, decide, own operational facts, or mutate external sources merely
because the loop is under validation.

### 11.3 AI non-authority

AI may assist within existing bounded capability responsibilities. It cannot:

- promote its output to Evidence, Fact, Prediction, or observed Outcome;
- decide which explanation is true;
- select or approve a Recommendation;
- make the human Decision;
- authorize or execute source mutation outside source authority;
- verify its own contribution or success;
- suppress contrary Evidence or uncertainty; or
- convert repeated acceptance into authority.

A favorable Outcome after AI assistance does not prove that AI caused the
Outcome or that its Interpretation was correct. The evidence must trace what the
human relied upon and what Change actually occurred.

### 11.4 Validation non-authority

The act of observing a loop must not become hidden management authority. A
future inquiry should not compel a Decision, privileged Recommendation, Change,
or continued participation to create a complete record. An interrupted,
no-action, or indeterminate loop may be the most valid result.

## 12. Failure and Falsification Conditions

The bounded value claim would not be supported when sufficient evidence shows:

- the loop completes but the intended Outcome does not materially improve;
- the result is no better than the credible comparison;
- improvement is temporary, displaced, or outweighed by burden or harm;
- Management OS produces more information or AI output but not better judgment
  or consequence;
- Recommendation acceptance does not alter the Decision or Outcome;
- Decision intent is lost before or during Change;
- execution completes but the operational condition remains unchanged;
- the observed improvement cannot be credibly related to the Change;
- semantic or authority boundaries must be violated to obtain the result; or
- Learning accumulates but does not improve later judgment or Action in a
  comparable situation.

The validation would fail to test the whole-loop proposition, rather than
falsify it, when a required position cannot be observed, no genuine human
Decision occurs, no source-owned Change occurs, or no Outcome can be verified.

This distinction matters: a falsified claim is informative evidence about the
fixed proposition; an untestable or incomplete loop is evidence that the chosen
case or observation boundary was inadequate.

## 13. Conditions Before Any Real Bounded Validation

Before a real validation could responsibly be considered, a separate authority
would need to establish that all of the following can be fixed without creating
missing canonical meaning:

### 13.1 Subject and value conditions

- one real, recurrent, material but responsibly bounded operational condition;
- one accountable human beneficiary and Decision owner;
- one pre-stated management-capacity change and intended operational Outcome;
- a whole-loop scope that reaches Outcome verification and permits Learning;
- explicit exclusions and maximum permitted conclusion; and
- materiality sufficient to test value without disproportionate exposure.

### 13.2 Evidence and observation conditions

- attributable pre-Decision and post-Change sources;
- a credible baseline, comparison, or pre-stated expected condition;
- a justified observation horizon;
- accessible contrary, negative, null, adverse, and missing Evidence;
- traceability from Understanding through human Decision and actual Change;
- identifiable confounders and concurrent interventions;
- an owned Outcome-verification judgment; and
- a plausible later opportunity to examine Learning.

### 13.3 Boundary conditions

- Decision remains human and source mutation remains source-owned;
- AI and Management OS remain assistive and non-authoritative;
- modeled output remains outside Evidence and observed Outcome;
- no mandatory Root Cause or new Diagnosis object is required;
- privacy, participation, harm, and authority concerns can remain bounded under
  existing applicable authority;
- interruption, correction, no-action, and indeterminate results remain
  legitimate; and
- the inquiry can proceed without inventing implementation, lifecycle,
  governance, or canonical product requirements.

### 13.4 Claim-integrity conditions

- the claim, counterclaims, falsification conditions, primary Outcome, balancing
  effects, and indeterminate conditions can be stated before observation;
- evidence sufficiency can be assessed per claim rather than by volume;
- positive, negative, null, and indeterminate results can all be retained;
- contribution language can remain bounded by alternative explanations; and
- one case will not be generalized into product-wide validation or work
  authority.

If a non-substitutable condition cannot be met, a future validation should not
claim to test the complete operational value proposition. That limitation may
indicate case-selection failure, insufficient evidence, or a separately
researchable architectural question; this paper does not classify it.

## 14. Recommendation

The next justified step is **not validation execution**. It is to consider,
through separate authority, whether one candidate case of the previously
recommended loop type can satisfy all pre-validation conditions in Section 13
without semantic, authority, evidence, or implementation leakage.

The candidate type remains:

> A recurrent, material but non-safety-critical operational deviation within a
> bounded team or process, owned by one accountable manager, with existing
> source evidence, genuine uncertainty and human judgment, one reversible
> source-owned Change, a near- or medium-term observable condition, manageable
> confounders, and meaningful comparable instances for Outcome and Learning.

Any future consideration should reject a candidate when Outcome cannot be
pre-stated and observed, contribution cannot be distinguished from mere usage,
human or source authority is ambiguous, the loop cannot reach verified
consequence, or the evidence boundary depends on implementation details not yet
authorized.

If a candidate satisfies the research conditions, that would establish only
that a bounded validation proposition can be considered. It would not start the
validation or authorize a prototype, Engineering Architecture, implementation,
delivery, or successor milestone.

## 15. Non-Decision and Non-Authorization

This paper creates no canonical validation protocol, object, capability,
lifecycle, governance authority, review gate, state, role, metric system,
implementation requirement, prototype, Engineering Architecture, milestone,
M9, stage, order, backlog task, delivery plan, or validation execution.

Canonical M7 and M8.1–M8.7 remain unchanged. M8 remains Completed / Closed.
Outcome remains the final consequence test; Learning remains downstream of
verified experience; human Decision and source ownership remain controlling;
semantic kinds remain distinct; Root Cause remains non-mandatory; and
Operational Diagnosis remains research framing only.
