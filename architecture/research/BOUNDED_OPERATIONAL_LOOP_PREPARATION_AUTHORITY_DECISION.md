## 1. Artifact Purpose

The purpose of this artifact is to provide one repository-visible place where a
future applicable human authority could record a decision about whether bounded
operational loop validation preparation may begin.

The artifact is distinct from:

- research, which supplies non-authoritative analysis;
- the decision package, which presents neutral options and consequences;
- the proposal, which describes possible preparation scope and preconditions;
- the authority-record structure, which defines how a future record should avoid
  ambiguity;
- scenario assessment and selection;
- validation authorization and execution; and
- any later Engineering Architecture, implementation, or delivery decision.

Until an existing accountable human authority supplies a valid decision under
applicable canonical governance, the only state of this artifact is:

## 3. Decision Identity

### 3.1 Decision considered

The future decision is:

> Whether to authorize, defer, request bounded additional decision evidence for,
> or decline a candidate-scenario identification and qualitative suitability-
> assessment preparation activity for a possible future bounded operational
> loop validation.

If authorized later, preparation would be limited to identifying real candidate
scenarios, examining existing suitability Evidence, applying qualitative and
non-ranked assessment dimensions, preserving rejection and indeterminate
conditions, and returning decision support to a separate human authority.

### 3.2 Decision unit

The unit of decision is **preparation only**. It is not:

- a candidate class;
- a real operational scenario;
- a validation claim;
- an operational Decision or Change;
- a prototype or product feature;
- an Engineering Architecture subject;
- an implementation package; or
- a milestone or backlog item.

### 3.3 Why preparation is separate from validation authorization

Preparation asks whether a credible candidate can be identified and evidenced
for later consideration. Validation authorization would ask whether one
separately selected candidate may be examined under a fixed case-specific value
claim, Evidence boundary, Outcome, comparison, attribution posture, ethical and
operational constraints, and execution boundary.

Those questions require different evidence and have different effects:

| Preparation decision | Validation-authorization decision |
| --- | --- |
| Concerns candidate identification and suitability Evidence | Concerns one separately selected candidate and a fixed value claim |
| Produces decision support only | Could permit a bounded validation activity under separate authority |
| Does not perform operational Investigation, Decision, Change, or Outcome observation | Would still remain distinct from operational human Decision and source-owned Change |
| May conclude no candidate or Indeterminate | Would require a selected candidate and complete validation boundary |
| Creates no validation result | Creates no result until separately executed and evaluated |

Preparation authorization therefore cannot be read as validation approval.

### 3.4 Why validation authorization is separate from execution

Even a later validation authorization would not itself:

- make the accountable operational Decision;
- execute a source-owned Change;
- observe or verify Outcome;
- establish Learning;
- prove product value; or
- authorize Engineering Architecture or implementation.

This artifact reaches none of those boundaries.

## 5. Available Decision Options

Exactly one option may be selected by a future attributable human decision. No
option is selected now.

### Option A — Authorized preparation

```text
Authorized preparation
```

**Permitted meaning:** a bounded candidate-identification and qualitative
suitability-assessment preparation activity may begin within explicitly fixed
purpose, scope, ownership, existing-source access, constraints, exclusions,
termination conditions, maximum output, and return-to-authority requirements.

**Required effect boundary:** no scenario selection, validation authorization or
execution, operational Change, prototype, implementation, Engineering
Architecture, milestone, or backlog follows.

### Option B — Deferred

```text
Deferred
```

**Permitted meaning:** the human authority does not currently authorize or
decline preparation and leaves the decision open for possible later
consideration.

**Required effect boundary:** no preparation, evidence request, automatic
reconsideration, task, negative finding, or passive authorization follows.

### Option C — Additional evidence requested

```text
Additional evidence requested
```

**Permitted meaning:** the human authority names bounded decision-relevant
Evidence needed before choosing among the decision options.

**Required effect boundary:** the request cannot become candidate assessment,
scenario selection, open-ended research, preparation, validation, data or
instrumentation design, implementation, or backlog work.

### Option D — Declined

```text
Declined
```

**Permitted meaning:** the proposed transition from completed research to the
bounded preparation activity is not authorized through this decision.

**Required effect boundary:** decline does not falsify Management OS value,
reject candidate classes on their merits, prohibit future research, authorize a
different direction, or create Engineering Architecture, implementation, or a
successor milestone.
