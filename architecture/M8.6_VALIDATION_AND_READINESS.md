## 1. Purpose

This document defines the implementation-independent Validation and Readiness
Framework for the integrated Management OS architecture established by
canonical M8.1–M8.5. It specifies the architectural objects, evidence qualities,
methods, dimensions, criteria, classifications, blocking conditions,
limitations, residual-risk treatment boundaries, and traceability needed to
support a bounded readiness claim.

The framework determines what must be demonstrated before later governed
engineering or implementation work may claim architectural readiness. It does
not perform M8.7 Integrated Architecture Review, approve an architecture,
canonicalize an artifact, authorize implementation, or declare M8 complete.

## 2. Authority Condition and Evidence Boundary

### 2.1 Authority condition

This candidate is prepared under the scope-bounded M8.6 Validation and
Readiness Order. Its controlling authorities and dependencies are:

- canonical M7 Governance Architecture;
- the Architecture Approved M8 Architecture Vision;
- canonical M8.1 Architectural Identity and Baseline Mapping;
- canonical M8.2 Capability and Ownership Coherence;
- canonical M8.3 Information, Evidence, and Handoff Architecture;
- canonical M8.4 Gap and Risk Assessment; and
- canonical M8.5 Evolution Architecture.

M8.6 defines a validation architecture. It does not change the authority,
meaning, status, ownership, findings, gaps, risks, recommendations, routes, or
invariants of those sources. A later review may evaluate this candidate, but
this document cannot approve itself or create canonical authority.

### 2.2 Evidence boundary

Permitted validation evidence is attributable architectural content and
governance evidence within the reviewed scope, including:

- fixed revisions of M8.1–M8.5 and their preserved review records;
- canonical M7 authority, review, and transition records relevant to the claim;
- the approved M8 Vision within its recorded authority condition;
- authoritative, supporting, and constraining sources within their recorded
  scopes and authority conditions;
- M8.4 findings, Gaps, Risks, Observations, Recommendations, Decisions, and
  Conclusions within their canonical object boundaries; and
- explicit traceability, comparison, contradiction, coverage, and dependency
  results derived from those sources without authority promotion.

Assertions, confidence, recommendations, conclusions, implemented behavior,
prototype behavior, tool output, or document presence are not sufficient merely
because they exist. They become validation evidence only when their provenance,
scope, relevance, authority, and limitations meet this framework.

## 3. Framing and Architectural Boundary

Management OS remains one architectural object distinct from the real
Operational System, external systems of record, human authority, and technical
realization. Validation tests that boundary; it does not redraw it.

Product Purpose and System Identity remain architectural-object-level framing
concerns. Their M8.1 association with P1 and P2 remains a responsibility lookup
aid and never makes them locally owned capability responsibilities or
validation-object owners.

The six M8.1 product responsibility layers remain responsibility views rather
than implementation tiers, process steps, or authority levels. The
cross-cutting Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety, and Quality plane
remains distinct from product layers. Canonical M7 Governance remains an
external Governance and Evolution constraint plane, not a product layer,
capability, validation dimension, or internal product mechanism.

## 4. Validation-Object Model

### 4.1 Object taxonomy

| Object | Purpose | Evidence relationship | Authority boundary | Explicit non-authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Validation subject | Names the exact architectural object, scope, revision, responsibility, relationship, or claim domain being evaluated | Must resolve to attributable current architecture or an explicitly bounded composite scope | Bounds what may be evaluated and what a result can describe | Does not make the subject valid, complete, approved, canonical, implemented, or ready |
| Validation claim | States one precise architectural proposition to be tested about the subject | Must be testable against one or more criteria and linked to supporting or contradicting evidence | Bounds the proposition a result may support | Does not establish truth, readiness, approval, priority, or work |
| Validation criterion | Defines the architecture-level condition against which a claim is evaluated | Must derive from an authoritative requirement, invariant, boundary, or explicitly identified assessment need | Controls evaluation within its cited source scope | Does not create a new requirement, governance gate, lifecycle state, or source authority |
| Validation evidence | Supplies attributable material relevant to a criterion and claim | Must meet the evidence-sufficiency model and retain provenance, authority condition, uncertainty, and scope | Supports evaluation but cannot own the verdict | Cannot issue its own result, promote itself, approve, decide, resolve a Gap, accept Risk, or authorize work |
| Validation method | Defines the implementation-independent analytical operation applied to evidence | Must identify what evidence it consumes, what comparison it performs, and its limitations | Produces a bounded analytical basis for a result | Does not define tools, scripts, automation, tests, CI, repository mechanics, or execution procedure |
| Validation result | Records the criterion-specific outcome supported by evidence and method | Must cite the claim, criterion, evidence, method, scope, and uncertainty | May state Pass, Fail, Indeterminate, or Not Applicable only for the evaluated criterion | Does not approve the subject, aggregate readiness by itself, alter evidence, or authorize correction |
| Finding | Records an evidence-backed condition exposed by a validation result | Must trace to one or more results and their evidence; it remains distinct from an M8.4 Finding unless separately established there | Owns the validation-stage statement of observed architectural condition | Does not become a Gap, Risk, Recommendation, Decision, correction, priority, or work by naming |
| Limitation | States a known boundary on evidence, method, coverage, currency, reproducibility, or inference | Must trace to the affected claim, evidence, method, or result | Restricts the scope and strength of any conclusion that relies on it | Does not imply failure, acceptance, waiver, treatment, approval, or permission to ignore the boundary |
| Residual risk | States a possible adverse consequence that remains after currently evidenced architectural conditions are considered | Must trace to a known Risk or to a validation finding and preserve uncertainty | Constrains the permitted readiness claim within the reviewed scope | Does not accept, treat, mitigate, waive, close, reprioritize, or assign the Risk |
| Readiness conclusion | Synthesizes criterion results, findings, limitations, and residual risks for one explicit scope and intended readiness claim | Must be completely traceable to the underlying chain and state contrary or indeterminate results | May state only what readiness is demonstrated within the bounded scope | Cannot approve itself, authorize engineering or implementation, change governance state, close a stage, or declare M8 complete |
| Readiness disposition | Assigns one deterministic readiness class to the bounded conclusion | Must derive from the classification rules and cite the conclusion and blocking-condition evaluation | Communicates architectural-readiness meaning only; governance disposition remains external | Does not create a lifecycle state, Architecture Approval, Canonical authority, risk acceptance, implementation authority, or stage transition |

### 4.2 Object separation rules

1. A subject is not a claim; naming an artifact does not assert its readiness.
2. A claim is not a criterion; the proposition under test cannot define its own
   passing condition.
3. A criterion is not evidence; a requirement does not prove conformance to
   itself.
4. Evidence is not a method or result; material does not analyze or judge
   itself.
5. A result is criterion-specific; it cannot silently become an integrated
   conclusion.
6. A Finding records a condition; it does not automatically become an M8.4 Gap
   or Risk or create corrective authority.
7. A Limitation constrains inference; it does not conceal or dispose of a
   failure.
8. Residual risk is visible exposure, not accepted exposure.
9. A readiness conclusion is an assessment synthesis, not an approval.
10. A readiness disposition is a classification, not a governance verdict or
    workflow state.

## 5. Validation Result Model

Every criterion produces exactly one of these bounded results:

| Result | Deterministic meaning | Required statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pass | Sufficient evidence demonstrates the criterion for the named scope | Supporting evidence, method, scope, and limitations |
| Fail | Evidence demonstrates that the criterion is not satisfied | Contradicting evidence, affected scope, and resulting finding |
| Indeterminate | Available evidence cannot support either Pass or Fail | Missing, conflicting, stale, or insufficient evidence and associated limitation |
| Not Applicable | The criterion is demonstrably outside the named subject scope | Evidence for non-applicability and the boundary preventing use |

`Not Applicable` cannot be used merely because evidence is absent. `Pass` does
not mean complete beyond the named criterion and scope. `Indeterminate` is not a
Pass, a Fail, or permission to infer readiness.

### 7.6 Cross-artifact criterion

Passing each artifact independently is necessary but not sufficient for an
integrated readiness claim. Cross-artifact validation must additionally show
that shared identity, ownership, terms, evidence, dependencies, handoffs,
assessment conditions, evolution constraints, and authority boundaries compose
without contradiction or semantic gaps hidden between artifact scopes.

## 8. Product-Wide Integrated Validation Model

Integrated validation evaluates one bounded architectural graph:

```text
Product identity and boundary (M8.1)
    constrains
Responsibilities and capability ownership (M8.1 / M8.2)
    constrain
Semantic movement and authority-preserving handoffs (M8.3)
    are assessed by
Evidence-backed Gaps, Risks, and assessment boundaries (M8.4)
    constrain
Admissible future architectural evolution (M8.5)
    is evaluated through
Validation claims, criteria, evidence, results, and bounded readiness (M8.6)

External canonical M7 governs every review and authority transition.
The cross-cutting plane constrains every product-architecture subject.
```

The arrows express architectural dependency, not runtime flow, workflow,
delivery order, or authority hierarchy.

An integrated validation must confirm:

1. **one object** — M8.1–M8.5 describe one Management OS architecture rather
   than unrelated document systems;
2. **one owner per meaning** — every capability responsibility retains its M8.2
   primary semantic owner and explicit non-ownership;
3. **authority-preserving movement** — M8.3 handoffs preserve source ownership,
   provenance, uncertainty, traceability, responsibility, and human authority;
4. **visible exposure** — M8.4 Gaps, Risks, limitations, and residual exposures
   remain explicit without correction, acceptance, or treatment;
5. **bounded evolution** — M8.5 directions, options, routes, increments, and
   sequencing cannot become product priority, implementation plan, or work;
6. **external governance** — canonical M7 remains outside product architecture
   and alone governs review and authority transition; and
7. **framing preservation** — Product Purpose and System Identity remain
   architectural-object-level concerns rather than local capability ownership.

Integrated coherence fails or remains indeterminate if a cross-artifact
contradiction, ownership ambiguity, authority conflict, untraceable claim, or
material evidence limitation prevents those confirmations.

## 9. Evidence Sufficiency Model

### 9.1 Sufficiency qualities

Evidence is sufficient for a claim only when all applicable qualities are
demonstrated:

| Quality | Requirement | Insufficiency signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attributable | Identifiable source, authority condition, scope, revision or stable identity, and derivation | Anonymous, unversioned, inferred, or source-detached material |
| Relevant | Directly bears on the named claim and criterion | Merely adjacent content, analogy, or evidence for another scope |
| Complete enough | Covers every material part of the criterion needed for the bounded claim | Selective evidence, omitted counterevidence, or unexamined required scope |
| Current enough | Represents the authoritative state applicable to the claim | Superseded, historical, stale, or future material treated as current |
| Reproducible where applicable | An independent reviewer can reach the same bounded analytical result from the stated evidence and method | Undisclosed inference, inaccessible basis, or result dependent on personal confidence |
| Scoped | States inclusions, exclusions, granularity, and applicability | Generalized conclusion from a narrower subject |
| Independently reviewable | Preserves enough provenance, method, uncertainty, and contrary evidence for independent judgment | Self-validating summary or conclusion without inspectable support |
| Authority-distinct | Retains the difference among normative, approved future, advisory, historical, exploratory, and other recorded conditions | Authority normalized or upgraded by use |

### 9.2 Evidence and non-evidence distinctions

- An **assertion** states something; it is not evidence of its own truth.
- **Confidence** expresses belief; it does not increase source authority or
  evidence coverage.
- A **Recommendation** proposes consideration; it does not prove need,
  correctness, or authorization.
- A **Conclusion** synthesizes supported results; it cannot be used upstream as
  a substitute for the evidence from which it derives.
- A **review disposition** records independent judgment within its scope; it
  does not prove implementation or operational conformance.
- **Implemented behavior** is evidence about realization only within an
  attributable scope; it cannot override canonical architecture.
- **Prototype alignment** is bounded exploratory evidence; it is not proof of
  product implementation conformance.

### 9.3 Sufficiency decision

Evidence sufficiency is evaluated per claim and criterion. A body of evidence
may be sufficient for bounded readiness and insufficient for product-wide or
later-work readiness. Missing evidence produces `Indeterminate`, not an assumed
Pass. Conflicting authoritative evidence produces a Finding and limitation
until the conflict is separately resolved under appropriate authority.

## 10. Validation-Method Taxonomy

| Method | Architectural operation | Valid output | Explicit boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source inspection | Examine attributable content, scope, status, definitions, and exclusions | Evidence inventory and bounded observations | No source editing or authority inference |
| Authority comparison | Compare claimed use with recorded source and artifact authority conditions | Authority-alignment result or conflict finding | No grant, normalization, consolidation, or transition |
| Semantic ownership analysis | Map each responsibility to its primary owner and non-owners | Ownership uniqueness and boundary result | No organizational, runtime, or implementation assignment |
| Dependency analysis | Examine direction, prerequisite, authority, conditionality, and semantic cycles | Dependency-integrity result and open prerequisites | No execution, delivery, or scheduling sequence |
| Contradiction analysis | Compare propositions that apply to the same scope for incompatible meaning | Confirmed compatibility, contradiction Finding, or indeterminate relation | No silent reconciliation or source precedence invention |
| Traceability analysis | Follow claim-to-source and claim-to-result relationships in both directions | Complete, broken, ambiguous, or bounded trace result | No repository tooling or mechanical link requirement |
| Coverage analysis | Compare declared scope with represented responsibilities, capabilities, handoffs, sources, or dimensions | Bounded coverage result and explicit omissions | Absence is not automatically a defect or Gap |
| Gap/Risk reconciliation | Verify that validation references preserve canonical M8.4 object state and residual exposure | Preservation result and mismatch Finding | No resolution, treatment, acceptance, reprioritization, or work creation |
| Review-record verification | Compare subject identity, authority, independence, findings, and disposition with the relied-upon review | Attributable review-eligibility result | Review evidence cannot replace the reviewed subject |
| Exact-revision verification | Compare the named subject identity with the identity actually evaluated | Match or mismatch result | No tool, command, repository, or automation prescription |

Methods may be combined, but each result must identify which method supports
which criterion. Method combination does not increase authority or erase the
limitations of any constituent method.

## 11. Readiness Classification Model

### 11.1 Classification boundary

Readiness classes are architectural assessment labels. They are not canonical
M7 lifecycle states, review dispositions, approvals, gates, stage statuses, or
work authorizations. A repository status can change only through its existing
governance authority, regardless of an M8.6 readiness class.

### 11.2 Deterministic classes

Classification first fixes one subject, assessed scope, intended architectural
readiness purpose, and evidence boundary. A missing or ambiguous purpose makes
the claim unsupported and therefore R0. The following procedure is applied in
order and stops at the first satisfied rule:

1. Assign **R0** when any applicable architectural blocker in Section 12 is
   present, evidence is insufficient to bound the affected scope, or the
   subject, scope, purpose, or evidence boundary is not fixed.
2. Otherwise assign **R2** when the claim is supported only while one or more
   explicit unresolved architectural conditions, prerequisites, or limitations
   continue to hold. A disclosed non-blocking residual risk alone does not make
   a conclusion conditional; R2 requires a condition on which claim support
   actually depends.
3. Otherwise assign **R3** only when the named purpose is submission of the
   complete fixed M8.1–M8.6 package to a separately governed M8.7 Integrated
   Architecture Review and all integrated-scope criteria pass.
4. Otherwise assign **R4** only when the named purpose is support for later
   engineering-architecture definition, all criteria for its explicitly named
   architectural scope pass, and that scope's ownership, dependencies,
   authority boundaries, limitations, and residual risks are complete enough
   to bound the engineering-architecture subject.
5. Otherwise assign **R1** when all applicable criteria pass for the explicitly
   bounded architectural scope and purpose, and every exclusion, limitation,
   and non-blocking residual risk remains visible.

| Class | Exclusive condition after precedence | Permitted claim | Explicit exclusion and prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R0 — Not Ready | Rule 1 applies | The named subject has not demonstrated readiness for the stated purpose | R1–R4 are excluded; R0 is not rejection, cancellation, correction authority, or global product failure |
| R1 — Ready Only Within Bounded Scope | Rules 1–4 do not apply and Rule 5 applies | The named subject is architecturally ready only for the stated scope and purpose | R0, R2, R3, and R4 are excluded; no readiness outside the scope, Risk acceptance, or permission for work follows |
| R2 — Conditionally Ready | Rule 1 does not apply and Rule 2 applies | The named subject demonstrates conditional architectural readiness for the stated purpose while every stated support condition remains explicit | R0, R1, R3, and R4 are excluded; no automatic reclassification or authority to satisfy a condition follows |
| R3 — Ready for Integrated Architecture Review | Rules 1–2 do not apply and Rule 3 applies | The fixed package is supportable as input to a separately governed M8.7 Integrated Architecture Review | R0, R1, R2, and R4 are excluded; M8.7 has not opened or passed, and no M8 completion, canonicalization, or implementation readiness follows |
| R4 — Ready for Later Engineering Architecture | Rules 1–3 do not apply and Rule 4 applies | The architecture can support separately governed engineering-architecture definition within the named scope | R0–R3 are excluded; no Engineering Architecture approval or authorization, implementation approval, delivery priority, backlog authority, or implementation work follows |

Exactly one class is therefore assigned to each conclusion. Where multiple
purposes are evaluated, each purpose receives its own conclusion and the
procedure is applied independently. R1 is the bounded-purpose fallback, not a
parent class for R2–R4. The highest number is not a maturity score, no class
automatically promotes to another, and a later change in architectural evidence
or conditions requires reclassification through the complete procedure.

## 12. Blocking-Condition Model

### 12.1 Meaning of block

A blocking condition prevents a stated readiness claim from being supported.
It does not create a governance gate, stop or authorize work by itself, assign a
priority, resolve a Gap, accept a Risk, or prescribe a correction. Existing M8.4
blocking classifications retain only their canonical scope; M8.6 neither
expands nor narrows them.

### 12.2 Universal architectural blockers

Any of the following blocks every readiness claim whose scope it materially
affects:

- subject identity or revision is not fixed and attributable;
- an applicable criterion lacks an authoritative basis;
- a material claim has no sufficient evidence;
- a relevant result is `Fail` or `Indeterminate` and the affected scope cannot
  be validly excluded;
- canonical sources contradict one another within the claim scope without an
  authoritative resolution;
- semantic ownership is duplicate, absent, transferred, or materially
  ambiguous;
- provenance, uncertainty, human authority, or source authority is lost;
- a required architectural dependency or prerequisite lacks an attributable
  basis or is treated as satisfied by assumption;
- a material M8.4 Gap, Risk, limitation, or residual exposure is hidden,
  reclassified, silently disposed, or used as implicit authorization;
- architecture depends for meaning on an implementation or delivery choice; or
- the claim collapses architectural readiness into approval, canonicalization,
  transition, or work authority.

### 12.3 Purpose-specific blockers

| Intended readiness claim | Additional blocking conditions |
| --- | --- |
| M8.7 Integrated Architecture Review | Any required M8.1–M8.6 subject is not fixed and attributable; any of RD-01–RD-12 is unevaluated for the integrated scope; cross-artifact traceability is materially broken; an unresolved contradiction prevents integrated evaluation; M8.4 conditions or M8.5 alternatives are presented as already disposed or selected |
| Later Engineering Architecture | The proposed engineering-architecture scope lacks a canonical product responsibility or unique semantic owner; required product dependencies or handoff meaning are absent; source or human authority boundaries are ambiguous; the claim would rely on an evolution option not established as architectural authority; limitations prevent a bounded architectural contract |
| Implementation Architecture | Required product and engineering architectural meaning is absent, contradictory, lacks an attributable authority basis, or is not traceable; ownership and authority boundaries cannot constrain realization; relevant residual exposures are hidden; implementation form would be asked to decide canonical product meaning |
| Architectural support for Implementation Work | Applicable product, engineering, or implementation architectural meaning is absent, contradictory, or not traceable for the assessed scope; human or source-authority boundaries are unclear; evidence cannot demonstrate architectural conformance boundaries; the readiness claim depends on implementation form to supply missing architectural meaning |

M8.6 may identify these architectural blockers and classify readiness. It cannot
authorize M8.7, later engineering architecture, implementation architecture, or
implementation work, and it does not define the governance or delivery process
for any of them.

### 12.4 Separate governance eligibility

Governance eligibility is evaluated outside M8.6 by the authority applicable to
the later action. It may include required approvals, canonical status, opening
or transition authority, and permission to begin later work. Those conditions
are not architectural blockers and do not change an M8.6 readiness class.

Architectural readiness does not grant governance eligibility or authorization.
Governance eligibility does not supply missing evidence, cure an architectural
blocker, or alter architectural readiness. A later action may require both a
supporting M8.6 readiness conclusion and separate governance eligibility, but
the two determinations remain distinct, are made under different authority, and
cannot substitute for one another. M8.6 owns architectural-readiness assessment
only.

## 13. Limitation and Residual-Risk Handling

### 13.1 Limitation classes

| Limitation class | Required treatment in a conclusion | Non-authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unresolved evidence | Identify missing, conflicting, stale, or inaccessible evidence and affected claims | Cannot assume a Pass or create evidence |
| Unimplemented approved architecture | Separate architectural authority from realization evidence and bound any conformance claim | Cannot imply implementation failure, delivery priority, or work authorization |
| Product-validation gap | Preserve the exact M8.4 Gap and the product claim it constrains | Cannot resolve, narrow, reprioritize, or assign the Gap |
| Maturity gap | State which source or capability maturity limits inference | Cannot normalize approved future, exploratory, advisory, or prototype meaning into current authority |
| Procedural limitation | Identify what independent judgment or governed record is unavailable | Cannot invent a review protocol, waive independence, or create a lifecycle state |
| Tooling limitation | State which evidence or analysis cannot be sufficiently reproduced or inspected | Cannot prescribe tooling, automation, scripts, CI, or repository mechanics |
| External dependency | Identify external source, human authority, operational reality, or system-of-record dependency and its boundary | Cannot absorb external ownership or treat unavailability as permission |

### 13.2 Residual-risk rule

Every readiness conclusion must list the relevant canonical M8.4 Risks and any
validation-stage residual risks, their evidence, uncertainty, affected scope,
current architectural controls, and limitations. It must state explicitly that
documentation and visibility are not acceptance or treatment.

A readiness class may coexist with residual risk only when the class's bounded
claim remains supported and the risk does not constitute an applicable blocker.
That statement is an assessment of claim support, not Risk acceptance. Risk
authority remains external.

## 14. Readiness Traceability Model

### 14.1 Required chain

Every readiness disposition must be traceable through this complete chain:

```text
Validation subject
    → Validation claim
        → Validation criterion
            → Validation evidence
                → Validation method
                    → Validation result
                        → Finding, if any
                            → Limitation, if any
                                → Residual risk, if any
                                    → Readiness conclusion
                                        → Readiness disposition
```

An absent optional object must be stated as absent with justification; it cannot
simply disappear from traceability.

### 14.2 Trace record requirements

For each link, the validation record must preserve:

- stable local identity within the assessment;
- upstream and downstream object identities;
- exact subject and evidence scope;
- source identity and authority condition;
- criterion origin;
- method and its limitation;
- result and uncertainty;
- contrary or missing evidence;
- effect on the conclusion; and
- explicit non-authority of the disposition.

Traceability is semantic and evidentiary. This document defines no repository
format, schema, identifier technology, automation, or tool.

### 14.3 Bidirectional checks

- Forward tracing confirms that no evidence or result is orphaned from a claim.
- Backward tracing confirms that every disposition statement is supported by a
  conclusion, results, criteria, evidence, and methods.
- Cross tracing confirms that shared evidence is applied only within compatible
  scope and authority.
- Contrary tracing confirms that failures, limitations, and residual risks are
  not omitted from synthesis.

dependency, authority, and traceability checks.
