# 1. Purpose

M8.2 defines the architectural capability model through which the existing
Management OS corpus forms one coherent product architecture.

It answers:

- which major product capabilities participate in Management OS;
- which architectural responsibility identifies each capability;
- which existing source uniquely owns each semantic scope;
- how capabilities compose and depend on one another without transferring
  ownership or authority;
- where apparent duplication is composition, projection, constraint, or a
  genuinely unresolved finding.

M8.2 does not redesign a capability or define how information crosses a
capability boundary. Those questions remain source-owned or reserved for later
authorized work.

# 3. Capability Identity Model

## 3.1 Definition

An **architectural capability** is a stable product ability to fulfill one
bounded operational responsibility under an identifiable semantic owner.

A capability identity consists of:

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Capability outcome | The product ability made available to the owner or another product responsibility. |
| Semantic contract | The concepts, distinctions, rules, and authority boundaries that give the ability meaning. |
| Primary semantic owner | The existing authoritative source, or explicitly partitioned source set, that owns that contract. |
| Product-layer placement | The M8.1 responsibility view in which the capability primarily participates. |
| Non-ownership boundary | The meaning, records, judgment, authority, or external action the capability cannot absorb. |
| Composition role | The reason the capability cooperates with other capabilities while retaining its own contract. |

## 3.2 Identity tests

A subject is a distinct capability only when all of these tests pass:

1. it produces a stable product ability rather than merely describing a screen,
   document, implementation component, provider, phase, or governance function;
2. its semantic contract can be distinguished from adjacent contracts;
3. an existing authoritative source owns that contract within a declared scope;
4. its cooperation with another capability does not require either capability
   to take ownership of the other's meaning;
5. its identity remains valid independently of implementation state or platform.

When a subject fails these tests, it is classified as a framing concern,
cross-cutting constraint, experience composition, projection, implementation
responsibility, external authority, or historical label rather than as another
semantic capability.

## 3.3 Capability versus other architectural objects

| Object | Relationship to capability identity |
| --- | --- |
| Product layer | A responsibility view in which capabilities participate; not a capability container or owner. |
| Authoritative source | Owns declared meaning; a document path is not itself the capability. |
| Module or page | May realize or present capability behavior; does not establish semantic ownership. |
| Record or aggregate | May be owned by a capability source; does not represent the complete capability. |
| Experience architecture | Composes or presents capability meaning without taking semantic ownership from source capabilities. |
| Cross-cutting plane | Constrains every applicable capability without becoming another ordinary capability. |
| Governance plane | Governs architectural change externally; owns no product capability. |
| Runtime or implementation | May later realize approved capability meaning; cannot define that meaning. |

# 4. Capability Ownership Model

## 4.1 Ownership distinctions

| Ownership or authority type | Owns | Does not own through this model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability semantic ownership | The bounded product meaning and responsibility contract of a capability. | Governance legitimacy, organizational accountability, source records outside scope, runtime execution, or implementation. |
| Source authority | The declared concepts, states, validation, mutation, retention, or policy within an authoritative source's scope. | Product-wide authority merely because another capability references the source. |
| Governance authority | Direction, review, judgment, transition, and legitimate architecture evolution under canonical M7. | Product behavior, capability semantics, or runtime control. |
| Organizational ownership | Real roles, decision rights, accountability, and action in the organization. | Software authority or capability meaning. |
| Human authority | Judgment, approval, responsibility, correction, and real-world consequence. | An automatable capability contract. |
| Runtime responsibility | Execution of an approved capability under applicable contracts and constraints. | Product truth, source meaning, approval, or semantic ownership. |
| Implementation responsibility | Technical realization and maintenance within separately approved work. | Architectural authority or permission to reinterpret a capability. |

## 4.2 Unique semantic ownership rule

Every capability has one identifiable primary semantic owner for each owned
meaning. A capability may cite a source set only when the members own explicit,
non-overlapping parts of a composite contract. The set does not merge sources,
create joint universal authority, or allow one member to govern another's scope.

Supporting, constraining, projecting, and consuming sources are not co-owners.
Authority does not propagate through dependency, reference, presentation,
recommendation, recurrence, similarity, convenience, or implementation.

# 7. Capability Interaction Model

## 7.1 Ownership-preserving cooperation

Capabilities cooperate through semantic composition. Composition means one
capability may depend on, reference, present, constrain, prepare for, or derive
bounded context from another capability while the originating owner retains its
meaning, state, validation, mutation, retention, and authority.

| Composition relation | Architectural meaning | Ownership safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference | A capability relates its work to a source-owned subject. | Reference creates no copy of source authority. |
| Context use | A capability uses authorized context within its own decision or reasoning boundary. | Context does not become truth or ownership in the receiving capability. |
| Projection | An experience capability presents source-owned meaning. | Presentation cannot change state, evidence weight, priority authority, or access. |
| Preparation | Assistance or orchestration prepares a proposal for an owning capability or human authority. | Preparation cannot imply approval or perform source mutation. |
| Constraint | A cross-cutting or source-owned rule limits capability behavior. | Constraint application does not transfer the constrained capability's semantics. |
| Historical relevance | Memory makes prior experience relevant to current work. | Prior experience remains historical context, not current Evidence. |
| Candidate formation | One capability prepares a candidate for another capability's governed review. | Candidate creation does not perform promotion, publication, or acceptance. |

These are architectural relationship classes, not messages, events, payloads,
APIs, state transitions, orchestration steps, or handoff protocols. M8.3 remains
responsible for any later authorized information, evidence, authority, and
handoff architecture.

## 8.3 Feedback without ownership cycles

Operational learning may make verified historical experience relevant to future
Investigation. This creates a relevance relationship, not a reverse authority
dependency: current Evidence and current judgment remain authoritative for the
current situation, while Memory retains historical context ownership.

Likewise, execution review may inform an Improvement Proposal, and approved
Knowledge may inform an SOP, without allowing the later artifact to rewrite the
earlier source or the earlier source to approve the later artifact.

# 9. Composition Principles

1. **One product, bounded capabilities.** Product coherence comes from composed
   responsibilities, not from one universal aggregate or universal owner.
2. **One meaning, one scoped owner.** Every semantic scope has one identifiable
   owner or an explicit non-overlapping source partition.
3. **Composition is not consolidation.** A coherent product view may relate many
   sources without merging documents, records, lifecycles, or authority.
4. **Projection is not ownership.** Home, Board, Workbench, mobile, and other
   experiences cannot acquire the meaning they present.
5. **Context is not truth.** Personal, operational, historical, modeled, and
   workspace context retain distinct provenance and authority limits.
6. **Preparation is not action.** Inquiry, orchestration, AI assistance, impact
   analysis, and transition coordination cannot imply approval or source
   mutation.
7. **Centrality is bounded.** Matter, Investigation, Case method, and Transition
   may coordinate broad work without becoming universal record owners.
8. **Dependency is non-transitive for authority.** Depending on an authoritative
   capability does not confer its authority on the dependent capability.
9. **History informs but does not govern the present.** Memory, Reviews,
   Insights, and Hypotheses cannot become current Evidence or decision authority
   through recurrence.
10. **External reality remains external.** No capability owns the real
    Operational System, organization, human accountability, or external system
    merely by representing or referencing it.
11. **Constraints do not become capabilities.** Trust and governance constrain
    product behavior and architecture evolution without becoming ordinary
    product services or semantic owners.
12. **Implementation cannot redefine composition.** Technical realization must
    follow separately approved capability meaning and ownership.

# 10. Ownership Invariants

1. Product Purpose and System Identity remain architectural-object-level
   framing concerns rather than P1- or P2-local capabilities.
2. Operational Matter uniquely owns enduring situation identity and macro-state.
3. Operational Investigation uniquely owns repeatable inquiry episodes and
   Investigation Context.
4. Operational Case Architecture owns the learning-and-change method, not Matter
   or Investigation identity.
5. The real Operational System remains external; the Operational Operating
   Model owns only its governed representation within scope.
6. Evidence, reasoning, hypothesis, modeled output, decision, execution,
   observed outcome, memory, and knowledge remain distinguishable.
7. Human judgment and approval remain authoritative; no capability can infer
   them from confidence, recurrence, urgency, or elapsed time.
8. Routine systems retain their records and domain semantics when referenced by
   Matter, Investigation, Transition, Memory, experience, or AI.
9. Operational Memory cannot rewrite source history or promote itself into
   Knowledge.
10. Knowledge Evolution cannot silently publish, invalidate, supersede, or turn
    experience into procedure without the owning approval path.
11. AI assistance cannot become semantic, mutation, decision, review,
    transition, organizational, or governance authority.
12. Identity and access constraints cannot create organizational authority or
    product meaning.
13. Experience composition and platform allocation cannot change capability
    availability, state, evidence weight, or source authority through
    presentation.
14. Governance remains external to product capability architecture.
15. Source authority conditions remain those recorded by their sources and
    canonical M8.1; M8.2 neither upgrades nor normalizes them.
