# 1. Purpose

M8.3 defines how product-wide information, evidence, context, uncertainty,
authority, and responsibility may cross Management OS capability boundaries
without transferring the semantic ownership established by canonical M8.2.

It answers:

- what kind of meaning is crossing a boundary;
- which capability owns that meaning before, during, and after interaction;
- what receiving capabilities may rely on, interpret, or produce;
- what provenance, uncertainty, authority, and traceability must remain visible;
- what constitutes a valid handoff without implying approval or mutation;
- where interruption, refusal, ambiguity, or missing authority stops progress;
- which product-wide questions remain unresolved rather than silently decided.

M8.3 describes semantic relations and architectural obligations. References to
movement, propagation, admission, return, interruption, and handoff express
meaning and responsibility only. They do not specify transport, timing,
sequence, workflow, message, event, state machine, interface, or technical
realization.

# 3. Architectural Vocabulary

The following kinds are distinct even when they concern the same subject.

| Kind | Architectural meaning | Primary semantic owner | May become another kind only when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Information | A bounded contribution, reference, observation, representation, or statement made available for legitimate use. It is not true, relevant, or evidentiary merely because it is available. | The source capability retains its source meaning; C1 owns original owner-contribution boundaries where applicable. | A receiving owner performs the explicit semantic act required for the new kind while preserving the source relationship. |
| Evidence | Information admitted for a declared question or judgment with classification, provenance, relevance, limitations, and authorized reference intact. | C9 Case Evidence governance owns evidence classification and influence; referenced source capabilities retain their records. | Evidence may support interpretation or decision, but admission never converts it directly into either. |
| Interpretation | A reasoned meaning attributed to information or evidence under explicit epistemic separation. | C12 Operational reasoning within its grammar; source-specific interpretations remain with their owning sources. | It is explicitly formed, attributable, challengeable, and not presented as observation or evidence. |
| Hypothesis | A contestable explanatory possibility that can organize inquiry without becoming fact. | C12 Operational reasoning. | It is explicitly proposed and remains distinguishable from evidence, interpretation, decision, and outcome. |
| Recommendation | A bounded, explainable proposed course, question, emphasis, or consideration offered without decision authority. | The capability authorized to prepare it within its source-owned contract; C19 assistance does not become its semantic owner by generating or presenting it. | A human with applicable authority explicitly adopts, rejects, or modifies it; adoption creates a decision, not a promoted recommendation. |
| Decision | Explicit accountable human judgment with rationale, relevant evidence relationship, authority boundary, and consequences preserved. | C14 Owner decision preservation under its partitioned M8.2 owners; accountable human authority remains external. | It is explicitly made by the authorized person. No recommendation, silence, continuation, automation, or downstream use is a decision. |
| Execution intent | The decision-authorized intended change and bounded responsibility prepared for source-owned execution. | C16 Operational Transition owns decision-to-execution continuity; the execution capability owns its own executable record and action scope. | An authoritative decision exists and the receiving execution source accepts only the responsibility it owns. |
| Execution outcome | An attributable observation of what occurred in source-owned execution, including incompleteness, variance, and residual uncertainty. | The source execution capability owns its execution record; C16 owns the outcome relationship to decision and intended condition. | It is returned with source identity, observation basis, scope, and uncertainty; it is not automatically a verified effect or learning. |
| Learning | A bounded conclusion about an episode, pattern, adaptation, or method whose evidence, uncertainty, and review boundary remain visible. | C8 owns learning-and-change method; C17 owns historical episode relationships and Experience Patterns within their distinct scopes. | Required reflection, comparison, or validation occurs under the owning capability; recurrence alone is insufficient. |
| Knowledge | Versioned, reviewable organizational meaning with challenge conditions, semantic history, and publication authority. | C18 Knowledge evolution. | A Knowledge Review Candidate is explicitly reviewed and promoted under C18 authority; learning is not knowledge by accumulation. |

These kinds form no automatic ladder. Movement between capabilities does not
promote a kind, increase its truth status, or transfer ownership. A change of
kind is a new, attributable semantic act owned by the capability authorized to
make that distinction.

# 4. Product-Wide Information-Flow Model

## 4.1 Flow unit

An architectural flow unit is meaning made available across a capability
boundary with enough accompanying context to prevent silent reinterpretation.
It is characterized conceptually by:

- its semantic kind from Section 3;
- the subject and bounded question to which it applies;
- its source capability and source authority;
- its current semantic owner;
- its provenance and relevant derivation relationship;
- its uncertainty, limitations, freshness, and sensitivity where applicable;
- the authority under which it may be used;
- the intended receiving responsibility;
- explicit non-authority and prohibited reinterpretations.

This characterization is an architectural completeness test, not a data model,
schema, payload, interface, or storage contract.

## 4.2 Flow relations

| Relation | Meaning | Ownership result | Invalid inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reference | A capability makes source-owned meaning available for bounded use. | Source ownership remains unchanged. | Availability means truth, evidence, consent, or mutation permission. |
| Admit | Evidence governance recognizes information as Evidence for a declared scope. | C9 owns evidence classification; the source retains its record. | Admission validates the source universally or approves an interpretation. |
| Interpret | Reasoning forms attributable meaning from information or Evidence. | C12 owns the interpretation; source and Evidence ownership remain unchanged. | Interpretation overwrites or corrects its inputs. |
| Contextualize | A capability applies authorized context to its own responsibility. | Context owner and receiving semantic owner retain separate scopes. | Context becomes identity, truth, authority, or permanent source mutation. |
| Recommend | An authorized capability prepares a bounded proposal. | The recommending contract remains source-owned; human decision authority remains external. | Recommendation is approval, instruction, or execution intent. |
| Decide | An authorized human makes explicit judgment and C14 preserves it. | Human accountability and C14 preservation remain distinct. | System continuation, selection, silence, or confidence constitutes approval. |
| Prepare | A capability makes decision-authorized meaning suitable for another owner. | Preparing capability owns preparation; receiver owns only its accepted scope. | Preparation creates or mutates a receiver-owned record. |
| Accept responsibility | A receiving capability explicitly takes responsibility within its existing contract. | No upstream meaning or authority transfers; only the receiver's owned responsibility begins. | Receipt alone is acceptance or an assignment of new authority. |
| Return outcome | A source-owned capability makes an execution observation available. | Execution record stays source-owned; C16 owns continuity to intent and outcome. | Reported completion proves effect, causality, or stabilization. |
| Relate historically | Memory relates an episode to prior episodes or patterns. | C17 owns relevance and historical relationship, not current truth. | Similarity proves cause or authorizes reuse. |
| Propose promotion | Learning is presented as a Knowledge Review Candidate. | Learning and candidate provenance remain visible; C18 owns review and promotion. | Repetition or candidate creation publishes knowledge. |

## 4.3 Product-wide direction

The architecture permits a non-linear, challengeable direction of meaning:

```text
owner contribution and source reference
        ↓ bounded availability
Matter / Operational System / Investigation context
        ↓ authorized reference
Evidence admission ↔ Inquiry and acquisition need
        ↓ evidence remains distinct
Interpretation ↔ Hypothesis ↔ Scenario comparison
        ↓ explicit human judgment only
Decision → Impact consideration → Execution intent
        ↓ source-owned responsibility acceptance
Execution observation → Outcome relationship → Verification
        ↓ bounded reflection
Learning → Memory relationship → Knowledge Review Candidate
        ↓ explicit knowledge review only
Versioned knowledge
```

Arrows mean permitted semantic dependency, not time order, workflow, transport,
automatic progression, completeness, authority hierarchy, or implementation.
Challenge, correction, interruption, and return to an earlier question remain
possible wherever an owning capability permits them.

# 6. Evidence Lifecycle Across Capabilities

The evidence lifecycle is a semantic accountability arc, not a product
workflow or record lifecycle.

| Architectural condition | Owning responsibility | Required preservation | Explicit non-effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potentially relevant information | Source capability; C10 or C11 may identify need | Source, question, access boundary, uncertainty, and reason sought | Not Evidence; no acquisition or access authority implied |
| Authorized reference | Source capability and applicable identity/access constraints | Source identity, scope, freshness, sensitivity, and correction relationship | Reference does not copy ownership or establish relevance |
| Evidence admission | C9 | Evidence class, declared use, provenance, limitations, qualitative influence, and source relationship | No truth declaration, universal validation, or approval |
| Evidentiary use | Receiving reasoning, inquiry, scenario, impact, decision, or verification capability within scope | Which Evidence informed which interpretation, hypothesis, comparison, decision, or verification | Use does not mutate Evidence or erase contrary material |
| Challenge or correction | Source owner and C9 within their distinct scopes | Original attribution, challenge basis, correction status, affected reliance, and unresolved uncertainty | No silent rewrite of prior interpretation or decision |
| Snapshot or summary | C9 | Authorized reference, derivation, scope, omitted limitations, and relationship to source Evidence | Summary is not source replacement or independent Evidence by default |
| Historical relevance | C17 | Episode boundary, evidence relationship, decomposed confidence, and non-causal status | Historical Evidence is not automatically current Evidence |
| Learning support | C8 or C17 within scope | Outcome, verification, contrary Evidence, uncertainty, and applicability boundary | Learning is not Knowledge or universal rule |
| Knowledge candidacy | C18 | Learning provenance, evidence basis, challenge conditions, uncertainty, and semantic history | Candidate creation does not publish or approve Knowledge |

Evidence may cease to be applicable, become contested, or require correction,
but its prior role remains traceable. A later correction changes what may now be
relied upon; it does not erase that earlier reasoning or decisions used the
then-available Evidence.

## 7.1 Context classes

| Context | Primary owner | Propagation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matter context | C4 | Carries situation identity and continuity, not Investigation work or domain records. |
| Operational System context | C5 and C6 within distinct scopes | Carries bounded real-system representation and governed topology, not real organizational authority. |
| Investigation Context | C7 | Carries episode-specific question and working boundary, not universal Matter or Case state. |
| Case method context | C8 | Carries learning-and-change method meaning, not Matter or Investigation identity. |
| Evidence context | C9 | Carries declared evidentiary use, provenance, limitations, and influence, not source ownership. |
| Decision context | C14 | Carries explicit judgment, rationale, evidence relationship, authority, and consequences, not execution ownership. |
| Transition context | C16 | Carries decision-to-intent-to-outcome continuity, not source execution records. |
| Historical context | C17 | Carries episode relationships, relevance, and confidence, not current truth. |
| Knowledge context | C18 | Carries version, applicability, challenge conditions, and semantic history, not decision authority. |
| Owner and workspace context | C3 | Carries approved personal context and emphasis only within partitioned scope, not identity or organizational knowledge. |

## 7.2 Bounded transition rule

A context transition occurs when one capability uses another capability's
context to exercise its own responsibility. The receiver must:

1. identify the originating context and its owner;
2. state the receiving responsibility and bounded question;
3. preserve distinctions between source context and local interpretation;
4. carry applicable provenance, uncertainty, freshness, sensitivity, and
   authority limitations;
5. avoid merging identities, states, records, or scopes;
6. make any locally produced meaning attributable to the receiving owner;
7. retain a traceable relationship back to the originating context;
8. stop or qualify use when the context is absent, ambiguous, stale,
   unauthorized, or incompatible.

Context propagation is selective, not total. Least necessary context and
purpose limitation apply across capability boundaries. Presentation and AI
assistance may shape attention to context but cannot enlarge its authority or
silently make it durable.

# 8. Authority-Preserving Handoff Model

## 8.1 Definition

An architectural handoff is an attributable boundary relation in which one
capability makes meaning and a bounded responsibility request available to
another capability, and the receiver may explicitly accept responsibility only
within its pre-existing semantic contract.

A handoff transfers neither semantic ownership nor source authority. It relates
two separately owned responsibilities.

## 8.2 Handoff completeness

A handoff is architecturally complete only when all applicable elements are
explicit:

| Element | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Origin | Which capability and source authority make the meaning available? |
| Semantic kind | Is it information, Evidence, interpretation, hypothesis, recommendation, decision, execution intent, outcome, learning, or Knowledge? |
| Subject and scope | What bounded object, question, episode, decision, or intended condition does it concern? |
| Provenance | What origin and derivation must remain attributable? |
| Uncertainty | What is unknown, assumed, contested, stale, incomplete, or modeled? |
| Authority | Who may rely, decide, accept responsibility, correct, or refuse, and within what scope? |
| Requested responsibility | Which existing receiving capability responsibility is requested? |
| Acceptance condition | What explicit recognition establishes that the receiver has taken only its owned responsibility? |
| Non-transfer boundary | Which meaning, records, authority, and accountability remain with the origin or outside the product? |
| Completion or return meaning | What may the receiver make available without claiming effect beyond its evidence? |
| Interruption condition | What ambiguity, missing authority, conflict, or failure prevents valid continuation? |

This is a semantic contract test, not a payload specification or protocol.

## 8.3 Handoff states as architectural conditions

The following labels describe responsibility conditions, not runtime states or
a lifecycle:

- **prepared:** the origin has made a bounded request and supporting meaning
  attributable;
- **accepted:** the receiver explicitly recognizes responsibility within its
  existing contract;
- **qualified:** the receiver can proceed only with declared limitations;
- **declined:** the receiver does not possess the responsibility, authority, or
  sufficient basis;
- **interrupted:** required identity, context, provenance, uncertainty, or
  authority is missing or contradictory;
- **returned:** the receiver makes its owned result, outcome, limitation, or
  unresolved condition available to the origin or another authorized consumer.

No condition is inferred from elapsed time, silence, visibility, presentation,
automation, or technical receipt.

# 9. Semantic Ownership During Interaction

1. The origin owns the meaning it produces within its canonical M8.2 contract.
2. The receiver owns only the new meaning it explicitly produces within its own
   contract.
3. A reference retains its source owner even when widely reused.
4. A derived interpretation owns neither its Evidence nor the source record.
5. Evidence classification does not acquire source-record ownership.
6. Presentation owns composition and attention, not presented domain semantics.
7. Orchestration owns preparation and explainable responsibility coordination,
   not Evidence, execution records, approval, or receiver responsibility.
8. Automation and C19 assistance act inside the invoking owner's contract and
   acquire no semantic, human, organizational, or governance authority.
9. Composition creates a coherent product experience without creating a
   composite universal owner.
10. Correction is performed by the owner authorized to correct the affected
    meaning; downstream consumers preserve the relationship to the correction
    rather than silently rewriting history.

Ownership transfer through orchestration, presentation, automation, or
composition is prohibited.

# 10. Decision-State Propagation

Decision-state propagation preserves the distinction among preparation,
recommendation, judgment, intent, execution, outcome, and learning.

| Decision-related meaning | Owner | Permitted downstream reliance | Prohibited conversion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decision question | C7, C10, or owning source within scope | Frames inquiry, Evidence, reasoning, or scenario work | Does not imply a need to decide or a preferred answer |
| Recommendation | Authorized recommending capability; C19 may assist | May be considered, challenged, modified, or rejected | Cannot become a decision through display, confidence, automation, or silence |
| Explicit decision | C14 preservation plus external accountable human authority | May constrain Impact Review and prepare Transition | Cannot be inferred, overwritten downstream, or treated as execution |
| Decision-relative impact | C15 | May inform owner disposition or bounded adaptation consideration | Cannot reconsider the decision or authorize execution |
| Execution intent | C16 continuity plus source-owned receiving contract | May request receiver-owned responsibility | Cannot create execution records or expand the decision's authority |
| Execution observation | Source execution owner | May inform C16 outcome continuity and C8 verification | Cannot prove intended effect, causality, stabilization, or learning |
| Stabilization or verification judgment | C16 or C8 within distinct scope | May inform Matter continuity, Memory, and learning | Cannot silently close Matter, publish Knowledge, or erase uncertainty |

Any material change to an explicit decision requires another attributable human
judgment. Downstream interpretation, new Evidence, execution variance, or
automation may expose a need for reconsideration but cannot mutate the preserved
decision.

# 11. Provenance, Uncertainty, and Traceability

## 11.1 Provenance propagation

Provenance accompanies every cross-capability use sufficiently to answer:

- who or what authoritative source originated the meaning;
- which capability owns its semantic kind;
- whether it is original, summarized, interpreted, modeled, recommended,
  decided, observed, learned, or promoted;
- what prior meaning it derives from without replacing;
- what correction, challenge, review, or version relationship applies;
- what access, sensitivity, purpose, and freshness constraints remain.

Derivation adds provenance; it does not replace origin. Summaries, projections,
AI-produced preparations, scenarios, recommendations, and historical patterns
must remain distinguishable from their sources.

## 11.2 Uncertainty propagation

Uncertainty is owned with the meaning to which it applies and remains visible
when that meaning crosses a boundary. Relevant forms include:

- missing or unavailable information;
- source or observation uncertainty;
- relevance and evidentiary limitations;
- interpretive uncertainty and competing hypotheses;
- assumption and model uncertainty;
- recommendation limitations;
- execution variance and incomplete observation;
- causal and outcome uncertainty;
- historical relevance and pattern-confidence uncertainty;
- knowledge applicability and challenge conditions.

A receiver may refine uncertainty only within its own semantic responsibility.
It must preserve upstream uncertainty and distinguish the refinement from the
source. Confidence, recurrence, model output, successful presentation, or
downstream use cannot erase uncertainty.

## 11.3 Traceability model

Traceability is the product-wide ability to reconstruct the attributable
semantic relationships among:

```text
source information
  ↕ authorized reference and correction
Evidence admission
  ↕ evidentiary use and challenge
interpretation / hypothesis / scenario / recommendation
  ↕ explicit rationale and uncertainty
decision
  ↕ decision authority and intended condition
execution intent / source-owned execution / observation
  ↕ variance, residual uncertainty, and verification
learning / historical relevance / Knowledge Review Candidate
  ↕ review, challenge conditions, and versioned Knowledge
```

Traceability preserves relationships, not copies or a universal record. Each
capability retains its owned meaning. A trace must expose semantic kind,
ownership, provenance, derivation, authority, uncertainty, correction, and
non-transfer boundaries relevant to the relationship.

# 12. Trust-Preservation Rules

The cross-cutting M8.1 Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety, and Quality
plane constrains every flow and handoff:

1. **Stable identity:** subject, person, Matter, Investigation, decision,
   source, and capability identities remain distinguishable.
2. **Purpose limitation:** information and context cross only for an explicit,
   bounded receiving responsibility.
3. **Least necessary meaning:** a receiver obtains only the context needed for
   that responsibility; product coherence does not justify universal access.
4. **Authorization preservation:** source access and use constraints remain in
   force downstream; a prior authorized use does not authorize every later use.
5. **Provenance continuity:** origin, derivation, correction, and version
   relationships remain attributable.
6. **Epistemic separation:** Evidence, interpretation, hypothesis, modeled
   content, recommendation, decision, observation, learning, and Knowledge are
   never collapsed.
7. **Uncertainty visibility:** unknown, assumed, contested, stale, incomplete,
   or modeled meaning remains qualified.
8. **Human authority:** accountable judgment, consent, acceptance, correction,
   and consequential action remain explicit and cannot be automated by
   inference.
9. **Sensitivity continuity:** privacy, confidentiality, and professional-safety
   constraints accompany use and may require withholding or interruption.
10. **Quality boundedness:** assistance or transformation is evaluated within
    the owning capability's contract and cannot substitute provider confidence
    for product authority.
11. **Correction without erasure:** corrections remain linked to prior reliance
    so the decision and learning record stays honest.
12. **No authority laundering:** repeated reference, aggregation, composition,
    presentation, automation, or technical success cannot grant authority.

# 13. Failure and Interruption Boundaries

## 13.1 Boundary conditions

| Condition | Required architectural response | Meaning that must not be claimed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown or conflicting identity | Interrupt or qualify the relation until identity is attributable. | Cross-record equivalence or continuity |
| Missing provenance | Treat meaning as unverified information with restricted reliance. | Evidence status or authoritative origin |
| Insufficient authority or access | Decline or bound use and preserve the unmet need. | Permission from availability or prior access |
| Kind ambiguity | Keep the least-promoted applicable kind and require explicit classification. | Evidence, decision, outcome, learning, or Knowledge by inference |
| Missing or stale context | Qualify applicability or interrupt the handoff. | Current relevance or completeness |
| Unresolved uncertainty | Preserve it and constrain downstream reliance. | Certainty from confidence, repetition, or urgency |
| Receiver outside responsibility | Decline acceptance and retain the unresolved responsibility gap. | Ownership reassignment or universal routing authority |
| Conflicting sources or interpretations | Preserve both with ownership and challenge relationships. | Silent reconciliation or chosen truth |
| Recommendation without explicit decision | Stop before execution intent. | Approval or authorization to act |
| Execution response without observation basis | Treat as an unverified report or unresolved return. | Verified outcome, effect, or stabilization |
| Outcome without comparison or adequate Evidence | Preserve residual uncertainty. | Causality, successful change, or learning |
| Learning without promotion authority | Retain bounded learning or candidacy. | Published or approved Knowledge |
| Automation outside bounded contract | Interrupt assistance and return authority to the owning capability or human. | Autonomous scope expansion or ownership |

## 13.2 Failure containment

A boundary failure remains attributable to the relation in which it occurs. It
does not silently mutate upstream meaning, force downstream acceptance, or
spread authority. Capabilities may expose missing responsibility, unusable
context, conflicting meaning, or insufficient authority as an unresolved
condition. M8.3 does not assign technical recovery, retry, compensation,
notification, escalation, or operational incident behavior.

# 15. Architectural Invariants

## 15.1 Information invariants

1. Semantic kind remains explicit across every boundary.
2. Availability does not establish truth, relevance, Evidence, or authority.
3. Source identity and semantic ownership remain attributable.
4. Derivation never replaces origin.
5. Evidence admission is explicit and scope-bounded.
6. Interpretation and hypothesis never overwrite Evidence.
7. Modeled content never becomes observation or prediction by presentation.
8. Recommendation never becomes decision without explicit human judgment.
9. Execution observation never becomes verified effect without applicable
   Evidence and owned verification judgment.
10. Learning never becomes Knowledge without C18 review and promotion.
11. Correction changes current reliance without erasing historical use.
12. Provenance, uncertainty, sensitivity, freshness, and authority limitations
    propagate as applicable.
13. Absence, refusal, interruption, and unknown remain representable and cannot
    be converted into positive meaning.
14. Semantic mutation must be explicit, attributable, and owned; implicit
    semantic mutation is prohibited.

## 15.2 Handoff invariants

1. Every handoff identifies an origin, receiver, semantic kind, bounded subject,
   requested existing responsibility, authority, and non-transfer boundary.
2. Preparation does not create receiver acceptance.
3. Receipt, visibility, silence, elapsed time, automation, or continued use does
   not imply acceptance or approval.
4. Acceptance starts only receiver-owned responsibility and transfers no source
   meaning or authority.
5. A receiver may decline or qualify a handoff without upstream mutation.
6. A returned result remains owned by the receiver for its semantic scope.
7. Orchestration cannot own the work, records, Evidence, or decision of either
   participant.
8. Presentation cannot confer authority or change semantic kind.
9. Automation cannot infer consent, approval, acceptance, or accountable
   judgment.
10. Composition cannot create a universal owner or joint product-wide source.
11. Conflicts and missing responsibility remain explicit findings rather than
    silent reassignment.
12. Failure remains bounded and does not propagate implicit authority.
