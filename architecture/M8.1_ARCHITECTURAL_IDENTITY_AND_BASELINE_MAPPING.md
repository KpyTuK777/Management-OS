# 1. Purpose

M8.1 establishes the architectural identity of Management OS as the object that
all subsequent M8 architecture describes.

The baseline begins with the system and its responsibilities. It then maps the
existing architecture corpus to those responsibilities. Documents are evidence
and authority sources for the architecture; they are not the organizing model of
the system.

M8.1 answers:

- what Management OS is as a system;
- where its architectural boundary lies;
- which enduring layers and responsibilities belong inside that boundary;
- what remains outside it;
- how existing sources govern each responsibility;
- where the current architecture contains gaps, overlaps, or unknowns.

# 2. Architectural Identity

## 2.1 System identity

**Management OS is an owner-governed operational intelligence system that helps
an operations manager turn operational signals into progressively stronger
understanding, explicit human decisions, bounded action, verified outcomes, and
reusable learning while preserving the independent authority of contributing
records and real organizational actors.**

Its enduring purpose is not to accumulate management information or automate
management. Its purpose is to improve operational capability by reducing
cognitive noise, preserving evidence and uncertainty, supporting judgment,
coordinating traceable change, and allowing verified experience to improve
future work.

The system identity is expressed by this architectural sequence:

```text
Operational reality and owner intent
    ↓ observed and contributed through authoritative sources
Operational continuity and bounded context
    ↓ investigated with evidence and explicit uncertainty
Human judgment and decision
    ↓ translated into bounded, source-owned action
Observed outcome and stabilization
    ↓ considered for governed learning
Improved future operational capability
```

This sequence describes architectural responsibility. It is not a new lifecycle
or protocol and does not replace any source-owned state model.

## 2.2 Architectural object

The architectural object is the complete Management OS product system:

- its owner-facing operational workspace;
- its capabilities for capture, context, investigation, reasoning, decision
  support, change coordination, outcome assessment, and learning;
- the authoritative records and governed references those capabilities use;
- bounded AI and automation participation;
- cross-cutting identity, provenance, uncertainty, authority, privacy,
  traceability, and quality responsibilities;
- the realization boundary through which approved architecture may later be
  implemented.

The architectural object is not any single module, aggregate, record, screen,
assistant, model, repository, or document set.

## 2.3 Relationship to the Operational System

The **Operational System** is the bounded real-world network of processes,
people, authority, flows, systems, controls, constraints, dependencies, and
feedback producing relevant outcomes.

Management OS is not that real-world system. Management OS helps the owner
understand and improve it through governed representations, evidence,
investigation, decisions, actions, and learning. The Operational Operating Model
represents relevant operational topology; it does not make Management OS the
organization or transfer real-world authority into software.

## 2.4 Relationship to Operational Matter and Investigation

An Operational Matter preserves continuity for one operational situation.
Operational Investigations are bounded, repeatable work episodes concerning a
Matter. Neither is identical to Management OS:

- Matter supplies enduring situation identity and lifecycle continuity;
- Investigation supplies bounded inquiry, evidence, and reasoning work;
- Management OS supplies the wider product environment in which Matters,
  Investigations, source-owned records, decisions, transitions, learning, and
  routine work remain related without collapsing into one aggregate.

## 2.5 Relationship to Watson and automation

Watson and other automated capabilities are bounded participants inside the
Management OS architecture. They may prepare, organize, retrieve, explain,
compare, challenge, or recommend within approved scope. They do not constitute
the system's identity and cannot become owner, organizational, semantic,
decision, review, transition, or governance authority.

# 3. Architectural Boundary

## 3.1 Inside the boundary

The Management OS architectural boundary contains responsibilities necessary to
provide a coherent owner-governed operational intelligence product:

- interaction through which the owner contributes, inspects, judges, approves,
  corrects, and directs;
- operational situation continuity and bounded work context;
- governed references to authoritative operational, organizational, execution,
  knowledge, and scheduling sources;
- evidence acquisition, classification, provenance, uncertainty, and
  decision-time traceability;
- reasoning support, inquiry, alternative exploration, and readiness support;
- explicit decision preservation and bounded handoff to source-owned action;
- outcome comparison, stabilization assessment, reflection, memory, and
  knowledge-evolution support;
- bounded assistance, recommendation, personalization, and adaptation;
- product-level responsibility allocation across capabilities and platforms;
- cross-cutting identity, access, privacy, safety, explainability, correction,
  revocation, quality, and governance conformance;
- architecture-to-realization boundaries for later separately approved work.

## 3.2 Outside the boundary

The following remain outside Management OS authority even when referenced or
supported by the product:

- the real organization and its Operational Systems;
- organizational roles, reporting lines, decision rights, policies, and
  accountabilities except as governed representations;
- human judgment, approval, responsibility, and real-world action;
- ERP, CRM, HR, finance, quality, project, task, calendar, communication,
  workflow, document, and other external systems of record;
- external people, counterparties, providers, and institutions;
- general management knowledge as independently true merely because the product
  can retrieve or apply it;
- model-provider behavior, infrastructure, repositories, and delivery processes
  as sources of product meaning;
- unapproved inference, prediction, recommendation, or generated content as
  fact, evidence, decision, or authority.

Outside does not mean irrelevant or inaccessible. It means that Management OS
may reference, prepare for, exchange with, or reason about the subject only
through applicable authority and without absorbing its ownership.

## 3.3 Boundary tests

A responsibility belongs inside the architecture only if at least one of these
conditions holds:

1. it is necessary to preserve the owner's coherent end-to-end operational
   experience;
2. it owns or constrains meaning, state, provenance, judgment support, handoff,
   or learning inside the product;
3. it governs how the product references an external authority without taking
   ownership from it;
4. it is a cross-cutting product obligation required across multiple
   capabilities;
5. it defines the boundary between approved product architecture and later
   realization.

A subject remains outside if Management OS merely observes, links, invokes, or
supports it and no approved source assigns the product ownership of its meaning
or decisions.

# 4. Product Layers and Constraint Planes

This model separates enduring product responsibilities from concerns that apply
across the product and from governance that remains external to the product.
The arrangement is a set of architectural views. Its numbering provides stable
references only; it does not express execution order, rank, control, authority,
containment, dependency, or vertical implementation.

```text
External canonical Governance and Evolution constraint plane
  governs architectural change, review, and authority transition
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Cross-cutting Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety,
and Quality plane applies across every product responsibility
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
P1  Owner Experience and Contribution
P2  Operational Continuity and Context
P3  Investigation, Evidence, and Reasoning
P4  Decision, Change, Execution Handoff, and Outcome
P5  Learning, Knowledge, and Operational Memory
P6  Assistance, Recommendation, and Adaptation
  ↔ enduring product responsibility views; no tier or workflow order
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
External operational reality and source authorities
```

The six product layers are mutually related responsibility views, not a stack.
The cross-cutting plane constrains all six without becoming a seventh product
layer. The external governance plane governs change to the architecture and the
authority state of its artifacts; it is not inside Management OS product
architecture.

## P1. Owner Experience and Contribution

Owns the coherent means by which the owner perceives the current situation,
contributes information, requests assistance, inspects reasoning, exercises
judgment, approves or rejects proposals, and maintains continuity across
contexts and platforms.

It does not own operational facts merely because it presents them, and
presentation emphasis cannot become evidence weight or decision authority.

## P2. Operational Continuity and Context

Owns product continuity across operational situations, repeatable work episodes,
current focus, relevant organizational context, relationships, and governed
references to source-owned contributions.

It does not become a universal aggregate, replace external source records, or
turn context into authority.

## P3. Investigation, Evidence, and Reasoning

Owns bounded inquiry, evidence needs, provenance, classification, uncertainty,
reasoning structure, competing explanations, modeled alternatives, and
decision-readiness support.

It does not declare real-world truth, make the owner's decision, or convert
modeled output and historical similarity into evidence.

## P4. Decision, Change, Execution Handoff, and Outcome

Owns preservation of explicit decision context, impact analysis, intended
operating condition, bounded coordination, source-owned execution handoff,
observed-outcome comparison, and stabilization reasoning.

It does not replace execution systems, perform unauthorized action, reconsider
an authoritative decision through impact analysis, or declare effect without
evidence.

## P5. Learning, Knowledge, and Operational Memory

Owns the governed relationship among historical episodes, reflection,
experience patterns, approved reusable knowledge, procedures, and future
relevance.

It does not rewrite history, make recurrence certain, treat prior experience as
current evidence, or promote learning into durable knowledge or procedure
without the owning approval path.

## P6. Assistance, Recommendation, and Adaptation

Owns bounded preparation, retrieval, explanation, inquiry support,
recommendation, approved personalization, and exact approved adaptation.

It does not own product truth, infer consent, take accountable decisions, mutate
source records outside explicit authority, or convert convenience into control.

## Cross-cutting Trust, Identity, Provenance, Privacy, Safety, and Quality Plane

Owns cross-cutting product obligations for stable identity, authorization
context, privacy, provenance, freshness, sensitivity, uncertainty,
explainability, correction, revocation, failure transparency, safety, and
quality evidence.

It applies across every product layer but does not create product,
organizational, or governance authority through technical enforcement or
quality measurement.

## External Canonical Governance and Evolution Constraint Plane

The canonical M7 Governance Architecture externally governs how architectural
work is directed, created, evidenced, independently reviewed, transitioned, and
evolved. It controls architectural-change legitimacy and authority transition;
it does not own Management OS product behavior or product-layer meaning.

This plane is not an internal product layer, product capability, authority
hierarchy, or runtime governance engine. M8.1 maps its constraint relationship
only and does not alter canonical M7 meaning.

# 8. Architectural Assumptions

1. Canonical M7 governance remains the controlling authority for M8 work.
2. Product Vision continues to own product purpose and the human-judgment
   boundary.
3. Existing canonical and approved sources remain authoritative within their
   recorded scopes until separately changed.
4. The Documentation Map is a useful authority lookup but does not itself own
   the meaning of the sources it lists.
5. Management OS remains one product even when responsibilities are allocated
   across modules, contexts, platforms, and external source systems.
6. Operational Matter is the enduring operational-situation identity, not the
   identity of the complete product.
7. The Operational System is real-world and external to product authority.
8. Human judgment, approval, accountability, and organizational authority remain
   outside automation authority.
9. An enduring architectural layer can be valid without implying a technical
   tier, runtime component, team, or delivery unit.
10. Gaps and overlaps may be identified without authorizing their correction.

# 9. Architectural Invariants

1. **One product identity:** Management OS remains one owner-governed
   operational intelligence system; no capability becomes a parallel product or
   universal authority.
2. **Reality remains external:** the real Operational System, organization, and
   outcomes cannot be reduced to or controlled by their software representation.
3. **Human judgment remains authoritative:** assistance, confidence, recurrence,
   urgency, execution success, and elapsed time cannot imply approval.
4. **Sources retain ownership:** composition, projection, retrieval, and
   reference do not transfer meaning, state, validation, mutation, or retention
   authority.
5. **Evidence remains distinct:** evidence, interpretation, hypothesis, modeled
   output, recommendation, decision, and outcome remain distinguishable.
6. **Situation continuity remains unique:** one Operational Matter identity
   preserves one operational situation across repeatable Investigations and
